# B01.CH04.P05 — Transition regimes: timing, phasing, exceptions, and phase-down paths

This paragraph specifies how a cap regime (or functional equivalents) is introduced without collapsing execution capacity, while preventing
transition design from becoming a permanent bypass surface. In OBA terms, a transition is **not** a “political rollout plan”; it is an
enforcement-critical mechanism that determines whether **caps bind**, whether **E is prevented from reconstituting via concentration**, and
whether **ΔM routing supports commons capacity rather than asset support**.

The transition regime must be rule-bound, auditable, and resistant to delay tactics. Where evidence is missing or enforcement rails are not
present, the system fails closed: compatibility claims are suspended and scaling is blocked until the transition rails are made executable.

---

## B01.CH04.P05.S01 — Sequencing: why the order (E reduction, GROSS≈NET, caps, ΔM→commons) matters

Sequencing determines whether the system reduces immediate burden pressure and extraction incentives before tightening accumulation bounds.

### Required sequencing logic (minimum; binding)
- **E reduction first-order:** begin dismantling toll layers and fee-stacks early to reduce incentives for evasion and sabotage.
- **GROSS≈NET stabilization early:** prioritize tail stabilization (share_below_alpha, p10/p25 R/B) so cap enforcement is not framed as
  “pure punishment” while burdens remain high.
- **Caps with audit rails:** do not tighten caps beyond enforceability; caps without consolidation and disclosure create selective enforcement
  and immediate bypass innovation.
- **ΔM→commons as bottleneck guard:** route monetary space into commons capex/opex to prevent transition-induced scarcity from regenerating
  E (housing/energy/care bottlenecks become toll surfaces if capacity is not built).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If caps tighten while essentials burdens (L) rise and E layers remain intact, the transition is mis-sequenced and must be redesigned.
- If ΔM routing primarily supports assets during transition, cap effectiveness will be undermined via price inflation and concentration.

## B01.CH04.P05.S02 — Existing wealth: grandfathering versus phase-down — criteria for legitimacy and effectiveness

Existing concentrations are the main test of whether W-MAX is real or symbolic. The transition must define how current exceedance is treated.

### Admissible treatment families (minimum; binding)
- **Phase-down (default admissible path):** exceedance is reduced over a defined schedule using standardized remedies (divestment,
  conversion, skimming to commons, expiry of certain claims).
- **Grandfathering (narrow admissibility):** allowed only if (i) it does not preserve durable gate power, (ii) it is time-bounded, and
  (iii) it includes full disclosure and consolidation (UBC) so it cannot function as an opacity shield.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Broad grandfathering that leaves top concentration intact is non-conforming (caps become “for the future only”).
- Opaque positions cannot be grandfathered: missing disclosure triggers conservative presumptions and/or escalation.

## B01.CH04.P05.S03 — Time paths: immediate cap, multi-year phase-down, or trigger-based transition (e.g., in crisis)

The regime must choose a time path that matches enforcement capacity while remaining binding.

### Permissible time paths (minimum; binding)
- **Immediate cap:** binding from start with automatic remedies. Requires strong valuation and enforcement capacity.
- **Multi-year phase-down:** fixed milestones and mandatory progress, with automatic consequences for non-compliance.
- **Trigger-based tightening:** pre-defined triggers (concentration metrics, systemic risk, crisis thresholds) that tighten caps; triggers must
  be rule-defined, not discretionary.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- All time paths MUST include: milestones, monitoring, and automatic consequences (no “we will review later”).
- Delay-by-litigation is not a valid compliance mode; disputed amounts may be held in escrow/lockbox where relevant.

## B01.CH04.P05.S04 — Emergency exception law under transition: emergency regimes without permanent “holes” in caps

Emergency exceptions are a predictable cap-erosion vector and must be designed as controlled, temporary deviations.

### Required emergency exception rails (minimum; binding)
- **Trigger criteria:** measurable activation thresholds and published evidence for activation.
- **Scope caps:** narrow scope by claim type, function, and duration; no open-ended categories.
- **Sunset + unwind:** automatic expiry and a mandatory unwind plan; renewals require re-trigger evidence.
- **Audit + publication:** exception registers (aggregated) including counts, categories, duration, and reasons.
- **No excess-return guarantees:** emergency measures may not create durable private toll positions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any emergency regime without trigger→action→sunset publication is non-conforming.
- Renewals without re-trigger evidence constitute permanent emergency drift and trigger compatibility escalation.

## B01.CH04.P05.S05 — Shock robustness: preventing the transition regime itself from maximizing capital flight/instability

Transition design must prevent predictable destabilization routes that would be used as political veto mechanisms.

### Required robustness controls (minimum; binding)
- **Predictable rules:** stable, published bases and timelines reduce “panic exit” incentives.
- **Structured remedies:** where forced liquidation would create systemic instability, use rule-bound phase-down, conversion, or escrow
  mechanisms rather than discretionary deals.
- **Credit/commons continuity:** ensure commons capacity build-up and productive credit routing so bottlenecks do not intensify into new E.
- **Cross-border coherence:** reporting and exit rules must be specified to prevent mobility-based bypass during transition.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Shock robustness cannot be used to justify non-binding caps. It justifies controlled, rule-bound mechanics, not carve-outs.

## B01.CH04.P05.S06 — Communication and enforcement: predictability, transparency, and the sanction path

Caps fail when enforcement is discretionary, opaque, or selectively applied. The transition must therefore publish enforcement rails.

### Required enforcement publication (minimum; binding)
- **Predictability pack:** definitions, valuation methods, consolidation rules, phase-down paths, milestones.
- **Transparency surface:** aggregated exceedance statistics, remedy choices, exception counts, audit coverage.
- **Sanction path:** escalation ladder (non-compliance → presumptions → penalties → forced remedies) with deadlines and appeal rules.
- **Due process without paralysis:** appeals exist, but do not suspend enforcement under opacity or flight risk; escrow/lockbox may apply.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If enforcement cannot be made uniform and auditable, the system must fail-closed: no cap-compatibility claim and no scaling until fixed.
