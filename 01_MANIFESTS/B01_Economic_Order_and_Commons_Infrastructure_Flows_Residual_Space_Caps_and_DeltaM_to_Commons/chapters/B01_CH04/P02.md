# B01.CH04.P02 — W-MAX: design variants (hard cap, progressive confiscatory edge, time-bound claims)

This paragraph specifies the admissible design space for **W-MAX**: mechanisms that bound wealth/claims accumulation in a way that is enforceable, avoidance-robust, and compatible with reallocation to commons.

## B01.CH04.P02.S01 — Hard cap: definition, threshold structure, and enforceability

A **hard cap** is an explicit upper bound on measured wealth (W) for the relevant unit (typically UBC-consolidated).

Minimum design requirements:

- **Defined base:** what counts in W (claims stack + relevant control/exposure), with look-through and valuation rules.
- **Threshold structure:** a clear boundary (W ≤ W-MAX) with defined treatment for proximity bands if used.
- **Enforcement mechanism:** an automatic remedy upon exceedance that does not depend on discretionary negotiation.
- **Valuation discipline:** rules for illiquid assets, private holdings, IP, concessions, and platform rights, including conservative presumptions under opacity.
- **Anti-bypass:** prohibition or neutralization of steering wealth into off-base forms (synthetics, nominee structures, “non-asset” gate rights).

Enforceability requires that the system can detect exceedance without relying solely on self-reporting, and can impose remedies through traceable procedures.

## B01.CH04.P02.S02 — Progressive edge: confiscatory marginal rates as a cap-equivalent (criteria for true cap effectiveness)

A **progressive confiscatory edge** is an admissible cap-equivalent only if it behaves like a cap in practice, i.e., it creates a **de facto upper bound** over time.

Criteria for true cap effectiveness:

- **Marginal rate must be sufficiently binding:** above a defined threshold, the marginal extraction must be high enough that accumulation does not continue materially in net terms.
- **No avoidance asymmetry:** the edge must be paired with consolidation and anti-avoidance so that shifting into non-taxed forms does not restore accumulation.
- **Time consistency:** the edge must be stable against political erosion and exception creep; otherwise it is not cap-equivalent.
- **Outcome evidence:** the system must publish cap-effectiveness indicators (exceedance distribution, concentration measures) showing that the edge produces bounded accumulation.

A progressive edge that merely “slows” growth but does not bound accumulation is not considered cap-equivalent.

## B01.CH04.P02.S03 — Time-bound claims: time limits on certain ownership rights/claims (renewal, expiry, buy-back)

A **time-bound claims** regime limits accumulation by imposing time constraints on certain rights, such as:

- **expiry / sunset:** rights lapse after a term unless renewed under defined conditions,
- **renewal discipline:** renewal requires meeting conditions (e.g., contestability, bounded returns, non-extractiveness),
- **buy-back / conversion:** automatic conversion or buy-back options that prevent perpetual private toll positions.

Time-bounded regimes are admissible when they:

- apply to claim forms that otherwise generate durable tolls (concessions, certain IP structures, platform gate rights),
- have non-discretionary renewal/expiry procedures,
- and include valuation and compensation rules that do not recreate an accumulation bypass.

## B01.CH04.P02.S04 — Exception categories: when and why exceptions can exist (and how abuse is prevented)

Exceptions to W-MAX are a primary cap-erosion vector and are therefore permitted only under strict constraints.

Permissible exception rationale categories (high-level):

- **Operational necessity:** specific asset forms that are required to operate productive capacity or commons infrastructure and cannot be decomposed without breaking function.
- **Commons stewardship:** assets held under a commons regime with inalienability and non-private benefit conditions (i.e., not personal wealth).
- **Temporary transition exceptions:** narrow, time-bounded exceptions during phase-down transitions, with explicit expiry.

Abuse prevention requirements:

- **No discretionary carve-outs:** exceptions must be rule-defined, not negotiated.
- **Transparency:** exceptions must be published in aggregate with counts, categories, and expiry structure.
- **Anti-self-benefit:** exceptions must not enable private consumption or private toll extraction under exception cover.
- **Sunset and review:** every exception category has a mandatory sunset and periodic review; exception expansion triggers a compatibility alarm.

## B01.CH04.P02.S05 — Reallocation path: what happens upon exceedance (skimming → commons, phase-down periods, repair paths)

W-MAX must specify a deterministic remedy path for exceedance. Minimum remedy families:

- **Skimming → commons:** the excess portion (or its equivalent) is redirected to commons build-up/maintenance funds under traceable governance.
- **Phase-down periods:** where immediate liquidation is destabilizing, a defined phase-down window applies with binding milestones and monitoring.
- **Repair paths:** standardized options to return under the cap without bespoke deals (divestment schedules, conversion to commons holdings, restructuring of control rights).

Remedy design constraints:

- remedies must not reintroduce extraction (no “fee for exemption” schemes),
- remedies must preserve auditability and enforceability,
- and remedies must not permit indefinite exceedance via delay tactics.

## B01.CH04.P02.S06 — Interaction with entrepreneurship: start/scale-up routes within a cap regime (without carve-outs that break the cap)

W-MAX must be compatible with productive entrepreneurship without creating cap-breaking carve-outs.

Required design elements:

- **Predictable founder pathways:** clear rules for how equity appreciation, control rights, and liquidity events are treated under the cap (including timing and valuation).
- **No perpetual dominance:** founder control structures and platform gate rights are not exempt from cap logic; control without title remains within the accumulation boundary.
- **Conversion options:** standardized pathways to convert private exceedance into commons-aligned forms (e.g., conversion to stewardship models) where appropriate, without bespoke negotiation.
- **Anti-avoidance continuity:** entrepreneurship is a common justification for avoidance structures; therefore founder routes must be paired with the same consolidation and disclosure rules as all other positions.

The principle is: innovation and scaling can occur, but **bounded accumulation remains binding**. The system permits productive formation while preventing the conversion of productive growth into durable rentier dominance.
