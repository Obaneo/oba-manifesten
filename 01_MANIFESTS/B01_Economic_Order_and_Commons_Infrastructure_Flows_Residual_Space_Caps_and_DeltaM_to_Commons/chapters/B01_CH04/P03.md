# B01.CH04.P03 — I-MAX: design variants (top incomes, bonus structures, carried interest, options)

This paragraph specifies the admissible design space for **I-MAX**: mechanisms that bound top income extraction and prevent income-based dominance from bypassing the cap regime through relabeling, deferral, or vehicle routing.

## B01.CH04.P03.S01 — Definition of “income”: wages, bonuses, dividends, interest, capital gains, in-kind benefits

**Income (I)** is defined as the set of value inflows attributable to a subject (individual/household/UBC) within the time window, regardless of label. The income base must include, at minimum:

- **Wages and salaries**
- **Bonuses and variable pay**
- **Dividends and distributions**
- **Interest and yield-like returns**
- **Realized capital gains** (where the definition set includes them as income events)
- **Royalties/fees** (where they function as personal income streams)
- **In-kind benefits and perks** with measurable value (housing, transport, services, expense accounts) where they substitute for income

A core anti-avoidance rule applies: if a flow functions as personal compensation or personal consumption power, it belongs in the I base unless explicitly excluded with a published rationale and a non-bypass proof.

## B01.CH04.P03.S02 — Top-income cap: absolute cap versus ratio-cap (e.g., top/median, top/floor) and criteria

I-MAX can be implemented as:

- **Absolute cap:** a fixed upper limit on annual (or windowed) income.
- **Ratio cap:** a bound expressed relative to a distribution reference, e.g.:
  - top/median,
  - top/floor,
  - top/p10 (or other declared reference).

Admissibility criteria for either form:

- **Bindingness:** the cap must actually bind at the top; symbolic thresholds are non-conforming.
- **Measurability:** the base must be observable and auditable with consolidated attribution.
- **Stability under manipulation:** the cap cannot be bypassed by shifting into untaxed or uncounted forms (perks, vehicles, deferral, capital gains relabeling).
- **Update discipline (for ratio caps):** the reference statistic must be defined and published with method and revision policy, to prevent political or definitional drift.

## B01.CH04.P03.S03 — Variable pay: bonus pools, performance pay, retention awards — re-mapping to I (anti-avoidance)

Variable pay is a primary bypass channel and must be fully mapped back into I.

Minimum rules:

- **All-in compensation:** bonuses, commissions, performance pay, retention awards, and similar instruments are included in the I base.
- **No “shadow compensation”:** expense accounts, carried travel, housing, club memberships, and non-cash benefits that substitute consumption are valued and included.
- **Deferral neutrality:** deferral does not remove inclusion; it shifts timing only under explicit, audited timing rules.
- **Relabeling resistance:** consultancy fees, licensing payments, and “service contracts” that function as compensation are requalified into I where control/benefit tests indicate personal income.

The classification is functional: if it buys personal consumption power or personal wealth build-up, it is income for I-MAX purposes.

## B01.CH04.P03.S04 — Carried interest/options: classification and valuation rules (moment of attribution)

Carried interest, options, RSUs, and similar instruments are treated as income events under explicit valuation and timing rules to prevent systematic deferral.

Minimum admissible approaches must define:

- **Attribution moment:** when the income is counted (grant, vest, exercise, realization), with rationale and anti-gaming controls.
- **Valuation method:** fair value or other declared method, including treatment of illiquidity and private valuations.
- **Look-through:** where carry is routed via partnerships, funds, or SPVs, the income is attributed to the ultimate beneficiary/controller.
- **Anti-timing manipulation:** rules to prevent deliberately timing exercises/realizations to evade I-MAX (e.g., spreading across vehicles, delaying recognition, swapping instruments).

If valuation is obstructed (opaque private marks, side letters), conservative presumptions and/or escrow/lockbox mechanisms apply under fail-closed discipline.

## B01.CH04.P03.S05 — Deferred/overseas income: deferred, foreign, and vehicle-based income (consolidation)

Deferred and cross-border routing is treated as a standard avoidance family.

Minimum requirements:

- **Deferred compensation inclusion:** deferred bonuses, phantom equity, and similar instruments remain included in the I base under timing rules that prevent indefinite postponement.
- **Foreign income consolidation:** foreign-sourced income is included under residence/UBC rules, subject to declared cross-border principles.
- **Vehicle-based income look-through:** income routed through companies, trusts, foundations, partnerships, or nominee structures is attributed to the ultimate beneficiary/controller when control/benefit tests are met.
- **Opacity response:** missing disclosure triggers conservative presumptions; “unknown” is not grounds for exclusion.

The objective is to make “income location engineering” non-profitable by default.

## B01.CH04.P03.S06 — Interaction W-MAX/I-MAX: how income tolls do not escape via W (and vice versa)

W-MAX and I-MAX must be co-designed to prevent cross-cap arbitrage:

- **I→W steering:** converting income into wealth forms (asset appreciation, retained earnings, vehicle holdings) must not allow income power to evade I-MAX; timing and attribution rules must capture compensation-like gains.
- **W→I steering:** using wealth to generate disguised income (loans against assets, related-party fees, synthetic cashflows) must not evade I-MAX and must be visible in attribution.
- **Unified consolidation (UBC):** both caps rely on the same control/benefit consolidation layer; inconsistencies create bypass routes.
- **No-arbitrage enforcement:** where classification between I and W is ambiguous, conservative mapping rules apply (fail-closed), and the burden of proof lies with the party benefitting from the ambiguity.

The system must treat W and I as coupled surfaces: bounding only one creates a predictable escape path through the other.
