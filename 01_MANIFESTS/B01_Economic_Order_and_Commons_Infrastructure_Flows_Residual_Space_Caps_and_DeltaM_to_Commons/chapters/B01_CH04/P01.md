# B01.CH04.P01 — What accumulation is in OBA terms (wealth, claims, access, power)

This paragraph defines accumulation as an economic object in OBA: not merely “having money”, but holding stacked claims and gate positions that confer durable control over flows, access, and options.

## B01.CH04.P01.S01 — Wealth as a claims stack: financial assets, real assets, IP, concessions, and platform rights

In OBA, **wealth (W)** is treated as a **claims stack**: a bundle of enforceable rights that can generate cashflows or confer control. The wealth base is therefore broader than liquid financial assets.

Minimum wealth-base families (non-exhaustive):

- **Financial assets:** equities, bonds, funds, deposits, loans receivable, structured products.
- **Real assets:** land and buildings, infrastructure ownership stakes, productive capital where held as assets.
- **IP and licensing claims:** patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets insofar as they generate enforceable rents or exclusion.
- **Concessions and permits:** long-term rights to operate or gate essential services (utilities, transport, spectrum, mining rights), including embedded renewal privileges.
- **Platform rights:** ownership stakes and/or contractual rights that grant take rates, ranking control, API gating, access to marketplaces, or control of identity/payment rails.

Wealth measurement must treat these as economically relevant even when they are held through vehicles, layered contracts, or nominal intermediaries.

## B01.CH04.P01.S02 — Control and access: economic power without legal ownership (control rights)

Accumulation is not only cashflow rights; it includes **control rights** that can be exercised without owning the underlying asset. Economic power can be achieved via:

- **governance control:** voting rights, board control, veto rights, golden shares, special control classes;
- **contractual control:** exclusivity, termination rights, covenants, step-in rights, franchising control, “most favored nation” clauses;
- **platform gate control:** the ability to set access conditions, ranking rules, take rates, or deplatform;
- **infrastructure gate control:** control over bottlenecks (rails, grids, logistics hubs, identity systems) that enables toll setting.

Therefore, the economic layer requires a **control/account access dimension** alongside ownership. Cap enforcement and anti-avoidance must address “control without title” as a first-class bypass route.

## B01.CH04.P01.S03 — Accumulation versus use: distinguishing productive deployment from rent-seeking holding

OBA distinguishes between:

- **Productive deployment:** using assets and capacity to produce output, deliver services, or maintain commons infrastructure (aligned with P and/or C outcomes).
- **Rent-seeking holding:** holding claims or gate rights primarily to extract tolls from access or from others’ production without proportional functional contribution (aligned with E).

This is not a moral distinction; it is a functional one. The same object (e.g., property, IP, network rights) can be:
- deployed to expand capacity and reduce burdens (P/C-aligned), or
- structured as a bottleneck to harvest scarcity premiums (E-aligned).

Cap design therefore must:
- bound the concentration of claims and gate positions,
- and prevent the conversion of productive deployment into durable rent-seeking dominance via legal engineering.

## B01.CH04.P01.S04 — Unit of measurement: individual, household, entity, and “ultimate beneficial control” (UBC)

Accumulation measurement cannot rely on a single legal unit, because accumulation is commonly fragmented across households and vehicles. Book 1 therefore treats the measurement unit as a **consolidated control unit**.

Minimum units used:

- **Individual:** baseline subject for W and I measurement where feasible.
- **Household:** relevant where wealth and income are pooled and where control is exercised jointly.
- **Entity:** legal persons (companies, trusts, foundations, SPVs) as accounting containers.
- **Ultimate beneficial control (UBC):** the consolidated economic unit that controls and benefits from claims and gate positions, regardless of legal form.

UBC requires look-through and economic-exposure rules:
- beneficial control over entities and assets,
- attribution of embedded exposure (synthetics, side letters),
- and consolidation across nominee arrangements.

If UBC cannot be established due to opacity or obstruction, the system must fail closed: classification and cap enforcement cannot grant “clean” status by default; instead, conservative presumptions and escalation are triggered.
