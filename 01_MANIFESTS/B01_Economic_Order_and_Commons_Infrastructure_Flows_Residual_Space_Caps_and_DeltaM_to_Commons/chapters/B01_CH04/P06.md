# B01.CH04.P06 — Anti-avoidance: reclassification, offshore, trusts/holdings, derivatives, tokenization

This paragraph specifies the minimum anti-avoidance rails required for W-MAX/I-MAX (and functional equivalents) to remain binding under
predictable gaming pressure. “Caps” are not a number; they are a **closure property** across legal, technical, and financial relabeling.
Therefore anti-avoidance is not an add-on: it is a structural requirement.

The rules below are expressed as admissibility and enforcement principles. Detailed schema, reporting formats, and legal templates belong in
the Codex (Book X), but the binding logic is fixed here.

---

## B01.CH04.P06.S01 — Reclassification: income→wealth, wealth→business, private→foundation — standard avoidance routes

Avoidance frequently occurs by changing labels while preserving economic exposure and control.

### Required anti-reclassification rules (minimum; binding)
- **Substance-over-form:** classify by economic function and exposure, not by legal label.
- **Income→wealth steering:** compensation-like flows routed into asset appreciation, retained earnings, or “investment vehicles” remain subject
  to I-MAX timing/attribution rules and cannot escape by deferral or relabeling.
- **Wealth→business steering:** shifting personal wealth into operating entities, SPVs, or “family offices” remains within W-MAX under UBC
  consolidation when control and benefit persist.
- **Private→foundation steering:** foundations/trust-like entities do not remove assets from the cap base when ultimate control/benefit is
  retained through appointment powers, benefit channels, or de facto governance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Where classification is ambiguous, apply conservative mapping and require the beneficiary of ambiguity to carry the burden of proof.
- Repeated reclassification patterns trigger escalation and presumptive treatment.

## B01.CH04.P06.S02 — Offshore and jurisdictions: beneficial ownership, CFC-like consolidation principles, exit routes

Cross-border routing is a primary bypass surface for both measurement and enforcement.

### Required cross-border rails (minimum; binding)
- **Beneficial ownership disclosure:** ownership/control must be traceable to ultimate beneficial control (UBC) across jurisdictions.
- **CFC-like consolidation logic:** where controlled foreign entities exist, their assets and income are consolidated into the relevant UBC base
  for W and I measurement.
- **Economic presence neutrality:** “mailbox” residence or minimal substance cannot be used to sever consolidation where control/benefit remains.
- **Exit route discipline:** relocation and re-domiciliation events must trigger defined reporting and settlement mechanisms so that exit is not a
  free bypass path.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Under non-cooperation or missing disclosure, fail-closed applies: conservative presumptions and enforcement escalation are triggered rather
  than granting exclusion by default.

## B01.CH04.P06.S03 — Trusts/holdings/vehicles: UBC rules and “look-through” valuation

Vehicles are used to fragment ownership, obscure valuation, and split control from benefit.

### Required vehicle rules (minimum; binding)
- **Look-through by default:** holdings, SPVs, partnerships, trust-like and foundation-like structures are treated as transparent for cap
  measurement when UBC control/benefit tests are met.
- **Control tests:** appointment/removal powers, veto rights, governance dominance, contractual step-in rights, or de facto control imply UBC.
- **Benefit tests:** preferential distributions, side letters, carried allocations, redemption rights, or consumption benefits imply UBC benefit.
- **Look-through valuation:** value is attributed based on economic exposure (not only book value), with conservative valuation under opacity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a vehicle structure prevents valuation or control attribution, the system must not “pause” the cap; it must apply conservative
  presumptions and/or require escrow/lockbox pending resolution.

## B01.CH04.P06.S04 — Derivatives and synthetic positions: exposure as ownership (economic exposure test)

Derivatives and structured products can replicate ownership without title and are used to bypass W-MAX and I-MAX attribution.

### Required exposure rules (minimum; binding)
- **Economic exposure test:** if a position delivers substantially the same upside/downside as owning the underlying asset or cashflow stream,
  it is treated as ownership-equivalent for cap measurement.
- **Embedded leverage and total return:** TRS, options, CFDs, and similar instruments must be consolidated into exposure measures.
- **Look-through to counterparties:** where exposure is routed via intermediaries, attribute to the ultimate controller/beneficiary.
- **Margin/collateral linkage:** collateral and margin arrangements that create effective control or priority claims are included in exposure
  consolidation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Opaque derivative books trigger heightened audit and conservative exposure estimation; missing position disclosure is not grounds for
  exclusion.

## B01.CH04.P06.S05 — Tokenization and digital claims: valuation, custody, and “wrapped ownership” detection

Digital claim forms can fragment, repackage, and obscure ownership and transfer channels.

### Required digital-claims rails (minimum; binding)
- **Valuation rules:** tokenized claims are valued using declared, auditable methods; volatility and liquidity constraints are explicitly
  handled.
- **Custody and control:** custody providers, multi-sig arrangements, and beneficial control of wallets/keys are treated as control surfaces.
- **Wrapped ownership detection:** tokens that represent claims on underlying assets (wrapped equities, synthetic exposure, yield-bearing tokens)
  are consolidated into the relevant W/I bases via the economic exposure test.
- **Transfer traceability:** required reporting must capture transfers that materially affect cap status, without requiring public person-level
  surveillance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If custody/control cannot be established, fail-closed applies: conservative presumptions, restricted recognition, and escalation.

## B01.CH04.P06.S06 — Enforcement toolkit: reporting duties, audit triggers, sanctions, and reallocation to commons

Anti-avoidance requires a minimal enforcement toolkit that is rule-bound and auditable.

### Required toolkit elements (minimum; binding)
- **Reporting duties:** periodic disclosures of ownership, control, exposure, vehicles, and material transfers (domestic and cross-border),
  keyed to UBC.
- **Audit triggers:** automatic triggers for forensics (structure complexity spikes, sudden relocations, synthetic exposure growth, repeated
  exceptions, persistent valuation disputes).
- **Sanctions:** escalating consequences for non-disclosure, obstruction, misreporting, or sham structures (including presumptive valuation and
  penalty multipliers).
- **Escrow/lockbox mechanisms:** where disputes exist, value is secured so appeal cannot function as a delay bypass.
- **Reallocation to commons:** exceedance remedies and sanctions must route outcomes into a non-private sink compatible with the order, not into
  private carve-out bargains.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Without enforceable reporting, triggers, and sanctions, caps cannot be claimed as binding. The system must fail-closed: no compatibility
  label and no scaling until the enforcement toolkit exists and is executable.
