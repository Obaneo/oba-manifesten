# B01.CH04.P04 — Functional equivalents: when “no cap” can still produce a cap effect (criteria)

This paragraph defines when a system without an explicit W-MAX or I-MAX can still qualify as cap-compatible because it implements a **functional equivalent** that demonstrably bounds accumulation over time.

## B01.CH04.P04.S01 — Definition “functional equivalent”: demonstrably bounding accumulation over time

A **functional equivalent** is a mechanism that, without using the label “cap”, produces the same operative effect:

- accumulation is **bounded** (not merely slowed),
- concentration cannot grow without triggering enforced remedies,
- and control/claim dominance cannot persist indefinitely.

The definition is outcome- and enforcement-based: an equivalent must be able to demonstrate that accumulation converges to an upper bound under realistic behavior and standard avoidance pressure.

## B01.CH04.P04.S02 — Criteria set: (i) measurability, (ii) avoidance robustness, (iii) cap effectiveness, (iv) reallocation to commons

To count as a functional equivalent, a mechanism must satisfy all of the following criteria:

1. **Measurability:**  
   The mechanism must operate on measurable bases (claims, exposure, control rights) with published definitions, methods, and revision policy. If it cannot be measured, it cannot be equivalent.

2. **Avoidance robustness:**  
   The mechanism must remain binding under standard avoidance families (reclassification, vehicles, offshore routing, synthetics, nominee control). If it is easily bypassed by form changes, it is not equivalent.

3. **Cap effectiveness (de facto upper bound):**  
   The mechanism must create an enforceable boundary effect: beyond a point, further accumulation is prevented or automatically neutralized (via divestment, conversion, expiry, confiscatory edge, or other binding remedy). “Lower growth” is insufficient.

4. **Reallocation to commons (or equivalent public benefit sink):**  
   Where the mechanism neutralizes exceedance, the excess must be routed into a non-private sink compatible with the order (typically commons build-up/maintenance) rather than redistributed back into private accumulation channels.

If any criterion fails, the mechanism is not a cap-equivalent and cannot substitute for W-MAX/I-MAX.

## B01.CH04.P04.S03 — Examples (categories): redesign of ownership rights, concessions with expiry, anti-monopoly split rules

Functional equivalents often arise from structural design rather than explicit cap numbers. Admissible example categories include:

- **Redesign of ownership rights:** converting perpetual claims into stewardship rights with bounded returns, transfer constraints, or mandatory conversion triggers that prevent indefinite private dominance.
- **Concessions with expiry and renewal discipline:** essential-service rights that expire and can only be renewed under contestable conditions with bounded returns, transparency, and anti-capture governance.
- **Anti-monopoly split rules with automatic remedies:** structural deconcentration rules that prevent sustained dominance (including platform gate power) by mandatory separation, interoperability requirements, and enforced divestment when thresholds are crossed.

These are examples of form; they qualify only if they satisfy the criteria set above (measurable, avoidance-robust, cap-effective, with reallocation sink).

## B01.CH04.P04.S04 — Forbidden pseudo-equivalents: measures that only “slow down” without enforcing an upper bound

The following do not qualify as functional equivalents:

- **Soft taxes without binding edge:** progressive measures that reduce net growth but still allow indefinite accumulation.
- **Voluntary codes or “best practices”:** non-enforced commitments, ESG-style pledges, or reporting-only regimes.
- **Temporary moratoria without permanence discipline:** time-limited measures that can be waited out without structural change.
- **Complexity-based friction:** making accumulation harder via paperwork or compliance burdens without actually bounding it (often generates new extraction ecosystems).
- **Discretionary exception regimes:** mechanisms that rely on case-by-case approvals or political bargaining (capture-prone).

A valid equivalent must bind without discretion and without being defeatable by waiting, relabeling, or structural arbitrage.

## B01.CH04.P04.S05 — Compatibility test: how to assess a new equivalent without semantic drift

Any proposed functional equivalent must pass a standardized compatibility procedure to prevent semantic drift:

1. **Mapping:** map the mechanism explicitly to W and/or I accumulation surfaces and specify which forms of claims/control it constrains.
2. **Indicators:** specify the measurable indicators that show cap effectiveness (exceedance distribution, concentration metrics, exposure consolidation).
3. **Avoidance stress test:** demonstrate behavior under standard avoidance families; specify detection and remedies.
4. **Enforcement path:** define the automatic remedy upon threshold crossing or dominance detection (no discretionary negotiations).
5. **Reallocation sink:** specify where exceedance value is routed (commons build/maintenance or equivalent), with traceability.
6. **Versioning:** publish definition-set ID, method ID, and revision policy; specify restatements if definitions change.

If any step is missing or relies on black-box justification, the proposal fails closed and cannot be labeled cap-equivalent.
