# B01.CH05.P01 — Typology of parasitic flows (interest/debt extraction, rent, monopoly, platform tolls, arbitrage)

This paragraph defines the minimum typology of parasitic (extractive) flow families that the economic layer must be able to identify,
measure, and phase down. The typology is functional: flows are classified by how they are generated (toll, gate, claim stack, lock-in),
not by the rhetoric used to justify them.

The typology is not exhaustive, but it is binding as a minimum. If an implementation cannot detect these families, it cannot credibly claim
E reduction.

---

## B01.CH05.P01.S01 — Interest/debt extraction: structural debt as a tollbooth (claims on future income)

Debt becomes parasitic when it functions as a durable toll gate on future income rather than as a bounded financing instrument.

**Core mechanism**
- A claim on future income is created and maintained such that repayment depends on continued rollover, penalties, or structural dependence,
  rather than on a productive project’s cashflow capacity.

**Typical signals**
- persistent high spreads relative to funding cost and risk,
- fee cascades (origination fees, servicing fees, penalty interest, late fees) that dominate the repayment profile,
- lock-in clauses (refinancing friction, cross-default, forced bundling),
- debt used to monetize scarcity in essentials (housing/energy/health) where exit is weak.

The phase-down objective is not “no debt”, but eliminating debt structures that rely on dependency and cascades as the profit engine.

---

## B01.CH05.P01.S02 — Rent and land rent: payment for access to scarce essentials (housing/location)

Rent becomes parasitic when it is a toll extracted from scarcity and block power rather than a cost-covering stewardship payment.

**Core mechanism**
- Payment is demanded primarily because access to a scarce essential (shelter, location) is controlled, not because proportional
  maintenance/service is being delivered.

**Typical signals**
- sustained rent levels disconnected from maintenance cost and quality,
- rent increases tracking scarcity premiums rather than service improvements,
- concentrated ownership and portfolio strategies that restrict effective supply,
- contract clamp (penalties, blacklisting, deposit capture, moving friction).

The phase-down objective is to collapse scarcity-toll behavior through capacity build-up, contestability, and governance rules, not to
micromanage preferences.

---

## B01.CH05.P01.S03 — Monopoly/oligopoly rent: pricing above competitive / functional bands

Monopoly rent is the extraction of price above functional cost bands sustained by market power rather than by superior production.

**Core mechanism**
- A supplier (or coordinated set) can set price above the competitive/functional range due to concentration, network control, regulation
  capture, or switching barriers.

**Typical signals**
- persistent excess margins without corresponding quality or cost justification,
- pricing that rises during shocks beyond cost movement,
- bundling and tying that prevents effective comparison,
- refusal to interoperate or allow substitution.

Phase-down mechanisms target market structure (splitting/interoperability), price discipline (tariffs/ceilings where structural), and
commons/public options in natural monopoly domains.

---

## B01.CH05.P01.S04 — Platform tolls and lock-in: access to networks/markets as a private gate

Platform tolls arise when a private gate controls access to a marketplace, audience, or critical network, enabling take-rate extraction and
behavioral control.

**Core mechanism**
- A gatekeeper charges for access to a network with strong lock-in (network effects, identity/payment coupling, ranking control) and can raise
  take rates without losing participation.

**Typical signals**
- rising take rates and fee-stack depth (payment + platform + ads + compliance layers),
- ranking opacity and pay-to-reach dynamics,
- API gating, portability obstruction, and data lock-in,
- deplatforming/block power without contestable due process.

Phase-down targets interoperability, portability, take-rate discipline, unbundling, and (where necessary) public options.

---

## B01.CH05.P01.S05 — Arbitrage and regulatory shopping: profit from rule differences rather than production

Arbitrage becomes parasitic when profits are primarily generated by exploiting rule gaps, timing differences, or jurisdictional mismatches,
without adding productive value.

**Core mechanism**
- Value is extracted by moving labels, locations, or contract forms to capture differential treatment, rather than by producing output or
  delivering services.

**Typical signals**
- complex routing through jurisdictions and vehicles with minimal functional substance,
- profit patterns disconnected from operational presence,
- repeated restructurings around thresholds, reporting windows, or definition boundaries,
- “innovation” narratives that coincide with opacity and enforcement friction.

Phase-down requires consolidation rules, denial of benefits for sham structures, and automatic presumptions under opacity.

---

## B01.CH05.P01.S06 — Hybrid flows: combinations and stacking (e.g., platform + finance + IP)

Parasitic extraction frequently appears as **stacked hybrids** rather than isolated mechanisms.

**Core mechanism**
- Multiple toll layers compound: platform gate + financing spread + IP licensing + monopoly pricing + contract clamp, producing cumulative
  burden pressure that is not visible when each layer is examined in isolation.

**Typical signals**
- multi-layer fee-stacks across the same access path,
- cross-contract steering (bundles) that forces participation in multiple toll layers,
- vertical integration that internalizes the stack and hides margins,
- complexity inflation that raises measurement cost and weakens contestability.

Hybrid flows require chain attribution (CH02), multi-layer decomposition, and remedies that address the stack (unbundling, interoperability,
requalification, and commons substitution) rather than treating each layer separately.
