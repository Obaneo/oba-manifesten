# B01.CH05 — Phase-down of parasitic flows (E)

This chapter specifies how parasitic/extractive flows are identified, decomposed, and phased down without semantic drift. It provides:
(1) a typology of E, (2) boundary handling for contested cases, (3) compatibility criteria for interventions, (4) instrument families,
(5) predictable avoidance patterns (“shift”), and (6) sequencing rules for implementation.

E reduction is treated as a **structural program**, not a moral narrative. Claims of “fairness” or “support” are insufficient: measures must
demonstrably reduce extractive components, improve R/B tails, and avoid creating new bypasses around caps.

---

## What this chapter is

- A **classification-to-intervention bridge**: from P/E/C decomposition to enforceable reduction measures.
- A **design constraint set**: what interventions must prove (E↓, R/B tail improvement, ΔG commons build-up, cap compatibility).
- A **shift-aware playbook**: predictable routes by which E migrates under pressure and how to detect and neutralize them.
- A **sequencing module**: quick wins vs structural rebuild vs institution-building, with evaluation loops.

---

## What this chapter is not

- Not a full sector manual (sector reference designs are in CH09).
- Not a cap chapter (caps are in CH04), though every E measure must be cap-compatible.
- Not an audit architecture chapter (minimum data and audit rails are in CH10), though this chapter defines audit expectations for E claims.

---

## Binding and constraints

- **Demonstrability:** “E reduction” must be measurable; relabeling does not count.
- **Distribution-first:** improvements must appear in R/B tails (p10/p25 and share below α), not only in averages.
- **Commons linkage:** durable E reduction typically requires commons substitution or public options in essentials/bottlenecks.
- **Cap compatibility:** interventions must not create accumulation bypasses (W/I escape routes).
- **Fail-closed:** if decomposition or impact cannot be verified, the measure cannot be certified as OBA-compatible.

---

## Chapter structure

- **P01 — Typology:** main classes of parasitic flows (debt/interest extraction, rent, monopoly, platform tolls, arbitrage, hybrids).
- **P02 — Edge cases:** contested boundaries (innovation rent, risk premia, scarcity types, externalities, decision protocol).
- **P03 — Compatibility criteria:** what an intervention must prove (E↓, R/B tails, commons ΔG/quality, cap compatibility, robustness, auditability).
- **P04 — Instrument families:** norming/contracts, price ceilings/tariffs, splitting/interoperability, commons alternatives, public options, mix rules.
- **P05 — Avoidance patterns (“shift”):** legal/technical/financial/international migration routes; detection and remedies.
- **P06 — Phasing plan:** sequencing, institution building, ΔM/caps interaction, transition stress, and evaluation without core break.

## Outputs expected from this chapter

When implemented (via Codex artifacts), this chapter should enable:

- A **publishable E indicator surface** (sector E shares, fee-stack depth, lock-in metrics, margin anomalies).
- A **standard intervention library** mapped to flow types, with required evidence packs and rollback conditions.
- A **shift-detection regime** that automatically flags migration routes and triggers requalification/sanctions.
- A **sequenced rollout plan** that prioritizes high-leverage essentials and bottlenecks while building audit capacity.

---

## Interfaces

- **CH02 (P/E/C):** provides the decomposition rules; CH05 defines how to act on E once identified.
- **CH03 (R/B):** E reduction must show up as lower L/T and improved R/B tails; otherwise it is cosmetic.
- **CH04 (caps):** prevents E from reconstituting via concentration and claim stacking; E measures must not open bypasses.
- **CH06 (commons):** commons substitution and public options are primary durable E-reduction mechanisms.
- **CH07 (ΔM):** allocation choices can either accelerate E reduction (commons build-up) or undermine it (asset support).
- **CH10 (audit):** provides the minimal data and audit architecture for verification and public contestability.
