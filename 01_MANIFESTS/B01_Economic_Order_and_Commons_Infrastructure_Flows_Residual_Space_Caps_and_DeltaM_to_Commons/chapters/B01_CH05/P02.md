# B01.CH05.P02 — Edge cases: innovation rent, risk compensation, scarcity versus artificial scarcity

This paragraph defines the boundary handling rules for contested cases where a surplus could be interpreted as productive reward (P) or as
parasitic extraction (E). Edge-case discipline matters because most washing strategies exploit ambiguity: they reframe tolls as “innovation”,
extraction as “risk”, and engineered scarcity as “natural”.

The purpose here is not to eliminate all surplus, but to enforce a decision grammar that keeps classification auditable and resistant to
semantic drift.

---

## B01.CH05.P02.S01 — Innovation rent: temporary surplus versus structural toll (duration and market-power criterion)

Innovation may justify temporary surplus to reward development and accelerate diffusion. It becomes parasitic when the surplus persists as a
structural toll supported by durable gate power.

### Boundary criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Duration criterion:** surplus is admissible only within a bounded, declared time horizon (explicit or functionally bounded) after which it
  must compress toward functional cost bands or convert into a different regime (e.g., open access/commons-like licensing).
- **Market-power criterion:** where surplus is maintained by exclusion, lock-in, or gate power rather than by ongoing functional superiority,
  it is classified as E.
- **Replication/contestability test:** if competitors cannot replicate or substitute due to contractual/legal gatekeeping rather than genuine
  capability constraints, the surplus is presumed extractive.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- “Innovation” is not a permanent license to extract. When duration and market-power criteria are violated, classification must shift to E and
  trigger phase-down remedies.

---

## B01.CH05.P02.S02 — Risk compensation: risk premium versus extraction (alternatives and proportionality criterion)

Some premium for risk-bearing is admissible. Risk narratives become an extraction shield when premiums exceed proportional bands or when the
risk is manufactured, offloaded, or insured away while premiums persist.

### Boundary criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Alternatives criterion:** if comparable risk can be borne through lower-cost or less extractive structures (commons/public options,
  diversified pooling, transparent pricing), excess premium is presumed E.
- **Proportionality criterion:** premiums must be bounded relative to observable risk and functional costs; persistent excess spreads imply E.
- **Risk transfer test:** if the actor claiming “risk” systematically transfers downside (via guarantees, bailouts, penalty cascades, or
  contract clamp) while keeping upside, the premium is presumed extractive.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If risk cannot be evidenced with auditable inputs, or if premium remains high after risk is reduced/insured, classification shifts to E.

## B01.CH05.P02.S03 — Scarcity: natural scarcity versus created scarcity (access and blockage criterion)

Scarcity can be real (physical constraints) or manufactured (policy, capture, bottlenecks, intentional withholding). The classification hinge
is whether scarcity is used as an access gate to extract tolls.

### Boundary criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Access criterion:** when payment primarily buys access to a bottleneck essential rather than paying for proportional service/maintenance,
  the scarcity premium is E.
- **Blockage criterion:** if scarcity persists due to controllable blockages (permits, capacity withholding, interoperability refusal,
  strategic underinvestment) rather than physical constraints, resulting premiums are presumed E.
- **Maintenance/capacity test:** if an actor profits from scarcity while underinvesting in maintenance/capacity, extraction is presumed.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Claims of “natural scarcity” must be supported by bottleneck evidence and capacity/maintenance accounting. Absent evidence, presume created
  scarcity and escalate.

## B01.CH05.P02.S04 — Public costs and externalities: when “profit” is effectively cost shifting (damage accounting)

A profit is not P if it is produced by shifting costs onto others or onto the future (health, environment, infrastructure degradation).

### Boundary criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Damage accounting:** when profits correlate with externalized damage (pollution, health harms, infrastructure wear) that is not paid for in
  the price, the surplus is presumed extractive unless fully internalized.
- **Deferred burden test:** where revenues are generated by degrading commons capacity (negative ΔG), the flow is non-productive by definition.
- **Remediation obligation:** if remediation/maintenance costs are borne by the public while profits remain private, treat the private surplus
  as E and trigger remedy paths.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If externalities cannot be measured, the system must not certify the activity as productive; it must treat it as high-risk and apply
  conservative presumptions.

## B01.CH05.P02.S05 — Boundary decision protocol: who decides, on what data, with which objection procedure

Edge cases require a standardized decision protocol to prevent capture and drift.

### Required decision protocol elements (minimum; binding)
- **Decision roles:** separation between classifier (technical), mandator (norm-setting), auditor (verification), and adjudicator (appeal).
- **Evidence set:** minimum observable inputs for the decision (cost bands, contract terms, lock-in proxies, concentration, capacity/maintenance
  indicators, exposure/consolidation where relevant).
- **Presumptions under opacity:** if required evidence is missing or obstructed, apply conservative classification and escalate (fail-closed).
- **Objection/appeal:** time-bounded contestation path with access to methods and inputs; no indefinite delay as a tactic.
- **Publication discipline:** publish aggregated decisions, criteria used, and revision history to prevent silent drift.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Edge-case classification without a documented evidence set and contestable procedure is non-conforming and must be treated as washing risk.
