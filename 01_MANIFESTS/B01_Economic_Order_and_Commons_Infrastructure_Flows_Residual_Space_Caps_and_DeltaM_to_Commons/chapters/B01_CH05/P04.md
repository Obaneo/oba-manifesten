# B01.CH05.P04 — Instruments per flow type (norming, price ceilings, splitting, commons alternatives, public options)

This paragraph enumerates the canonical instrument families used to phase down parasitic flows. Instruments are not “policies” in the party
sense; they are **structural control primitives** applied to specific extraction mechanisms (toll, lock-in, monopoly margin, fee-stack,
scarcity premium, gate power). Each instrument family must be deployable via templates and must produce measurable effects on E and on
household burdens (L/T) and thus on R/B.

The selection principle is: choose the minimum intervention that collapses the extraction mechanism while preserving productive function (P)
and strengthening commons capacity (C) where required.

---

## B01.CH05.P04.S01 — Norming and standard contracts: constrain toll structures and asymmetric terms

Norming instruments target extraction embedded in contracts and terms rather than in “prices” alone.

### Canonical norming tools (minimum; binding)
- **Standard contract templates** for essentials domains (housing, credit, utilities, platform access) with prohibited clauses.
- **Prohibited clause sets:** lock-in penalties, unilateral term changes, hidden fee triggers, forced bundling, abusive indexation.
- **All-in pricing disclosure:** require a single comparable all-in price metric and prohibit junk-fee fragmentation.
- **Portability and exit rights:** mandatory portability of identity, data, accounts, and service history where relevant.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Norming must be enforceable and auditable: published templates, change logs, and violation handling.
- If norming increases complexity or creates a “compliance theatre” industry without E decline, it is mis-specified and must be redesigned.

## B01.CH05.P04.S02 — Price ceilings and tariff regulation: where market power is structural (utilities/essentials)

Price/tariff regulation is admissible where competition cannot discipline pricing due to natural monopoly, high switching costs, or essential
dependence.

### Canonical tariff tools (minimum; binding)
- **Tariff build-up transparency:** publish cost components, allowed margin bands, indexation rules, and revision discipline.
- **Bounded returns:** regulated margin ceilings for monopoly segments; prohibit excess-return guarantees.
- **Baseline access design:** progressive structures (baseline affordable; luxury consumption more expensive where applicable) to protect R/B
  tails.
- **Junk-fee bans:** prohibit fixed-charge structures and surcharges that function as regressive tolls or fee-stacks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Tariff regimes must include contestability and audit-of-the-auditor; otherwise regulation becomes a capture surface.
- “Ceilings” that are offset by hidden fees, quality degradation, or scarcity creation fail the instrument intent.

## B01.CH05.P04.S03 — Splitting and interoperability: dismantling bottlenecks (platforms, networks)

Structural bottlenecks generate durable extraction. Splitting and interoperability instruments target gate power directly.

### Canonical structure tools (minimum; binding)
- **Unbundling/splitting:** separate monopoly segments from contestable segments (infrastructure vs services; platform core vs commerce layer).
- **Interoperability mandates:** open standards, APIs, and portability requirements that restore contestability and reduce switching costs.
- **Non-discriminatory access:** prevent self-preferencing, API gating, and exclusion tactics that preserve toll power.
- **Data and identity portability:** required export/import and switching windows, with audit logs for refusals and failures.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Interoperability must be real (functionally usable), not “paper APIs”.
- If splitting/interoperability produces vendor lock-in via proprietary compliance tooling, it is a capture risk and requires redesign.

## B01.CH05.P04.S04 — Commons alternatives: public/commons provisioning as structural substitution for toll markets

Where markets structurally regenerate tolls, commons substitution is the durable instrument.

### Canonical commons substitution tools (minimum; binding)
- **Commons provisioning in essentials:** build/maintain capacity in housing, energy grids, care continuity, and digital baseline services.
- **Governance minima:** access rules, maintenance-first budgeting, transparency, contestability, and anti-capture rotation.
- **Boundary rules:** prohibit private toll layering around commons access (mandatory intermediaries, exclusivity, pay-to-reach).
- **ΔG measurement:** capacity/coverage and quality indicators must be published; commons status requires demonstrable maintenance and reach.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons claims without governance/access/maintenance evidence are commons-washing.
- Commons that require surveillance or punitive gatekeeping to function are non-conforming and must be redesigned.

## B01.CH05.P04.S05 — Public option: a fallback that disciplines private providers

A public option is a specific instrument pattern: a baseline provider exists next to private providers to create a contestable outside option.

### Canonical public option tools (minimum; binding)
- **Baseline service package:** a defined minimum offering with transparent pricing/terms and guaranteed portability/exit.
- **Non-extractive pricing:** bounded margins, no fee-stacks, and predictable indexation.
- **Operational continuity:** maintenance-first, incident discipline, and published service levels.
- **Discipline effect:** the private market must either match terms or lose users; otherwise the option is toothless.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A public option that is unreachable (waiting times, low coverage) is not an option; it is symbolic.
- A public option must not become a bureaucratic toll gate; admin friction counts as L and must be minimized.

## B01.CH05.P04.S06 — Instrument mix per sector: when to combine (and when combinations are counterproductive)

Most sectors require instrument mixes. Mix design is constrained by mechanism logic and by avoidance pressure.

### Combination rules (minimum; binding)
- **Start with mechanism diagnosis:** identify the dominant extraction mechanism(s) first (monopoly margin, fee-stack, lock-in, scarcity rent).
- **Layer instruments coherently:** e.g., norming + portability to reduce lock-in, then interoperability to restore contestability, then public
  option/commons where contestability cannot hold.
- **Avoid “double-compensation”:** do not subsidize burdens while leaving tolls intact; subsidies often become leakage into E.
- **Plan for shift:** every instrument mix must include detection and requalification paths for predictable migration routes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a mix improves averages but worsens tails (share below α, p10/p25 R/B), it is counterproductive even if politically attractive.
- If an instrument mix increases complexity and measurement load without shrinking E, it is drifting into compliance theatre and must be
  simplified or replaced.
