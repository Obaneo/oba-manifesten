# B01.CH05.P05 — Avoidance patterns: “shift” of E into new forms (legal, technical, international)

This paragraph defines the standard “shift” families by which extraction migrates when a specific toll channel is constrained. Shift is not an
edge case; it is the default response to E phase-down. Therefore every E-reduction program must include a shift grammar: how E moves, how it
is detected, and how it is neutralized without discretionary bargaining.

The core principle: if an activity preserves the same extraction mechanism (toll, lock-in, gate power, fee-stack) under a new label or rail,
it remains E and must be requalified.

---

## B01.CH05.P05.S01 — Legal shifts: reclassification into services, licenses, consultancy, franchising

Legal shifts change the contract label while preserving the cashflow extraction.

### Canonical legal shift patterns (minimum; binding)
- **Toll → “service”:** access charges reframed as service/management fees, “support plans”, or compliance costs.
- **Rent → “package”:** basic access bundled into service bundles where the essential component is unavoidable.
- **Interest → “fee”:** credit pricing moved from interest into origination/servicing/penalty fees to evade caps or ceilings.
- **Wage → consultancy/license:** compensation routed as invoiced services, IP licensing, or royalty streams while dependency and control remain.
- **Platform take rate → franchise:** the gate becomes a “franchise fee” with contractual exclusivity and control rights.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Substance-over-form applies: if control/dependency and toll extraction persist, classification remains E.
- Repeat legal shifts trigger presumptive requalification and escalated audit.

---

## B01.CH05.P05.S02 — Technical shifts: bundling, dark patterns, API locks, data extraction as a new toll

Technical shifts preserve extraction by redesigning the user interface, the switching surface, or the technical rails.

### Canonical technical shift patterns (minimum; binding)
- **Bundling:** hiding tolls inside bundles (must-have add-ons) so “optional” fees become functionally mandatory.
- **Dark patterns:** pricing/choice manipulation to steer toward higher fee paths or to raise effective switching costs.
- **API/interop locks:** refusing interoperability, degrading third-party access, or using “security” claims to justify gating.
- **Identity/payment coupling:** tying essential access to proprietary identity, payment, or device ecosystems.
- **Data extraction as toll:** extracting data as the price for access, then monetizing it; “free” becomes rent via surveillance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If the design increases switching costs or creates non-contestable gate power, it is treated as an extraction surface even if prices appear
  stable.
- “Security” and “anti-fraud” are not exemptions for lock-in; they must be evidenced and bounded.

---

## B01.CH05.P05.S03 — Financial shifts: securitization, fee-stacks, synthetic exposures, shadow banking

Financial shifts move extraction into financing structures, intermediation layers, and engineered claims.

### Canonical financial shift patterns (minimum; binding)
- **Securitization:** converting receivables into tradable claims that add servicing/structuring fee layers and weaken contestability.
- **Fee-stack multiplication:** proliferation of intermediaries (brokers, servicers, advisors, “platform finance”) each taking a cut.
- **Synthetic exposure:** recreating ownership/toll rights through derivatives when direct holdings are constrained (TRS/options/CFDs).
- **Shadow banking growth:** off-balance-sheet conduits that preserve leverage and extractive spreads outside regulated surfaces.
- **Collateral-driven scarcity:** financing designs that inflate asset values and turn essentials into collateral-based toll circuits.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Economic exposure is treated as ownership-equivalent where relevant; synthetic replication does not escape classification.
- Under opacity, conservative presumptions apply and the burden of proof lies with the party benefiting from complexity.

---

## B01.CH05.P05.S04 — International shifts: transfer pricing, IP boxes, re-domiciliation, treaty shopping

International shifts exploit jurisdictional mismatches to preserve extraction while weakening enforcement.

### Canonical international shift patterns (minimum; binding)
- **Transfer pricing:** moving margins to low-tax or low-scrutiny entities while operational substance remains elsewhere.
- **IP boxing:** routing profits as royalties to IP entities to convert operational surplus into durable licensing tolls.
- **Re-domiciliation:** relocating entities or control centers to escape caps, E norms, or disclosure obligations.
- **Treaty shopping:** exploiting treaties to reduce withholding and to block effective consolidation.
- **Nominee and layered ownership:** fragmenting beneficial ownership to defeat UBC attribution.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Consolidation and beneficial control override legal residence labels where control/benefit remain.
- Non-cooperation triggers fail-closed: presumptions, escalated enforcement, and restricted recognition where necessary.

## B01.CH05.P05.S05 — Detection: red flags and anomalies (margin extremes, fee-stacks, concentration, lock-in metrics)

Shift detection must be systematic and minimally sufficient to catch migration patterns without requiring total surveillance.

### Required detection surface (minimum; binding)
- **Margin anomalies:** persistent excess margins, sudden margin spikes, or unexplained band breaks relative to cost movements.
- **Fee-stack depth:** growth in intermediary counts, total fee share, junk-fee emergence, or fee re-labeling patterns.
- **Concentration indicators:** rising market power metrics, bottleneck control, vertical integration that hides stacks.
- **Lock-in metrics:** switching-cost proxies, portability failure rates, exclusion patterns, penalty incidence.
- **Contract drift:** proliferation of complex terms, non-competes/exclusivity, API restrictions, opaque indexation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Indicators must be published in aggregate with method_id and definition-set references; “we monitor internally” is insufficient.
- Where indicators cannot be observed, the system must treat the domain as high-risk and apply conservative enforcement rules.

## B01.CH05.P05.S06 — Remedy: automatic requalification, sanctions, and reallocation to commons

Shift remedies must be rule-bound, automatic, and capable of collapsing the incentive to migrate extraction.

### Required remedy set (minimum; binding)
- **Automatic requalification:** treat shifted forms as the same underlying flow type (E) when functional tests are met (substance-over-form).
- **Contract remedies:** ban or rewrite prohibited terms, require unbundling, restore portability, enforce open access conditions.
- **Structural remedies:** mandate interoperability, splitting, or conversion to commons/public options when gate power persists.
- **Sanctions:** escalating penalties for mislabeling, opacity, or obstruction, including presumptive treatment and penalty multipliers.
- **Reallocation to commons:** where monetary penalties or skims arise, route to commons build/maintenance rather than into discretionary deals.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If shift remedies rely on case-by-case bargaining, the system invites capture and becomes non-conforming. Remedies must be template-driven,
  auditable, and repeatable.
