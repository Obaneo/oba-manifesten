# B01.CH05.P06 — Phasing plan: sequencing (quick wins, structural interventions, long-term institution building)

This paragraph specifies the phasing logic for an E phase-down program. “Phase-down” is not a single reform; it is a multi-stage sequence
that must (i) deliver early visible burden relief, (ii) dismantle structural bottlenecks that regenerate tolls, and (iii) build durable
institutions that prevent drift and capture. The phasing plan is therefore a **design for persistence** under predictable resistance and shift.

All phasing choices must remain compatible with caps (W-MAX/I-MAX) and must route investment capacity (ΔM and fiscal capacity where relevant)
toward commons build-up rather than asset support.

---

## B01.CH05.P06.S01 — Quick wins: measures with high E reduction and low implementation complexity

Quick wins are admissible when they collapse obvious toll mechanisms without requiring full structural rebuild.

### Quick-win selection criteria (minimum; binding)
- **High E leverage:** targets fee-stacks, junk fees, abusive contract terms, and clear monopoly margins.
- **Low dependency on new institutions:** enforceable via existing regulator/enforcement capacity with minimal new data systems.
- **Immediate tail relevance:** produces measurable improvement in burdens (L/T) and R/B tails within short windows.

### Typical quick-win families (illustrative; non-exhaustive)
- junk-fee bans and all-in disclosure,
- standardized contracts with prohibited clauses (lock-in penalties, unilateral term changes),
- portability/exit rights (data/identity/account portability),
- targeted tariff simplification where fixed charges are regressive tolls.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Quick wins must not be “subsidy cosmetics” that leave extraction intact.
- If quick wins trigger shift (CH05.P05), automatic requalification and escalation must activate.

---

## B01.CH05.P06.S02 — Structural interventions: redesign bottlenecks (housing/energy/platforms/finance)

Structural interventions target the mechanisms that regenerate E: scarcity bottlenecks, natural monopolies, platform gates, and financial claim
stacks.

### Required structural intervention targets (minimum; binding)
- **Housing:** scarcity construction, land rent capture, leverage-driven price loops, landlord concentration, contract clamp.
- **Energy:** grid/utility natural monopoly pricing, tariff toll structures, vendor lock-in in control systems, underinvestment in capacity.
- **Platforms:** take-rate extraction, ranking gate power, API gating, identity/payment coupling, portability obstruction.
- **Finance:** debt cascades, fee-stack intermediation, securitization stacks, collateral-driven asset inflation loops.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Structural interventions must include interoperability/unbundling or commons/public options where contestability cannot hold.
- Structural interventions must not create privileged concessions that become new claim stacks (cap compatibility gate).

---

## B01.CH05.P06.S03 — Institution building: audit capacity, data-commons, governance rails, enforcement

Durable E reduction requires institutions that can detect shift, prevent drift, and enforce remedies uniformly.

### Required institution build (minimum; binding)
- **Audit capacity:** independent audit functions, forensics capability, and audit-of-auditors discipline.
- **Data-commons and minimal datasets:** enough observables to measure E, R/B distribution, concentration/lock-in, and ΔG/quality without
  surveillance drift.
- **Governance rails:** transparent rulebooks, version control, exception discipline, and contestability procedures.
- **Enforcement infrastructure:** trigger-based audits, standard sanctions, escrow/lockbox where needed, and anti-capture rotation rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If the system cannot measure and enforce, it cannot claim E reduction. Missing rails trigger fail-closed (no certification, no scaling).

---

## B01.CH05.P06.S04 — Sequencing with caps and ΔM: when caps tighten and when commons accelerate

E phase-down sequencing must be coordinated with cap tightening and ΔM routing, or it will be reversed by asset inflation and concentration.

### Required coordination logic (minimum; binding)
- **Caps tighten as shift pressure rises:** as E channels are closed, avoidance shifts to accumulation structures; cap enforcement must strengthen
  in parallel, not lag.
- **Commons accelerate where bottlenecks bind:** when scarcity drives E (housing/energy/care), accelerate commons capex/opex to expand capacity
  and collapse scarcity tolls.
- **ΔM routing discipline:** monetary space may not be used to “stabilize” asset prices or privileged returns during phase-down; it must be
  routed through allowed channels into commons and productive capacity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If ΔM primarily supports assets during phase-down, E will reconstitute via price and concentration effects and the program fails.

---

## B01.CH05.P06.S05 — Transition stress: prevent the phase-down itself from producing scarcity/instability

Phase-down changes prices, margins, and labor allocation. Poor sequencing can create shortages and instability that are then used to justify
rollback.

### Required stress controls (minimum; binding)
- **Continuity plans for essentials:** predefine how service continuity is protected during structural changes (housing throughput, grid
  reliability, platform interoperability transitions, credit restructuring).
- **Capacity buffers:** ensure maintenance-first staffing and operational capacity in key systems to prevent degradation.
- **Anti-profiteering rails in shocks:** prevent crisis conditions from reopening toll channels under emergency rhetoric.
- **Labor transition support (where relevant):** anticipate displacement from parasitic sectors and reallocate toward maintenance/commons/P.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Transition stress cannot justify reopening extraction channels permanently. Emergency measures must be temporary, auditable, and sunset-bound.

---

## B01.CH05.P06.S06 — Evaluation and adjustment: periodic recalibration without core break (versioning + compatibility check)

E phase-down is iterative. The system must learn without semantic drift and without hollowing the core.

### Required evaluation loop (minimum; binding)
- **Cadence:** periodic review plus event-driven review when anomalies or shocks occur.
- **Outputs:** E indicators, R/B distribution (tails + median), lock-in/concentration metrics, ΔG/quality signals, shift incidents.
- **Restatements:** if definition or method changes materially affect reported outcomes, publish restatements or explicit non-restatement with
  rationale.
- **Rollback triggers:** pre-defined conditions under which an intervention is paused/rolled back (tails worsen, lock-in rises, commons
  degrades, shift overwhelms enforcement).
- **Compatibility check:** every adjustment is tested against the core pillars and cap compatibility; no silent parameter drift.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If adjustments are made without version control, published rationale, and compatibility testing, the program is drifting and must be halted
  until governance discipline is restored.
