# B01.CH03.P01 — Definitions of B, T, N, L, R and the relation GROSS → NET → RESIDUAL SPACE

This paragraph fixes the economic-layer household accounting chain used throughout Book 1. The chain is not merely descriptive; it defines the primary order variable (R) and the ratio form (R/B) used for stability and compatibility tests.

## B01.CH03.P01.S01 — B (gross): income components and delimitation (labour, capital, transfers)

**B (gross)** denotes total gross inflow to the household within the defined time window. B is defined as a **component set**, not as a single number by convention.

Minimum component families:

- **Labour:** wages, salaries, overtime, bonuses, self-employment labour income where it is functionally labour.
- **Capital/claims:** dividends, interest, rents received, royalties, realized capital gains where included by the definition set.
- **Transfers:** public transfers, benefits, allowances, and other defined transfer inflows.
- **In-kind (where defined):** non-cash provisions counted as income equivalents only if the definition set explicitly includes them and prescribes a valuation method.

Delimitation rules must specify:
- what is included/excluded (e.g., one-off asset sales vs realized gains),
- treatment of deferred compensation,
- treatment of in-kind benefits,
- and consolidation rules where household income is routed via vehicles.

## B01.CH03.P01.S02 — T (levies): taxes/premiums/contributions — what counts and what does not

**T (levies)** denotes mandatory household outflows to public or quasi-public regimes within the time window that count as levies under the definition set.

T includes (as applicable):
- **Taxes:** income tax, payroll-related taxes, local taxes classified as levies.
- **Premiums/contributions:** mandatory social insurance contributions, health insurance premiums where mandatory.
- **Quasi-levies:** regulated charges that are functionally mandatory for baseline participation, only if the definition set classifies them as T rather than L.

T excludes:
- discretionary donations and voluntary insurance,
- private fees paid to access essentials (these are candidates for L),
- penalties and late fees unless explicitly classified (typically treated as E signals rather than baseline T).

The boundary between T and L must be explicit and versioned to prevent drift (e.g., shifting burdens from T into fee-stacks counted as “service charges”).

## B01.CH03.P01.S03 — N = B − T: net as an intermediate step (not the end indicator)

**N (net)** is defined mechanically as:

- **N = B − T**

N is an intermediate quantity. It is not, by itself, the order indicator, because:
- net can rise while mandatory burdens rise faster,
- net can be cosmetically improved by shifting costs into L-like fee-stacks,
- and distributional tails can deteriorate while averages improve.

N is therefore used only as the bridge from gross inflow to residual space.

## B01.CH03.P01.S04 — L (mandatory fixed burdens): definition, minimum set, and exclusions

**L (mandatory fixed burdens)** denotes the minimum basket of unavoidable burdens required for baseline participation and survival within the time window, under the definition set. L is defined by **categories + boundary rules**, not by ad hoc lists.

Minimum category families (baseline):
- **Housing** (rent/mortgage payments within defined boundaries + mandatory housing charges),
- **Energy** (baseline energy cost for habitation),
- **Basic insurance** (mandatory baseline coverage where applicable),
- **Basic mobility/communication** (baseline participation requirement, where defined).

L must be bounded by:
- **unavoidability** (can the household realistically exit?),
- **substitutability** (are low-cost substitutes realistically available without clamp?),
- **minimum quality** (baseline adequacy),
- and **local variation rules** (permitted parameter space without redefining the category).

Exclusions:
- discretionary consumption and lifestyle upgrades,
- elective luxury services,
- voluntary add-ons unless they are functionally non-avoidable due to market structure (which must be explicitly justified and audited).

L is the primary drift risk variable; it therefore requires version control, reference budgets, and audit procedures (expanded in P03).

## B01.CH03.P01.S05 — R = N − L: residual space as an order variable (function and interpretation)

**R (residual space)** is defined as:

- **R = N − L**

R is the primary household order variable because it represents:
- the household’s **buffer** against shocks,
- the ability to **exit** clamp conditions (switch providers, move, refuse abusive terms),
- the space for **participation** beyond survival,
- and the degree to which the economy’s structure is producing burdens that compress freedom of choice.

R must be analyzed distributionally (percentiles, tail share below α) rather than as a mean value.

## B01.CH03.P01.S06 — Computation frames: time window (month/year), household type, equivalisation/normalisation

All quantities (B, T, N, L, R) must be computed within explicit frames:

- **Time window:** month, quarter, or year (declared). Conversions must be method-defined and consistent.
- **Household type:** a declared classification (single, couple, children count, dependents) used for comparability.
- **Equivalisation/normalisation:** rules that allow comparison across household sizes and compositions (e.g., equivalence scales), with versioning and disclosed assumptions.

Required reporting elements:
- definition-set ID,
- method ID (including equivalisation),
- uncertainty handling rules,
- and revision/restatement policy when definitions change.

Without fixed computation frames, R/B and α become non-comparable and drift-prone, undermining compatibility claims.
