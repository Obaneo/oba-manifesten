# B01.CH03.P02 — Threshold form R/B ≥ α: function, interpretation, and bands

This paragraph defines the threshold form **R/B ≥ α** as the household-facing order test. It specifies why the ratio form is used, what α means, what may vary (bands), how α is updated over time, and what must be published.

## B01.CH03.P02.S01 — Why a ratio (R/B) rather than only an absolute amount

An absolute residual amount (R in currency units) is not comparable across households with different gross inflows, tax structures, or income volatility. The ratio **R/B** is used because it:

- normalizes residual space to the scale of household inflow,
- captures **structural burden pressure** independent of nominal income level,
- enables distributional comparison across household types after equivalisation,
- and is more robust to nominal inflation effects when paired with explicit indexation rules.

The ratio form also directly exposes Goodhart risks: policies that raise B while raising L proportionally (or more) will not improve R/B, preventing “gross-growth masking” of burden growth.

## B01.CH03.P02.S02 — Meaning of α: shock absorption, freedom of choice, participation, and commons access

**α** is the minimum residual-space ratio required for the order to be considered compatible at the household level. It is not a “wellbeing score”; it is an operational threshold representing four capacities:

1. **Shock absorption:** ability to absorb predictable shocks (repairs, short income dips) without immediate debt spirals or arrears cascades.
2. **Freedom of choice / exit:** ability to switch providers, relocate, refuse abusive terms, or reduce dependency without being clamped by switching costs.
3. **Participation capacity:** ability to participate in social/economic life beyond mere subsistence (time and minimal discretionary room), as defined by the order’s baseline participation standard.
4. **Commons access effectiveness:** ability to actually use baseline commons provisions (including travel/admin friction) such that “rights” are not purely nominal.

α therefore binds together income, burdens, and access frictions into a single order-facing threshold while remaining compatible with multi-metric reporting (α is a gate, not the whole dashboard).

## B01.CH03.P02.S03 — Bands and context: differences by life phase/household type without losing the core

α may vary only within **declared bands** tied to household type and life phase, under rules that preserve comparability and prevent drift.

Permissible variation must satisfy:

- **Parameterization, not redefinition:** α can have a family of values (α_h) by household type, but the definition of B and L categories may not be silently altered to “fit” α.
- **Monotonic discipline:** variation must be justified by predictable structural differences (e.g., dependency burden, unavoidable care costs) rather than by discretionary consumption patterns.
- **Public rationale:** each band requires a published rationale and the evidence base for why the band differs.
- **No exception inflation:** bespoke exceptions are disallowed; only predefined household types and declared bands apply.

If a system needs frequent ad hoc exceptions, it indicates that the definitions (especially L boundaries or equivalisation) are unstable and must be redesigned.

## B01.CH03.P02.S04 — Dynamics: how α co-moves with inflation/energy/housing (indexation rules)

α must not be frozen as a political number while the burden structure moves. The system therefore requires explicit **indexation rules** governing how α (or its underlying assumptions) updates with macro conditions that structurally affect burdens:

- **Inflation:** general price-level movement affects the purchasing power of residual space; indexation must avoid nominal illusion.
- **Energy and housing shocks:** when essentials experience structural price breaks, α must respond through a defined rule (either by updating α, updating L reference budgets, or both) rather than by ad hoc political intervention.
- **Lag and revision discipline:** indexation windows (monthly/quarterly/annual) and revision mechanics must be declared, with restatements where necessary.

Indexation must remain compatible with fail-closed discipline:
- if the required indices are unavailable or contested, the system must publish uncertainty and, where necessary, suspend compatibility claims rather than silently “hold α constant”.

## B01.CH03.P02.S05 — Minimum reporting: which statistics are published (medians, p10/p25/p75, not only averages)

Compatibility evaluation and public contestability require a minimum reporting set. At minimum, publish:

- **R/B distribution:** median, p10, p25, p75 (and optionally p90/p95 where relevant).
- **Share below α:** proportion of households under the threshold (overall and by allowed segmentation variables).
- **Trend over time:** changes in the above statistics across declared windows, with method continuity or explicit restatements.
- **Uncertainty and definition IDs:** definition-set ID, method ID, uncertainty bands (where proxies used), and revision/restatement policy.

Averages alone are explicitly insufficient: they can improve while the tail worsens, and they are highly sensitive to top-end shifts. The required set is therefore designed to make tail risk and distribution fractures visible.
