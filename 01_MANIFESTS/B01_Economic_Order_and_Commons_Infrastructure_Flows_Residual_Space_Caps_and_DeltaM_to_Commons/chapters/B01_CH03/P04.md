# B01.CH03.P04 — Distribution profiles: why medians/percentiles matter more than averages

This paragraph fixes the reporting discipline for R/B and related indicators. The economic layer is evaluated by how it shapes **distribution tails** and **floor stability**, not by whether averages can be made to look good.

## B01.CH03.P04.S01 — Why averages mislead under skewed distributions

Household incomes and burdens are typically **skewed** and often **heavy-tailed**. Under such distributions:

- a small number of very high B or very low L observations can pull the mean, masking widespread burden pressure;
- policy interventions can raise the mean while increasing tail risk (more households below α);
- averages obscure heterogeneity in clamp exposure (lock-in, fee-stacks, volatile bills) that primarily affects lower and middle cohorts;
- manipulation is easier: cosmetic net gains for some groups can offset losses elsewhere in the average.

Therefore, average-only reporting is structurally compatible with “order by narrative”: it permits claims of success while the experienced order variable (residual space) remains fragile for large fractions of the population.

## B01.CH03.P04.S02 — Core profiles: median R/B, p10/p25, and share below α

Minimum distribution reporting for the household order indicator must include:

- **Median R/B:** the central tendency robust to tails.
- **p10 and p25 of R/B:** lower-tail metrics that directly track floor stability and early-warning deterioration.
- **Share below α:** the proportion of households failing the threshold condition R/B ≥ α (the primary compatibility gate metric).
- **Optional but recommended:** p75/p90/p95, and tail volatility measures, when evaluating shift risks or top-end dynamics.

These profiles must be reported:
- over declared time windows (with trend lines),
- with definition-set and method IDs,
- and with restatements when definitions change.

If a policy claims improvement but:
- p10/p25 stagnate or fall,
- or the share below α rises,
then the system must treat the claim as non-validated regardless of what happens to the mean.

## B01.CH03.P04.S03 — Segmentation: region, sector, household type (without identity labels)

Distribution profiles must be segmentable to detect structural fractures and localized clamp regimes, but segmentation must remain compatible with neutrality constraints.

Permitted minimum segmentation families:

- **Region:** geographic units relevant to housing/energy/mobility burdens and service availability.
- **Sector (exposure):** linkage to labour-market sector or essential-service sector exposure where it affects income stability or burdens (e.g., energy-intensive regions).
- **Household type:** equivalised household categories (single, couple, children count, dependents), as defined in the computation frame.

Segmentation rules:

- **No identity labels:** segmentation must not be based on identity categories; it must rely on functional and structural descriptors (region, sector, household type).
- **Privacy-safe reporting:** publish only aggregated statistics with suppression for small-n and explicit uncertainty treatment.
- **Comparability:** segmentation definitions must be stable and versioned; changes require publication and (where needed) restatements.

The purpose of segmentation is diagnostic: to reveal where order compatibility fails first (rising share below α, tail deterioration, volatility spikes) and to force corrective design rather than narrative smoothing.
