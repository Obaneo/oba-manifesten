# B01.CH03.P05 — Policy lever set: which knobs directly move R/B (without breaking the core)

This paragraph enumerates the economic lever families that move **R/B** through the chain **B → T → N → L → R**. It is a structural map: it does not prescribe party programs, but specifies which levers exist, what they affect, and which failure modes (especially cosmetic improvements) must be prevented.

## B01.CH03.P05.S01 — Raise B: wage structure, productivity, minimum and floor guarantees (OBA-compatible)

R/B can improve by raising **B** (gross inflow), but only when the increase is not immediately neutralized by rising burdens (L) or shifted extraction (E). OBA-compatible B levers include:

- **Wage structure improvements:** raising low and median wages, narrowing extreme dispersion where it is sustained by market power rather than function.
- **Productivity tied to P (not E):** productivity gains that reflect real output/service/maintenance improvements rather than fee-stack optimization.
- **Minimum and floor guarantees:** baseline income floors or stabilizers that reduce tail volatility and prevent sub-α clustering, provided they do not become bureaucratic clamp mechanisms.

Validation condition: B increases must be evaluated against **distributional R/B outcomes** (p10/p25 and share below α), not against averages.

## B01.CH03.P05.S02 — Redesign T: shift away from labour/consumption toward extraction/accumulation (link to E and caps)

R/B can improve by reducing or redistributing **T**, but the design must be consistent with P/E/C and caps:

- **Shift the tax base away from labour/consumption** where it compresses the floor, toward:
  - **extractive flows (E)** and fee-stack components,
  - **accumulation bases** aligned with W-MAX/I-MAX logic,
  - and **monopoly/land-rent** components where applicable.
- **Incidence-first design:** the economic payer matters more than the legal label; tax design must be tested for who actually bears the burden.
- **Anti-arbitrage alignment:** T redesign must not open bypass routes (relabeling income, offshoring, vehicle shifting).

This lever is structurally linked to CH04 (caps) and CH05 (E reduction): if T is redesigned while E remains intact, the system often produces hidden L or compensating extraction elsewhere.

## B01.CH03.P05.S03 — Lower L: housing/energy/health costs via commons, norming, public options

The most direct path to improving R/B for the floor is lowering **L** (mandatory burdens), especially in essentials. OBA-compatible L levers include:

- **Commons substitution:** expanding commons provisioning in essentials where markets structurally produce toll/lock-in.
- **Norming and standard contracts:** banning clamp clauses, fee cascades, and abusive indexation; enforcing transparent all-in pricing.
- **Public options:** creating a baseline provider next to private providers to discipline prices and reduce lock-in.
- **Maintenance-first capacity build:** increasing real capacity and reliability (ΔG and quality) so scarcity premiums do not regenerate.

Lowering L is not equivalent to suppressing visible prices while compensating via hidden fees or deteriorating quality; the L reduction must be real and auditable.

## B01.CH03.P05.S04 — Reduce E as an indirect lever: lower toll and interest structures → lower L and T / higher R

Reducing **E** is often the highest-leverage indirect method because E commonly propagates into:

- **L:** higher rents, higher interest, higher fees in essentials and access channels;
- **T:** higher fiscal burdens required to compensate for privately extracted rents or to stabilize households after extraction-induced shocks.

Therefore, E reduction can raise R by:
- compressing fee-stacks, tolls, and scarcity premiums,
- reducing debt-service extraction and penalty cascades,
- restoring contestability and exit, which prevents future E regrowth.

This lever requires CH02 (P/E/C classification) and CH05 (phase-down program) to be operational, otherwise “E reduction” becomes a slogan rather than a measurable process.

## B01.CH03.P05.S05 — Trade-offs and sequencing: quick wins vs structural measures; avoid cosmetic improvements

R/B can be cosmetically improved while the underlying order worsens. The main sequencing rules:

- **Quick wins are allowed** (temporary relief, targeted supports) only if they are:
  - traceable,
  - time-bounded,
  - and do not entrench E (no fee-stack subsidy, no asset-price support as a hidden channel).

- **Structural measures dominate** long-run compatibility:
  - capacity build and commons substitution,
  - durable E phase-down,
  - cap effectiveness and anti-avoidance,
  - measurement and audit discipline.

- **Avoid cosmetic improvements:** examples include:
  - raising B while L rises faster (R/B flat or worse),
  - lowering visible prices while hidden fees grow,
  - shifting burdens outside the L definition set,
  - one-off transfers while lock-in regimes remain intact.

The evaluation rule is simple: improvements must show up in **distributional R/B metrics** (p10/p25 and share below α) and must be explainable by structural changes rather than by accounting tricks.
