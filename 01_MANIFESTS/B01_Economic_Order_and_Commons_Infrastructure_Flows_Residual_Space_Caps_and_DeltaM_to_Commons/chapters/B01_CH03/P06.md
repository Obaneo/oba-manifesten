# B01.CH03.P06 — Manipulation risks (Goodhart): mitigations in definitions, audit, and public reporting

This paragraph specifies the manipulation surface for R/B and the minimum mitigations required to keep the indicator meaningful. The principle is simple: if an indicator becomes a target, actors will optimize the number unless the system is designed to resist gaming.

## B01.CH03.P06.S01 — Goodhart patterns: relabel L, shift costs outside definitions, temporary subsidies as cosmetics

Minimum Goodhart patterns to assume and actively defend against:

- **Relabel L:** shifting discretionary or luxury items into “mandatory” categories to justify larger interventions, or conversely narrowing L to cosmetically raise R/B.
- **Shift costs outside definitions:** moving burdens into categories not counted as L or T (e.g., bundling into “optional” add-ons that are practically unavoidable; pushing costs into time/admin friction not captured).
- **Temporary subsidies as cosmetics:** short-term transfers that raise N without changing the underlying fee-stack/lock-in structure, causing the effect to vanish or reverse when the subsidy ends.
- **Fee-stack substitution:** lowering one visible charge while expanding hidden fees, penalties, or required intermediaries.
- **Timing games:** shifting payments across time windows (deferrals, balloon payments, one-off credits) to improve measured R/B in the reporting period.

These patterns are not edge cases; they are default strategic responses once R/B becomes politically salient.

## B01.CH03.P06.S02 — Definition mitigations: hard boundaries, exclusions, benchmark rules, revision discipline

Definition-level defenses prevent the indicator from being redefined into compliance:

- **Hard boundaries for L:** explicit category definitions, inclusion rules, and exclusions; no ad hoc expansions or shrinkage.
- **Anti-bundling separation:** mandatory vs non-mandatory components must be separable; if not separable due to opacity, trigger audit and/or conservative treatment rather than accepting provider labels.
- **Reference budgets and benchmarks:** L is tied to a published baseline adequacy benchmark, not to observed spending patterns; prevents lifestyle creep.
- **Revision discipline:** changes to L, equivalisation, or α rules require published rationale, impact analysis, and version control; material changes trigger restatements or explicitly declared non-restatement with reasons.
- **Consistency checks:** enforce coherence between the L definition and commons baseline promises (a “baseline right” that is unreachable due to friction counts as a defect, not a definitional escape).

## B01.CH03.P06.S03 — Audit mitigations: sampling, anomaly detection, consolidation (through chains)

Audit-level defenses ensure definitions are not bypassed through structure:

- **Sampling with escalation:** routine sampling plus increased coverage in essentials and high-risk sectors; red flags trigger forensic audits.
- **Anomaly detection:** detect sudden shifts in:
  - L composition shares,
  - fee-stack depth,
  - penalty incidence,
  - contract term changes (lock-in proxies),
  - unexplained regional breaks or discontinuities.
- **Consolidation through chains:** prevent extraction from being hidden in intermediaries by consolidating fee layers and attributing flows through supply chains and platform stacks (link to CH02).
- **Exception monitoring:** growth in exceptions, bespoke deals, or “special regimes” is itself an audit trigger; exceptions must be logged and published in aggregate.

## B01.CH03.P06.S04 — Public reporting mitigations: transparent definitions, revisions, open calculation rules, independent review

Public contestability is a manipulation defense. Minimum requirements:

- **Publish definitions and methods:** definition-set ID, method ID, L reference budgets, α bands/indexation rules, equivalisation method, and uncertainty handling.
- **Publish revisions and restatements:** corrections must be visible; no silent changes.
- **Open calculation rules:** enough detail for external replication of indicators (within privacy constraints).
- **Independent review:** periodic third-party replication/audit, including scrutiny of definition changes and exception regimes.
- **Multi-metric triangulation:** R/B is a gate, not the only number; publish supporting metrics (lock-in proxies, fee-stack indicators, arrears, volatility) so R/B cannot be optimized by shifting harm elsewhere.

## B01.CH03.P06.S05 — Fail-closed principles: what happens under measurement uncertainty or data gaps (no “free pass”)

When evidence is missing, uncertain, or blocked, the system must fail closed:

- **No compatibility label under missing observables:** if the minimum observation set for L, T, or equivalisation is missing, R/B cannot be used to certify compatibility.
- **Explicit “unknown” categories:** missingness is reported as unknown; it is not imputed away.
- **Conservative presumptions under opacity:** where bundling/terms prevent separation, treat the case as high-risk and escalate to audit rather than granting benefit of the doubt.
- **Stop rules:** if measurement blackouts, vendor lock-in, or exception explosions occur, scaling and compatibility claims are suspended until the evidence chain is restored.
- **Mandatory restatement on discovery:** once better data becomes available and materially changes prior results, a restatement is required.

Fail-closed is the enforcement of epistemic discipline: the system does not “declare success” when it cannot verify the mechanism.
