# B01.CH02.P05 — Edge cases: mixed flows and multi-actor chains

This paragraph specifies edge-case decision rules where P/E/C classification is commonly contested or structurally obscured. The aim is to prevent semantic arbitrage (“it’s innovation”, “it’s risk”, “it’s just rent”, “it’s just a platform service”, “it’s public-private so it’s fine”) from functioning as an extraction shield.

## B01.CH02.P05.S01 — Innovation and IP: when temporary surplus is P versus when it becomes E (duration/market-power criterion)

IP-related flows (licensing fees, royalties, exclusivity payments) can be P or E depending on whether they represent bounded, time-limited reward for innovation or a durable toll gate.

Classification hinges on two criteria:

- **Duration criterion:**  
  Surplus can be treated as P-like only when the premium is time-bounded or clearly decays toward functional cost bands. Persistent premium without an expiry/decay mechanism is a red flag for E.

- **Market-power criterion:**  
  If the premium is sustained primarily by non-contestable exclusion power (lock-in, standards control, portfolio dominance, litigation threats, compatibility sabotage), then the premium component is E unless justified by measurable ongoing functional contribution (maintenance, support, continuous improvement) within bounded bands.

Operational rule: separate **functional service/maintenance** of IP (candidate P) from **pure exclusion premium** (candidate E). Under opacity, fail closed.

## B01.CH02.P05.S02 — Interest and credit: productive credit versus structural debt extraction (function/alternatives criterion)

Interest and credit flows are classified by their economic function and by the availability of non-extractive alternatives.

- **Productive credit (P-aligned):** credit that demonstrably finances productive capacity or commons build-up and is priced within bounded cost+risk bands, with transparent terms and no fee-stack cascades.

- **Structural debt extraction (E):** credit mechanisms that primarily function as a tollbooth on future income, sustained by dependency, rollovers, penalty structures, collateral leverage, or unavoidable access gating (especially in essentials like housing, energy arrears, healthcare, and education).

Key decision criteria:

- **Function criterion:** what is credit financing—capacity build/maintenance vs consumption smoothing under structural burdens vs refinancing of prior extraction?
- **Alternatives criterion:** if non-extractive rails exist (public/commons buffers, baseline access, regulated products) but are blocked, credit pricing and fee-stacks are presumed E.
- **Cascade criterion:** penalty interest, junk fees, collections ecosystems, and refinancing friction point to E components.

Operational rule: split the spread into functional funding cost + bounded risk + bounded operations (candidate P) and any excess driven by dependency/opacity/penalty cascades (E).

## B01.CH02.P05.S03 — Rent and land: cost-covering stewardship versus land rent as E (scarcity/claim criterion)

Housing and land flows often mix stewardship function with scarcity-based toll extraction.

- **Cost-covering stewardship (P-aligned):** payment that covers maintenance, operations, and bounded management of a resource, with transparent cost basis and contestable terms.

- **Land rent as E:** payment attributable to controlling access to scarce location/land without corresponding build-up of capacity or quality—i.e., a scarcity/claim premium.

Decision hinges on:

- **Scarcity criterion:** if scarcity is structural and the owner captures premium without contributing to capacity/quality, that premium is E.
- **Claim criterion:** if the payment is primarily for the right to exclude others from access to a scarce essential, absent functional justification, it is E.
- **Stewardship split:** genuine maintenance and service provision can be P, but the scarcity premium remains E.

Operational rule: treat maintenance/operations separately (P) and isolate location/scarcity premium (E) using defined benchmarks and transparency requirements. Under missing disclosure, presume E for the premium component.

## B01.CH02.P05.S04 — Platforms: service (P) versus toll/lock-in (E), and data-commons (C)

Platform flows are structurally mixed: platforms provide real services but can also operate as private gates with take-rate extraction and lock-in.

Classification requires decomposing:

- **Platform service (P):** hosting, matching, logistics support, fraud control, dispute handling, payments processing, and other measurable functional delivery with bounded margins.
- **Platform toll / lock-in (E):** take rates above functional cost bands, pay-to-reach ranking, API gating, self-preferencing, forced bundling, data extraction as a condition of access, and switching-cost exploitation.
- **Data-commons (C) (where applicable):** contributions to shared data infrastructure governed as commons, with non-exclusive availability, bounded uses, and traceable public benefit.

Operational rule: treat “the take rate” as a composite that must be decomposed into functional cost and premium; treat lock-in mechanisms as E signals; treat any “data commons” claim as C only under explicit governance/access/maintenance rules.

## B01.CH02.P05.S05 — Public/private hybrids: concessions, PPP, quasi-public bodies — split rules for P/E/C

Hybrids (concessions, PPPs, quasi-public operators) are not exempt from P/E/C decomposition. Public mission language is not a classification rule.

Split rules:

- **P component:** functional delivery and maintenance that meets service levels, with transparent costs and bounded margins.
- **C component:** contributions that build/maintain commons capacity and preserve non-exclusive access under public governance minima.
- **E component:** guaranteed excess returns, opaque “availability payments” that exceed functional bands, fee-stacks via subcontractor cascades, vendor lock-in, and contract terms that create non-contestable toll gates around essentials.

Operational requirements:
- disclose contract economics sufficiently for decomposition,
- publish margin and fee-stack indicators,
- include renegotiation/termination triggers when E signals persist,
- and fail closed on black-box concessions where attribution cannot be audited.

These edge-case rules are intentionally conservative: when classification is ambiguous and evidence is blocked, the default is not “trust”; it is **audit escalation or conservative presumption**.
