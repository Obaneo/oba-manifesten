# B01.CH02.P03 — Basic accounting: attribution of flows to actors and positions

This paragraph specifies how flows (and their P/E/C components) are attributed in a way that is robust to legal form games, multi-layer stacks, and internal pass-through charging.

## B01.CH02.P03.S01 — Actor versus position: owner, manager, executor, platform, financier as distinct roles

Attribution is performed to **positions**, not only to named legal entities. A single actor may occupy multiple positions, and a single position may be represented by multiple legal entities.

Minimum position types (non-exhaustive):

- **Owner / claimant:** holds the cashflow right, title, or priority claim (legal or economic).
- **Manager / controller:** controls decisions, sets terms, or can block access (de facto or de jure).
- **Executor / operator:** performs production/service/maintenance activity (functional delivery).
- **Platform / gate operator:** controls access to a network/market/identity/payment rail; can impose fees or ranking rules.
- **Financier / balance-sheet position:** provides funding/credit and holds collateral/security interests; can enforce covenants or extract spreads.

The purpose of separating roles is to prevent semantic collapse where “the firm” is treated as a unitary node even though extraction is often generated by specific gate or claim positions embedded in or around it.

## B01.CH02.P03.S02 — Attribution principle: “who receives what” and “who controls/can block” (ultimate control)

Attribution requires two parallel questions:

1. **Receipt attribution (cashflow rights):**  
   *Who receives what?* Identify the recipient(s) of each flow component (including fees, spreads, royalties, carry, service charges), within the declared time window.

2. **Control attribution (block power):**  
   *Who controls or can block the flow or the access condition that generates it?* Identify ultimate control/block positions that determine:
   - pricing and fee schedules,
   - contract terms, renewals, termination/disconnection,
   - access gating (platform entry, ranking, identity lockout, payment denial),
   - scarcity enforcement (capacity throttling, exclusive routes).

Where receipt and control diverge (common in licensing, concessions, platform stacks, and financial structures), both must be recorded. For compatibility and enforcement, **ultimate control** is decisive for responsibility and for anti-avoidance.

## B01.CH02.P03.S03 — Consolidation: through holdings/vehicles (economic reality over legal form)

Flow attribution must consolidate through intermediary legal forms when they are used to fragment ownership, mask control, or shift location.

Consolidation rules (high-level):

- **Look-through:** treat holdings/SPVs/trust-like/foundation-like vehicles as transparent for attribution when they are controlled for the benefit of an ultimate controller.
- **Economic reality over legal form:** contractual side letters, revenue shares, preferred returns, and embedded options count if they create economic exposure.
- **Ultimate beneficial control (UBC):** allocate receipt/control to the ultimate controller(s) where control tests indicate de facto or de jure control, even if legal title is separated.

If consolidation cannot be performed due to missing disclosure or obstructed registries, classification and compatibility claims must **fail closed** for the affected components.

## B01.CH02.P03.S04 — Chain attribution: distributing P/E/C across multiple links (supply chain, platform stack)

Flows are often **stacked** across chains: supplier → distributor → platform → payment rail → consumer; or borrower → broker → servicer → securitization SPV → investors.

Chain attribution requirements:

- **Identify the chain:** list links and positions that take a share of the gross flow.
- **Decompose per link:** assign P/E/C components at each link based on function, cost basis, and gate power.
- **Locate E layers:** explicitly flag where toll/fee-stack/lock-in components attach (platform take-rate, brokerage fees, financing spreads, service charges, licensing tolls).
- **Prevent “E laundering”:** no link may relabel an upstream toll as “cost” without evidence. Pass-through claims require auditable cost bases.

Chain attribution is mandatory where:
- the payer cannot realistically exit without switching costs (essentials, network services),
- gate operators set unilateral terms,
- or fee-stacks indicate multi-layer extraction.

## B01.CH02.P03.S05 — Prevent double counting: rules for gross/net flows and internal pass-through charging

To keep indicators interpretable and auditable, the model enforces anti-double-counting rules:

- **Gross vs net discipline:**  
  - *Gross* is used to map the full chain of who takes what.  
  - *Net* is used for aggregation so the same value is not counted multiple times across internal transfers.

- **Internal pass-through charging:**  
  Internal cross-charges (management fees, internal licensing, transfer pricing, shared-service markups) must be treated as **internal** unless they correspond to externally evidenced function and cost. Otherwise, they are prime candidates for E classification.

- **Consolidated reporting units:**  
  For sector and economy-level indicators, use consolidated units where intra-group flows are eliminated, while preserving traceability for audits.

- **Reconciliation requirement:**  
  Any published P/E/C aggregates must reconcile to observable totals within declared tolerances, with an explicit handling of unknowns and estimation error.

These rules ensure that reported E shares, fee-stack depth, and sector decompositions are not artifacts of accounting structure or internal billing games.
