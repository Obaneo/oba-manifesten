# B01.CH02.P04 — Node types (household, firm, financial entity, state, commons institution)

This paragraph defines the minimum node types used in the flow network and specifies what must be observable for each type to support P/E/C attribution, R/B analysis, caps enforcement, and commons measurement.

## B01.CH02.P04.S01 — Household: income mix, burdens, access to commons

A **household node** is the unit where the order indicator (B→T→N→L→R, and R/B ≥ α) is evaluated.

Minimum household-relevant observables:

- **Income mix (B):** labour income, capital income, transfers, and in-kind components where defined.
- **Levies (T):** taxes/premiums/contributions that apply to the household within the time window.
- **Mandatory burdens (L):** the L-minimum basket components and their contract conditions (fees, indexation, penalties, lock-in).
- **Residual space (R):** derived from N − L under the defined method.
- **Commons access:** whether and how the household accesses baseline commons provisions (coverage, eligibility, exclusion events, waiting times), without requiring person-level surveillance.

Households are not treated as “consumers” only; they are the locus where extraction becomes visible as burdens, lock-in, and reduced choice/exit capacity.

## B01.CH02.P04.S02 — Firm: production, pricing, market power, surplus distribution

A **firm node** is a production and contracting unit that can generate P, host E layers, and (in some forms) contribute to commons capacity.

Minimum firm-relevant observables:

- **Production/service function:** what is delivered (output category), including maintenance duties where applicable.
- **Pricing and fee structure:** price components, surcharges, service charges, subscription fees, take rates.
- **Cost basis and margins (minimum categories):** sufficient to distinguish functional delivery from excess toll components.
- **Market power signals:** concentration, switching costs, exclusivity, bottleneck control, vertical integration, self-preferencing.
- **Surplus distribution:** how surplus is paid out (wages, dividends, buybacks, fees, royalties, interest), including internal pass-throughs.

Firms are treated as node+position bundles: the same legal firm may contain platform gate positions, licensing positions, and financing positions that must be separated for attribution.

## B01.CH02.P04.S03 — Financial entity: claims, interest, securitization, collateral circuits

A **financial entity node** holds and structures claims and can generate E via spreads, fee-stacks, and collateral-based allocation circuits.

Minimum financial-entity observables:

- **Claim inventory:** loan books, securities, derivatives exposure, guarantees, and priority claims.
- **Cashflow extraction:** interest spreads, fees, servicing charges, origination/brokerage fees, carry/management fees.
- **Collateral regime:** what collateral is accepted, haircut/valuation rules, margin calls, enforcement rights.
- **Repackaging structures:** securitization chains, SPVs, tranching, embedded leverage, side letters.
- **Control and allocation power:** underwriting criteria, covenants, de-risking decisions, credit gating, payment-rail denial.

Financial entities are central for consolidation rules and for detecting when monetary expansion channels feed asset inflation rather than productive or commons capacity.

## B01.CH02.P04.S04 — State: fiscality, regulation, order enforcement, public allocation

A **state node** is a constitutive order actor in the economic layer, with powers and duties that affect flows through fiscality, regulation, enforcement, and allocation.

Minimum state-relevant observables:

- **Fiscal flows:** tax bases, levy structures, transfers, earmarks, and collection/execution costs.
- **Regulatory flows and permissions:** licensing, concessions, permits, boundary rules, and enforcement actions.
- **Order enforcement:** audit capacity, sanctions, dispute/appeal structures, exception regimes.
- **Public allocation:** procurement, subsidies (where permitted), public options, and commons budgeting (capex/opex).
- **ΔM interface:** where the state participates in or governs ΔM channels (facilities, guarantees, public banks), with traceability and anti-capture rails.

The state node is not treated as a generic redistributor; it is modeled as an order institution with specific boundary conditions and failure modes (including state capture risk).

## B01.CH02.P04.S05 — Commons institution: governance, access, maintenance, non-exclusive provisions

A **commons institution node** is defined by regime characteristics, not by ownership label. It is the node type used to represent commons as a base layer.

Minimum commons-institution observables:

- **Governance minimum:** decision body, mandate, rotation/anti-capture rules, conflict resolution, transparency and audit logs.
- **Access regime:** baseline access rules, conditional access, exclusion criteria, re-entry paths, and contestability (objection/appeal).
- **Maintenance system:** maintenance cycles, service levels, backlog indicators, incident logs, continuity plans.
- **Non-exclusivity rules:** default non-exclusive availability with explicit boundary rules for capacity and abuse.
- **Outputs:** ΔG(t) (capacity/coverage/reach) and **quality indicators** (availability, reliability, accessibility), with versioning and revision policy.

Commons institutions must be able to demonstrate that “commons” is not a label over a private toll structure. Where evidence is missing, conformance claims fail closed.
