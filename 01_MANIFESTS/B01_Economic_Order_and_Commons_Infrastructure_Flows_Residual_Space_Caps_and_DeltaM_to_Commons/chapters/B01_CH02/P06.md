# B01.CH02.P06 — Measurement strategy: the minimum you must observe to distinguish P/E/C

This paragraph specifies the minimum evidence required to operationalize P/E/C in a way that is auditable, publishable, and robust against relabeling. Where the minimum cannot be observed, classification must fail closed and/or trigger audit escalation.

## B01.CH02.P06.S01 — Minimal observation set: transaction flow, price, cost, claim type, control power, access form

A P/E/C classification requires, at minimum, an observation set that covers both cashflows and the rights/gates that generate them. Minimum fields (conceptual, not schema):

1. **Transaction flow:** who pays whom, how often, under which contract/rail, and for what nominal category.
2. **Price/charge:** the all-in price paid by the payer, including fees, surcharges, add-ons, penalties, and effective spreads.
3. **Cost basis (minimum):** enough cost-category information to distinguish functional cost from excess premium (direct costs, operations, maintenance, funding cost where relevant).
4. **Claim type:** what right or claim justifies the flow (contract fee, rent, interest, royalty, platform take rate, concession payment, etc.).
5. **Control power / blockability:** who sets terms and who can block access/continuity (ultimate control, termination/disconnection, ranking, denial).
6. **Access form:** how the payer obtains access (contestable market, gated platform, monopoly concession, subscription lock-in), including switching-cost markers.

Without these elements, “P vs E” becomes a narrative assertion rather than a testable classification.

## B01.CH02.P06.S02 — Proxies and uncertainty: when approximation is allowed and how error margins are made explicit

Approximation is permitted only under explicit rules that preserve auditability:

- **Proxy admissibility:** a proxy may substitute for a missing field only when (i) the proxy is defined, (ii) the proxy has known limitations, and (iii) it is correlated to the missing field in a way that can be tested.
- **Uncertainty disclosure:** every proxy-based classification must carry:
  - an explicit **uncertainty band** or confidence category,
  - a list of **assumptions**,
  - and a statement of what evidence would overturn the classification.

- **No silent imputation:** missing information must not be silently “filled in” by model defaults. Unknowns remain explicit (“unknown” is a valid category).

- **Fail-closed threshold:** where the uncertainty is too large to support a non-extractive claim, the classification cannot certify “low E”; it must either (i) remain unclassified, (ii) be conservatively presumed, or (iii) trigger audit.

## B01.CH02.P06.S03 — Audit logic: sampling, exceptions, red flags (markup extremes, lock-in indicators, interest/rent levels)

Audit strategy is risk-based but must be reproducible and resistant to capture:

- **Sampling discipline:** sampling is allowed only when the sampling method is explicit, repeatable, and adequate for the risk profile. Essentials and gated markets typically require higher coverage.
- **Exceptions discipline:** any exception regime (special contract forms, bespoke pricing, carve-outs) is itself a red flag and must be logged, published in aggregate, and periodically reviewed.
- **Red flags (minimum families):**
  - **Markup extremes:** margins/spreads far outside functional bands, persistent excess margins, unexplained variability.
  - **Fee-stack depth:** multiple stacked intermediaries, proliferation of add-on fees, penalty cascades.
  - **Lock-in indicators:** exclusivity, termination penalties, portability loss, identity/payment lockouts, long minimum terms.
  - **Interest/rent level anomalies:** rates/premia inconsistent with risk and funding cost, or rents inconsistent with stewardship cost and maintenance quality.
  - **Opacity:** missing disclosures, black-box pricing, proprietary models used as justification without audit packs.

Red flags trigger a standard escalation path: anomaly → audit request → forensic access (if needed) → requalification/remedies.

## B01.CH02.P06.S04 — Publishable indicators: E share per sector, P/C build-up, concentration and lock-in metrics

The purpose of measurement is not only internal classification but public contestability. Minimum publishable indicator families:

- **E share per sector:** a sector-level decomposition indicator showing the estimated extractive component share, with method and revision policy.
- **P/C build-up:** indicators for productive capacity and commons contribution (including ΔG(t) and quality where relevant).
- **Concentration metrics:** measures of market structure (e.g., concentration ratios, dependency graphs) sufficient to detect persistent gate power.
- **Lock-in metrics:** switching-cost proxies, contract clamp incidence, portability success/failure rates, termination/disconnection events (aggregated).
- **Fee-stack indicators:** average and tail fee-stack depth, add-on fee shares, spreads, and junk-fee incidence.

All publishable indicators must include:
- **definition-set ID**, **method ID**, **uncertainty bands**, and **restatement policy**.

## B01.CH02.P06.S05 — Version control of definitions: how changes in P/E/C rules remain traceable (anti-drift)

Because P/E/C classification is vulnerable to semantic drift, rule changes must be versioned as strictly as code:

- **Definition-set versioning:** every indicator and classification output must state which definition version was used.
- **Change logs:** changes must be logged with rationale, expected impact, and backward-compatibility notes where possible.
- **Restatements:** when definition changes materially alter historical indicators, restated series must be published (or an explicit non-restatement decision with reasons).
- **Drift alarms:** rising exceptions, widening uncertainty, or unexplained changes in E share without structural interventions trigger a drift review.

The objective is to make “definition politics” auditable: changes are allowed, but never hidden, never silent, and never untestable.
