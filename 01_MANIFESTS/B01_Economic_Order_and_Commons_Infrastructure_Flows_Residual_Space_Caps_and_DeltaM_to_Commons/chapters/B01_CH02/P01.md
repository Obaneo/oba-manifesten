# B01.CH02.P01 — The economy as a flow network: nodes, edges, ownership, and access

## B01.CH02.P01.S01 — Network definition: nodes (actors/institutions), edges (claims/transactions), time window

For the purposes of Book 1, the economy is modeled as a **flow network** over a specified **time window**.

- **Nodes** are actors or institutions that can hold positions and participate in flows, including (non-exhaustive): households, firms, financial entities, public bodies, and commons institutions.
- **Edges** represent directed **flows** between nodes. A flow is an economically relevant movement of value or obligation, including:
  - payments for goods/services,
  - transfers and contributions,
  - rents, fees, interest, dividends,
  - in-kind provision and service delivery where defined as measurable flows,
  - changes in claim positions that imply cashflow rights or control rights within the time window.

The model is **time-bounded**: every flow observation and every derived indicator is defined relative to a declared time window (e.g., month, quarter, year). Aggregation across windows is allowed only with an explicit method and revision rules.

This network representation is used to:
- locate where **production (P)** occurs,
- isolate where **extractive layers (E)** attach to essential flows,
- and identify where **commons contribution (C)** is built or eroded.

## B01.CH02.P01.S02 — Ownership versus access: why access/use is modeled as a separate layer

The model distinguishes **ownership** from **access/use** because economic power and burden can arise without formal ownership.

- **Ownership** refers to legal title or formal claim to an asset, right, or cashflow.
- **Access/use** refers to the ability to use, consume, operate, or benefit from an asset/service, possibly under conditions, prices, or restrictions that create lock-in.

This separation is required because:
- many extractive mechanisms operate as **gates on access** (subscriptions, licensing, platform entry, toll layers, exclusivity), not as direct ownership;
- households experience order primarily through **access conditions and mandatory burdens (L)**, even when ownership sits elsewhere;
- control and blockage can be exercised through access rules (deplatforming, disconnection, termination clauses) even without asset ownership.

Therefore, the network must carry both:
- **ownership/claim relations** (who holds the right),
- and **access relations** (who can use what, under which constraints and switching costs).

## B01.CH02.P01.S03 — Claims and rights: contracts, platforms, permits, IP, collateral as flow carriers

In this model, **claims and rights** are treated as first-class objects because they determine which flows exist, who can block them, and who can extract rents from them. Typical claim/rights carriers include:

- **Contracts:** employment contracts, leases, service agreements, supply contracts, loan agreements, insurance policies, concessions, and procurement contracts.
- **Platforms and network gate rights:** marketplace access, app-store distribution, ranking and visibility rights, API access, payment rails, and identity/account access.
- **Permits and licenses:** regulatory permissions, quotas, land-use rights, spectrum allocations, and professional licenses where they gate essential access.
- **Intellectual property (IP):** patents, copyrights, trade secrets, and licensing rights that can generate cashflows or enforce exclusion.
- **Collateral and security interests:** liens, pledges, margin arrangements, and repo-style structures that create priority claims and can drive collateral-based allocation.

Each carrier is relevant insofar as it:
- generates a recurring flow,
- enables a toll/fee-stack/lock-in mechanism,
- creates blockage power (ability to deny access),
- or shifts control without changing legal ownership.

Downstream chapters use this representation to decompose observed cashflows into **P/E/C**, attribute them to positions and ultimate control, and define the minimum evidence required to audit these mechanisms.
