# B01.CH02.P02 — Definitions of P (productive), E (extractive/parasitic component), C (commons contribution)

This paragraph fixes the operational criteria for decomposing flows into **P**, **E**, and **C**. These are functional classifications applied to **flow components**, not moral judgments about actors.

## B01.CH02.P02.S01 — P: criteria for productive value creation (output, service, maintenance, reproduction)

A flow component is classified as **P (productive)** when it is attributable to delivering and maintaining real economic capacity under contestable conditions and bounded margins. P includes (non-exhaustive):

- **Output provision:** goods and services delivered that satisfy defined needs or demand.
- **Service delivery:** performance of a function that users consume (including delivery, operation, support) where the function is real and evidenced.
- **Maintenance and upkeep:** activities and spending that preserve or restore capacity, reliability, and safety (maintenance-first logic).
- **Reproduction of capacity:** training, education, care, and other capacity-supporting functions where they are part of an explicit economic provisioning regime.

Operationally, a component qualifies as P when:
- it has a **functional basis** (identifiable service/output/maintenance),
- the **cost basis** is reasonably characterizable (direct/indirect cost categories exist),
- compensation is within a **bounded band** relative to functional costs and risk, and
- it is not primarily sustained by gate power, opacity, or scarcity construction.

## B01.CH02.P02.S02 — E: criteria for extraction (toll/claim above functional cost; lock-in; scarcity construction)

A flow component is classified as **E (extractive)** when it is attributable to a toll on access or a claim on others’ flows that is not justified by functional costs, legitimate bounded risk compensation, or commons maintenance.

E includes (non-exhaustive) components arising from:

- **Toll/claim above functional cost:** margins, fees, spreads, or charges that exceed what can be justified by functional delivery + bounded risk + bounded operations.
- **Lock-in and switching-cost leverage:** cashflows sustained by contract clamp, portability loss, exclusivity, penalties, identity lockouts, or platform dependence.
- **Scarcity construction:** deliberate restriction of capacity/supply, bottleneck control, or artificial constraints that raise prices without producing corresponding capacity or quality.
- **Fee-stacks and intermediary cascades:** multiple layers of charges that accumulate around essentials or unavoidable channels, especially where each layer adds little functional value.
- **Market power without contestability:** monopoly/oligopoly pricing sustained by barriers rather than by superior function, with persistent excess margins.

A component should be presumed E under:
- **opacity** (missing disclosure of fees/costs/terms),
- **non-contestable gate power** (the payer cannot realistically exit),
- or **structural concentration** with persistent excess margins.

## B01.CH02.P02.S03 — C: criteria for commons contribution (build/maintain/access; non-exclusive availability)

A flow component is classified as **C (commons contribution)** when it funds or performs the building, maintenance, and governed provision of a commons regime under non-exclusive availability rules and transparency constraints.

C includes (non-exhaustive):

- **Build-up (capex):** capacity creation for commons infrastructure (physical, digital, service capacity).
- **Maintenance (opex):** upkeep that preserves reliability, safety, accessibility, continuity, and service levels.
- **Access provisioning:** funding and operation that expands baseline access under explicit rules, with contestability and due process.
- **Governance and audit capacity:** contributions that enable transparent governance, dispute mechanisms, and auditability (as required by the order).

Operationally, a component qualifies as C when:
- the recipient (or mechanism) is governed as a **commons institution/regime** (access + maintenance + governance),
- **non-exclusivity** is the default (with explicit boundary rules for exclusion),
- and the contribution is **traceable** to measurable commons outputs (ΔG(t) and quality).

## B01.CH02.P02.S04 — Mixed forms: when a flow is split into P+E or P+C (attribution rule)

Many real-world flows are mixed. Book 1 therefore treats P/E/C as **decompositions**, not labels for entire sectors or contracts.

A flow must be split when:
- it contains a **functional service component** plus a **toll component** (P+E), or
- it contains a **functional service component** plus an **explicit commons contribution** (P+C).

The split is performed by an attribution rule that:
1. identifies the **functional basis** and its cost bands (candidate P),
2. identifies **commons-regime contributions** that are traceable to commons outputs (candidate C),
3. assigns the remainder—where sustained by gate power, scarcity construction, or excess markups—to **E**.

If a split cannot be performed due to missing evidence, the classification must **fail closed**: the component cannot be certified as “non-extractive” by default.

## B01.CH02.P02.S05 — Negative and zero values: when C or P can be zero/negative (degradation, depletion, damage)

P and C are not assumed positive by definition. They can be **zero** or **negative** when the flow’s functional or commons effect is neutral or harmful.

- **P = 0** when a claimed service/output cannot be evidenced or is purely circular/internal without delivering externally relevant function.
- **P < 0** when the activity causes net degradation (e.g., systematic harm, depletion, damage) that exceeds any functional benefit within the accounting boundary, using the defined damage-accounting rule set.

- **C = 0** when a purported commons contribution does not increase or maintain commons capacity/quality (no measurable ΔG/quality linkage).
- **C < 0** when the activity labeled “commons” in fact erodes commons capacity (underinvestment, access restriction, capture, quality collapse), or when it creates new toll layers around the commons regime (“commons-washing”).

Negative classification requires explicit boundary and evidence rules (including uncertainty handling) and must be published with the method and revision policy when used in public reporting.
