# B01.CH06.P01 — Definition of commons in OBA: not “public property” and not “private”, but an order of use and maintenance

This paragraph defines **commons** as an OBA order object. Commons is not defined by who “owns” an asset (state, cooperative, nonprofit, or
private), but by whether a stable regime exists that guarantees **access**, enforces **maintenance**, and constrains **gate power** so that the
service does not regress into a toll market.

In OBA, commons exists to reduce mandatory burdens (L), stabilize R/B tails, and substitute for domains where markets structurally produce
lock-in, scarcity rents, or fee-stacks (E). Therefore commons must be specified as a regime with rights, governance, financing, measurement,
and boundary rules—not as a moral label.

---

## B01.CH06.P01.S01 — Definition: commons as a regime of access + maintenance + governance (not merely an ownership form)

### Definition (binding)
A **commons** is a regime that satisfies all three conditions:

1. **Access regime:** baseline access is defined, rule-bound, and contestable; access is not mediated by private toll layers as the default.
2. **Maintenance regime:** maintenance and continuity are enforced as first-class obligations (opex/capex discipline), preventing degradation and
   scarcity-by-neglect.
3. **Governance regime:** decision-making, transparency, conflict resolution, and anti-capture constraints exist such that the access and
   maintenance rules cannot be quietly hollowed out.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If any of the three regimes (access, maintenance, governance) is absent or unenforceable, the system is not a commons; it is a provision
  that can drift into privatized toll dynamics.

---

## B01.CH06.P01.S02 — Delimitation: commons versus public service, state ownership, cooperative, and market good

Commons overlaps with familiar categories but is not identical to them.

### Delimitation principles (binding)
- **Commons ≠ state ownership:** state-owned assets can still be run as toll gates or under-maintained monopolies; ownership alone is not
  evidence of commons.
- **Commons ≠ public service label:** “public” branding without enforceable access/maintenance/governance is cosmetic.
- **Commons ≠ cooperative:** cooperatives may be commons-compatible or may be exclusionary clubs; compatibility depends on the regime rules.
- **Commons ≠ market good:** markets can supply goods around commons, but the commons core is defined by baseline access and boundary rules that
  prevent toll layering on essentials.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Classification depends on regime properties and evidence, not on institutional form or rhetoric.

---

## B01.CH06.P01.S03 — Non-exclusivity and conditions: when exclusion is permitted (capacity, abuse, contribution)

Commons is **non-exclusive by default**, but exclusion is sometimes required to preserve capacity and integrity.

### Permissible exclusion grounds (minimum; binding)
- **Capacity constraints:** scarcity protocols (quotas, scheduling, priority rules) when capacity is objectively limited.
- **Abuse prevention:** exclusion for defined abusive behaviors that threaten safety, continuity, or infrastructure integrity.
- **Contribution conditions:** where contribution is required (time/money/maintenance duties), exclusion may occur only after a defined process,
  proportionality test, and repair/re-entry path.

### Procedural requirements (binding)
- **Proportionality:** exclusion must be minimal and time-bounded relative to the violation.
- **Contestability:** objection/appeal routes must exist, with time limits and published criteria.
- **Re-entry path:** exclusions require repair paths; permanent exclusion is an exceptional measure requiring higher justification.

## B01.CH06.P01.S04 — Anti-privatization rule: which elements must be legally inalienable

Commons requires legal hard points, otherwise it can be re-privatized via concessions, outsourcing, or asset sales.

### Inalienable elements (minimum; binding)
- **Baseline access rule:** the right-to-access baseline cannot be sold, gated, or converted into a discretionary privilege.
- **Governance constraints:** anti-capture rules, transparency duties, and contestability procedures cannot be removed by contract.
- **Boundary rules:** prohibitions on toll layering, exclusivity capture, and lock-in around commons access must be enforceable.
- **Maintenance obligations:** maintenance-first funding and minimum service levels must not be optional or degradable without escalation.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the regime can be sold or hollowed out through private contracts without public review and audit, it is not durable and fails commons
  classification.

---

## B01.CH06.P01.S05 — “Commons-washing” detection: signals that something is called commons but functions as a private toll

Commons-washing is the use of commons language while the system behaves as a toll market.

### Commons-washing signals (minimum; binding)
- **Access is nominal but not real:** long delays, hidden gatekeeping, or discretionary denial without contestable rules.
- **Toll layering persists:** mandatory intermediaries, fee-stacks, or “service plans” are required to access the baseline.
- **Closed governance:** decisions are opaque, exceptions are frequent, and user challenge rights are absent.
- **Maintenance neglect:** chronic backlog growth, service degradation, or scarcity-by-neglect while budgets “exist on paper”.
- **Vendor lock-in:** proprietary control systems or contracts make exit impossible and normalize private gate power.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If commons-washing signals are present, the system is treated as non-commons until redesigned and evidenced (fail-closed on labeling).
