# B01.CH06.P02 — Rights layer: access, use, contribution, exclusion, re-entry

This paragraph specifies the **rights layer** required for a commons regime to be stable, contestable, and non-captured. “Commons” is not a
single right; it is a bundle of rights and duties that must be explicitly defined to prevent drift into either (i) privatized toll gating or
(ii) bureaucratic gatekeeping that functions as a hidden toll (administrative L).

Rights are defined as rule-bound and procedurally enforceable. Every rights rule must be compatible with non-exclusivity by default, privacy
minimalism, and anti-stigmatization constraints.

---

## B01.CH06.P02.S01 — Access rights: baseline access, priority rights, conditional access (criteria)

Access is the primary commons output: it defines who may reach the baseline service and under what conditions.

### Required access right types (minimum; binding)
- **Baseline access:** a defined minimum package available by default, with clear eligibility and minimal proof burden.
- **Priority rights:** explicit priority ordering when capacity is scarce (e.g., vulnerability, essential dependence, critical continuity).
- **Conditional access:** conditions are permitted only when necessary (capacity, safety, abuse prevention) and must be rule-bound.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Minimal proof burden:** access must not require excessive documentation, time, or digital hurdles; administrative friction counts as harm.
- **Non-discrimination:** eligibility criteria must be auditable and must not operate as covert exclusion via proxies.
- **Contestability:** denial must be explainable and challengeable via a time-bounded procedure.

---

## B01.CH06.P02.S02 — Use rights: bands, fair use, quotas, and scarcity protocols

Use rights define how the commons is consumed, preventing both overuse and gatekeeping.

### Required use rules (minimum; binding)
- **Use bands:** define normal use ranges for baseline access (what counts as ordinary use versus luxury/high-load use).
- **Fair use principle:** prohibit arbitrary restriction when capacity exists; restrictions must be justified by capacity/safety.
- **Quotas (when needed):** apply only under measured scarcity; quotas must be transparent and subject to review.
- **Scarcity protocols:** define what happens under shortfall (rationing rules, priority tiers, temporary measures, and sunsets).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Scarcity protocols must not be a permanent excuse for underinvestment; persistent scarcity triggers maintenance/capacity escalation (CH06.P04).
- Quotas must not become hidden exclusion for specific groups via administrative complexity.

---

## B01.CH06.P02.S03 — Contribution duties: maintenance, time, money, data — what counts as contribution and how it is weighted

Commons may require contributions to remain sustainable, but contribution must not become a private toll or a stigma device.

### Contribution categories (minimum; binding)
- **Maintenance contributions:** direct work, stewardship duties, or participation in upkeep where appropriate.
- **Time contributions:** participation duties only when essential to governance/maintenance and not exclusionary by default.
- **Financial contributions:** tariffs/fees are permissible only within essentials-protective rails and anti-regression constraints.
- **Data contributions:** permitted only when necessary for operation/audit; data maximalism and secondary use are prohibited.

### Weighting and limits (binding)
- Contribution rules must be **transparent**, **proportionate**, and **non-extractive**.
- Contribution must not be designed to create **new fee-stacks** or mandatory intermediaries.
- Under inability to contribute, repair paths must exist; contribution cannot function as a poverty trap.

---

## B01.CH06.P02.S04 — Exclusion: grounds, proportionality, term, and objection/appeal

Exclusion is a last-resort tool to preserve capacity and integrity, not a convenience for governance.

### Permissible grounds (minimum; binding)
- **Capacity enforcement:** repeated, documented violation of scarcity protocols.
- **Abuse/safety:** defined behaviors that threaten safety, integrity, or continuity.
- **Contribution breach:** only after notice, reasonable accommodation, and a defined repair offer.

### Procedural constraints (binding)
- **Proportionality:** minimal scope, minimal duration, and clear linkage to the breach.
- **Time-bounded term:** exclusions must specify a duration and review date.
- **Objection/appeal:** fast and accessible appeal routes; decisions must be reproducible and logged.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Exclusion without due process converts commons into discretionary gate power and is non-conforming.

---

## B01.CH06.P02.S05 — Re-entry: repair paths, amnesty criteria, and anti-stigmatization rules

Commons must include re-entry to prevent permanent exclusion and long-run stratification.

### Required re-entry design (minimum; binding)
- **Repair paths:** explicit steps to restore access (repayment plans, behavior commitments, mediation, safety training, etc.), tailored to the
  breach type.
- **Amnesty criteria:** time-based or condition-based amnesty for minor breaches, to avoid lifetime exclusion.
- **Anti-stigmatization:** re-entry must not require humiliation rituals, public labeling, or invasive monitoring.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A commons regime without re-entry produces durable exclusion classes and will drift into coercive administration.

---

## B01.CH06.P02.S06 — Rights portability: what happens under relocation, job change, or regime change

Portability prevents lock-in and makes access real across life changes.

### Required portability rules (minimum; binding)
- **Relocation:** access rights transfer across regions within defined timelines; waiting time/eligibility must not be reset without explicit
  justification.
- **Job change:** access must not depend on employer attachment; employer-based gatekeeping is treated as clamp risk.
- **Regime change:** when a domain transitions from market to commons (or hybrid), users must have defined migration paths and continuity
  guarantees.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If rights are non-portable, commons becomes a local lock-in mechanism and undermines its own anti-toll purpose.
