# B01.CH06.P03 — Governance minimum: decision-making, conflict resolution, transparency, rotation

This paragraph specifies the **governance minimum** for commons institutions. Commons fails when governance drifts into (i) vendor/elite capture,
(ii) opaque exception culture, or (iii) bureaucratic gatekeeping that recreates toll-like friction. Governance minimum is therefore defined as
a structural template: a decision body with a bounded mandate, a conflict and appeal architecture, transparency discipline, and rotation rules.

Governance may vary locally (“fork space”), but only within explicit compatibility constraints. Variants are allowed; core loss is not.

---

## B01.CH06.P03.S01 — Minimum structure: body, mandate, quorum, and basic decision types

### Required governance structure (minimum; binding)
- **Governing body:** a defined organ responsible for access, maintenance, budgets, and boundary rule enforcement.
- **Mandate:** a published mandate that binds the body to baseline access, maintenance-first discipline, and anti-toll boundary rules.
- **Quorum rules:** defined quorum thresholds for ordinary decisions, budget decisions, exclusion decisions, and emergency measures.
- **Decision types (minimum set):**
  - **Access rules** (baseline, priority, conditional access),
  - **Maintenance plans and budgets** (capex/opex, backlog thresholds),
  - **Procurement and contracting** (open standards, anti–vendor lock-in),
  - **Exclusion and re-entry decisions** (rights-layer enforcement),
  - **Exception handling** (temporary deviations with triggers and sunsets).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If access/maintenance/procurement decisions are made without a defined body, quorum, and mandate, commons governance is discretionary and
  non-conforming.

---

## B01.CH06.P03.S02 — Decision-making: consensus/majority/lot — selection criteria per decision type

Commons governance may use different decision procedures depending on the decision type.

### Permissible decision modes (binding)
- **Consensus:** suitable for small-scope rule changes where buy-in is critical and blockage risk is low.
- **Majority vote:** suitable for routine operational decisions and budget allocations where timely execution is necessary.
- **Lot/rotation assignment:** suitable for oversight roles, review panels, or allocation of limited governance positions to reduce clique
  formation.

### Selection criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Impact and reversibility:** high-impact and hard-to-reverse decisions require stricter thresholds and greater transparency.
- **Capture risk:** where capture risk is high, prefer structures that reduce repeated control by the same networks (rotation/lot/independent review).
- **Execution speed:** emergency protocols require fast paths, but must remain rule-bound and sunset-limited.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Governance that uses “emergency speed” as a standing justification for opaque decisions is non-conforming.

---

## B01.CH06.P03.S03 — Conflict resolution: escalation ladder, independent arbitration, and sanction paths

Commons needs a conflict system that prevents both coercion and paralysis.

### Required conflict architecture (minimum; binding)
- **Escalation ladder:** staged process (informal resolution → formal complaint → independent review → arbitration/adjudication).
- **Independent arbitration:** a functionally independent arbiter for disputes involving access denial, exclusion, procurement conflict, or
  allegations of capture.
- **Sanction paths:** defined sanctions for abuse, fraud, corruption, vendor violations, or governance misconduct (including removal/rotation).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Time-bounded resolution:** dispute procedures must have deadlines; indefinite delay is treated as de facto denial.
- **Evidence discipline:** decisions must cite rule IDs, logs, and measurable facts where applicable.

---

## B01.CH06.P03.S04 — Transparency: open books, audit log, public reporting (without privacy breach)

Transparency is required to make commons contestable and to detect commons-washing and capture.

### Required transparency surface (minimum; binding)
- **Open books (aggregate):** budgets, spending categories, procurement awards, and maintenance backlogs must be publishable in aggregate.
- **Audit log:** tamper-evident logs for decisions, exceptions, access-rule changes, and procurement change orders.
- **Public reporting:** periodic reporting of access coverage, waiting times, service levels/quality, backlog distributions, and incidents.
- **Privacy discipline:** publish aggregates with suppression and join-safety; never publish personal-level dossiers as a governance tool.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If transparency cannot be maintained (vendor lock-in, black-box tooling, secrecy by default), the commons status fails closed until corrected.

---

## B01.CH06.P03.S05 — Rotation and anti-capture: terms, incompatibilities, and conflicts of interest

Capture is treated as a default adversary. Governance must be designed so no group can consolidate control indefinitely.

### Required anti-capture rules (minimum; binding)
- **Term limits and rotation:** defined terms for key roles and mandatory rotation.
- **Incompatibilities:** restrictions on simultaneous roles in vendors, regulated providers, or procurement beneficiaries.
- **Conflict-of-interest disclosure:** mandatory disclosures for decision-makers and auditors; published in compliant form.
- **Cooling-off rules:** time-bound constraints on revolving-door movement between vendors/regulated actors and governance/audit roles.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If key roles become permanent or conflicts are undisclosed, governance is captured in practice even if formal rules exist.

---

## B01.CH06.P03.S06 — Fork compatibility: how governance variants are allowed without core loss

Commons governance can vary by sector and locality, but variants must remain compatible with the commons regime definition (CH06.P01).

### Allowed fork space (bounded; admissible)
- decision procedure choices (consensus/majority/lot) per decision type,
- local parameterization of access priorities and scarcity protocols,
- institutional composition and representation models.

### Non-negotiables (binding)
- baseline access must exist and be contestable,
- maintenance-first budgeting and backlog discipline must exist,
- transparency and audit logging must exist,
- anti-capture rotation and incompatibilities must exist,
- boundary rules against toll layering and lock-in must remain enforceable.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If a governance variant weakens any non-negotiable, it is not a permissible fork; it is a regime change and must not be labeled commons.
