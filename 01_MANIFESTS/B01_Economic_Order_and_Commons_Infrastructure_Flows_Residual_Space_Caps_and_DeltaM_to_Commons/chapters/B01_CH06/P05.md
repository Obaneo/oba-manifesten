# B01.CH06.P05 — Commons as a substitute for parasitic markets (where and when)

This paragraph specifies when commons is used as a structural substitute for markets that reliably regenerate parasitic extraction (E). The
objective is not “state expansion” but **toll elimination**: where a domain is structurally prone to monopoly, lock-in, or scarcity rents,
commons provides an exit rail that collapses the toll mechanism and stabilizes household burdens (L), thereby improving R/B tails.

Commons substitution is therefore treated as an engineering response to recurring market failure modes, not as a preference for any ownership
ideology.

---

## B01.CH06.P05.S01 — Selection criterion: where markets structurally produce toll/lock-in (essentials, networks, natural monopolies)

Commons substitution is triggered when the market is structurally unable to remain contestable without producing toll layers.

### Selection criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Essential dependence:** domains where households cannot realistically opt out (housing, energy baseline, basic care, core digital rails).
- **Network effects / natural monopolies:** infrastructure where duplication is inefficient and gate power becomes durable (grids, rails,
  identity/payment rails, platform networks).
- **High switching costs / lock-in:** contract structures or technical dependencies that make exit costly or impossible.
- **Scarcity rent dynamics:** persistent scarcity that yields structural rent extraction (land/location, bottlenecked supply chains).
- **Fee-stack ecosystems:** intermediation layers that grow over time (brokerage, servicing, billing/claims, platform take-rate stacks).
- **Repeated E indicators:** sustained margin anomalies, concentration growth, or lock-in proxies that persist despite regulation attempts.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Substitution must be justified by measurable structural features and E indicators, not by narrative preference.

---

## B01.CH06.P05.S02 — Substitution patterns: full replacement, hybrid layer, or a public option next to the market

Commons substitution can take multiple forms depending on feasibility and risk.

### Permissible patterns (minimum; binding)
- **Full replacement:** the baseline service is provided as commons, and private provision is limited to non-essential add-ons that do not
  undermine baseline access or create toll bypasses.
- **Hybrid layer:** a commons core (infrastructure + baseline access) with contestable private services on top under strict boundary rules
  (interop, portability, fee transparency).
- **Public option next to the market:** a commons-aligned baseline provider exists as a standing alternative that disciplines private pricing and
  contract terms through credible exit.

### Selection criteria (binding)
- choose **full replacement** where monopoly/lock-in is intrinsic and toll regrowth is otherwise unavoidable,
- choose **hybrid** where contestable layers can exist without gate power,
- choose **public option** where markets remain partly contestable but require an exit rail to prevent toll dominance.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A “hybrid” that leaves the gatekeeper layer private and non-contestable is not hybrid; it is commons-washing.

## B01.CH06.P05.S03 — Transition: user migration, contract-break risk, and continuity guarantees

Substitution requires migration mechanics that prevent disruption and prevent the old toll structure from trapping users.

### Required transition mechanics (minimum; binding)
- **User migration plan:** clear pathways for households and firms to move to commons provision with minimal friction.
- **Contract unwind rules:** standardized rules for terminating or converting lock-in contracts (fees, penalties, and exclusivity clauses are
  constrained or voided where they function as E).
- **Continuity guarantees:** service continuity during transition (fallback capacity, temporary bridging, incident response).
- **Procurement and capacity build:** build execution capacity and maintenance readiness first; avoid “announce then fail” transitions.
- **Dispute flow:** fast, reproducible dispute procedures for migration conflicts (billing errors, eligibility disputes, termination disputes).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If transition relies on voluntary exit under punitive penalties, commons substitution is not real and will not collapse E.

---

## B01.CH06.P05.S04 — Interaction with caps/E reduction: how commons substitution lowers E and raises R/B

Commons substitution is not a standalone module; it interacts with caps (CH04) and E phase-down (CH05) and must be evaluated via R/B outcomes
(CH03).

### Interaction logic (minimum; binding)
- **E reduction mechanism:** commons replaces toll revenue streams by providing baseline access without fee-stack intermediation and by removing
  gate power.
- **R/B effect:** reducing essentials burdens (L) increases residual space (R) and stabilizes tails; effects must be measured distributionally.
- **Cap compatibility:** substitution must not create new accumulation carriers (concessions, privileged claims, vendor lock-in rents) that
  bypass W-MAX/I-MAX.
- **Feedback loop:** as commons expands (ΔG), scarcity rents fall and the remaining private layers become more contestable; failure to expand
  capacity leads to scarcity-by-neglect and E regrowth.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Commons substitution is compatible only if it demonstrably reduces E indicators and improves R/B tails without creating new privileged claim
  structures.
