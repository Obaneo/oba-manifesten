# B01.CH06.P06 — Measurement: ΔG(t) and quality indicators (not only quantity)

This paragraph specifies the measurement surface required to validate commons claims. Commons cannot be validated by budgets, organizational
charts, or “program counts”. It is validated by whether **commons capacity and coverage grow (ΔG(t))**, whether **quality and continuity** are
maintained, and whether improvements are **distributionally real** rather than concentrated in already-served segments.

Measurement must be sufficient to detect commons-washing, scarcity-by-neglect, and capture—while remaining strictly privacy-minimal. All
outputs must reference pinned definitions and must publish method discipline (method_id, revision policy, and uncertainty handling where
proxies are used).

---

## B01.CH06.P06.S01 — ΔG(t): definition of commons growth (capacity, reach, coverage)

ΔG(t) measures commons growth over time as infrastructure capacity and reach—not as spending.

### ΔG(t) components (minimum; binding)
- **Capacity:** measurable service capacity (units, throughput, staffing capacity, slots, bandwidth, etc., domain-specific).
- **Reach:** geographic and functional reach (where the service is available; which core functions are covered).
- **Coverage:** share of the relevant population or demand that can realistically access baseline provision within published constraints
  (lead times, eligibility, capacity caps).

### Required reporting (minimum; binding)
- ΔG(t) must be reported as a **time series** with baseline, changes, and attribution to major interventions where feasible.
- Distinguish **gross additions** from **net additions** after degradation, closures, or maintenance failures.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If ΔG(t) is flat or negative while the system claims “commons expansion”, it is washing or mis-specified. Scaling is blocked until corrected.

---

## B01.CH06.P06.S02 — Quality: availability, reliability, accessibility, and user satisfaction (measurable)

Commons must be measured as service quality and continuity, not only as nominal access.

### Required quality dimensions (minimum; binding)
- **Availability:** whether baseline provision is reachable when needed (coverage + lead times + breach rates).
- **Reliability:** continuity and stability (uptime, failure rates, disruption frequency; domain-specific).
- **Accessibility:** practical usability (disability access, language access where relevant, digital inclusion; non-discrimination in practice).
- **User satisfaction (measurable):** procedurally collected experience signals that are structured, contestable, and not reduced to a single KPI.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A commons that exists but is unreliable, inaccessible, or chronically delayed is functionally absent. Quality failure is a regime breach, not
  a PR issue.

---

## B01.CH06.P06.S03 — Equity metrics: who benefits (distribution), without identity categories

Commons success is distributional: it must reduce burdens and increase access especially where needs are highest, without resorting to identity
profiling.

### Required equity reporting (minimum; binding)
- **Distribution of access:** access/lead-time outcomes across regions, household types, and other non-identity segmentation axes.
- **Tail visibility:** p10/p25-style visibility for access measures (lead time tails, denial/abandonment tails), not only averages.
- **Burden linkage:** where relevant, show effects on mandatory burdens (L components) and R/B tails (cross-link to CH03), in privacy-safe form.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If commons improvements concentrate in already-served areas while underserved segments remain locked out, the system is non-conforming and
  requires redesign or reallocation.

---

## B01.CH06.P06.S04 — Auditability: measurement rules, revisions, and version control (anti-drift)

Measurement itself is a capture surface. Rules must be versioned and contestable to prevent semantic drift.

### Required auditability rails (minimum; binding)
- **Method discipline:** publish method_id, definition references, aggregation rules, and uncertainty handling.
- **Revision policy:** define when series are restated, how corrections are published, and how comparability is preserved.
- **Changelog:** versioned release notes for metric definitions and thresholds (no silent changes).
- **Reproducibility:** publish sufficient aggregate data and calculation rules for third-party replication (within privacy constraints).
- **Integrity logs:** tamper-evident logs for measurement inputs, transformations, and publication events.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If measurement cannot be audited or replicated due to proprietary tooling, missing logs, or silent revisions, commons claims fail closed until
  the measurement system is repaired.
