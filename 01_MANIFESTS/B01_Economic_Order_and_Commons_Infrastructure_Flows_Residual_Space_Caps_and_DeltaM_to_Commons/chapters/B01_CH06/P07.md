# B01.CH06.P07 — Relation commons–state–market: interface contracts and boundary rules

This paragraph specifies how commons interfaces with state institutions and market actors without collapsing into privatized toll markets or
bureaucratic gatekeeping. Commons is not “outside the market”; it coexists with private provision, procurement, and specialized operators.
Therefore the interface must be governed by **boundary rules** (what is prohibited), **contract standards** (how procurement and delivery are
structured), and **anti-capture rails** (interoperability, data governance, legal inalienability).

The goal is to permit efficient service delivery while preventing three drift paths: (i) toll layering around baseline access, (ii) exclusive
concessions that become accumulation carriers, and (iii) vendor lock-in that eliminates contestability.

---

## B01.CH06.P07.S01 — Boundary rules: what markets may not do around commons (tolls, exclusive access, lock-in)

Boundary rules define prohibited behaviors at the commons perimeter.

### Prohibited boundary behaviors (minimum; binding)
- **Toll layering on baseline access:** mandatory intermediaries, fee-stacks, or “service plans” required to reach baseline provision.
- **Exclusive access gating:** exclusivity contracts that deny users the ability to use the commons baseline or to switch providers.
- **Lock-in mechanisms:** contractual or technical switching barriers (penalties, proprietary formats, identity lockouts) that prevent exit.
- **Self-preferencing and gate power:** providers leveraging a role in commons delivery to block competitors or steer users into extractive
  add-ons.
- **Bundling that converts baseline into premium:** bundling baseline access with unrelated paid features such that baseline becomes practically
  unreachable without payment.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If boundary rules are not enforceable in practice (no detection, no sanctions, no contract remedies), the commons perimeter will be captured
  and commons status must fail closed.

---

## B01.CH06.P07.S02 — Interface contracts: procurement, supply contracts, SLAs, and open standards

Interface contracts are how commons engages operators and vendors. Bad contracts create toll gates by design.

### Required contract standards (minimum; binding)
- **Procurement transparency:** tender criteria, award rationales, and change orders are logged and publishable in aggregate.
- **SLAs tied to commons outputs:** service levels must map to access/quality/continuity metrics (CH06.P06), not to vendor-internal KPIs.
- **Open standards requirement:** contracts must require open interfaces, documented formats, and portability.
- **No exclusivity by default:** exclusivity must be exceptional, time-bounded, justified by capacity constraints, and compatible with exit.
- **Audit rights:** commons governance and auditors must have audit access to performance data, incident logs, and cost structures relevant to
  toll detection.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Contracts that prevent audit, enforce proprietary lock-in, or allow uncontrolled fee add-ons are non-conforming and must be rewritten or
  terminated.

## B01.CH06.P07.S03 — Data and interoperability: preventing vendor lock-in and platform capture

Data and interoperability are the most common route to durable capture.

### Required interoperability rails (minimum; binding)
- **Portability:** users must be able to move accounts, records, and entitlements where applicable (privacy-safe, secure).
- **Open APIs / formats:** baseline interoperability requirements for systems that touch access, scheduling, billing, identity, and incident
  reporting.
- **Escrow / exit assistance:** contractual triggers for source escrow or operational handover in case of vendor failure or termination.
- **Join-safety and privacy minimalism:** data sharing must remain purpose-limited and avoid building a surveillance substrate.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If interoperability is lost (vendor-gated APIs, proprietary formats, closed identity rails), commons becomes dependent and non-contestable;
  this is a capture breach requiring remediation.

---

## B01.CH06.P07.S04 — Legal protection: inalienability, public review, and anti-privatization clauses

Commons requires legal hard points to resist privatization via concessions, asset sales, or governance hollowing.

### Required legal protections (minimum; binding)
- **Inalienability clauses:** baseline access rules, governance minima, and boundary rules cannot be sold or contracted away.
- **Public review:** material changes to access rules, governance structure, or asset disposition require public review and published rationale.
- **Sunsets for concessions:** time-limited concessions with mandatory re-tendering and compatibility review; no perpetual franchises.
- **Anti-conversion clauses:** prohibit conversion of commons assets into private accumulation carriers without a compatibility process and
  supermajority-style thresholds (where applicable).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If commons can be privatized through ordinary procurement or administrative decisions without hard review gates, the regime is not durable and
  fails commons classification.

---

## B01.CH06.P07.S05 — Escalation and repair: what happens under mismanagement, capture, or degradation of commons

Commons must have predefined repair paths. Otherwise failure becomes permanent or is “fixed” via privatization reflexes.

### Required escalation ladder (minimum; binding)
- **Early-warning triggers:** backlog growth, service-level breach rates, access denial patterns, vendor dependence growth, exception explosions.
- **Forensic audit mode:** escalation to deeper audits of procurement, cost structures, governance conflicts, and decision logs.
- **Corrective actions:** contract rewrite, operator replacement, governance reset, capacity rebuild, maintenance funding ring-fencing.
- **Continuity safeguards:** fallback operators and temporary bridging to preserve baseline access during repair.
- **Public restatement:** publish what failed, what changed, and how metrics are restated (no silent repair).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If capture or degradation is detected and no effective repair is executed, commons status is suspended and the system must not be scaled until
  repaired.
