# B01.CH06.P04 — Maintenance and financing: capex/opex, stewardship, quality norms

This paragraph specifies the **maintenance-first** discipline that makes commons durable. Commons fails when it is treated as a one-off build
(capex) without enforceable operations and maintenance (opex), or when financing is structured to reintroduce toll layers and rent-seeking.
Therefore commons financing is specified as an infrastructure regime: investment logic, operating cycles, stewardship accountability, quality
norms, and funding sources must be explicit and auditable.

---

## B01.CH06.P04.S01 — Capex: investment logic, prioritization, and depreciation within a commons regime

Commons capex exists to build capacity and eliminate scarcity rents, not to create assets for speculative appreciation.

### Capex logic (minimum; binding)
- **Capacity-first prioritization:** prioritize projects that remove bottlenecks in essentials and increase coverage (ΔG).
- **Backlog reduction:** prioritize renovation/repair where existing stock is degraded; scarcity-by-neglect is treated as an E enabler.
- **Lifecycle accounting:** capital projects must include lifecycle maintenance plans and replacement schedules.
- **Depreciation discipline:** depreciation is treated as real maintenance obligation; ignoring it is a hidden underfunding pathway.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A capex program without lifecycle maintenance and depreciation discipline is non-conforming because it produces future scarcity and drift.

---

## B01.CH06.P04.S02 — Opex: structural operations, maintenance cycles, and minimum service levels

Opex is not “overhead”; it is the continuity layer that prevents degradation and lock-in.

### Required opex regime (minimum; binding)
- **Maintenance cycles:** defined inspection and maintenance cycles with backlog thresholds and escalation rules.
- **Minimum service levels:** explicit baseline service package and continuity requirements (uptime/availability/throughput as appropriate).
- **Incident discipline:** incident logging, response SLAs, and corrective action procedures.
- **Staffing as infrastructure:** staffing and training requirements are included as maintenance prerequisites.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If service levels collapse or backlogs grow structurally, commons status is breached; scaling is blocked until maintenance capacity is restored.

---

## B01.CH06.P04.S03 — Stewardship: roles (steward/manager, supervisor, user), responsibilities, liability

Stewardship allocates responsibility so that failure is traceable and repairable.

### Required role model (minimum; binding)
- **Steward/manager:** responsible for day-to-day operations, maintenance execution, procurement compliance, and reporting.
- **Supervisor/oversight:** independent oversight on budgets, quality norms, procurement, and exception discipline (“audit of the operator”).
- **Users:** defined participation and feedback channels; users are not “owners” but have contestability rights and procedural standing.

### Liability and accountability (binding)
- Duties and liabilities must be explicit (who is accountable for backlog growth, service failures, procurement breaches).
- Removal/rotation triggers must exist under repeated breaches.

---

## B01.CH06.P04.S04 — Quality norms: output norms, safety, accessibility, and continuity

Commons must be measurable as real-world service quality, not as organizational existence.

### Required quality norms (minimum; binding)
- **Output norms:** define what “baseline provision” means in operational terms (service package and performance bands).
- **Safety norms:** safety standards and incident reporting requirements.
- **Accessibility norms:** disability access, language access where relevant, usability, and non-discrimination in practice.
- **Continuity norms:** uptime/availability targets and redundancy expectations for critical essentials.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- “Access exists” claims require quality and continuity evidence. Chronic quality failure is a commons breach, not an implementation detail.

---

## B01.CH06.P04.S05 — Financing mix: contributions, tariffs, public funds, ΔM→commons (link to CH07)

Financing must preserve baseline access, prevent toll layering, and ensure maintenance-first continuity.

### Permissible financing sources (minimum; binding)
- **Contributions:** general contributions or earmarked contributions where incidence is transparent and anti-regressive.
- **Tariffs/charges:** permitted only if they do not undermine baseline access and do not create fee-stack ecosystems.
- **Public funds:** budget funding with ring-fenced maintenance commitments and transparent overhead.
- **ΔM→commons:** admissible where routed via compatible channels (CH07), with traceability and leakage tests.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Financing must be **ring-fenced for maintenance**; maintenance budgets cannot be drained to cover unrelated shortfalls.
- Financing structures must not require mandatory intermediaries that introduce toll layers.

---

## B01.CH06.P04.S06 — Perverse incentives: preventing underinvestment, overuse, and rent-seeking within commons

Commons can fail internally through misincentives even without privatization.

### Required anti-misincentive measures (minimum; binding)
- **Underinvestment prevention:** backlog thresholds and escalation triggers; publish backlog distributions and breach rates.
- **Overuse control:** scarcity protocols and fair-use bands (CH06.P02), designed to be non-stigmatizing and contestable.
- **Rent-seeking prevention:** prohibit insider contracting, self-dealing, and “maintenance as a vendor rent” via opaque tooling.
- **Exception control:** exception registers and sunsets; repeated exceptions are treated as governance failure and trigger review.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If commons becomes an internal rent machine (vendor capture, self-dealing, exception culture), it is non-conforming until governance and
  procurement are reset.
