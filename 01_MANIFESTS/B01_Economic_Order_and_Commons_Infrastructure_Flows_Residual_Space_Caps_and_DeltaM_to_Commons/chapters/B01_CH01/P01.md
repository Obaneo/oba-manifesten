# B01.CH01.P01 — Purpose and scope of Book 1

## B01.CH01.P01.S01 — Purpose: apply the Kernel Spec to the economic layer (no re-definition)

Book 1 specifies how the **Kernel Spec** is applied to the **economic layer**: how the economy is described, classified, constrained, and audited under OBA. It is an **application and operationalization layer**, not a new canon.

Accordingly, this book:

- **inherits** definitions, axioms, and invariants from the Kernel Spec and treats them as binding;
- **introduces no competing definitions** for core terms (including B,T,N,L,R; P/E/C; W-MAX/I-MAX; ΔM; commons);
- **translates** kernel objects into economic procedures: flow decomposition, attribution, consolidation, indicators, audit routes, and stop rules;
- **fails closed**: if a claim cannot be made measurable and auditable, it is not eligible for an “OBA-compatible” label in the economic layer.

Any apparent conflict between this book and the Kernel Spec is resolved by **kernel precedence**: the Kernel Spec controls meaning; this book controls economic application.

## B01.CH01.P01.S02 — Scope: flows (P/E/C), residual space (R/B ≥ α), caps (W-MAX/I-MAX), commons, and ΔM allocation channels

The scope of Book 1 is limited to the economic mechanisms required to make OBA testable and enforceable:

1. **Flows (P/E/C):** modeling the economy as a flow network and decomposing flows into productive value (P), extractive components (E), and commons contribution (C), including mixed flows and edge cases.
2. **Residual space (R/B ≥ α):** defining and measuring the household chain **B → T → N → L → R**, treating **R/B** and its distribution (tails, percentiles, share below α) as the primary order indicator.
3. **Caps (W-MAX / I-MAX):** defining bounded accumulation for wealth and income, including functional equivalents, transition regimes, and anti-avoidance requirements.
4. **Commons as base layer:** specifying commons as an economic infrastructure regime (access, governance, maintenance, financing), and defining ΔG(t) and quality as auditable outputs.
5. **ΔM allocation channels:** defining monetary space (ΔM), permitted and forbidden channels, allocation criteria, governance, anti-capture rails, and public accountability outputs.

Out of scope are party-political programs, rhetorical persuasion, and any attempt to “solve” order conflicts without measurable mechanisms. Where the book references implementation artifacts (schemas, templates, validators, audit packs), it does so by pointing to **Book X (Codex)** rather than duplicating them here.
