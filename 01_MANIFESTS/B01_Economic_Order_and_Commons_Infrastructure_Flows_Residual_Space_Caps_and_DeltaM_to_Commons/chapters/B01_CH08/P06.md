# B01.CH08.P06 — Legal anchoring: constitutive rules (caps, commons, audit) and exception law

This paragraph specifies the legal hard points that prevent OBA from degrading into discretionary governance, carve-outs, and emergency drift.
Because caps, commons, and audit powers are coercive and politically contested, they must be anchored as **constitutive rules**: difficult to
hollow out, procedurally contestable, and enforceable through a clear sanction and repair hierarchy.

The purpose of legal anchoring is not rigidity for its own sake. It is to ensure that (i) core invariants cannot be traded away in exchange for
short-term relief, (ii) exceptions remain temporary and auditable, and (iii) compatibility claims are grounded in legal enforceability rather
than voluntary adherence.

---

## B01.CH08.P06.S01 — Constitutive rules: what must be in “higher law” (caps, commons inalienability, audit duty)

### Constitutive minimum (binding)
The following must be legally non-optional:

- **Caps integrity:** the legal existence of W-MAX and I-MAX (or approved functional equivalents) and the duty to enforce them with consolidation
  and anti-avoidance powers.
- **Commons inalienability:** baseline access rules and critical commons assets/regimes cannot be sold, franchised, or contracted away without a
  high-threshold review process.
- **Audit duty and powers:** independent audit bodies must exist, with legal powers to inspect, compel information, and publish constrained
  summaries; “audit optionality” is incompatible with OBA.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If these elements can be altered by ordinary administrative decisions or routine procurement contracts, OBA is structurally unstable and fails
  the durability requirement.

---

## B01.CH08.P06.S02 — Enforceability: sanction hierarchy, repair paths, and legal protection

OBA requires enforceability that is predictable (to reduce arbitrariness) and repair-oriented (to reduce political backlash and sabotage).

### Required enforceability architecture (minimum; binding)
- **Sanction hierarchy:** graded sanctions from correction orders and contract rewrites up to financial penalties, license loss, divestment
  remedies, and criminal enforcement where appropriate.
- **Repair paths:** predefined remediation paths that restore compatibility (restatements, template rewrites, procurement resets, governance
  reconfiguration).
- **Legal protection and due process:** contestability via objection/appeal, access to calculations and evidence, and independent adjudication.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Enforcement that is selective, opaque, or endlessly delayed is non-enforcement. Where enforceability is not credible, compatibility claims are
  suspended.

---

## B01.CH08.P06.S03 — Exception law: when emergency is allowed, how temporary, and how return is enforced

Exception law is treated as a controlled mechanism, not as a political shortcut.

### Emergency admissibility (minimum; binding)
- **Trigger-based:** exceptions activate only on measurable triggers (defined thresholds, method_id, published evidence).
- **Scope-bounded:** exceptions are limited in domain, geography, cohort, and instrument type.
- **Audit-bounded:** full logging and publishable dashboards (trigger → action → outcome → incident log) are mandatory.

### Temporariness and return (binding)
- **Sunsets:** all emergency measures have an expiry date by default.
- **Unwind plans:** activation includes a written unwind plan and a restoration path to baseline rules.
- **Renewal discipline:** renewal requires re-trigger evidence; “continuous emergency” is treated as drift.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- An emergency regime without a sunset + unwind + dashboard is non-conforming and triggers escalation to governance reset.

## B01.CH08.P06.S04 — Anti-hollowing: sunset clauses, supermajority thresholds, and public re-affirmation

Hollowing usually happens through small exceptions and procedural erosion. Anti-hollowing rules are the countermeasure.

### Required anti-hollowing protections (minimum; binding)
- **Sunset clauses:** for exceptions, concessions, and special regimes; default expiration prevents permanence by inertia.
- **Supermajority thresholds (where applicable):** high-threshold approval for weakening caps, privatizing commons, reducing audit powers, or
  expanding exception scope.
- **Public re-affirmation:** periodic re-affirmation requirements for the constitutive pillars, with published evidence of continuing necessity
  and effects.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If core instruments can be weakened by simple majority under crisis rhetoric without measured evidence, the regime is capture-prone and not
  durable.

---

## B01.CH08.P06.S05 — Compatibility linkage: legal review against the OBA core (reference to Kernel Spec and CH12)

Legal change must be checked against the OBA core rather than argued case-by-case in political language.

### Required compatibility procedure (minimum; binding)
- **Mapping:** any proposed legal change is mapped to pillars (R/B ≥ α, caps, E reduction, commons build-up, ΔM→commons discipline).
- **Indicator impact:** specify which indicators will improve/worsen and how that is measured (method_id, definitions, uncertainty).
- **Audit plan:** specify what evidence will be collected and what would falsify the claim.
- **Decision and publication:** publish the decision rationale and the compatibility assessment (privacy-safe).
- **Versioning:** changes are versioned with changelog and restatement rules; no silent semantic drift.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If legal reforms cannot be evaluated against the compatibility check (CH12) with measurable evidence, they cannot be labeled OBA-compatible.
