# B01.CH08 — State, fiscality, and debt under OBA: role allocation and boundary conditions

This chapter specifies the **state-facing boundary layer** of OBA in the economic domain: what the state must do as an order institution, how
fiscality is designed to press down extraction (E) while stabilizing R/B, when debt is productive versus parasitic, how expenditure is framed
as commons capacity building, and which constitutive legal anchors prevent drift through emergency regimes and exceptions.

The state is treated neither as “the planner of everything” nor as a neutral bystander. It is an **order guarantor** with explicit
responsibilities (enforcement, audit, boundary rules) and explicit failure modes (state capture, bureaucratic toll gates, exception drift).

---

## What this chapter is

- A **role delimitation** for the state: what is constitutive (order enforcement, auditability, boundary rules) versus what belongs to commons
  governance or contestable provision.
- A **fiscal architecture**: shifting incidence away from labour/consumption toward extraction/accumulation, while preventing new toll layers.
- A **debt classification**: productive debt criteria versus parasitic debt criteria, with restructuring rules that do not hollow out caps.
- An **expenditure framework**: commons budgeting as infrastructure building (ΔG + quality) versus redistribution as a partial tool.
- A **social security framing**: residual space (R/B) stabilization and tail protection, with automatic stabilizers and execution constraints.
- A **legal anchoring** module: constitutive rules, enforceability, emergency law discipline, and anti-hollowing constraints.

---

## What this chapter is not

- Not a policy wishlist or party program; it specifies compatibility conditions and failure modes.
- Not an argument for maximal state ownership; commons is defined by regimes (CH06), not by ownership labels.
- Not permissive of discretionary exception culture; emergency regimes are permitted only under strict triggers, sunsets, and unwind plans.

---

## Binding constraints

- **Neutrality across subject positions:** enforce order on flows/positions, not identities.
- **Anti-toll discipline:** taxes and programs must not generate fee-stacks, mandatory intermediaries, or administrative toll ecosystems.
- **Cap compatibility:** fiscal and debt tools must not create bypass routes around W-MAX/I-MAX.
- **Maintenance-first:** expenditure legitimacy is tied to ΔG + quality and continuity, not to budget size.
- **Fail-closed:** under missing evidence, opaque routing, or capture signals, compatibility claims are suspended until corrected.

---

## Chapter structure

- **P01 — State role delimitation:** order guarantor vs economic actor; subsidiarity; neutrality; democratic anchoring; state capture risk.
- **P02 — Fiscality:** incidence-first shift toward E/accumulation bases; anti-arbitrage; transparency of revenues and destination.
- **P03 — Debt regime:** productive vs parasitic debt; restructuring/forgiveness discipline; interest/claim limits; crisis mechanics without
  debt-extraction relapse.
- **P04 — Expenditure framework:** commons budgeting vs redistribution; priorities; separation from ΔM allocation; evaluation and stop/go rules.
- **P05 — Social security:** R/B stabilization; automatic stabilizers; commons-based minima; labour/time effects; execution risks and repairs.
- **P06 — Legal anchoring:** constitutive rules in higher law; enforceability; emergency law with sunsets; anti-hollowing constraints; linkage to
  compatibility review.

## Interfaces

- **CH03 (R/B):** fiscality, social security, and expenditure must show measurable effects on tails (share_below_alpha, p10/p25).
- **CH04 (caps):** taxation, debt relief, and emergency programs must not create cap bypass or privileged claim preservation.
- **CH05 (E phase-down):** fiscal tools should target E sources (fee-stacks, monopoly rents) and avoid relabeling.
- **CH06 (commons):** expenditure is evaluated primarily through commons outputs (ΔG + quality) and maintenance discipline.
- **CH07 (ΔM):** fiscal spending and ΔM allocation must remain conceptually and procedurally distinct (avoid dual mandates and blurred authority).
- **CH10 (audit):** all state instruments require auditability, logging, revision discipline, and contestability.
