# B01.CH08.P03 — Debt regime: when debt is productive and when it is structurally parasitic

This paragraph specifies debt as an order object that can be either (i) a productive allocation instrument supporting P and commons capacity or
(ii) a parasitic claims stack that amplifies extraction (E), raises mandatory burdens (L), and destabilizes R/B tails. OBA therefore requires a
classification regime for debt instruments, constraints on interest and fee structures, and restructuring rules that preserve caps integrity
and prevent bailout-style relapse into asset/claim support.

The core distinction is functional: **what does the debt finance**, **what enforcement and cashflow rights does it create**, and **does it
produce lock-in and pass-through burdens**.

---

## B01.CH08.P03.S01 — Debt as an instrument: credit for production/commons versus debt as a claims stack

Debt is not evaluated by legal form (“bond”, “loan”, “mortgage”) but by its economic function.

### Functional framing (binding)
- **Debt as instrument (productive):** debt finances build-up or maintenance of productive capacity (P) or commons capacity (ΔG), with bounded
  returns and enforceable transparency.
- **Debt as claims stack (parasitic):** debt primarily creates a durable claim on future income streams, strengthened by asymmetry, lock-in,
  or collateral dynamics that inflate assets and feed accumulation.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If debt’s primary effect is to expand a toll interface (fee-stacks, lock-in, asset inflation), it is treated as parasitic regardless of the
  stated purpose.

---

## B01.CH08.P03.S02 — Productive debt: criteria (capex/commons build-up, revenue/maintenance coverage, bounded toll)

### Productive debt criteria (minimum; binding)
Debt is classified as **productive** only if all criteria hold:

- **Use-of-funds discipline:** funds are tagged to P capex or commons capex/maintenance (ΔG), not to asset price support.
- **Coverage logic:** repayment is covered by real cashflows or budgeted maintenance coverage, not by perpetual refinancing.
- **Bounded return:** interest and fees are bounded to functional costs (funding cost + risk + operations) and do not embed an E spread.
- **Transparency and auditability:** contracts, costs, and performance are auditable; no black-box securitization.
- **No lock-in escalation:** terms do not rely on penalty cascades, exploitative defaults, or coercive collateral seizure patterns.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If productive debt cannot be audited or is dependent on collateral appreciation, it fails closed into parasitic classification until proven otherwise.

---

## B01.CH08.P03.S03 — Parasitic debt: criteria (rollover dependence, fee-stacks, collateral-driven asset inflation, regressive burden)

### Parasitic debt criteria (minimum; binding)
Debt is classified as **parasitic** if one or more of the following hold structurally:

- **Rollover dependence:** the system requires repeated refinancing to remain solvent (debt as a perpetual rent claim).
- **Fee-stack design:** junk fees, penalty interest cascades, servicing stacks, or opaque spreads dominate the cost structure.
- **Collateral-driven inflation:** debt expansion fuels asset price inflation (especially in essentials), increasing L via rents or entry costs.
- **Regressive incidence:** burdens fall disproportionately on low R/B cohorts through higher effective rates, penalties, and lock-in.
- **Asymmetric enforcement:** enforcement rights function as clamp power (garnishment, eviction leverage, identity/payment lockouts).
- **Opacity and repackaging:** securitization/structuring that hides economic exposure and prevents attribution of gains and harms.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Under opacity, the classification is conservative: presumed parasitic until disclosure and auditability remove the presumption.

## B01.CH08.P03.S04 — Restructuring and forgiveness: when legitimate, and how to prevent hollowing out caps

Restructuring is legitimate when it removes parasitic extraction and restores stability; it is illegitimate when it socializes losses to preserve
private accumulation or bypass caps.

### Legitimate restructuring triggers (minimum; binding)
- **Tail collapse risk:** R/B tails breach α persistently (arrears clustering, cascading defaults) due to burdens that are structurally induced.
- **Parasitic classification:** the instrument is parasitic by design (fee-stacks, rollover dependence, exploitative enforcement).
- **Shock events:** externally evidenced shocks (energy/inflation/supply disruption) cause temporary insolvency that would otherwise trigger mass
  cascades.

### Permissible restructuring tools (bounded; binding)
- rate reduction, fee waivers, principal write-down, maturity extension, conversion into commons-compatible instruments, standardized repayment
  plans with caps-compatible conditions.

### Caps integrity constraints (binding)
- **No claim preservation carve-outs:** restructuring must not preserve oversized claims via bailout routes; excess positions remain subject to caps.
- **No discretion deals:** restructuring must use templates and publishable criteria; bespoke negotiations are capture surfaces.
- **Loss allocation discipline:** where lenders profited from opacity or asymmetry, burden of proof and loss share shift to them.

---

## B01.CH08.P03.S05 — Interest and claim limits: limits that support E reduction (link to CH05 and CH07)

Interest and fees can function as a primary E channel. Limits are therefore order tools, not “market interventions”.

### Required limit types (minimum; binding)
- **All-in cost disclosure:** standard APR/all-in cost metrics including fees, penalties, and mandatory add-ons.
- **Fee caps:** caps on penalty fees, servicing fees, and add-on stacks; prohibit compounding penalty cascades.
- **Margin discipline in essentials:** stricter limits where credit touches essentials (housing, energy arrears finance, basic care).
- **Anti-repackaging constraints:** limits apply through securitization and intermediaries; no fee extraction via structure layering.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the system allows fee-stacks to reappear via intermediaries (collections, servicing, fintech wrappers), limits are non-conforming until
  enforced at the stack level (CH05 “shift” logic).

---

## B01.CH08.P03.S06 — Crisis mechanics: what happens under shocks (inflation, energy) without reintroducing debt extraction

Crisis regimes are where debt extraction often expands under “emergency necessity”. OBA requires crisis protocols that preserve continuity
without relapse into asset/claim support.

### Crisis protocol requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Trigger discipline:** crisis measures activate only under measurable triggers with published thresholds and method_id.
- **Temporariness:** sunsets and unwind plans are mandatory; renewals require re-trigger evidence.
- **Burdens-first response:** prioritize L stabilization (essentials price/baseline access) and arrears prevention over creditor protection.
- **Freeze/bridge tools:** temporary payment pauses, arrears freezes, standardized restructuring pathways, and fallback commons access.
- **No asset-support relapse:** measures must not be routed primarily to preserve asset prices or private balance sheets at the top.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Any crisis response that preserves claim stacks by inflating assets, expanding bailout discretion, or suspending auditability is treated as a
  regression breach and triggers fail-closed on compatibility claims.
