# B01.CH08.P05 — Social security and GROSS≈NET: residual space as a stability target

This paragraph specifies social security as an **order stabilizer** evaluated through residual space (R/B) rather than through program labels or
aggregate spending. In OBA, the primary function of social security is to prevent **sub-α clustering** and to dampen tail cascades (arrears,
defaults, loss of access to essentials) without creating new extraction channels or administrative toll gates.

Social security must therefore be rule-bound, measurable, and compatible with commons substitution: wherever feasible, minimum security shifts
from cash-only compensation toward baseline commons access that directly reduces L.

---

## B01.CH08.P05.S01 — Social security as an R/B stabilizer: preventing sub-α clusters

### Core function (binding)
- Social security must stabilize **R/B tails** by preventing persistent populations from falling below α due to shocks, transitions, or
  structural burdens.

### Required outcome surface (minimum; binding)
- **share_below_alpha** (distributional, privacy-safe).
- **Tail volatility** and tail-event frequency for R/B (not only medians).
- **Arrears and cascade indicators** in essentials domains (housing, energy, critical bills), as aggregate early-warning proxies.
- **Time-to-recovery** proxies after shock events (aggregated).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If program coverage expands but sub-α clustering persists or grows, the system is compensating for extraction or failing execution; redesign is
  mandatory.

---

## B01.CH08.P05.S02 — Automatic stabilizers: triggers, indexation, and anti-Goodhart protection

OBA prefers automatic stabilizers over discretionary relief because discretion is a capture surface.

### Required stabilizer features (minimum; binding)
- **Trigger rules:** explicit, measurable triggers for expansions (unemployment spikes, essentials price breaks, shock indicators).
- **Indexation rules:** parameters (floors, eligibility thresholds, benefit levels) co-move with inflation/energy/housing burdens to preserve
  real stabilization.
- **Sunsets and unwind discipline:** temporary expansions unwind according to rule-bound conditions; renewals require re-trigger evidence.
- **Anti-Goodhart rails:** prevent manipulation via relabeling burdens, shifting costs outside definitions, or temporary cosmetic subsidies.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Stabilizers without published triggers, indexation logic, and unwind rules are treated as discretionary drift and are non-conforming.

---

## B01.CH08.P05.S03 — Minimum provision via commons: when social security shifts to baseline commons (in-kind)

Where burdens are driven by essentials toll markets, security is more robust when delivered as baseline access rather than compensatory cash.

### Shift criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Essentials dominance:** L is driven by structural scarcity or monopoly interfaces (housing, energy baseline, basic care, core digital access).
- **Toll/lock-in evidence:** fee-stacks, lock-in proxies, margin anomalies, or denial patterns indicate structural E.
- **Continuity requirement:** baseline access must be reliable and measurable (ΔG + quality), not nominal.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- In-kind commons provision must not become a stigmatizing subsystem; baseline access must remain universalizable and contestable.

---

## B01.CH08.P05.S04 — Work/time effects: relation to labour markets, bargaining power, and the phase-down of parasitic labour

Social security affects labour power and transition feasibility; this is not secondary—it's a stability mechanism.

### Required design constraints (minimum; binding)
- **Exit power:** security increases bargaining power by making exit from abusive/locked-in work relations feasible.
- **Transition support:** stabilizers must support E→P/C transitions (reskilling, mobility, temporary income smoothing) without coercive
  conditionality.
- **No disciplining via scarcity:** security must not be designed primarily as labour discipline (punitive sanctions, humiliation gates), because
  that recreates clamp power and administrative L.
- **Compatibility with parasitic labour phase-down:** as toll structures collapse, some jobs disappear; security must buffer identity and income
  shocks to prevent sabotage dynamics.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If security design increases precarity during transition (higher tail volatility, higher arrears), it is non-conforming regardless of headline
  employment metrics.

---

## B01.CH08.P05.S05 — Execution risks: bureaucratic toll gates, exclusion, and repair mechanisms

The dominant failure mode of social security is not “abuse”; it is **execution friction** that functions as a toll gate.

### Primary execution risks (minimum; binding)
- **Administrative L:** long cycle times, excessive proof burden, repeated reassessments, complex eligibility logic.
- **Digital exclusion:** access dependent on brittle identity systems or vendor-controlled platforms.
- **Selective execution:** regional/municipal divergence without transparent explanation and correction paths.
- **Compliance theatre:** dashboards without remedies; errors not restated; appeals backlogs.

### Required repair architecture (minimum; binding)
- **Time norms and fast provisional relief:** interim support while disputes resolve.
- **Objection/appeal routes:** time-bounded, reproducible decisions with access to calculations and evidence.
- **Restatement discipline:** public correction of systemic errors (aggregate), with versioned changes to rules/templates.
- **Fail-closed rule:** if execution cannot be audited or becomes a toll gate, compatibility claims are suspended until repaired.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the cost of accessing a right (time, stress, humiliation, digital hurdles) is structurally high, the right is effectively absent and the
  security layer fails its function.
