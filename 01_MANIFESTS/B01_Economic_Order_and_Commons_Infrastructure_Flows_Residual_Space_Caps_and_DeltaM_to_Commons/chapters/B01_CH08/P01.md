# B01.CH08.P01 — The state as an order institution: role delimitation (not “steer everything”, but guarantee order)

This paragraph defines the state’s role in OBA as an **order institution**: a guarantor of enforceable rules over flows, positions, and
interfaces. The state is not specified as the controller of all production, nor as a passive referee. It is specified as the holder of
constitutive functions without which caps, commons, and anti-extraction rails cannot exist in practice.

The core problem solved here is **role confusion**: when the state mixes enforcement with discretionary economic steering, it becomes either a
capture target or a toll gate itself. OBA therefore requires explicit delimitation, subsidiarity rules, neutrality across subject positions,
democratic anchoring, and anti-capture constraints.

---

## B01.CH08.P01.S01 — Order guarantor versus economic actor: constitutive tasks (enforcement, audit, boundary rules)

### Constitutive tasks (minimum; binding)
The state must provide, at minimum:

- **Enforcement capacity:** the ability to enforce caps, boundary rules, and contract standards with predictable sanction paths.
- **Audit capacity:** independent audit bodies with powers to inspect flows, consolidation structures, procurement, and measurement systems.
- **Boundary rule enforcement:** prevention of toll layering, lock-in, exclusive gating, and accumulation bypass structures at critical
  interfaces (essentials, infrastructure, platforms, finance).
- **Legal integrity functions:** courts or adjudication pathways that ensure contestability and due process for state actions.

### Delimitation rule (binding)
- When the state acts as an **economic actor** (operator, buyer, investor), it must remain bound by the same transparency, auditability, and
  anti-toll constraints as any other actor. “Public” does not exempt.

---

## B01.CH08.P01.S02 — Subsidiarity: what belongs to commons governance and what remains a state monopoly

OBA uses subsidiarity to prevent centralization drift and to keep commons contestable and adaptable.

### Subsidiarity allocation (minimum; binding)
- **Belongs to commons governance (default):**
  - access and use rules within commons regimes (within national minima),
  - maintenance execution and local prioritization,
  - local procurement within open-standards rails,
  - dispute mediation at the first tiers.

- **Remains state monopoly (non-delegable core):**
  - legal enforcement powers (sanctions, coercive inspection, fraud prosecution),
  - meta-audit (“audit of auditors”) and capture forensics,
  - setting and guarding the constitutive minima (caps integrity, boundary rules, transparency duties),
  - cross-border enforcement and treaty-level anti-arbitrage coordination.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Delegation is non-conforming if it removes enforceability or creates un-auditable discretionary power at local level.

## B01.CH08.P01.S03 — Neutrality across subject positions: enforce order on flows/positions, not on identities

OBA order enforcement targets **flows and positions** (P/E/C, claims, control/block power, exposure), not identity categories.

### Neutrality requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Flow-based targeting:** rules apply to measurable flow structures (fee-stacks, monopoly rents, lock-in clauses, exposure concentration).
- **Position-based targeting:** rules apply to roles and power positions (gatekeepers, controllers, ultimate beneficial control), not to
  demographic identity.
- **Evidence-based enforcement:** sanctions and remedies must cite rule IDs, measurable breaches, and logged facts.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Identity-based enforcement (as a substitute for flow analysis) is treated as order failure: it is both gameable and destabilizing, and it
  undermines contestability.

## B01.CH08.P01.S04 — Public legitimation: why order instruments (caps, audits) must be democratically anchored

Order instruments are coercive by nature. Without legitimation, enforcement becomes politically reversible or selectively applied.

### Legitimation requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Mandate clarity:** caps, audit powers, and boundary rules must be established by publicly legible law with defined objectives and limits.
- **Contestability:** procedural routes for objection/appeal must exist for affected parties, with time bounds and access to calculations.
- **Transparency:** publication duties for decision rationales, exception registers, and audit summaries (privacy-safe).
- **Separation of functions:** decision, execution, and oversight must be separated to reduce capture and selective enforcement.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If instruments operate through opaque discretion (“trust us”), they become capture surfaces and fail legitimacy under OBA.

## B01.CH08.P01.S05 — “State capture” risk: where the state can become a toll gate, and how to prevent it

The state can itself become an extraction layer through bureaucracy, licensing monopolies, discretionary permits, or vendor-locked systems.

### State capture / toll-gate risks (minimum; binding)
- **Administrative tolls:** excessive proof burdens, long cycle times, complex procedures that function as gating for rights.
- **Vendor lock-in:** proprietary systems that control measurement, eligibility, identity, or allocation—making policy un-auditable.
- **Exception culture:** carve-outs, discretionary deals, and emergency regimes without sunsets.
- **Selective enforcement:** enforcement targeted by power, not by rule breach, producing legitimacy collapse.

### Prevention measures (minimum; binding)
- **Audit of the state:** meta-audits, rotation of key oversight roles, and publishable integrity indicators.
- **Procedure minimalism:** standard templates, time norms, and “no silent edits” discipline for administrative rules.
- **Open standards procurement:** interoperability requirements, exit clauses, and escrow/hand-over triggers.
- **Exception discipline:** triggers, scope caps, sunsets, unwind plans, and public restatements.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If state systems become un-auditable or exception-driven, OBA compatibility claims fail closed until governance is reset and the toll dynamics
  are removed.
