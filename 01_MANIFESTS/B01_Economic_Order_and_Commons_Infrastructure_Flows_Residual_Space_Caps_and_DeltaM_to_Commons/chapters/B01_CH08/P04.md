# B01.CH08.P04 — Expenditure framework: commons budgeting versus classical redistribution

This paragraph specifies the expenditure logic of OBA. Public spending is not validated by the size of budgets or the number of programs; it is
validated by **outputs**: (i) commons capacity and quality (ΔG + service continuity), (ii) reduced mandatory burdens (L) and stabilized R/B
tails, and (iii) demonstrable reduction of extraction (E) rather than compensating for it.

OBA distinguishes **commons budgeting** (capex/opex as infrastructure building and maintenance) from **classical redistribution** (cash transfers
or tax-benefit adjustments). Redistribution can be necessary, but it is often insufficient because it can leave toll structures intact and can
become a permanent compensatory layer over unchanged E.

---

## B01.CH08.P04.S01 — Commons budgeting: capex/opex as infrastructure building (ΔG + quality as outputs)

Commons budgeting is the primary expenditure form in OBA because it replaces toll markets rather than subsidizing participation in them.

### Commons budgeting requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Capex/opex pairing:** every capex program includes an enforceable opex/maintenance plan (no build-without-maintain).
- **ΔG targeting:** spending must map to measurable commons growth (capacity, reach, coverage) and quality (availability, reliability,
  accessibility).
- **Maintenance-first ring-fencing:** maintenance budgets are protected against reallocation; backlog thresholds trigger escalation.
- **Service-level contracts:** outputs are specified as service packages and performance bands, not as “projects delivered”.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A commons budget that cannot demonstrate ΔG + quality outcomes is treated as non-evidence and is subject to redesign or scope reduction.

---

## B01.CH08.P04.S02 — Classical redistribution: when necessary, when insufficient (and when it leaves E untouched)

Redistribution can stabilize tails and correct incidence, but it can also become an endless patch over extraction.

### When redistribution is necessary (admissible; bounded)
- **Tail stabilization:** preventing sub-α clusters under shocks or transition stress.
- **Bridging gaps:** temporary support while commons substitution and E phase-down are built.
- **Automatic stabilizers:** rule-bound stabilizers that prevent collapse without discretionary gatekeeping.

### When redistribution is insufficient (binding)
- When **E structures remain intact** (fee-stacks, monopoly rents) and transfers simply pay the toll.
- When transfers increase **administrative L** through complex eligibility and proof burdens.
- When redistribution is used as a substitute for **commons build-up** and **E elimination**.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If redistribution rises while E indicators remain stable or grow, the system is compensating for extraction rather than dismantling it.

---

## B01.CH08.P04.S03 — Spending priorities: essentials, bottlenecks, substitution of toll markets (housing/energy/basic healthcare)

Spending prioritization is incidence-first and bottleneck-driven.

### Priority order (minimum; binding)
1. **Essentials baseline and continuity:** domains where L dominates and tail risk originates (housing, energy baseline, basic care).
2. **Bottleneck removal:** capacity constraints that sustain scarcity rents (permits, build capacity, grid constraints, staffing pipelines).
3. **Commons substitution projects:** projects that create credible exit from toll markets (public options, commons core layers).
4. **Enforcement and audit capacity:** execution capacity is infrastructure; without it, everything drifts.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Spending that increases total outlays but fails to reduce essentials burdens (L) and stabilize R/B tails is non-conforming until reprioritized.

## B01.CH08.P04.S04 — Interaction with ΔM: distinguish fiscal spending from ΔM allocation (avoid dual mandates)

OBA treats fiscal spending and ΔM allocation as distinct powers with different risk profiles.

### Separation requirements (binding)
- **Distinct mandates:** fiscal authorities cannot substitute for ΔM governance and vice versa; responsibilities must be explicit.
- **No blurred routing:** programs funded via ΔM must be traceable as ΔM routes (CH07) and must pass leakage tests.
- **Unified outcome evaluation:** although mandates differ, outcomes are evaluated on the same surfaces (ΔG, R/B, E).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Blurred mandates create discretionary power and capture surfaces; where separation is absent, compatibility claims fail closed until remediated.

---

## B01.CH08.P04.S05 — Evaluation framework: output measurement (R/B, E, commons) and stop/go criteria

Expenditure must be governed by measurable outputs and explicit stop/go rules.

### Required evaluation surface (minimum; binding)
- **R/B outcomes:** distributional reporting (median, p10/p25, share_below_alpha; tail volatility and arrears proxies where relevant).
- **E outcomes:** sector indicators for fee-stack depth, margin anomalies, lock-in metrics; show whether spending reduced toll structures.
- **Commons outcomes:** ΔG(t) + quality/continuity indicators; backlog distributions and breach rates.
- **Method discipline:** method_id, definition references, uncertainty handling, and revision policy.

### Stop/go criteria (binding)
- **Stop rule A:** spending scales up while R/B tails worsen or essentials burdens rise without an external shock → halt and redesign.
- **Stop rule B:** spending cannot be audited (black-box vendors, missing logs, opaque attribution) → freeze until repaired.
- **Stop rule C:** repeated exceptions or procurement anomalies → escalate to forensic audit and governance reset.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- “We spent the money” is not evidence. Only outcome-linked, auditable outputs constitute evidence under OBA.
