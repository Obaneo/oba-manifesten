# B01.CH09.P02 — Housing: rent/land rent, stock, access, commons housing, anti-speculation

Housing is the dominant essentials burden (L) in many economies and a primary channel through which extraction (E) and accumulation (W) reinforce
each other: scarcity rents become capitalized into prices; leverage amplifies bidding power; and households become locked into contracts and
locations. An OBA-compatible housing redesign therefore prioritizes **burden reduction**, **exit from lock-in**, and **capacity/maintenance
build-up** rather than policies that support asset values.

This paragraph specifies (i) where E concentrates in housing, (ii) how a commons/public housing layer provides credible exit, (iii) how price
and contract regimes prevent toll dynamics, (iv) how W-MAX compatibility is enforced against speculation, and (v) what must be measured to
detect washing and regression.

---

## B01.CH09.P02.S01 — E sources: land rent, scarcity construction, leverage, and subletting chains

Housing E is produced by bottlenecks and gate power, not by “housing as such”.

### Primary E sources (minimum; binding)
- **Land rent under scarcity:** location scarcity converts access into a toll, especially where planning and infrastructure create windfall gains.
- **Scarcity construction:** artificial constraints (permit throttling, hoarding, vacancy manipulation, slow-roll maintenance) that sustain rents.
- **Leverage as bidding power:** credit expansion and investor leverage increase prices and thus burdens; the gain is captured as rent/asset uplift.
- **Fee-stacks and intermediaries:** brokers, management layers, servicing fees, and transaction stacks that do not add housing quality or capacity.
- **Subletting and chain extraction:** multi-layer landlord chains where each layer extracts margin (rent-to-rent, short-term conversion, platform
  intermediation).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Any program that increases leverage demand or stabilizes prices without expanding capacity/maintenance is treated as an E amplifier and is
  non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P02.S02 — Commons/public layer: housing stock as commons infrastructure (access/maintenance)

OBA treats housing as infrastructure capacity: build and maintain stock that provides baseline access and disciplines private rents through
credible exit.

### Commons/public layer requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Baseline access package:** defined minimum quality and continuity (habitability, safety, repair response) with transparent allocation rules.
- **Maintenance-first:** ring-fenced maintenance budgets; backlog thresholds trigger escalation; “run-down stock” is a capacity breach.
- **Governance minima:** open books, audit logs, contestable decisions, anti-capture constraints on operators and vendors.
- **Non-speculative design:** commons housing cannot function as a speculative asset; resale and upside must be bounded by regime rules.
- **Throughput capacity:** stock and process capacity for moves (relocation) to reduce clamp power (waiting time and deposit portability where relevant).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- “We have public housing” is not evidence. Evidence is measurable access, lead times, quality continuity, and visible burden effects on cohorts.

## B01.CH09.P02.S03 — Price and contract regime: norming, ceilings, standard contracts, transparency requirements

Where private provision remains, OBA requires rails that prevent toll dynamics and contract clamps.

### Required price/contract rails (minimum; binding)
- **Transparent price build-up:** publishable decomposition of rent/service charges; prohibit opaque add-on fees.
- **Bounded increases:** indexed, bounded rent increase rules with auditable justifications for deviations (repairs/upgrades tied to costs).
- **Standard contracts:** prohibited clauses list (excessive exit penalties, abusive deposit terms, arbitrary eviction mechanisms).
- **Portability/exit:** reduce moving friction; limit penalty fees; standardize notice periods; prevent blacklisting without due process.
- **Look-through ownership transparency:** disclose beneficial control for large landlords and portfolio aggregators (concentration detection).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If “regulation” increases paperwork burden or creates compliance consultancies (administrative E), it fails and must be redesigned.

## B01.CH09.P02.S04 — Anti-speculation and caps: interaction with W-MAX (asset accumulation) and avoidance routes

Housing is a primary accumulation carrier. W-MAX compatibility requires controlling concentrated landlord positions and preventing avoidance via vehicles.

### Caps compatibility requirements (minimum; binding)
- **W-base inclusion and consolidation:** real estate holdings are counted through funds, SPVs, nominees, and family/household aggregation rules.
- **Economic exposure detection:** include synthetic exposure (derivatives, total return claims, rent-backed structures) where they replicate ownership.
- **Remedies under exceedance:** divestment, conversion of stock into commons regimes, levy/clawback, or regulated wind-down paths.
- **Anti-arbitrage controls:** prevent shifting from direct ownership to “management rights”, concessions, or platform gate rights that recreate toll power.
- **Transaction discipline:** anti-flip rules, vacancy discouragement mechanisms, and anti-hoarding enforcement (jurisdiction-parameterized).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A housing policy that leaves concentrated landlord positions intact while subsidizing tenants is compensation for W-based extraction and is
  non-conforming unless paired with enforceable anti-accumulation remedies.

## B01.CH09.P02.S05 — Access and R/B: burden reduction as a direct GROSS≈NET lever

Housing is the largest lever on L. Housing policy is therefore evaluated on R/B outcomes, not on price indices alone.

### Required outcome logic (minimum; binding)
- **L reduction:** reduce housing burden share for low and median cohorts; reduce volatility and tail events (arrears/evictions).
- **Exit power:** reduce lock-in so households can change jobs/regions without punitive costs.
- **Tail stabilization:** reduce sub-α clustering driven by housing cost shocks; improve time-to-recovery after shocks.
- **Avoid cosmetic substitution:** cash subsidies that raise rents are treated as E pass-through unless bounded by price rails.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If housing “support” raises demand without increasing supply/commons exit, it increases L over time and fails the GROSS≈NET objective.

## B01.CH09.P02.S06 — Sector metrics and red flags: price/income, vacancy, fee-stacks, concentration

Housing requires a minimum publishable metric surface to detect washing, capture, and regression.

### Required housing indicators (minimum; binding)
- **Burden metrics:** housing burden ratio (rent/mortgage + mandatory charges) by cohort; distribution (median, p10/p25) and tails.
- **Price/income:** price-to-income and rent-to-income bands; show dispersion and tail stress.
- **Access metrics:** waiting times for commons/public housing; move throughput; homelessness/shelter demand (aggregate).
- **Vacancy and hoarding:** vacancy rates, duration bands, and anomalies suggestive of withholding or speculative vacancy.
- **Fee-stacks:** broker/management/service charge shares; abnormal growth in intermediary margins.
- **Concentration:** landlord portfolio concentration, beneficial control clusters, and fund/SPV dominance indicators.
- **Lock-in:** exit penalty incidence, eviction rates, dispute volumes and outcomes, portability success.

### Red flags (binding)
- Price stability celebrated while burdens (L) rise for tails.
- Housing subsidies rising while rent indices outpace wages (pass-through).
- Commons stock exists but waiting times and backlogs grow persistently (capacity breach).
- Concentration rising via vehicles while “mom-and-pop landlord” narratives dominate (avoidance mask).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Without these indicators (or acceptable privacy-safe substitutes), housing policy cannot be certified as OBA-compatible.
