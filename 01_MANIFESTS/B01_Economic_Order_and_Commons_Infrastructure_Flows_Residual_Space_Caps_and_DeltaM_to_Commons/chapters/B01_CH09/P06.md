# B01.CH09.P06 — Labour and time (linked to sectors): reducing parasitic labour, re-allocating work

Sector redesign changes labour demand. When extraction (E) collapses structurally, many roles that existed to operate toll gates, enforce lock-in,
optimize fee-stacks, or navigate bureaucratic friction become unnecessary—or must be repurposed. OBA does not treat this as collateral damage to
ignore; it treats it as a **primary transition surface**. The success condition is not “jobs preserved”, but **functions reallocated** from E
maintenance toward production (P), commons capacity and maintenance (C/ΔG), and essential services.

This paragraph provides a cross-sector account of how labour and time shift under OBA-compatible redesign, which transition buffers are needed to
avoid sabotage and backlash, and which metrics must be published to prove that the system is reallocating function rather than merely displacing
harm.

---

## B01.CH09.P06.S01 — Mechanism: E↓ → fewer toll activities → labour reallocation toward maintenance/commons/P

E-heavy sectors contain labour that is economically “real” but functionally parasitic: it exists because the system produces toll points.

### Mechanism (binding)
- **E collapse removes toll work:** fewer brokerage layers, claims-processing ecosystems, rent-seeking compliance theatre, and lock-in enforcement.
- **Demand shifts to P and C:** more work is required where there are real bottlenecks: building capacity, maintaining infrastructure, delivering
  essentials, and operating commons institutions.
- **Execution becomes infrastructure:** administration, audit, and dispute resolution are treated as capacity (ΔG of governance), not overhead.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If E reduction is claimed but “E work” simply migrates into new labels (consultancy, compliance vendors, outsourced intermediaries), the system
  is washing; redesign must target the new toll layer.

---

## B01.CH09.P06.S02 — Function transition: reskilling, absorption capacity, and temporary buffers

Reallocation requires absorption capacity. Without it, the transition produces frictional unemployment, resentment, and operational sabotage.

### Required transition architecture (minimum; binding)
- **Function mapping:** map roles tied to E mechanisms (fee optimization, gatekeeping, claims intermediation) to adjacent P/C roles (maintenance,
  service delivery, capacity build, quality assurance).
- **Reskilling rails:** funded, time-bounded reskilling with recognized competencies; avoid credential inflation as a new lock-in.
- **Absorption planning:** identify capacity gaps and scale pathways (housing build teams, grid maintenance, care staffing, IT interoperability teams).
- **Temporary buffers:** income and time buffers during transitions (wage insurance, portability of rights, interim public employment pathways).
- **No stigma design:** transition instruments must not require humiliating compliance rituals; administrative friction is treated as hidden L.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the transition relies on “the market will absorb them” without capacity evidence and buffers, it is non-conforming due to predictable sabotage risk.

## B01.CH09.P06.S03 — Time redistribution: shorter workweek as an order effect (provided commons/essentials reduce burdens)

Time is a constraint variable. If essentials burdens (L) fall and insecurity tails stabilize, societies can translate productivity into time.

### Time redistribution logic (binding)
- **Precondition:** burdens and volatility must decline (housing/energy/care/digital); otherwise shorter workweek becomes a transfer of risk to households.
- **Order effect:** as E layers shrink and commons capacity expands, required labour hours for “toll navigation” decline, enabling time sovereignty.
- **Rights portability:** time rights (leave, care time, transition time) must be portable to avoid employer-based lock-in.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If time policy is introduced while essentials burdens remain high or care infrastructure is underbuilt, it produces hidden L and fails the lifeworld constraint.

## B01.CH09.P06.S04 — Risks: frictional unemployment, skill bottlenecks, transition costs

The transition is attackable. OBA requires anticipating and neutralizing predictable failure points.

### Core risks (minimum; binding)
- **Frictional unemployment:** timing mismatch between E collapse and P/C build-up.
- **Skill bottlenecks:** insufficient capacity in trades, care, grid operations, audit, and interoperability engineering.
- **Transition costs:** relocation, childcare gaps, certification delays, and temporary income dips.
- **Incumbent sabotage:** deliberate slow-roll by actors losing rents; vendor capture via “transition consulting”.
- **New lock-ins:** credential barriers, proprietary tooling, or platform dependence that reintroduce clamp dynamics.

### Standard mitigations (binding)
- staged sequencing aligned to absorption capacity;
- early investment in training pipelines and apprenticeship rails;
- mobility support and childcare/care capacity expansion;
- procurement rules against vendor lock-in; open standards and modular tooling;
- publishable transition dashboards and escalation triggers.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If these risks are not explicitly addressed with triggers and mitigations, scaling must be blocked.

## B01.CH09.P06.S05 — Metrics: parasitic-labour share, time use, stress/security indicators

Labour reallocation must be proven with evidence, not asserted with narrative.

### Required indicators (minimum; binding)
- **Parasitic-labour share proxies:** sectoral employment in roles strongly coupled to E mechanisms (fee optimization, claims/billing stacks,
  gatekeeping intermediaries), with transparent classification rules.
- **E→P/C transition rates:** flows of workers from E-linked roles into P/C roles, with time-to-placement distributions.
- **Absorption capacity metrics:** vacancies and training throughput in essential build/maintenance roles; backlog indicators tied to staffing.
- **Time-use indicators (aggregated):** working hours distribution, schedule predictability proxies, administrative time burden proxies.
- **Stress/security proxies (aggregated):** income volatility, arrears incidence, appeal/drop-off rates in transition programs, and time-to-recovery after shocks.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If parasitic-labour roles decline while unemployment or insecurity rises without buffers and absorption, the transition design is invalid and must
  be redesigned before further E reduction is executed.
