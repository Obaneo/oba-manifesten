# B01.CH09 — Reference implementations per sector: housing, energy, healthcare, platforms

This chapter provides **reference implementations**: sector-level redesign patterns that translate the OBA economic pillars into operational
sector architectures. Each sector is treated as a flow-and-interface system where extraction (E) typically concentrates at **bottlenecks**:
scarcity points, monopoly interfaces, contract clamps, and gatekeeping platforms. The chapter shows how to redesign these sectors so that
(i) E collapses structurally (not cosmetically), (ii) commons/public options create real exit, (iii) caps remain enforceable (no bypass via
sector structure), and (iv) the household outcome surface improves measurably (R/B tails stabilize; L declines in essentials).

The intent is not to prescribe a single national model, but to publish **templates that are forkable** within invariants: the same diagnostic
and redesign steps apply across jurisdictions, while parameters (thresholds, rollout sequencing, governance choices) can vary via a
compatibility procedure.

---

## What this chapter is

- A **method chapter** (P01) that defines how to redesign any sector in OBA terms.
- Four **sector exemplars** (P02–P05): housing, energy, healthcare, platforms.
- A **cross-cutting labour/time module** (P06) that translates sector redesign into labour reallocation logic.
- A **reusable sector canvas** (P07) that standardizes inputs, outputs, evidence, and versioning.

---

## What this chapter is not

- Not a collection of “policy ideas” detached from measurement. Every redesign must bind to indicators (E, R/B, ΔG, lock-in metrics).
- Not a moral critique of sectors. The unit of analysis is the structure: bottlenecks, contracts, market power, and claims.
- Not a substitute for the Codex. Templates and validators live in Book X; this chapter defines the normative structure and reference patterns.

---

## Binding invariants applied here

Every sector redesign must satisfy:

- **P/E/C discipline:** identify and reduce E at its structural sources (fee-stacks, monopoly rents, lock-in contracts).
- **R/B discipline:** show measurable improvements in burdens (L) and residual space profiles (median + tails; share_below_alpha).
- **Commons discipline:** build and maintain commons capacity (ΔG + quality) where markets produce structural toll/lock-in.
- **Caps compatibility:** prevent accumulation bypass via sector-specific vehicles, concessions, and gate rights.
- **Auditability + contestability:** publishable indicator surfaces, logged decisions, and dispute/appeal rails.

## Chapter structure

- **P01 — Method:** sector delimitation, bottleneck mapping, lever selection, sequencing, metrics, and risk analysis.
- **P02 — Housing:** scarcity → land rent/toll dynamics → commons housing + price/contract rails + anti-speculation.
- **P03 — Energy:** natural monopolies → tariff and investment rails → grids as commons + resilience discipline + ΔM capex channels.
- **P04 — Healthcare:** admin tolls and chain capture → access/quality minima + professional autonomy + interoperable data rails.
- **P05 — Platforms:** take-rates and ranking power → interoperability/portability + data commons constraints + public option triggers.
- **P06 — Labour & time:** how E collapse reallocates work toward P/maintenance/commons; transition buffers and metrics.
- **P07 — Sector canvas template:** a machine-readable/compareable template for any sector, with versioning and compatibility checks.

## How to use this chapter

### For design
1. Start with **P01** and fill the **sector canvas** (P07).
2. Identify dominant **E sources** and bottlenecks (P01.S02).
3. Choose a lever mix: **E reduction**, **commons/public option**, **caps enforcement hooks**, **R/B improvement pathway**.
4. Produce an ex-ante sequencing plan and the required indicator set.

### For audit/review
1. Verify that sector boundaries and interfaces are defined (P01.S01).
2. Check that E reduction is structural (no relabeling) and that pass-through does not raise L.
3. Confirm commons outputs (ΔG + quality) and maintenance discipline.
4. Confirm caps compatibility (no SPV/ concession/ gate-right bypass).
5. Demand publishable metrics and risk mitigations before scaling.

## Interfaces

- **CH02 (flows):** sector diagnostics use flow decomposition and attribution rules.
- **CH03 (R/B):** sector success is tested on household outcome surfaces (L and R/B profiles).
- **CH04 (caps):** sector structures must not become accumulation carriers.
- **CH05 (E phase-down):** sector levers must anticipate “shift” patterns and prevent E migration.
- **CH06 (commons):** commons definitions, governance minima, and boundary rules apply to sector public options.
- **CH07 (ΔM):** sector investment channels must pass leakage tests and avoid asset support.
- **CH10 (audit):** minimum data and consolidation requirements define what evidence is admissible.
