# B01.CH09.P01 — Method: how to redesign a sector as “OBA-compatible” (P/E/C + R/B + commons + caps)

This paragraph specifies a reusable method for sector redesign under OBA. A sector is treated as a **flow-and-interface system**: value and
burdens propagate through chains; extraction (E) concentrates at bottlenecks; and household outcomes are observed as burdens (L) and residual
space profiles (R/B). A sector redesign is OBA-compatible only if it (i) collapses E structurally, (ii) increases commons capacity and quality
(ΔG), (iii) blocks accumulation bypass around caps, and (iv) improves R/B distributions—especially tails—using measurable evidence.

The method is designed to be forkable: parameters may differ by jurisdiction, but the diagnostic steps, evidence requirements, and stop rules
are invariant.

---

## B01.CH09.P01.S01 — Sector delimitation: what counts as a sector, a chain, and critical interfaces

Sector redesign begins with clear boundaries. Without delimitation, accountability dissolves and E shifts hide in “adjacent” layers.

### Required delimitation elements (minimum; binding)
- **Sector boundary:** define what services/goods are in scope and what is out of scope.
- **Chain boundary:** define upstream and downstream links that materially determine price, access, and quality (inputs, logistics, financing,
  platforms, concessions).
- **Critical interfaces:** identify interfaces where users must pass (payment rails, identity, contracts, allocation lists, grid connection,
  platform ranking, licensing).
- **Actor and position map:** list actor types and positions (operator, gatekeeper, financier, regulator, auditor, steward), including UBC where
  relevant.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If critical interfaces are left out of scope, the redesign is presumed incomplete and cannot claim compatibility.

---

## B01.CH09.P01.S02 — Bottleneck mapping: where toll/lock-in/land rent/fee-stacks arise (E sources)

E arises where bottlenecks create gate power. Mapping bottlenecks is therefore the central diagnostic step.

### Bottleneck scan (minimum; binding)
- **Scarcity points:** capacity constraints (land, permits, grid capacity, staff, beds, compute, spectrum, logistics).
- **Natural monopoly segments:** infrastructure layers where competition is structurally limited (grids, rails, identity rails, app stores).
- **Contract clamp points:** standard contract terms that create lock-in (exit penalties, exclusivity, portability loss, blacklisting).
- **Intermediation stacks:** intermediary layers that can stack fees (brokers, servicers, billing/claims, payments, compliance vendors).
- **Gatekeeping mechanisms:** allocation lists, rankings, referral networks, licensing, proprietary formats/APIs.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the redesign cannot name the dominant bottlenecks and show how they are neutralized or governed, it is washing-by-architecture.

## B01.CH09.P01.S03 — Sector levers under OBA: (i) E reduction, (ii) commons/public option, (iii) caps compatibility, (iv) R/B effect

Sector interventions must be expressed in four lever families, each with explicit evidence obligations.

### Lever family 1 — E reduction (binding)
- eliminate or cap fee-stacks,
- constrain monopoly rents with regulated bands,
- remove lock-in clauses and restore portability,
- impose interoperability where gate power is technical.

### Lever family 2 — Commons / public option (binding)
- build or expand commons capacity where markets structurally produce tolls,
- design baseline access packages and continuity rules,
- governance minima: transparency, contestability, maintenance-first, anti-capture.

### Lever family 3 — Caps compatibility (binding)
- map W/I exposure and control positions in the sector (UBC look-through),
- prevent bypass via concessions, SPVs, nominal ownership, synthetic exposure,
- define remedies under exceedance: divestment, conversion to commons, claim limits.

### Lever family 4 — R/B effect (binding)
- show how interventions reduce L (prices/burdens) and stabilize R/B distributions (median + tails),
- prevent pass-through that raises burdens under the guise of “regulation costs”.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A sector plan that states only instruments (“we regulate X”) without mapping to these four levers is non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P01.S04 — Sequencing: quick wins versus structural rebuild (link to CH05 and CH07)

OBA uses sequencing to prevent two common failures: (i) reforms that create transition scarcity and backlash, and (ii) reforms that subsidize
assets and entrench E.

### Sequencing discipline (minimum; binding)
- **Quick wins:** target obvious fee-stacks, junk fees, abusive contract clauses, and transparency gaps with low build requirements.
- **Structural rebuild:** invest in bottleneck relief and commons capacity (skills, permits, grid/housing stock, care capacity, interoperability).
- **Shift awareness:** anticipate E migration into adjacent layers (CH05) and pre-specify anti-shift checks.
- **Channel discipline:** where investment is needed, specify whether funding is fiscal or ΔM-linked (CH07) and pass leakage tests.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Sequencing that relies on asset support or temporary exceptions without an unwind path is treated as regression risk.

## B01.CH09.P01.S05 — Metrics: minimum indicators per sector (E share, prices/burdens, access, quality, lock-in)

A sector redesign is only real if it can be measured. Metrics are not “KPIs to optimize”; they are an evidence surface to detect drift and
washing.

### Minimum metric surface (binding)
- **E indicators:** fee-stack depth, margin anomalies, take-rate measures, concentration, gatekeeping incidence.
- **Burdens (L) indicators:** sector burden share of household budgets; volatility; arrears/disconnects/evictions where relevant (aggregated).
- **Access indicators:** coverage, eligibility-to-served ratios, lead times/wait times, denial/deferral rates.
- **Quality indicators:** reliability, safety incidents, continuity metrics, maintenance backlogs, uptime where applicable.
- **Lock-in indicators:** portability success, switching cost proxies, exit penalties, contract clamp incidence.

### Method discipline (binding)
- publish definition-set references, method_id, revision policy, and uncertainty handling where approximation is used.

## B01.CH09.P01.S06 — Risk analysis: avoidance/capture/shortages and standard mitigations

Sector plans fail mainly through predictable risk families: avoidance (shift), capture (procurement/governance), and shortages (capacity collapse).

### Required risk register (minimum; binding)
- **Avoidance/shifts:** legal relabeling, technical bundling, financial repackaging, cross-border routing; specify detection red flags and automatic
  remedies.
- **Capture:** vendor lock-in, lobbying carve-outs, exception culture, selective enforcement; specify governance safeguards and meta-audit triggers.
- **Shortages:** capacity bottlenecks and transition stress; specify build plans, staffing pipelines, and fallback continuity measures.

### Mitigation discipline (binding)
- each risk must have: (i) trigger indicators, (ii) escalation path, (iii) stop rule, and (iv) repair path (template redesign, governance reset).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A sector plan without a risk register and stop rules is non-conforming because it cannot prevent regression under pressure.
