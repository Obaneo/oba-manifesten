# B01.CH09.P04 — Healthcare: access, professional autonomy, data, scale, commons provisions

Healthcare is an essentials domain where failure manifests directly as tail risk: untreated conditions, financial distress, and loss of agency.
In OBA terms, healthcare redesign is evaluated by whether it (i) guarantees baseline access without stigma or gatekeeping tolls, (ii) prevents
administrative and contractual extraction (E), (iii) builds and maintains capacity and quality (ΔG + continuity), and (iv) reduces mandatory
burdens (L) and uncertainty costs that destabilize R/B profiles.

This paragraph specifies a reference pattern: define a non-marketable core with commons-like provisioning rules; eliminate administrative tolls
and chain extraction; protect professional autonomy against management capture and metric gaming; treat interoperability and data governance as
critical rails; and publish a minimum metric surface that makes waiting times, overhead, and lock-in auditable.

---

## B01.CH09.P04.S01 — E sources: administrative toll, chain formation, IP/contract monopolies, insurance frictions

Healthcare E often grows “inside” the system as layers that extract from care pathways rather than improving outcomes.

### Primary E sources (minimum; binding)
- **Administrative toll:** billing/claims complexity, prior authorizations, paperwork burdens, coding games, compliance theatre that consumes
  clinical time and creates intermediary ecosystems.
- **Chain formation and financialization:** ownership chains and roll-ups that extract via fees, management contracts, and cost-cutting that
  degrades capacity/quality.
- **IP and contract monopolies:** exclusive rights and contracting structures that sustain prices above functional bands without proportional
  quality gain.
- **Insurance frictions:** network lock-in, denial patterns, opaque coverage rules, and time-burdened appeals functioning as a gate.
- **Vendor lock-in:** proprietary EHR/LIS systems and closed data interfaces that create switching costs and gatekeeping power.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If “efficiency reforms” increase admin layers, vendor dependency, or denial frictions, they are E growth under a managerial label and are
  non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P04.S02 — Access as a baseline right: commons provisions and minimum coverage (non-marketable core)

OBA treats baseline healthcare access as constitutive for order stability: it cannot be contingent on ability to pay, network position, or
bureaucratic endurance.

### Baseline access requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Non-marketable core:** define minimum coverage pathways/services that must be reachable in practice (not only on paper).
- **Wait-time rails:** publish maximum lead-times per pathway; breaches trigger escalation and capacity remediation.
- **Non-discrimination:** auditable triage rules and allocation procedures; prohibited exclusion patterns via pricing or bureaucracy.
- **Due process:** rapid objection/appeal routes with reproducible decision rules; second-opinion rails where needed.
- **Continuity and fallback:** continuity guarantees for chronic care; emergency access that is not a debt trap.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- “Free but unreachable” (chronic wait-time breaches without escalation) is equivalent to denial and is non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P04.S03 — Professional autonomy: governance against management capture and metrics gaming

Healthcare quality depends on professional judgment and care pathways; metric regimes can easily become capture tools.

### Governance requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Autonomy protection:** clinical decision-making is insulated from purely financial target regimes; conflicts of interest are disclosed.
- **Separation of functions:** clear role split between mandate setting, operations, audit, and appeals (no closed loops).
- **Triangulated quality measurement:** no single KPI; quality is assessed via multiple dimensions (safety, continuity, effectiveness, dignity,
  access), with procedure-bound qualitative inputs where appropriate.
- **Anti-Goodhart rails:** forbid optimizing a metric at the expense of tails (wait times, exclusions, denial patterns) or clinical integrity.
- **Capacity-first discipline:** staffing and training pipelines treated as infrastructure; chronic understaffing is a capacity breach.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If performance management produces selection, denial, or “gaming” behavior, governance must be redesigned; cosmetic KPI revisions are not sufficient.

## B01.CH09.P04.S04 — Data and interoperability: data as commons (with privacy/boundary rules)

Healthcare data is a high-power rail: it can enable continuity and auditability, or become a surveillance and vendor-capture instrument.

### Interoperability and data-governance requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Open standards:** mandatory interoperability for records, referrals, and billing surfaces; portability and migration tooling.
- **Boundary rules:** prohibit secondary commercialization, profiling, and non-care uses beyond tightly defined purposes.
- **Tiered access:** public aggregates, regulated audit access, and confidential PII tiers with logging and retention caps.
- **Vendor lock-in prevention:** procurement rules require open APIs, escrow/exit triggers, and auditability of critical systems.
- **Algorithmic governance (where used):** triage/decision tools must be auditable, bias-tested, and contestable; black-box tools are non-conforming.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If interoperability cannot be achieved without expanding surveillance scope, the system must fail closed and redesign: care continuity cannot be
bought by turning healthcare into a data extraction regime.

## B01.CH09.P04.S05 — Cost structure and R/B: reducing mandatory burdens and uncertainty costs

Healthcare harms R/B via two channels: direct burdens (premiums, copays, bills) and uncertainty (risk of catastrophic costs and administrative delay).

### Required cost and burden logic (minimum; binding)
- **L reduction:** reduce mandatory out-of-pocket burdens for baseline care; prevent surprise billing and opaque add-ons.
- **Uncertainty reduction:** make coverage and eligibility predictable; shorten decision cycles; provide provisional relief during disputes.
- **Anti-toll pricing discipline:** cap or eliminate fee-stacks (billing intermediaries, mandatory networks, add-on administration fees).
- **Shock absorption:** ensure that health shocks do not trigger debt cascades; use standardized restructuring and protection rules (link CH08.P03).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If reforms reduce headline spending while increasing denial friction, time burden, or catastrophic tail events, they are non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P04.S06 — Sector metrics and red flags: waiting times, overhead ratio, price variation, lock-in

Healthcare requires a minimum publishable metric surface that exposes access reality, administrative extraction, and capacity health.

### Required healthcare indicators (minimum; binding)
- **Access:** coverage reach, denial/deferral rates, and pathway-specific wait times (median + tails); breach rates against published maxima.
- **Quality:** safety incident rates, continuity indicators, outcome proxies (triangulated), and experience inputs via procedure-bound reporting.
- **Capacity:** staffing ratios, vacancy/turnover proxies, training pipeline capacity, bed/clinic capacity, and backlog distributions.
- **Administrative overhead:** admin-time share, billing/claims cost share, prior-authorization volumes, appeal volumes and durations.
- **Price variation:** price dispersion for standardized procedures and supplies; anomaly detection for IP/contract monopolies.
- **Lock-in:** network restriction incidence, provider switching friction, data portability success rates, vendor concentration in core IT.

### Red flags (binding)
- Wait times treated as “new normal” without escalation (capacity breach).
- Overhead ratios rising while “digital transformation” spend rises (admin E regrowth).
- Vendor dominance in EHR/claims tooling with low portability (lock-in drift).
- Denial patterns rising while costs are “controlled” (cost-shifting via gatekeeping).
- Chain consolidation correlated with margin extraction and quality decay.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Without these indicators (or privacy-safe equivalents), healthcare policy cannot be certified as OBA-compatible.
