# B01.CH09.P03 — Energy: grids as commons, pricing mechanism, investment channel, security of supply

Energy is an essentials domain with strong natural monopoly segments (grids, networks) and high shock sensitivity. In OBA terms, energy policy is
evaluated by whether it (i) reduces mandatory burdens (L) and stabilizes R/B tails, (ii) prevents toll dynamics and crisis profiteering (E),
(iii) builds and maintains infrastructure capacity (ΔG + quality), and (iv) allocates investment through channels that do not relapse into asset
support or accumulation bypass.

This paragraph specifies the energy sector redesign pattern: grids as commons core infrastructure; tariff rails that protect baseline access and
constrain extraction; investment discipline (including ΔM-to-commons where used); and measurable resilience outputs.

---

## B01.CH09.P03.S01 — E sources: natural monopolies, market power, scarcity and volatility rents

Energy E tends to concentrate at interfaces where users cannot exit: network access, billing, and crisis pricing.

### Primary E sources (minimum; binding)
- **Natural monopoly rents:** excess returns extracted from network segments where competition is structurally limited.
- **Market power in supply layers:** concentration in generation, retail supply, or trading that enables margin expansion beyond functional bands.
- **Scarcity rents:** rents arising from capacity constraints (grid congestion, insufficient generation, insufficient storage).
- **Volatility rents:** profit from price swings amplified by opaque tariffs, hedging asymmetry, or opportunistic pass-through.
- **Fee-stacks:** add-on charges, billing fees, connection fees, and opaque “service” layers that function as tolls.
- **Lock-in:** contracts and switching frictions (penalty fees, bundling, opaque comparisons) that prevent competitive discipline.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Any regime that allows sustained excess returns in monopoly segments without published cost bands and remedy mechanisms is non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P03.S02 — Grids as commons: governance, maintenance, access, and interoperability

The grid is the commons core: it must be governed for continuity, reliability, and non-discriminatory access.

### Commons grid requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Access as a right:** non-discriminatory connection and service access rules, with due process for disconnection (only under bounded criteria).
- **Maintenance-first discipline:** ring-fenced maintenance budgets; backlog thresholds trigger escalation; reliability is treated as ΔG-quality.
- **Transparent governance:** decision logs for investments, congestion management, procurement, and outage response (publishable as aggregates).
- **Interoperability:** open standards for metering, grid IT, and integration interfaces; vendor lock-in triggers and exit assistance.
- **Contestability:** objection/appeal routes for allocation decisions, tariffs, and disconnection events; audit-on-the-auditor.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A grid operator that cannot publish reliability, maintenance, and procurement integrity surfaces is a capture risk and cannot be treated as a
  commons steward.

## B01.CH09.P03.S03 — Pricing mechanism: tariff structures that protect essentials and constrain E

Energy pricing must prevent baseline exclusion, reduce regressivity, and block fee-stacks.

### Tariff rail requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Baseline access band:** a minimum energy package priced to protect essential usage (parameterized by region/season) as an L-reduction tool.
- **Progressive structure:** low baseline rates and higher marginal pricing for luxury consumption (anti-regressive incidence).
- **Transparent build-up:** publishable decomposition into network costs, generation costs, policy components, and bounded margins.
- **Bounded returns in monopoly segments:** regulated margin bands with automatic review triggers when exceeded.
- **No junk fees:** prohibit opaque add-ons and billing fee-stacks; all-in effective price must be legible and comparable.
- **Switching and portability:** constrain contract lock-in; standardize comparison formats; enforce portability of data and contract history.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If tariff complexity functions as a user-facing toll gate or encourages intermediary “optimizers”, the pricing mechanism is non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P03.S04 — Investment and ΔM: capex channels toward grid capacity/scale-up without asset bubbles

Energy transition requires capex; OBA requires channel discipline so investment does not become asset support.

### Investment discipline (minimum; binding)
- **Capacity-first targeting:** invest in bottlenecks (grid reinforcement, storage, interconnectors, demand-response infrastructure) that reduce
  scarcity rents and improve reliability.
- **Maintenance pairing:** capex plans include opex and lifecycle maintenance obligations; “build then neglect” is forbidden.
- **Procurement rails:** open standards, modular procurement, escrow/exit triggers to prevent vendor capture.
- **ΔM channel discipline (where used):** ΔM-funded energy investment must route to commons capex/opex with traceability and leakage tests; no
  primary routing to asset valuation support for private holders.
- **No bubble subsidies:** prohibit demand-side subsidies that mainly raise prices without increasing capacity or reducing L.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If investment programs correlate with rising asset values and persistent scarcity rents while reliability and burdens do not improve, the channel
  is leaking into accumulation and must be redesigned.

## B01.CH09.P03.S05 — Security of supply: reserves, redundancy, crisis protocols

Security of supply is an order obligation, not a market outcome.

### Required resilience architecture (minimum; binding)
- **Reserves and redundancy:** reserve margins, storage readiness, and contingency capacity thresholds with published breach triggers.
- **Crisis protocols:** trigger-based, time-bounded emergency measures with dashboards (trigger → action → outcome → sunset).
- **Continuity priorities:** explicit priority rules for critical services and vulnerable cohorts; non-discretionary where possible.
- **Anti-profiteering rails:** crisis pricing and trading behavior monitored via publishable indicators; enforcement hooks pre-defined.
- **Rapid repair capacity:** maintenance teams, spare parts, and supplier resilience treated as infrastructure capacity.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Emergency measures without sunsets, dashboards, and unwind plans are treated as permanent emergency drift and fail compatibility.

## B01.CH09.P03.S06 — Sector metrics and red flags: price volatility, profit margins, congestion, underinvestment

Energy requires a minimum publishable metric surface that exposes volatility, capture, and maintenance collapse.

### Required energy indicators (minimum; binding)
- **Affordability/burdens:** energy burden ratio by cohort; arrears and disconnections (aggregate); baseline access coverage.
- **Price volatility:** volatility bands, spike frequency, and pass-through measures; share of price explained by congestion/scarcity.
- **Profit and margin indicators:** bounded return compliance for monopoly segments; abnormal spreads in retail/trading layers.
- **Congestion and capacity:** congestion hours, curtailment, connection lead times, and reinforcement pipeline status.
- **Reliability:** outage frequency/duration metrics; incident logs (aggregated); mean time to restore.
- **Maintenance:** backlog size/age distributions; deferred maintenance indicators; critical asset health indices.
- **Vendor lock-in risk:** concentration of critical IT/control vendors; change-order frequency; exit readiness indicators.

### Red flags (binding)
- Rising disconnections/arrears while “average bills” are stable (tail harm).
- High congestion rents persisting over years without capacity build (bottleneck capture).
- Margin expansion during crises without corresponding cost evidence (profiteering).
- Maintenance backlog growth paired with capex announcements (build theatre).
- Vendor concentration rising in grid control systems (lock-in drift).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Without these indicators (or privacy-safe equivalents), energy policy cannot be certified as OBA-compatible.
