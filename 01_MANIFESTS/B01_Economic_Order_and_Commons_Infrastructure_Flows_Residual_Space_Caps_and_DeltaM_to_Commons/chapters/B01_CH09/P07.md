# B01.CH09.P07 — Template: “sector canvas” reusable for other domains

This paragraph defines a reusable **sector canvas**: a standardized template for describing, auditing, and versioning sector redesign plans under
OBA. The purpose of the canvas is to prevent “policy prose” from floating free of the invariant pillars. A sector plan is admissible only if it
can be rendered into the canvas fields, supported by minimum data, tested against decision rules, and maintained under version control.

The canvas is designed for (i) comparability across sectors and jurisdictions, (ii) auditability and contestability, and (iii) shift-resistance:
it forces explicit mapping of where E sits, how exit is created, how caps are protected, and how household outcomes (R/B) are expected to improve.

---

## B01.CH09.P07.S01 — Canvas fields: bottlenecks, E sources, commons/public option, caps compatibility, R/B effect, ΔM channel

### Sector canvas fields (minimum; binding)

**A. Delimitation**
- `sector_id`
- `sector_boundary` (in-scope / out-of-scope)
- `critical_interfaces[]` (user must pass points)
- `chain_map[]` (key upstream/downstream links)

**B. Bottlenecks and E sources**
- `bottlenecks[]` (capacity/scarcity points, monopoly segments, gate interfaces)
- `e_sources[]` (fee-stacks, rents, lock-in mechanisms, ranking power, administrative toll)
- `lock_in_map[]` (contract clamp points, switching costs, dependency graphs)

**C. Commons / public option design**
- `commons_option` (none / hybrid / full)
- `baseline_access_package` (what is guaranteed)
- `governance_minima` (transparency, contestability, maintenance-first, anti-capture)
- `maintenance_plan` (capex/opex, backlog thresholds, continuity obligations)
- `procurement_rails` (open standards, exit triggers, vendor dominance checks)

**D. Caps compatibility**
- `ubc_map[]` (beneficial control mapping)
- `w_i_exposure_map[]` (where accumulation and income extraction sit in the sector)
- `bypass_risks[]` (SPVs, concessions, control rights, synthetics, tokenized claims)
- `remedy_set[]` (divestment, conversion-to-commons, claim limits, levies, wind-down paths)

**E. R/B effect (household outcome surface)**
- `l_components[]` (which mandatory burdens are targeted)
- `rb_target_profile` (which cohorts/tails; distributional targets)
- `shock_paths[]` (which tail events are dampened: arrears, disconnections, evictions, denial cascades)
- `time_friction_proxies[]` (administrative burden, waiting times, scheduling clamp where relevant)

**F. ΔM / investment channel (if used)**
- `funding_sources[]` (fiscal, ΔM, mixed)
- `dm_channel_design` (channel → recipient → destination trace)
- `leakage_tests[]` (asset/claim support prohibitions; pass-through detection)
- `sunset_unwind_plan` (for temporary measures)

**G. Evidence and audit**
- `definition_set_refs[]` (pinned terms, method_id policy)
- `minimum_dataset_refs[]` (CH10 alignment)
- `audit_plan` (sampling/full coverage, triggers, escalation, dispute flow)
- `publication_surface[]` (what is published; privacy tiering)

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If a sector plan cannot be expressed in these fields, it is not a plan; it is narrative and is non-conforming.

## B01.CH09.P07.S02 — Minimal data per field: what must be measured to fill the canvas (link to CH10)

The canvas requires a minimum measurable substrate; otherwise it collapses into guesswork.

### Minimal dataset per canvas domain (minimum; binding)
- **Flows and pricing:** transaction flows, tariff tables, fee schedules, price bands, contract terms (standardized where possible).
- **Costs and margins (where needed):** minimum cost categories and margin proxies sufficient to detect fee-stacks and excess returns.
- **Control and ownership:** beneficial control signals, concession rights, platform rule-control, major contractual gate rights.
- **Capacity and backlog:** stock/capacity measures, lead times, congestion, waiting times, maintenance backlogs and SLA breach rates.
- **Access and outcomes:** coverage, denial/deferral, arrears/disconnects/evictions (as applicable), quality incidents, continuity metrics.
- **Lock-in:** portability success rates, switching-cost proxies, contract clamp incidence, dependency graphs (aggregated).
- **Uncertainty handling:** explicit “unknown” categories, error bands for proxies, and revision rules (no silent filling).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- When data are missing at minimum level, the plan must fail closed: no compatibility claim and no scaling.

## B01.CH09.P07.S03 — Decision rules: when a sector plan is OBA-compatible (link to CH12)

The canvas is evaluated by explicit decision rules to prevent semantic drift.

### Compatibility decision rules (minimum; binding)
A sector plan is OBA-compatible only if it satisfies all of the following:

1. **E reduction is structural:** dominant E sources are identified and neutralized (not compensated by subsidies).
2. **Exit exists in practice:** interoperability/portability or commons/public option provides credible exit from gate power.
3. **Caps are enforceable:** no accumulation bypass remains via sector-specific control rights, vehicles, concessions, or synthetics.
4. **R/B improves measurably:** burdens (L) and tail events decline; improvements are distributional (median + tails), not average-only.
5. **ΔG is positive (where commons involved):** commons capacity and quality are built and maintained under maintenance-first discipline.
6. **Auditability and contestability exist:** publication surface and dispute/audit architecture are defined; no black boxes.
7. **Fail-closed is specified:** data gaps or integrity breaches suspend claims and trigger redesign before scaling.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If any rule cannot be proven with admissible evidence, the plan must be treated as non-conforming until repaired.

## B01.CH09.P07.S04 — Anti-avoidance: standard check for shifts (legal/technical/international)

Every sector plan must include a default “shift scan”: how E will try to migrate when old tolls are blocked.

### Standard shift scan (minimum; binding)
- **Legal shifts:** relabeling fees as “services”, moving rents into licensing, franchising, consultancy, and side contracts.
- **Technical shifts:** bundling, dark patterns, API gating, proprietary formats, identity lock-in, new “mandatory” intermediaries.
- **Financial shifts:** securitization, fee-stacks in financing, synthetic exposure, off-balance conduits, collateral loops.
- **International shifts:** transfer pricing, treaty shopping, re-domiciliation, offshore ownership/control concealment.

### Required anti-shift components (binding)
- `shift_red_flags[]` (detectable signals)
- `automatic_requalification_rules[]` (substance-over-form presumptions)
- `escalation_path` (audit → remedy → structural intervention)
- `stop_rules` (when scaling halts due to recurring shift patterns)

## B01.CH09.P07.S05 — Versioning: how sector canvases evolve without a core break (traceability)

Sector canvases must evolve, but changes must be traceable and non-destructive to the core pillars.

### Versioning discipline (minimum; binding)
- **Version identifiers:** `canvas_version`, `definition_set_version`, `benchmark_version` where relevant.
- **Changelog:** what changed, why, expected impacts, and compatibility mapping (no silent edits).
- **Backward compatibility:** changes should be parametric where possible; core rule changes require explicit restatement and review.
- **Deprecation policy:** old templates are deprecated with timelines and migration steps; no “soft drift” via informal practice.
- **Public restatements:** when indicators or methods change, publish restatements so time-series comparisons remain honest.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A sector plan without traceable versioning is a capture surface. Without changelog + restatement discipline, it is non-conforming.
