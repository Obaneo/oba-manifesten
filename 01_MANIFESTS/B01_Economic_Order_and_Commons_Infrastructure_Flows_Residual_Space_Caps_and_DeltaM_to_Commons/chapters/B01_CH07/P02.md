# B01.CH07.P02 — Allocation channels: which routes exist (and which are OOA reflexes)

This paragraph enumerates the canonical **ΔM allocation channels** and classifies which channel families are admissible, high-risk, or
forbidden under OBA. Channel choice is not neutral: each channel has a default transmission pattern (who benefits first, what gets stabilized,
which intermediaries gain control) and therefore a default drift risk.

The objective is not to eliminate all channels, but to impose an explicit routing discipline: ΔM must preferentially build commons capacity and
stabilize the R/B floor, while preventing relapse into asset-support reflexes that inflate W and regenerate E.

---

## B01.CH07.P02.S01 — State channel: public investment, transfers, commons budgets (conditions)

The state channel routes ΔM via public budgets and public investment programs.

### Admissible state-channel uses (minimum; binding)
- **Commons capex/opex:** funding build-out and maintenance of commons infrastructure with measurable ΔG and quality outputs.
- **R/B floor stabilization:** time-bounded buffers and stabilizers that reduce tail collapse and arrears cascades.
- **Transition instruments:** reskilling/mobility and bottleneck relief where linked to measurable E reduction and capacity build-up.

### Required conditions (binding)
- **Template-bound deployment:** no bespoke deals; all allocations use standard templates and audit packs.
- **Traceability:** ΔM → program → recipient class → procurement/outputs must be recorded and publishable in aggregate.
- **No asset-support leakage:** programs must not function as disguised price support for assets (housing price subsidies, guaranteed excess
  returns).

---

## B01.CH07.P02.S02 — Bank/credit channel: credit creation, balance-sheet expansion, collateral-driven allocation

The bank/credit channel routes ΔM through private or quasi-private credit creation and balance-sheet expansion.

### Admissible bank/credit-channel uses (minimum; binding)
- **Productive and commons-linked lending:** credit routed to production capacity and commons build/maintenance under constrained margins,
  transparency, and use-of-funds tagging.
- **Debt restructuring rails:** reducing parasitic debt cascades (E) with standardized terms and bounded fees.

### Primary risks (structural)
- **Collateral bias:** credit expansion gravitates toward assets used as collateral, inflating asset prices and reinforcing W concentration.
- **Intermediation fee stacks:** multiple fee layers (origination/servicing/insurance/collections) regenerate E.
- **Selective access:** credit allocation can reproduce gate power and exclusion, worsening R/B tails.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Credit-channel ΔM must be constrained by collateral rules, transparency, bounded returns, and audit triggers; otherwise it is treated as a
  high-risk channel.

---

## B01.CH07.P02.S03 — Market/asset channel: asset purchases, liquidity injections, valuation support (OOA reflex risk)

The market/asset channel routes ΔM by purchasing assets, injecting liquidity into markets, or otherwise supporting valuations.

### Default effect profile (structural)
- **Asset inflation:** raises valuations and benefits asset holders first.
- **W accumulation reinforcement:** increases concentration and strengthens claim stacks.
- **Indirect and uneven R/B transmission:** any household benefit is delayed, uncertain, and often offset by higher essentials prices.

### Conformance stance (binding)
- This channel is treated as an **OOA reflex risk** and is presumptively non-compatible unless a narrow, evidenced case is made that it does
  not primarily function as asset support and that it has a bounded, auditable route to commons outputs and R/B tail stabilization.

---

## B01.CH07.P02.S04 — Direct commons channels: commons funds, capex vehicles, maintenance funds, public option

Direct commons channels route ΔM into commons build-up without relying on asset valuation or private gatekeepers as the primary transmission
mechanism.

### Admissible direct commons-channel forms (minimum; binding)
- **Commons funds:** ring-fenced funds with governance minima, access rules, and published ΔG/quality outputs.
- **Capex vehicles:** standardized vehicles financing build-out (housing supply, grid capacity, care capacity, digital baseline) with open
  procurement rails and anti-capture constraints.
- **Maintenance funds:** dedicated opex/maintenance financing to prevent degradation (negative ΔG) and to stabilize service continuity.
- **Public option build-out:** capacity funding for baseline providers that discipline private toll markets through contestable exit options.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Direct commons channels must remain procurement-safe (anti–vendor lock-in), auditable, and bound by maintenance-first discipline.

---

## B01.CH07.P02.S05 — Forbidden/risk channels: allocation that primarily feeds asset inflation and W accumulation

Certain channel behaviors are incompatible because their dominant effect is accumulation reinforcement.

### Forbidden/risk channel characteristics (binding)
- **Primary asset-price support:** interventions designed to hold or raise asset prices, especially in essentials (housing/land).
- **Guaranteed excess returns:** concessions or structures that guarantee high private returns funded by ΔM.
- **Opaque intermediation layers:** routing through black-box vehicles, proprietary models, or non-reproducible allocation logic.
- **Bypass creation:** channels that create new accumulation bypasses around W-MAX/I-MAX (privileged structures, synthetic claim expansion).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If an allocation’s primary transmission is asset inflation and W accumulation, it is forbidden/risk and must be blocked or redesigned.

---

## B01.CH07.P02.S06 — Channel selection rule: when which channel is OBA-compatible (ex-ante criteria)

Channel selection must be decided ex-ante using published criteria, not improvised under pressure.

### Required selection criteria (minimum; binding)
- **Impact target:** does the channel primarily increase ΔG commons capacity/quality and/or stabilize R/B tails?
- **E effect:** does the channel reduce E mechanisms (tolls/lock-in/fee-stacks) or does it create new ones?
- **Cap compatibility:** does the channel avoid reinforcing W/I concentration or creating bypass structures?
- **Traceability:** can the flow be traced end-to-end with publishable aggregates and method discipline?
- **Robustness to shift/capture:** does the channel minimize incentives for avoidance and reduce capture surfaces (plurality, open standards)?
- **Fail-closed readiness:** can the channel be paused under missing data/opacity without collapsing essentials continuity?

### Decision rule (binding)
- Prefer **direct commons channels** and **state-channel commons build/maintenance** where feasible.
- Permit **bank/credit channels** only under tight collateral, fee, and transparency constraints.
- Treat **market/asset channels** as presumptively non-compatible and require exceptional, audited justification if ever used.
