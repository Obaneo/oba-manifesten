# B01.CH07.P01 — What ΔM is in the OBA context (creation/space/expansion) and why it is core power

This paragraph defines **ΔM** as the order-relevant variable capturing monetary expansion/space and its allocation into the economy. In OBA,
ΔM is treated as **core power** because allocation choices determine which positions gain control, which claims are stabilized, and which
institutions can build durable capacity (commons) versus inflating private accumulation.

ΔM is not “macro plumbing” separate from politics. It is a constitutive lever that can either (i) build commons capacity (ΔG), reduce E and
stabilize R/B tails, or (ii) reproduce extraction and concentration via asset support and privileged channels.

---

## B01.CH07.P01.S01 — Definition of ΔM: monetary expansion/space (channels, accounting form, time window)

**ΔM** denotes the increment of monetary space or monetary expansion within a defined time window, regardless of the institutional form by
which it is realized.

### Definition components (minimum; binding)
- **Time window:** ΔM is always measured over an explicit period (e.g., month/quarter/year); comparisons require identical windows.
- **Accounting form:** ΔM may arise through balance-sheet expansion, liquidity provision, credit creation, asset purchases, guarantees, or other
  mechanisms that increase effective purchasing capacity or funding space.
- **Channel identity:** ΔM is not meaningful without its routing. Every ΔM instance is recorded as ΔM → channel → recipient class → end use
  (traceability requirement).
- **Netting discipline:** where relevant, ΔM reporting must clarify gross creation versus net effects after reversals/unwinds.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- ΔM that cannot be linked to a channel and end destination is treated as non-auditable and fails closed (no compatibility claims).

---

## B01.CH07.P01.S02 — ΔM as an order lever: why allocation determines the power structure (not “technical”, but constitutive)

ΔM determines order because it assigns **who receives funding space first**, under which terms, and with which constraints. This changes the
relative power of positions in the flow network (CH02).

### Order effects (minimum; binding)
- **Position reinforcement:** allocation can entrench gatekeepers (banks, platforms, asset holders) or can bypass them via direct commons
  channels.
- **Claim stabilization:** allocation can stabilize existing claim stacks (asset prices, collateral values) or can shift capacity toward
  production and maintenance.
- **Institution shaping:** sustained ΔM routing shapes institutional ecosystems (which systems grow, which become indispensable, which become
  capture points).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If ΔM allocation predictably strengthens private toll positions or asset appreciation as the primary transmission mechanism, it is treated as
  an order regression risk and must be constrained or prohibited.

---

## B01.CH07.P01.S03 — Relation to GROSS≈NET and E: how ΔM choices directly propagate into R/B and parasitic flows

ΔM routing propagates into household outcomes through prices, burdens, and the survival of extraction layers.

### Propagation pathways (minimum; binding)
- **R/B floor effects:** ΔM can stabilize R/B tails by funding commons baselines (lowering L), supporting transition buffers, and preventing
  arrears cascades.
- **E amplification risk:** ΔM routed into asset/market channels can inflate essentials prices (housing, land, energy bottlenecks), increasing L
  and recreating scarcity rents.
- **Fee-stack reinforcement:** credit-driven channels can generate intermediation fee ecosystems that persist as E even when nominal rates fall.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- ΔM is compatible only if its dominant, measured effect is (i) commons capacity and continuity, (ii) R/B floor stabilization, and (iii)
  measurable E reduction—not asset inflation and claim reinforcement.

## B01.CH07.P01.S04 — Legitimation: why ΔM must fall under order-democratic control (link to Book 3)

Because ΔM allocates order power, it requires explicit legitimation and contestable governance.

### Legitimation requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Mandate clarity:** objectives and constraints must be published (priority order, forbidden channels, cap compatibility).
- **Contestability:** decision rationales, channel choices, and exception uses must be challengeable via defined procedures.
- **Auditability:** independent audit functions must be able to trace and verify ΔM routing and outcomes; no black boxes.
- **Separation of powers:** decision, execution, audit, and appeal functions must be separated to prevent capture.

ΔM governance therefore belongs under the order-democratic architecture specified in Book 3: it is constitutive power that must be bounded by
rules, transparency, and contestability rather than insulated discretion.
