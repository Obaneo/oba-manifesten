# B01.CH07.P04 — Governance: who decides, which checks, which public accountability

This paragraph specifies the governance architecture for ΔM allocation. Because ΔM is constitutive power (CH07.P01), governance must prevent
three failure modes: (i) **opaque discretion** (“trust the experts”), (ii) **capture** (sector/vendor/bank dominance), and (iii) **emergency
drift** (temporary measures that become permanent asset support).

Governance is defined as a role separation plus a public accountability surface. The goal is not to maximize participation, but to ensure
decisions are rule-bound, contestable, and auditable while preserving execution speed in shocks.

---

## B01.CH07.P04.S01 — Decision bodies: role split (allocation body, audit body, commons governance, judicial review)

ΔM governance requires separation of function to prevent concentration of block power.

### Required role model (minimum; binding)
- **Allocation body (mandator):** sets priorities, selects channels, approves programs within the rulebook, and publishes rationales.
- **Execution operators:** implement allocations via templates (program delivery, procurement, disbursement) with tamper-evident logs.
- **Commons governance bodies:** steward commons funds/capex/maintenance with governance minima (access, maintenance-first budgets, contestability).
- **Audit body (independent):** verifies traceability, tests leakage, audits outcomes, and audits the auditors (meta-audit).
- **Judicial review / adjudicator:** provides binding resolution for disputes on legality, compatibility, and due process, without enabling
  indefinite delay tactics.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Any design where the same body controls allocation, execution, and audit is non-conforming (capture surface by design).

---

## B01.CH07.P04.S02 — Mandate and constraints: what is discretionary and what is rule-bound (fail-closed where necessary)

ΔM decisions must be bounded by an explicit mandate. Discretion exists only inside a constrained parameter space.

### Rule-bound elements (binding)
- **Goal hierarchy:** commons continuity/build-up → R/B floor → E reduction (CH07.P03).
- **Forbidden/risk channels:** asset-support reflex channels and privileged return guarantees are disallowed or presumptively non-compatible
  unless explicitly authorized under a narrow, audited exception rule.
- **Traceability requirements:** end-to-end logging and publishable aggregates are mandatory.
- **Minimal conditions:** cap compatibility, no new toll structures, measurability, and fail-closed readiness are mandatory.

### Discretionary space (bounded; admissible)
- choice among eligible proposals that pass minimal conditions,
- parameter tuning (scale, timing, geographic focus) within published bands,
- sequencing choices under a published priority matrix.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Under missing evidence, opacity, or unmeasurable routing, allocation pauses or is constrained; “we cannot measure but we proceed” is
  non-conforming.

---

## B01.CH07.P04.S03 — Checks & balances: vetoes, quorum rules, rotation, incompatibilities, transparency requirements

Checks and balances prevent capture and selective enforcement while preserving operational capacity.

### Required checks (minimum; binding)
- **Quorum and vote discipline:** defined decision thresholds for standard allocation vs emergency allocation vs exceptions.
- **Veto surfaces:** limited veto rights for audit body or adjudicator when minimal conditions are violated (traceability failure, forbidden
  channel, cap bypass).
- **Rotation and term limits:** rotation of key roles; avoid permanent incumbency in allocation/audit leadership.
- **Incompatibilities:** cooling-off and conflict-of-interest rules (no simultaneous vendor/sector roles, no revolving-door shortcuts).
- **Transparency requirements:** decision logs, exception registers, procurement summaries, and method/version references are published in
  aggregate.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If checks exist only “on paper” (no veto power, no publication, no rotation), governance is cosmetic and fails the anti-capture objective.

---

## B01.CH07.P04.S04 — Public accountability: publication duties, reporting cadence, exceptions regime (and why)

Public accountability is the mechanism that makes ΔM governance contestable and falsifiable. It is not PR.

### Required publication surface (minimum; binding)
- **Decision log:** what was decided, under which rule, with which rationale and expected mechanism (ex-ante hypothesis).
- **Channel reporting:** ΔM volumes by channel and recipient class (aggregated), including use-of-funds categories.
- **Outcome reporting:** ΔG/quality outputs, R/B distribution impacts (p10/p25/median; share_below_alpha), and E indicators where relevant.
- **Method discipline:** method_id, definition set IDs, revision policy, and uncertainty bands when proxies are used.
- **Exception register:** counts and categories of exceptions, duration, triggers, and sunsets; renewals require re-trigger evidence.

### Cadence (binding)
- **Periodic cadence:** regular reporting interval (e.g., quarterly) plus annual restatement discipline.
- **Event-driven cadence:** immediate reporting for shock regimes and emergency allocations.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Emergency allocations without a public trigger→action→outcome→sunset trail are non-conforming.

---

## B01.CH07.P04.S05 — Objection and appeal: a procedural route for contestation without blocking the system

Contestability must exist, but cannot become a paralysis tool. Procedures must be time-bounded and evidence-based.

### Required contestability design (minimum; binding)
- **Standing:** defined who may challenge (citizens/organizations/auditors/affected parties) and on which grounds (rule breach, leakage,
  opacity, cap bypass, selective enforcement).
- **Access to inputs:** challengers receive the relevant published methods, aggregated data, and decision rationale; classified data handling is
  defined but cannot become a blanket shield.
- **Time-bounded process:** deadlines for filing, response, and resolution; no infinite deferral.
- **Interim controls:** where plausibility of breach is high, interim pauses/escrow/constraints may apply to prevent irreversible harm.
- **Precedent and publication:** aggregated outcomes of challenges are published to build a stable rulebook over time.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A system that cannot be challenged is not legitimate; a system that can be blocked indefinitely is not executable. Governance must satisfy
  both constraints simultaneously.
