# B01.CH07.P03 — Allocation criteria: a priority matrix (commons build-up, R/B stabilization, E reduction)

This paragraph specifies the ex-ante criteria by which ΔM allocations are selected and ranked. The objective is to prevent discretionary,
politically-driven routing and to ensure ΔM functions as an order instrument: it must build durable capacity, stabilize household tails, and
accelerate E reduction—without recreating accumulation through asset inflation or privileged gate positions.

Allocation is governed by a priority hierarchy and a decision matrix. The hierarchy sets what ΔM is *for*. The matrix constrains how choices
are evaluated under uncertainty, time pressure, and shift risk.

---

## B01.CH07.P03.S01 — Goal hierarchy: (1) commons build-up/continuity, (2) stabilize the R/B floor, (3) accelerate E reduction

ΔM decisions must follow a fixed goal order to prevent drift into asset support or symbolic distribution.

### Goal hierarchy (binding)
1. **Commons build-up and continuity:** prioritize ΔG-positive investments and maintenance funding that expands reliable access to essentials and
   prevents degradation of core infrastructure.
2. **Stabilize the R/B floor:** reduce tail collapse and cascades (share_below_alpha, arrears/disconnections, time-to-recovery) through
   targeted buffers and burden reduction.
3. **Accelerate E reduction:** use ΔM to dismantle toll markets by financing substitution (commons/public options) and removing bottlenecks.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Allocations that do not measurably contribute to (1) or (2) cannot be justified as “ΔM for people” merely by aggregate growth or market
  stabilization claims.

---

## B01.CH07.P03.S02 — Minimal conditions: no bypass of W-MAX/I-MAX, no new toll structures, measurability is mandatory

Before any ranking, every candidate allocation must pass minimal admissibility checks.

### Minimal conditions (minimum; binding)
- **Cap compatibility:** no channel may materially reinforce W accumulation or create bypass structures around W-MAX/I-MAX.
- **No new toll structures:** allocations may not entrench gatekeepers, create guaranteed excess returns, or expand fee-stack ecosystems.
- **Traceability:** ΔM must be traceable end-to-end (creation → channel → recipient class → destination).
- **Measurability:** the intervention must define indicators, method_id, and revision discipline; black-box effects are disqualifying.
- **Fail-closed readiness:** under missing evidence or opacity, the allocation can be paused or constrained without collapsing essentials
  continuity.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If minimal conditions cannot be satisfied, the proposal is not eligible for ΔM allocation.

---

## B01.CH07.P03.S03 — Priority matrix: criteria axes (impact, speed, robustness, avoidance risk, maintenance burden)

Eligible proposals are ranked using a matrix that makes trade-offs explicit and auditable.

### Required matrix axes (minimum; binding)
- **Impact on ΔG + quality:** expected effect on commons capacity/coverage and service quality/continuity (not spending size).
- **Impact on R/B tails:** expected effect on p10/p25 R/B, share_below_alpha, arrears and recovery dynamics.
- **Impact on E mechanisms:** expected reduction of tolls/lock-in/fee-stacks (including displacement risk).
- **Speed to effect:** time-to-real-world impact on burdens and continuity (not time-to-announcement).
- **Robustness:** resilience to shocks and to implementation variance; dependence on fragile assumptions.
- **Avoidance risk:** likelihood that extraction shifts into new forms as a response; availability of automatic remedies.
- **Maintenance burden:** ongoing opex/maintenance requirements and the ability to fund and staff them (maintenance-first discipline).
- **Execution complexity:** governance and procurement complexity; capture surface expansion risk.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- High-impact projects that cannot be maintained (negative ΔG risk) or that expand capture surfaces are not “high impact”; they are deferred or
  redesigned.

---

## B01.CH07.P03.S04 — Targeting: why distribution profiles (p10/p25/median) are central rather than averages

ΔM allocations must be evaluated by distributional outcomes, not by averages or GDP-style aggregates.

### Targeting principles (binding)
- **Tails-first:** the primary success indicators are share_below_alpha and lower percentiles (p10/p25) of R/B.
- **Median as secondary:** median improvement matters, but cannot substitute for tail stabilization.
- **Avoid average masking:** average gains can coincide with tail worsening (price inflation, selective access, gate capture).
- **Segment visibility:** where ΔM is geographically or sectorally routed, effects must be measured in the relevant segments (within privacy
  rails), not only nationally aggregated.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If an allocation improves averages while worsening tails (or increasing essentials-L volatility), it fails the targeting logic even if it
  “stimulates the economy”.

---

## B01.CH07.P03.S05 — Ex-ante / ex-post test: simulation criteria up front; realized outcomes + correction path afterwards

ΔM governance must include both ex-ante justification and ex-post accountability, with a correction mechanism that is not discretionary.

### Ex-ante requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Hypothesis statement:** explicit expected mechanisms (ΔM → channel → commons/price/burden → R/B and E outcomes).
- **Indicator set:** pre-defined indicators, baselines, and expected effect bands; method_id and definition-set references.
- **Leakage tests:** explicit identification of where funds could leak into asset support, fee-stacks, or privileged returns.
- **Stop conditions:** pre-defined triggers for pausing or redesigning (e.g., asset-price spikes, lock-in growth, tail deterioration).

### Ex-post requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Outcome reporting:** realized effects on ΔG/quality, R/B distribution, and E indicators, including timing and lag structure.
- **Restatements and revisions:** transparent correction when methods or data change; no silent edits.
- **Correction path:** if outcomes breach stop conditions, re-route, unwind, or redesign is mandatory—no “continue and hope”.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A ΔM program without ex-ante leakage tests and ex-post correction rules is non-conforming because it cannot be falsified or controlled.
