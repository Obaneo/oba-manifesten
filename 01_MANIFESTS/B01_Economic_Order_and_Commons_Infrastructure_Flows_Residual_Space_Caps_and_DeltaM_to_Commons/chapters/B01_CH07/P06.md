# B01.CH07.P06 — Interaction with banks/financial sector: credit, collateral, risk models, repackaging

This paragraph specifies how the banking/financial sector interfaces with ΔM routing under OBA. Finance is treated as an allocation layer that
can either (i) deliver productive and commons-linked credit or (ii) regenerate accumulation and extraction through collateral bias, fee-stack
intermediation, and shadow claim circuits.

The design goal is to permit finance as a **service layer** where it is transparent and bounded, while blocking structural patterns that turn
ΔM into asset inflation, W concentration, and E regrowth.

---

## B01.CH07.P06.S01 — Credit as an allocation mechanism: how OBA steers credit toward production/commons rather than assets

Credit is admissible as a channel only if it is steered away from collateral-driven asset loops and toward production and commons capacity.

### Steering requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Use-of-funds tagging:** credit created or supported via ΔM must be tagged to admissible uses (productive capex, commons capex/opex,
  maintenance, restructuring that collapses E cascades).
- **Bounded returns:** margins, fees, and spreads must be bounded; junk-fee fragmentation is prohibited.
- **No demand-side asset support:** credit programs must not be designed primarily to raise asset prices (especially essentials like housing/land).
- **Contestable access:** eligibility rules must be auditable and must not create gatekeeping via opaque scoring or discriminatory proxies.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the dominant outcome of a credit program is asset appreciation or increased collateral leverage, the routing is non-conforming and must be
  reclassified as forbidden/risk channel behavior.

---

## B01.CH07.P06.S02 — Collateral regime: what counts as collateral, and how this can fuel accumulation and asset inflation

Collateral policy is a hidden constitution of credit allocation. It determines what assets become privileged accumulation carriers.

### Collateral discipline (minimum; binding)
- **Collateral classification:** define eligible collateral categories and apply a bias toward productive assets and commons-linked claims rather
  than scarce essentials and speculative assets.
- **Haircuts and limits:** apply conservative haircuts and concentration limits to reduce procyclical leverage and asset bubbles.
- **Essentials protection:** prevent the collateral regime from inflating essentials (housing/land) by amplifying credit supply into scarce
  markets.
- **Look-through exposure:** where collateral is routed through vehicles, apply economic exposure/UBC look-through to prevent concentration
  bypass.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- A collateral regime that predictably inflates scarce essentials or concentrates W is incompatible with ΔM-to-commons objectives and must be
  redesigned.

## B01.CH07.P06.S03 — Risk models: model risk, pro-cyclicality, and required transparency

Risk models are a primary source of hidden gate power. They can enforce procyclical credit rationing and legitimize opaque exclusion.

### Risk-model requirements (minimum; binding)
- **Transparency:** model inputs, objective functions, and constraint sets must be auditable (at least to regulators/auditors) and not fully
  proprietary black boxes.
- **Procyclicality controls:** explicit countercyclical rules to prevent simultaneous contraction in downturns that worsens R/B tails.
- **Fairness without identity profiling:** enforce neutrality on subject positions (flows/positions), while still preventing discriminatory
  outcomes via auditable proxy tests and appeal routes.
- **Appeal and explanation:** affected parties must have a contestable path to challenge decisions; “model says no” is not sufficient.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If risk allocation is determined by non-auditable models or vendor-locked scoring, the channel becomes a capture surface and fails closed.

## B01.CH07.P06.S04 — Repackaging: securitization, fee-stacks, shadow banking — detection and constraints

Repackaging allows extraction to return as layered claims and intermediation fees even when nominal credit is “cheap”.

### Constraints (minimum; binding)
- **Securitization discipline:** where allowed, require full disclosure of cashflow structure, fees, servicing rights, and risk retention.
- **Fee-stack caps:** cap cumulative fee shares across origination–servicing–collection chains; prohibit fee cascades that function as E.
- **Shadow conduit restrictions:** constrain off-balance-sheet vehicles that recreate leverage and obscure beneficial control.
- **Chain attribution:** apply CH02 chain attribution to identify where E accumulates in the stack.

### Detection triggers (minimum; binding)
- sudden growth in securitized volumes in targeted domains,
- rising servicing/collection fee shares,
- proliferation of intermediaries and side letters,
- opacity in ultimate beneficial control or economic exposure.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Under opacity or missing disclosures, apply conservative presumptions and restrict the product/channel until auditable.

## B01.CH07.P06.S05 — Banks as a service layer: conditions under which banks may serve commons capex/opex

Banks may participate as operators for commons financing only if they remain service providers rather than gatekeepers extracting tolls.

### Service-layer conditions (minimum; binding)
- **Standardized interfaces:** common contract templates, open reporting formats, and portability across operators.
- **Bounded margins and fees:** regulated service fees; no hidden spreads, steering commissions, or bundling.
- **No exclusive control:** avoid single-bank dependence; ensure plurality and the ability to reassign servicing without disruption.
- **Audit integration:** banks must provide end-to-end logs and accept audit rights; refusal triggers loss of eligibility.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If a bank’s role creates lock-in, fee-stacks, or selective access beyond published rules, it becomes an E layer and must be requalified or
  replaced.

## B01.CH07.P06.S06 — Exits and resolution: what happens under non-compliance or systemic risk (no bailouts that break caps)

OBA requires predefined exit and resolution regimes that preserve continuity without converting crises into accumulation support.

### Required exit/resolution rules (minimum; binding)
- **No bailouts that break caps:** support measures may not stabilize private W concentration or guarantee privileged returns.
- **Continuity-first resolution:** protect essential payment rails and commons financing continuity while imposing losses on claim stacks where
  appropriate.
- **Reassignment capability:** ability to move servicing/execution to alternative operators (plurality prerequisite).
- **Sanction path:** loss of eligibility, forced restructuring, or resolution actions under non-compliance with transparency and fee bounds.
- **Emergency discipline:** if temporary stabilization is required, it must have a published trigger, scope caps, sunset, and unwind plan.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If crisis response defaults to asset-price support and claim preservation, ΔM governance regresses and the system fails the compatibility
  test.
