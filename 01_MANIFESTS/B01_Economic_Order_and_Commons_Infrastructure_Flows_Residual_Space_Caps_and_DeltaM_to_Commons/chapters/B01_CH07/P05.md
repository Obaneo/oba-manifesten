# B01.CH07.P05 — Anti-capture rails: auditability, plurality, transparent rules, fail-closed principles

This paragraph specifies the minimum anti-capture rails required for ΔM governance to remain compatible over time. Capture is treated as a
default risk because ΔM concentrates incentives: sectors and intermediaries will seek privileged routing, exceptions, and opaque channels.
Anti-capture is therefore not an ethical aspiration; it is an engineering constraint on decision systems.

The rails below define (i) what must be traceable, (ii) how monopoly over execution is prevented, (iii) how semantic drift is blocked, and
(iv) how the system responds under uncertainty without granting free passes.

---

## B01.CH07.P05.S01 — Auditability: full trace from ΔM → channel → recipient → final destination (no black boxes)

Auditability is the non-negotiable foundation: without traceability, ΔM becomes discretionary power.

### Required trace elements (minimum; binding)
- **Creation event record:** time window, accounting form, and legal authority for the ΔM instance.
- **Channel classification:** state / bank-credit / market-asset / direct commons, plus sub-channel identifiers.
- **Recipient class:** the immediate recipient category (commons fund, public agency, bank facility, operator, contractor class).
- **Destination mapping:** end-use categories (capex/opex/maintenance, buffer/stabilizer, restructuring, procurement lot), with linkage to
  outputs.
- **Integrity logs:** tamper-evident logs for decisions, disbursements, procurement changes, and revisions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Trace must be end-to-end. Partial traces (“we know volumes, not destinations”) are treated as non-auditable.
- Proprietary models and non-reproducible allocation logic are disallowed as decisive black boxes.

---

## B01.CH07.P05.S02 — Plurality: multiple execution routes to prevent vendor/sector capture

Capture often occurs by monopolizing the execution layer (single vendors, single banks, single platform rails). Plurality is the structural
countermeasure.

### Required plurality measures (minimum; binding)
- **Multiple implementers:** avoid single-provider dependence for critical systems; support multiple operators where feasible.
- **Interoperability by design:** open standards and portability to prevent switching barriers and lock-in at the infrastructure layer.
- **Procurement diversity:** disallow procurement structures that enforce single-vendor lock-in through proprietary interfaces or closed data.
- **Fallback capacity:** maintain public/commons fallback execution capability for critical domains (so “vendor exit” is possible).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If plurality cannot be maintained (due to tooling, contracts, or capacity), the system must treat the domain as high capture risk and apply
  tightened constraints or restructure the execution design.

---

## B01.CH07.P05.S03 — Transparent rules: pre-specified allocation rules, public definitions, version control

Capture proceeds through semantic manipulation: exceptions, changing definitions, and shifting criteria. Transparency and version control are
the countermeasure.

### Required transparency discipline (minimum; binding)
- **Pre-specified rules:** published goal hierarchy, channel admissibility, selection criteria, and stop conditions.
- **Public definitions:** stable public definitions for categories used in ΔM decisions (recipient classes, end-use categories, “commons”
  qualification).
- **Version control:** changelog of rule updates, rationale, backward compatibility notes, and restatement policy where outputs change.
- **Exception discipline:** public exception categories, triggers, and sunset rules; no silent carve-outs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If rules change without publication and versioning, governance is drifting and loses compatibility status.

---

## B01.CH07.P05.S04 — Fail-closed: under uncertainty/missing data, no release; temporary stop and re-evaluation

Fail-closed prevents the “uncertainty loophole” where opacity becomes a route to privileged allocation.

### Required fail-closed mechanics (minimum; binding)
- **Eligibility gating:** proposals lacking minimal evidence (traceability, cap compatibility, leakage test) are not eligible.
- **Temporary stops:** when indicators breach thresholds (asset-price spikes, lock-in growth, missing logs), pause or constrain releases.
- **Conservative presumptions:** under obstruction, treat the outcome as high-risk and apply stricter constraints rather than permissive routing.
- **Re-evaluation protocol:** defined time-bounded re-assessment procedure with published results and correction actions.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Under uncertainty, the system must not proceed by discretion. Proceeding without evidence is a compatibility breach.

---

## B01.CH07.P05.S05 — Anti-lobby rails: contact register, cooling-off, disclosure of interests, sanctions

Lobbying and revolving doors are primary capture vectors in allocation systems.

### Required anti-lobby rails (minimum; binding)
- **Contact register:** publish meetings and communications related to ΔM routing decisions (aggregated where privacy/security requires).
- **Disclosure of interests:** mandatory disclosures for decision-makers and key staff; conflict-of-interest enforcement.
- **Cooling-off rules:** time-bound restrictions on role switches between regulated sectors/vendors and allocation/audit bodies.
- **Sanctions:** enforceable penalties for undisclosed conflicts, prohibited contacts, or shadow influence channels.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If influence channels are opaque, the system is not contestable and cannot claim legitimacy for ΔM routing.

---

## B01.CH07.P05.S06 — Red-teaming and stress tests: periodic attack simulations on allocation channels and measurement rules

Anti-capture must be continuously tested because adversaries adapt. Red-teaming formalizes this.

### Required testing regime (minimum; binding)
- **Attack simulations:** simulate legal/technical/financial/international shift routes that attempt to turn ΔM into asset support or privileged
  returns.
- **Measurement stress tests:** test whether published indicators can be gamed (Goodhart), whether proxies fail under shocks, and where data
  gaps create loopholes.
- **Capture drills:** simulate vendor lock-in, audit suppression, exception explosions, and emergency drift scenarios.
- **Remediation plans:** for every failed test, publish corrective actions, deadlines, and re-test requirements.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the system refuses adversarial testing or does not remediate failed tests, it is drifting toward capture and must fail closed until
  corrected.
