# B03.CH06.P01 — Purpose and delimitation: movement as an implementation interface, not as an ideology

This paragraph defines the **movement layer** in Book 3 as a **governance and implementation interface**: the organisational,
coalitional, and operational capacity required to translate OBA order criteria into **durable democratic practice** under
continuous pressure from incentives, shocks, and strategic counter-moves. The movement is treated as an **instrumental system**
that protects **mandate discipline**, **institutional independence**, and **contestability**, rather than as a doctrine, identity,
or partisan worldview.

The chapter’s purpose is to prevent a recurring failure mode: **order institutions** (caps, commons governance, audit regimes,
allocation rules) are adopted formally but become **unenforced**, **exception-stacked**, or **captured** when the political support
base cannot sustain monitoring, staffing, legitimacy, and escalation. Therefore, Book 3 specifies movement mechanics as part of
the OBA implementation surface.

---

## B03.CH06.P01.S01 — Why order institutions without a support base tend to trigger regression

Order-democratic institutions impose **binding constraints** (caps, non-stackable exceptions, auditability, no black-box ΔM),
which predictably generate concentrated opposition from actors holding **claim and veto positions**. Without a support base that
can maintain **public understanding**, **staffing capacity**, **monitoring**, and **escalation**, the system drifts into familiar
regression paths:

- **Enforcement decay**: sanction rate ↓, enforcement latency ↑, selective non-enforcement.
- **Exception normalisation**: “temporary” carve-outs renew and stack until invariants are hollowed out.
- **Semantic drift**: definitions shift in practice (or in tooling/procurement) without a visible change process.
- **Capture through logistics**: institutions become dependent on vendors, experts, or intermediaries that control access to
  measurement, implementation, or narrative.

A movement is therefore required not to “win debates”, but to keep the order system **operationally defensible**: it supplies
the legitimacy, personnel pipelines, and watchdog capacity that make hard constraints politically survivable.

---

## B03.CH06.P01.S02 — Delimitation: this chapter describes organisation and coalition dynamics, not policy detail

This chapter does **not** specify sector policy, ideological objectives, or a party programme. It specifies the **organisational
grammar** that allows any OBA-conform programme to remain:

- **Test-axis disciplined** (E / caps / commons-ΔG / R/B / ΔM),
- **Evidence-bounded** (public definition set, reproducibility, audit logs),
- **Contestable** (objection/appeal, independent audit),
- **Anti-capture** (disclosure, rotation, vendor exitability, funding hygiene).

Policy content and parameter choices (thresholds, pacing, prioritisation) remain elsewhere (Book 1, Book 2, and the Kernel Spec);
this chapter constrains how actors coordinate so that those choices do not degrade the constitutive rails.

---

## B03.CH06.P01.S03 — Relation to Book 2: transition conflict versus durable movement

Book 2 focuses on **transition mechanics**: sequencing, instruments, crisis handling, and implementation pathways. This chapter
adds the **political-sociological layer** that determines whether a transition survives contact with:

- **short-term loss concentration** (visible costs before benefits),
- **swing-group dynamics** (status-loss anxiety, timing asymmetry),
- **elite counter-moves** (legal obstruction, credit tightening, narrative attacks),
- **institutional sabotage** (bureaucratic delay, procurement lock-in, measurement blackouts).

In other words: Book 2 provides the toolkit; Book 3 (here) specifies the **support system** that keeps the toolkit usable over
time without collapsing into exception politics.

---

## B03.CH06.P01.S04 — Output: machine-readable building blocks for coalition, organisational, and channel design

The deliverable of this chapter is a set of **re-usable building blocks** that can be instantiated across jurisdictions and
contexts without redefining OBA. These blocks include:

- **Subject-position templates** (actor roles, exposures, incentives, veto points).
- **Coalition architecture patterns** (core/swing/fault-line mapping; erosion signals).
- **Organisational form modules** (party/union/commons association/network federation/dual structure).
- **Support infrastructure requirements** (knowledge tooling, funding disclosure, training pipelines, media resilience).
- **Operational loops** for governance: monitoring → escalation → rollback/re-authorisation under drift.

These outputs are intended to be **portable and testable**: they define what must exist (and what must be observable) for a
movement to qualify as a credible implementation interface rather than a rhetorical wrapper.
