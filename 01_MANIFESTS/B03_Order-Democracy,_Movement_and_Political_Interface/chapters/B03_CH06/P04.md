# B03.CH06.P04 — Organisational forms: structures for coordination and continuity

This paragraph defines the organisational forms through which an OBA-aligned movement can persist long enough to translate
order criteria into mandates, institutions, and commons practice without collapsing into capture, fragmentation, or episodic
mobilisation. The central requirement is **continuity under pressure**: the organisation must survive electoral cycles,
crisis windows, media shocks, infiltration attempts, and internal conflict—while maintaining **language discipline** (test axes),
**mandate discipline** (testable commitments), and **evidence discipline** (auditability, logs, and version control).

No single organisational form satisfies all requirements. OBA therefore treats organisational design as a **portfolio problem**:
different forms specialise in distinct interfaces (electoral, labour, commons governance, local execution, technical replication).
The forms below are described as **functional modules** with identifiable failure modes and required rails.

---

## B03.CH06.P04.S01 — Party form: electoral interface, mandate discipline, capture risk

The party form is the primary interface with electoral mandate granting. Its value is **agenda-setting**, **legislative control**,
and the ability to bind the state to a public programme. Its central risk is **capture via funding, candidate pipelines, and
message discipline** that drifts into slogans rather than testable commitments.

Functional strengths:
- Converts order criteria into **mandate text** with timelines, thresholds, and hard stops.
- Controls appointments and institutional setup (audit/oversight design, procurement rules).
- Provides a single accountable channel for “who promised what.”

Failure modes:
- Donor and vendor capture (quiet carve-outs; procurement lock-in; black-box tooling).
- Semantic drift (“OBA language” without enforcement or contestability).
- Short-termism: sacrificing invariants for coalition management.

Required rails:
- Public mandate scorecards; defined exception rules; disclosure and rotation regimes; procurement exitability; independent audit
of mandate execution.

---

## B03.CH06.P04.S02 — Union/professional association: leverage via labour and expertise

Unions and professional associations provide **structural leverage** (work stoppage capacity, bargaining power, sectoral
knowledge) and an **integrity backbone** for execution-heavy institutions. They are crucial where OBA implementation depends on
high-trust professional work: inspection, care, education, infrastructure operations, auditing.

Functional strengths:
- Enforcement capacity through **workplace power** and procedural knowledge.
- Early detection of sabotage, measurement blackouts, and façade compliance.
- Training and standards for roles that require discretion under evidence discipline.

Failure modes:
- Insider capture (protecting incumbents, blocking transparency, trading exceptions for privileges).
- Sectoral parochialism (defending rent positions within the union/profession).
- Politicisation that replaces test axes with identity blocs.

Required rails:
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure; separation between bargaining and audit roles; public reporting of integrity incidents;
alignment to open calculation rules and contestability requirements.

---

## B03.CH06.P04.S03 — Commons association/cooperative: governance and access with stewardship

Commons associations/cooperatives are the organisational home for **access governance**, **maintenance discipline**, and the
translation of “commons substitution” into operational reality. Their legitimacy comes from transparent boundary rules, service
minima, and measurable ΔG maintenance outcomes.

Functional strengths:
- Creates visible “living proof” via ΔG improvements and service continuity.
- Provides durable governance channels for users and stewards (complaints, prioritisation, escalation).
- Anchors non-discrimination and anti-gating rules in concrete practice.

Failure modes:
- Façade commons (closed access under commons language; private toll gates reintroduced).
- Capture by operators/vendors; governance fatigue; participation inequality.
- Under-maintenance and backlog drift (commons decay).

Required rails:
- Decision logs; maintenance ring-fencing; open dashboards; contestable allocation; procurement exitability; hard stops for
black-box scoring or gating innovations.

---

## B03.CH06.P04.S04 — Network federation: local cells with standards and interoperability

Network federations provide **scale without central brittleness**: local cells can act, learn, and adapt while remaining
compatible via shared standards (definitions, metrics, disclosure formats, and escalation protocols). This form is essential for
distributed implementation and resilience against repression or leadership failure.

Functional strengths:
- High redundancy and survivability: local continuity even if a national layer fractures.
- Faster feedback loops: local measurement, incident reporting, and proof of implementation.
- Interoperable expansion: new cells join by adopting standard interfaces.

Failure modes:
- Fragmentation and semantic drift (cells diverge, “OBA” becomes ambiguous).
- Local capture (strongmen, vendor lock-in, local scapegoating).
- Coordination collapse under crisis (no shared decision protocols).

Required rails:
- Shared compatibility metadata; version-pinned definition sets; federation charters with non-negotiables; fork protocol usage;
cross-audit between cells; common incident taxonomy and public changelog discipline.

---

## B03.CH06.P04.S05 — Dual structure: public movement + technical core (audit/measurement expertise)

The dual structure separates **political mobilisation** from **technical replication and audit capability**. The public movement
specialises in coalition building, communication, and mandate pressure; the technical core specialises in definitions, measurement,
replication tooling, and detection of drift/capture. The separation exists to prevent technical authority from becoming political
unaccountability, while also preventing politics from eroding evidence discipline.

Functional strengths:
- Maintains epistemic continuity across electoral cycles and crises.
- Enables “show-your-work” governance: open calculation rules, reproducible audits, restatements.
- Detects OBA-washing early and supports hard-stop triggers with evidence.

Failure modes:
- Technocratic overreach (technical core becomes an unaccountable veto player).
- Political hollowing (movement ignores the technical core when inconvenient).
- Capture of tooling (vendor dependence; black-box measurement regimes).

Required rails:
- Clear role separation; open standards; second implementations; transparent governance of the technical core; public interfaces
for contestability (appeal, independent review); mandatory disclosure of funding and conflicts for technical maintainers.
