# B03.CH06.P07 — Interaction with institutions: how the movement safeguards “order” after victory

This paragraph specifies how an OBA-aligned movement must interact with executive institutions, audit bodies, commons governance,
and courts *after* achieving electoral or political success. The core risk is that “victory” converts the movement into an
incumbent machine that drifts into **exception politics**, **black-box allocation**, **vendor capture**, or **semantic drift**.
Therefore this paragraph defines the post-victory interface as an **order-preservation protocol**: mandates must be translated into
testable assignments, staffing must be anti-capture by design, and implementation must remain contestable and auditable.

---

## B03.CH06.P07.S01 — Mandate translation: election win → testable assignment (KPIs + rails)

An electoral mandate is not an operational specification. To prevent mandate laundering (“we were elected, therefore whatever we do
is legitimate”), the movement must translate political goals into **testable assignments** that preserve the constitutive rails.

### Translation rule (binding)
- Every mandate promise MUST be mapped to the **test axes** (E / caps / commons-ΔG / R/B / ΔM) with:
  - a measurable target (or explicitly “unknown” with a measurement plan),
  - a definition-set reference (Kernel Spec version),
  - an audit method and logging requirements,
  - a timeline with decision points and hard stops,
  - an exception profile (allowed scope + sunsets + rollback requirements).

### KPI discipline (anti-gaming)
- KPIs MUST be paired with:
  - uncertainty bands,
  - red-flag indicators (exception ratio↑, sanction rate↓, reproducibility↓),
  - “unknown discipline” (fail-closed for key invariants if measurement is degraded).

Failure modes:
- “Values mandate” used to bypass auditability.
- Parametric choices disguised as constitutive changes.
- KPI theatre: dashboards that cannot be reproduced externally.

---

## B03.CH06.P07.S02 — Staffing and execution: competence, integrity, capacity (anti–revolving doors)

Implementation quality determines whether OBA becomes a façade. Post-victory staffing must therefore be treated as an **order
instrument** with explicit anti-capture rails.

### Staffing requirements (binding)
- Key roles in execution, audit, and oversight MUST have:
  - competence criteria (domain, measurement, enforcement),
  - integrity criteria (conflict-of-interest rules, disclosure duties),
  - capacity criteria (staffing levels that prevent backlog-driven exceptions).

### Anti–revolving doors rails
- Cooling-off periods MUST apply for high-risk role transitions (vendor ↔ regulator/auditor; lobby ↔ oversight).
- Procurement-facing and audit-facing roles MUST be protected from:
  - performance incentives tied to sanction volume or budget extraction,
  - dependency on vendors for core calculation rules (no black-box enforcement).

Failure modes:
- “Friendly” appointments that reduce sanction rate and expand exceptions.
- Vendor dependence that makes audit impossible without supplier permission.
- Understaffing used as justification for permanent discretionary enforcement.

---

## B03.CH06.P07.S03 — Monitoring: public scorecards, sanction rate, exception ratio

Post-victory legitimacy is sustained by **continuous contestable monitoring**, not by rhetoric. Monitoring must be public enough to
detect drift, but structured to avoid gaming and scapegoating.

### Monitoring minimum (binding)
- Public scorecards MUST include:
  - E trajectory (and where extraction moved),
  - cap integrity signals (carve-outs, threshold tricks, enforcement selectivity),
  - commons quality/ΔG metrics (maintenance backlog, access continuity),
  - ΔM channel distribution (no black boxes; prohibited channels),
  - enforcement indicators: sanction rate, enforcement latency, recidivism,
  - exception indicators: exception ratio, renewals, selectivity.

### Replicability requirement
- Scorecards MUST be reproducible from published definition sets and logged inputs (privacy-safe aggregation allowed, but audit
  access must exist).

Failure modes:
- Closed dashboards (“trust us”) → black-box politics.
- Monitoring used for propaganda rather than correction.
- Selective visibility: publishing only favourable indicators.

---

## B03.CH06.P07.S04 — Correction: rollback demands, hard stops, reauthorisation under drift

Monitoring without correction is theatre. The movement must retain a correction posture even toward “its own” government. The
correction toolkit is hierarchical: rollback first, then hard stops, then reauthorisation.

### Correction ladder (binding)
- If drift signals cross thresholds (exceptions↑, sanction rate↓, reproducibility↓, black-box allocation):
  1) **Rollback demand**: reverse the implicated decision/contract/tooling change; restore prior auditable baseline.
  2) **Hard stop trigger**: suspend allocation/decision execution where measurability/auditability is compromised.
  3) **Reauthorisation requirement**: if deviation is constitutive (non-negotiables impacted), a new mandate is REQUIRED.

### Non-suspensive defaults
- Objections and disputes MUST not be allowed to paralyse enforcement when regression risk is high; any suspensive effect must be
explicit and justified under proportionality and evidence standards.

Failure modes:
- “Temporary emergency” becomes permanent exception stack.
- Drift is rationalised as pragmatism while invariants erode.
- Hard stops avoided to protect incumbency reputation.

---

## B03.CH06.P07.S05 — Commons interface: governance seats, steward training, conflict escalation

The movement’s post-victory interface includes a specific obligation to protect commons-democracy from being hollowed out via gating,
façade governance, or maintenance neglect.

### Interface requirements (binding)
- The movement MUST support:
  - representation pathways into commons boards (with rotation and disclosure),
  - steward training in boundary rules, logging, and contestability,
  - escalation channels for access disputes, prioritisation conflicts, and maintenance failure,
  - procurement rails to prevent vendor lock-in inside commons operations.

### Conflict escalation integration
- Commons conflicts MUST be handled through the standard escalation architecture:
  complaint → mediation → arbitration/tribunal → judicial review, with evidence discipline (logs, metrics, decision rationale).

Failure modes:
- Commons used as a label while access is privately gated.
- Maintenance decay masked by narrative (“budget crisis”) while exceptions accumulate.
- Governance captured via board stacking or vendor dependence.
