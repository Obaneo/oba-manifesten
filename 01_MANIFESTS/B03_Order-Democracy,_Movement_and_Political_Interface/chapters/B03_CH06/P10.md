# B03.CH06.P10 — Evaluation: movement as a repeatable system (not a one-off campaign)

This paragraph focuses on the **evaluation** of movement progress, emphasizing that movements should be **sustainable** and **repeatable**, rather than ephemeral, and should incorporate systems for **long-term monitoring** and **adaptation**. The goal is to track whether a movement remains true to its invariants while scaling and evolving, and whether the movement can be **institutionalised** in ways that align with OBA principles.

It also outlines **evaluation metrics** to track growth, retention, training, and the integrity of funding sources. **Incident reviews** will focus on issues like infiltration, drift, and reputational risks. The section concludes by describing how to ensure **version control** over strategies, and how to facilitate a **handover protocol** between movements, institutions, and commons governance.

---

## B03.CH06.P10.S01 — Metrics: growth, retention, training, funding integrity, channel resilience

Movement evaluation is deeply tied to **long-term sustainability**, and requires clear metrics to track the movement’s **growth**, **retention** of key participants, and its **financial integrity**. Tracking the **resilience** of channels used for coordination (e.g., media, communication) is also critical.

### Metrics considerations:
- **Growth**: Increase in participation, scope, or impact over time (number of members, geographic spread, etc.).
- **Retention**: The movement's ability to keep core members engaged over the long term, preventing attrition and burnout.
- **Training**: Ensuring that members are consistently trained in **OBA** principles and **movement infrastructure**.
- **Funding integrity**: Monitoring sources of funding for transparency, accountability, and resistance to capture.
- **Channel resilience**: How resilient are the movement’s communication and coordination channels in the face of external pressures?

### Conformance requirements (binding):
- Any growth in the movement MUST align with **OBA principles**, and its financial sources must comply with **anti-capture measures**.
- Retention rates MUST be tracked and include measures for addressing **dissatisfaction or misalignment** with core principles.

---

## B03.CH06.P10.S02 — Incident reviews: infiltration attempts, drift, reputational risks

An important aspect of long-term movement sustainability is managing and **reviewing incidents**. This includes **infiltration attempts**, **drift** (internal deviation from OBA principles), and **reputational risks**.

### Incident types:
- **Infiltration attempts**: External forces or interests attempting to infiltrate and alter the movement’s direction for capture.
- **Drift**: Subtle or overt changes in the movement’s direction, especially as it grows or adapts.
- **Reputational risks**: Any damage to the movement’s reputation that may compromise its legitimacy and ability to align with core values.

### Conformance requirements (binding):
- **Incident reports** must be generated for each type of event and be reviewed regularly.
- **Drift** must be addressed through **re-alignment strategies** (e.g., reaffirming OBA principles, rolling back harmful changes).
- **Reputational risks** must be managed through **transparency and accountability** mechanisms to ensure no compromise on **OBA legitimacy**.

---

## B03.CH06.P10.S03 — Version control: strategy adjustments with traceability

For a movement to evolve **sustainably**, there must be formal controls for **versioning** movement strategies and **adapting** them in response to external challenges, growth, or changing circumstances.

### Key elements:
- **Version control**: The movement must track **strategy changes** with full transparency and traceability to prevent misalignments with the OBA framework.
- **Strategic adjustments**: Regular, structured reviews of the movement’s goals, tactics, and alignment with OBA principles.
- **Traceability**: All strategy shifts must be **documented**, with clear explanations of why the changes were made and what **impact analysis** was performed.

### Conformance requirements (binding):
- Each strategic change MUST be linked to **OBA principles** and documented in a **traceable changelog**.
- Movement leaders must **explain the reasoning** behind any shift in direction and demonstrate **compatibility** with OBA’s non-negotiables.

---

## B03.CH06.P10.S04 — Handover: movement → institutions → commons governance (handover protocols)

As the movement matures, there may be a **handover** of responsibilities and decision-making powers from the grassroots level to more formal **institutions** or structures. This process must be **carefully managed** to avoid misalignments or breakdowns in the OBA framework.

### Handover considerations:
- **Movement → Institutions**: As the movement grows, certain functions (policy decisions, funding oversight, etc.) will be transferred to formal institutions.
- **Institutions → Commons governance**: In the end, the goal is to ensure that the movement is integrated into **commons governance** to become part of the broader democratic structure.
- **Handover protocol**: A formal process must be defined for ensuring the seamless transition of power without undermining core OBA principles.

### Conformance requirements (binding):
- Any handover MUST be governed by **clear protocols** that ensure full alignment with **OBA non-negotiables**.
- The **handover process** must be reviewed by independent auditors to ensure **accountability** and **public transparency**.

---

## B03.CH06.P10.S05 — Link to the democratic consistency test (B03.CH12): inputs and thresholds

Finally, the movement must be continuously evaluated against the **Democratic Consistency Test** (see B03.CH12). The **inputs** (data, strategies, outcomes) and **thresholds** for democratic legitimacy are essential to ensure that the movement remains aligned with its principles as it scales.

### Inputs and thresholds:
- **Inputs**: The movement’s activities, policies, and decisions must be regularly evaluated using **objective criteria** derived from **OBA principles**.
- **Thresholds**: A set of **thresholds** must be established for what constitutes **OBA-consistency** and **democratic legitimacy**.

### Conformance requirements (binding):
- **Regular evaluations** MUST be performed to assess whether the movement continues to meet the **Democratic Consistency Test** criteria.
- Any failure to meet the thresholds for **OBA consistency** must trigger an **escalation process** and a re-evaluation of movement strategy.
