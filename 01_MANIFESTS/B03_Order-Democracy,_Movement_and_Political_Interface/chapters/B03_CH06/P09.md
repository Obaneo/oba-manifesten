# B03.CH06.P09 — Internal conflicts: schisms, orthodoxy, and fork dynamics inside the movement

This paragraph defines **internal conflict** as a predictable systemic risk within an OBA-aligned movement. Because OBA is anchored in **constitutive rails** (caps, commons, auditability, contestability, anti-avoidance), internal disagreements are not merely “political differences”; they can become **order-critical** if they attempt to weaken invariants, normalise exceptions, or reframe the movement back into legacy left/right scripts.

The paragraph provides a conflict typology (ideological, strategic, capture-related), and it defines the minimum governance needed to prevent internal disputes from degrading into **semantic drift**, **façade unity**, or uncontrolled fragmentation. It also provides an **internal fork protocol** and **reintegration conditions** so that plurality remains compatible with OBA’s non-negotiables.

---

## B03.CH06.P09.S01 — Ideological drift: relapse into left/right instead of order axes

Movements drift when internal discourse reverts from OBA’s **order grammar** (E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM, measurability, contestability) to legacy **left/right** positioning. This drift turns invariant rails into identity markers and reintroduces “camp loyalty” dynamics that are structurally easy to capture.

### Drift signatures
- Debates framed as **identity loyalty** (“who are we?”) rather than **test axes** (“what is the extraction and how is it measured?”).
- Policy disputes argued via **symbolic alignment** rather than **compatibility constraints** and evidence.
- Labelling internal opponents as traitors/heretics without specifying **which invariant is threatened**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Internal deliberation MUST default to the **order axes** and MUST explicitly name which invariants are implicated.
- Any proposed change MUST be formulated as a **testable delta** (inputs/outputs/thresholds), not as ideological rhetoric.
- Where discourse cannot be translated into the order grammar, it MUST be treated as **non-operational** (no decision basis).

---

## B03.CH06.P09.S02 — Strategy conflict: speed versus coalition retention; hard versus soft sequencing

Even when actors share the same invariants, they will disagree on **sequencing** and **tempo**. This is a legitimate internal tension: fast moves risk coalition breakage; slow moves risk regression and capture.

### Typical conflict pairs
- **Hard sequencing**: immediate enforcement, rapid cap binding, aggressive anti-avoidance.
- **Soft sequencing**: phased implementation, larger buffers for swing groups, gradual institutional ramp-up.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Strategy disputes MUST be treated as **parametric conflicts** unless they imply weakening non-negotiables.
- Sequencing proposals MUST include:
  - **Pacing plan** (timeline + milestones),
  - **coalition risk model** (swing-group exposure, compensations),
  - **hard stops** (conditions under which slowing becomes regression or speeding becomes destabilisation).
- No sequencing choice MAY introduce **stackable exceptions** or **black-box allocation** as a “temporary” shortcut.

## B03.CH06.P09.S03 — Capture conflict: “pragmatic” exceptions versus invariant protection

Internal capture conflict appears when a faction argues for “pragmatic” exceptions that, in practice, erode the rails: carve-outs, opaque tools, vendor dependency, selective enforcement, or emergency normalisation. This conflict is the movement’s most dangerous because it can mimic realism while implementing hollowing-out.

### Capture signatures
- “Temporary” measures with **no sunsets**, or sunsets that are routinely renewed.
- Exceptions justified without **impact registration**, without **rollback plan**, or without **audit access**.
- Appeals to “governability” used to block contestability and evidence disclosure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any exception proposal MUST include **sunset + rollback + non-stackability + impact registration**.
- If auditability, contestability, or definition stability is weakened, the proposal MUST be treated as **constitutive**, not pragmatic.
- If evidence is missing, the movement MUST apply a **fail-closed** stance: treat the exception as non-conform until proven otherwise.

## B03.CH06.P09.S04 — Internal fork protocol: when separation is legitimate

When internal conflict crosses from parametric disagreements into constitutive deviation (rails, definitions, auditability), the movement requires a formal fork protocol to prevent “shadow variants” and semantic blending.

### Fork triggers
- A faction seeks to weaken **caps bindingness**, allow **structural carve-outs**, or tolerate **permanent exception regimes**.
- A faction rejects **contestability** (appeal rights, evidence access) or demands **closed computation**.
- A faction changes **definitions** outside canonical governance (Kernel Spec references).

### Fork protocol (minimum)
- **Declaration**: explicit statement of the contested rail(s) and the proposed deviation.
- **Classification**: variant (parametric) vs fork (constitutive) vs incompatibility.
- **Labeling**: mandatory public name label consistent with compatibility rules.
- **Asset separation**: governance, funds, tooling, and registries separated to prevent confusion and capture-by-infrastructure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No faction MAY continue to use an OBA status label while deviating on non-negotiables without declaring compatibility status.
- Any fork MUST publish a **delta map** against the canonical rails, including auditability and contestability impacts.

## B03.CH06.P09.S05 — Reintegration: conditions for compatible cooperation

Reintegration is possible and desirable when conflict is parametric or when a fork corrects itself and returns within compatibility rails. Reintegration is not “unity at any cost”; it is a testable return to shared invariants.

### Reintegration conditions
- **Rail alignment**: explicit acceptance of non-negotiables (caps bindingness, commons openness + maintenance discipline, auditability, contestability).
- **Rollback of erosions**: removal of stackable exceptions, reopening of computation rules, restoration of audit access.
- **Evidence repair**: restatements/bridging where definitions or metrics were shifted.
- **Governance repair**: conflict-of-interest corrections, rotation where capture was implicated.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Reintegration MUST be conditional on passing a **compatibility check** and MUST not proceed on the basis of rhetoric alone.
- Joint action is allowed under **interface agreements** (shared dashboards, shared audit protocols) even when political strategy differs, provided the invariants remain intact.
