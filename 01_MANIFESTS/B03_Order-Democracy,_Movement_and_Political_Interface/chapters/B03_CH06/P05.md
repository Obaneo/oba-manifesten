# B03.CH06.P05 — Support structures: infrastructure that prevents the movement from becoming ephemeral

This paragraph specifies the **support structures** required for an OBA-aligned movement to persist beyond episodic campaigns
and survive the predictable pressures that otherwise dissolve it: crisis cycles, media shocks, infiltration, donor capture,
internal schisms, and implementation fatigue. In OBA terms, “movement durability” is an **operational property**: the movement
must continuously reproduce (a) the **canonical language** (test axes), (b) **evidence discipline** (replicable methods), and
(c) **mandate discipline** (testable commitments + hard stops), while remaining resilient to capture and semantic drift.

Support structures are therefore treated as **infrastructure**, not “nice-to-have” activism tooling. Each structure below is
defined by (i) what it produces, (ii) how it is audited, and (iii) its failure modes and rails.

---

## B03.CH06.P05.S01 — Knowledge infrastructure: definitions, explainers, replication tooling

Knowledge infrastructure is the movement’s **anti-drift engine**. It ensures that public messaging, mandate text, and policy
implementation stay aligned with the canonical sources (Kernel Spec and dependent books) without redefinition.

Core components:
- **Definition access**: version-pinned definition sets; referenceable IDs; change logs and restatement notes.
- **Explainers**: translation layers that map canonical test axes to concrete proposals without creating shadow definitions.
- **Replication tooling**: open calculation rules, test harnesses, and minimal datasets/templates enabling third parties to
  reproduce key metrics (E, caps signals, ΔG/commons quality, R/B profiles, ΔM distribution).

Failure modes:
- “Explainers” quietly redefining terms (semantic drift).
- Tooling captured by vendors (black-box metrics; proprietary dependencies).
- Knowledge as personality cult (authority without reproducibility).

Required rails:
- Public version control; explicit references instead of paraphrase for canonical claims; second implementations where feasible;
audit trails for published numbers and dashboards.

---

## B03.CH06.P05.S02 — Financial infrastructure: transparent funding, anti-capture rails, disclosures

Financial infrastructure prevents the movement from being forced into dependence on donors, platforms, or vendors that can
extract concessions via funding. Under OBA, funding is a **capture surface** and must be governed as such.

Core components:
- **Transparent funding ledger**: donor classes, thresholds, beneficial control where relevant, and public disclosures.
- **Funding caps and firewalling**: rules that prevent concentrated influence; bans on hidden in-kind support (services, tooling).
- **Budget discipline**: ring-fenced funding for knowledge/replication capacity and legal defence (continuity functions).

Failure modes:
- Donor capture (quiet carve-outs; exception politics).
- Funding volatility (boom/bust mobilisation; staff churn).
- Covert vendor sponsorship (tooling dependency as leverage).

Required rails:
- Disclosure + conflict registers; diversified funding mix; audited accounts; procurement-style controls for tools/services; explicit
sanctions for undisclosed support.

## B03.CH06.P05.S03 — Organisational ops: training, onboarding, role clarity, conflict procedures

Organisational operations convert enthusiasm into durable capacity. The movement must be able to **train roles**, keep
institutional memory, and resolve conflicts without fragmentation.

Core components:
- **Training pipelines**: language discipline (test axes), evidence discipline (logs, reproducibility), and mandate discipline
  (hard stops, exception rules).
- **Onboarding and role clarity**: defined roles (organisers, analysts, stewards, auditors, comms) with boundaries and escalation
  paths.
- **Conflict procedures**: complaint → mediation → arbitration/tribunal → escalation; documentation requirements; non-suspensive
  defaults to prevent sabotage via procedural stacking.

Failure modes:
- Volunteer burnout and turnover (loss of continuity).
- Internal capture (gatekeeping roles; opaque selections).
- Schism via unmanaged conflict (ideological relapse, scapegoating, drift).

Required rails:
- Rotation and disclosure; documented runbooks; incident reviews; clear fork protocol for irreconcilable disagreements; compulsory
records for decisions and disciplinary actions.

---

## B03.CH06.P05.S04 — Media and distribution channels: reach without platform dependence

Media and distribution channels are a resilience requirement: the movement must reach the public and maintain informational
continuity without being hostage to single platforms, algorithmic chokepoints, or captured outlets.

Core components:
- **Channel diversity**: multiple independent distribution paths (owned channels, federated networks, offline infrastructure).
- **Message integrity**: canonical references, stable terminology, and explicit uncertainty/unknown discipline when data is incomplete.
- **Rapid correction loop**: corrections, restatements, and versioned updates that prevent confusion campaigns from locking in.

Failure modes:
- Platform dependence (deplatforming, algorithmic throttling, narrative capture).
- Slogan drift (loss of testability; ideology substitution for order criteria).
- Crisis misinformation (measurement blackouts; panic exceptions normalized).

Required rails:
- Publish primary artifacts (definitions, scorecards, logs) in durable repositories; mirror content across channels; treat every
public claim as referenceable and correctable; avoid black-box “trust us” narratives.

## B03.CH06.P05.S05 — Commons projects as “living proof”: visible ΔG and service minima

Commons projects provide the movement’s strongest legitimacy asset: **observable, measurable improvements** that demonstrate
commons substitution and maintenance discipline in practice. They anchor coalition support because benefits are concrete and
distributed.

Core components:
- **Pilot commons**: services/infrastructure with clear access rules, service minima, maintenance logs, and public dashboards.
- **ΔG visibility**: measurable quality/maintenance gains (coverage, reliability, backlog reduction) with traceable methods.
- **Contestability**: complaint channels, appeal paths, and independent audits of prioritisation and allocation.

Failure modes:
- Façade commons (closed access; private tolls reintroduced).
- Under-maintenance (backlog drift; loss of credibility).
- Capture by operators/vendors (procurement lock-in; governance hollowing).

Required rails:
- Open access within boundary rules; public maintenance commitments; procurement exitability; hard stops for black-box allocation
or opaque prioritisation; incident reporting with corrective actions and restatements when metrics shift.
