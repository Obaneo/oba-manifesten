# B03.CH06.P06 — Mobilisation strategies: from framing to institutional pressure

This paragraph outlines how OBA movements can strategically mobilise public support and institutional pressure in line with the
principles of order-democracy. The strategies here represent the **systematic application of OBA principles** (test axes,
evidence discipline, and mandate structure) to real-world mobilisation, aiming to convert ideological support into actionable
commitments and tangible outcomes.

---

## B03.CH06.P06.S01 — Language discipline: speak in test axes (E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM)

Language discipline ensures that the movement remains grounded in **testable metrics** and **consistent terminology**.
By consistently framing demands and claims within the established **test axes** (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM), the movement prevents
shift towards rhetorical or ideological positioning that lacks measurable impact.

Core components:
- **Test axes**: Use established order criteria (E-reduction, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM) to frame all policy proposals, public claims, and
  demands.
- **Avoidance of rhetoric**: Bypass non-testable appeals, vague slogans, or emotional appeals that undermine OBA’s **auditability**.
- **Consistency**: Use only well-defined terms and reference the **Kernel Spec** when necessary to clarify claims.

Failure modes:
- Slogan-driven activism (e.g., “change for change's sake”).
- Ideological drift (e.g., turning political values into rhetorical constructs with no measurable basis).
- Lack of clarity or vagueness leading to coalition fragmentation.

Required rails:
- Every claim must be **testable** within the OBA framework.
- External definitions or new terms should be avoided unless absolutely necessary.
- Engage in **restatements** of key claims with new data to maintain clarity and consistency.

---

## B03.CH06.P06.S02 — Issue bundling: essentials, housing, care, energy—tie to E-reduction

Issue bundling is the **strategic grouping of multiple urgent needs** under a single mobilisation framework, facilitating broader
coalition-building and policy coherence. By linking key sectors (housing, care, energy) to overarching OBA principles (e.g., E-reduction),
the movement ensures its core principles are applied across all areas of public concern.

Core components:
- **Essentials**: Housing, healthcare, and energy are key areas that directly impact the **quality of life** and public welfare.
- **Linking to E-reduction**: Every sector must align with the core OBA principle of **E-reduction** to ensure policy coherence.
- **Broad appeal**: Bundling sectors increases reach and allows the movement to tap into broader constituencies.

Failure modes:
- Fragmentation of demands leading to lack of policy coherence (e.g., isolated campaigns without OBA framework).
- Non-alignment of sector-specific demands with OBA principles (e.g., housing policies without E-reduction).
- Capture of issue areas by external players that dilute core principles (e.g., privatisation of housing without democratic oversight).

Required rails:
- Use **E-reduction** as the overarching principle for all sectors.
- Ensure that bundled sectors share common goals and measurable indicators.
- Avoid splitting issues into incompatible or separate advocacy streams that lack a unifying framework.

---

## B03.CH06.P06.S03 — Targeting: chokepoints and veto players (institutional, market, infrastructure)

Targeting strategic **chokepoints** and **veto players** is crucial for shifting power dynamics in order-democracy.
By identifying and focusing on the most powerful stakeholders or critical infrastructure nodes, the movement can maximise
its leverage while avoiding diffusion of effort.

Core components:
- **Chokepoints**: Focus on key decision-making hubs, such as **financial institutions**, **legislative bodies**, and **media** outlets
  that shape public discourse and policy decisions.
- **Veto players**: Identify stakeholders who can block reform efforts, such as large corporations, monopolistic entities, or
  entrenched political figures.
- **Power concentration**: Targeting ensures that the movement’s efforts are concentrated where they can yield the most significant
  impact.

Failure modes:
- Overextending resources by spreading the campaign too thin across multiple targets.
- Focusing on low-impact areas that fail to shift the balance of power.
- Empowering the wrong players or inadvertently reinforcing existing power structures.

Required rails:
- Maintain strategic focus on institutional, market, and infrastructure nodes that hold real power.
- Engage in **public scorecards** and **monitoring** to ensure progress at these key points.
- Use **escalation** techniques to pressure stakeholders and build momentum towards change.

## B03.CH06.P06.S04 — Escalation: campaign → policy commitments → implementation monitoring

Escalation is a key component of **mobilisation dynamics**. It ensures that the movement starts with broad, flexible demands and gradually
moves towards binding commitments and measurable policy implementation. By focusing on **campaign escalation** through policy
commitments and ongoing monitoring, the movement maintains momentum and pushes for actual, enforceable results.

Core components:
- **Campaign phase**: Build awareness, engagement, and mobilisation through **public pressure** and **media visibility**.
- **Policy commitments**: Once initial awareness is established, secure formal commitments from policymakers, institutions, and other
  key stakeholders.
- **Implementation monitoring**: Ensure policies are implemented according to the OBA framework, with clear accountability
  mechanisms and **hard stops** to prevent slippage.

Failure modes:
- **Deflation**: Losing momentum after initial gains, without pressing for **binding commitments**.
- **Policy dilution**: Allowing policy commitments to weaken through lack of clarity or non-enforcement.
- **Implementation failure**: Monitoring systems being bypassed or ignored, resulting in ineffective execution.

Required rails:
- Formal commitments should be linked to **hard stops** and **monitoring frameworks**.
- Use **public accountability** systems such as dashboards and scorecards to maintain pressure on stakeholders.
- Ensure **escalation ladders** are in place for non-compliance or policy drift.

---

## B03.CH06.P06.S05 — Infiltration resistance: screening, disclosure, rotation, audit of finances

Infiltration resistance ensures that the movement remains immune to capture by vested interests, corporations, or external political
agendas. By enforcing **screening**, **disclosure**, and **rotation** procedures, the movement safeguards against exploitation
and corruption.

Core components:
- **Screening**: Prioritise the recruitment of individuals and organisations aligned with the core OBA principles and values.
- **Disclosure**: Mandate transparency around funding sources, alliances, and affiliations to prevent covert influence.
- **Rotation**: Establish rotating roles and term limits to prevent power concentration and ensure a continual flow of fresh ideas.

Failure modes:
- External capture (e.g., corporate interests infiltrating leadership).
- Lack of transparency leading to covert influence (e.g., undisclosed donations or political ties).
- Entrenchment of power through lack of role rotation and unaccountable leadership.

Required rails:
- Conduct regular **financial audits** and **public disclosure** of all donations and affiliations.
- Ensure **role rotation** and clear conflict-of-interest rules.
- Use **anti-capture mechanisms** such as **disclosure registers** and **financial transparency** to prevent infiltration.
