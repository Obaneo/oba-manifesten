# B03.CH06.P03 — Coalition logic: stable core, swing groups, and fault lines

This paragraph defines coalition logic for OBA as a **structural stability problem** rather than an ideological alignment
problem. Coalitions form and persist when groups experience a **credible net gain** along the order axes (E reduction,
commons substitution/ΔG improvement, R/B stabilisation, binding caps, and contestable ΔM allocation) within politically tolerable
time horizons. Coalitions fracture when benefits are **delayed, uncertain, or invisible** while losses are **immediate and
concentrated**, or when counter-moves succeed in shifting blame through exceptions, semantic drift, and crisis narratives.

Coalition design must therefore be treated as an engineering task with explicit assumptions about:
- **Timing** (who pays first, who gains first),
- **Visibility** (what can be measured and shown),
- **Burden concentration** (local shocks, sectoral disruption),
- **Counter-move capacity** (finance/platform veto power, media capture),
- **Exception discipline** (whether invariants are defended under pressure).

---

## B03.CH06.P03.S01 — Core coalition: direct interest in E-reduction and commons substitution

A durable OBA coalition has a **stable core** whose material position improves directly when toll and claim extraction is reduced
and when essentials are substituted by commons provisioning. This core typically includes actors with high exposure to fee stacks,
lock-ins, and essentials volatility, and actors whose work depends on functional public systems.

Core coalition properties:
- **Immediate gains** from removing toll positions (E↓) in housing-adjacent services, platforms, energy/telecom, and finance fees.
- **High salience** of commons quality (ΔG↑): access, uptime, maintenance backlog reduction.
- **Low tolerance** for semantic drift: they need enforcement and contestability to prevent regression.

Core coalition stability increases when OBA delivers early, measurable wins (fee-stack removal, gating bans enforced, service minima
met) and when enforcement is perceived as **non-selective** and **evidence-bounded**.

## B03.CH06.P03.S02 — Swing groups: timing asymmetry (fast losses versus slow gains)

Swing groups are structurally defined by **timing asymmetry**: they can be persuaded by credible stabilisation, but are also the
first to defect when costs hit early and benefits arrive late. They often carry moderate buffers and high sensitivity to
perceived status loss (housing, pensions, local security).

Key swing dynamics:
- **Fast losses**: asset repricing, compliance friction, sectoral disruption, visible tax/price changes.
- **Slow gains**: commons build-out, reduced extraction, long-run productivity, lower volatility.
- **Narrative susceptibility**: “unworkable,” “punishing the middle,” “bureaucracy,” “surveillance state.”

Design implication: OBA sequencing must front-load **R/B buffers** and **visible anti-extraction wins** while insulating swing
groups from concentrated transition costs (targeted compensation, paced rollouts, credible sunset/rollback discipline).

## B03.CH06.P03.S03 — Fault lines: sector, region, generation, education, housing tenure

Fault lines are recurrent cleavage planes where OBA implementation creates **differential exposure** even if the system is
net-positive. Typical fault lines include:

- **Sector**: tradable vs non-tradable, regulated vs platform-dependent, capital-intensive vs labour-intensive.
- **Region**: places that bear infrastructure scarcity, job transitions, or energy shocks more acutely.
- **Generation**: younger cohorts with low assets vs older cohorts with pensions/housing exposure.
- **Education**: unequal access to administrative capacity and policy literacy.
- **Housing tenure**: owners vs renters; mortgage holders vs outright owners.

If these fault lines are not explicitly managed, they become channels for scapegoating, exception pressure, and selective
enforcement narratives. Movement practice must therefore include **fault-line mapping**, targeted stabilisers, and transparent
measurement of distribution profiles (R/B) at relevant granularities.

## B03.CH06.P03.S04 — Elite splitting: production versus finance, export versus domestic

OBA is politically feasible when it produces **elite splits** that break unified top-tail opposition. The core structural split is
often between actors whose profits depend on **productive throughput** and competitive cost conditions, versus actors whose profits
depend on **claim positions** (platform tolls, financial intermediation rents, asset inflation).

Common split patterns:
- **Production vs finance**: producers benefit from lower toll costs and stable demand; finance benefits from claim stacking and
  volatility monetisation.
- **Export vs domestic toll regimes**: export sectors may prefer predictable cost and infrastructure reliability; domestic
  rent-seekers prefer protected choke points.
- **Competitive SMEs vs gatekeeping platforms**: SMEs gain from interoperability and anti-lock-in; platforms lose take-rate power.

Movement strategy should actively stabilise and publicise these splits by tying OBA to **cost-of-doing-business reductions**
(E↓), **infrastructure reliability** (ΔG↑), and **auditability** (anti-black-box procurement).

## B03.CH06.P03.S05 — Coalition erosion: exceptions, sequencing conflicts, crisis politics

Coalitions erode through predictable mechanisms that weaken the invariant rails while preserving surface rhetoric.

Primary erosion channels:
- **Exception stacking**: repeated “temporary” carve-outs that hollow caps, weaken commons rules, or reintroduce toll positions.
- **Sequencing conflict**: groups bear costs before buffers and commons capacity arrive; swing groups defect.
- **Crisis politics**: shocks are used to normalise emergency powers, black-box allocation, or selective enforcement.
- **Selective enforcement**: sanctions applied asymmetrically, producing legitimacy collapse.
- **Measurement blackouts**: dashboards degrade, definitions drift without changelog, reproducibility falls.

Anti-erosion requirements (binding for movement discipline):
- Maintain a public **exception register** with sunsets and rollback plans.
- Enforce **non-suspensive defaults** where sabotage risk exists (no paralysis via procedure stacking).
- Protect measurement continuity and open calculation rules; treat black-box allocation as a hard-stop condition.
- Sequence reforms so that **R/B buffers and commons capacity** precede or coincide with disruptive changes.
