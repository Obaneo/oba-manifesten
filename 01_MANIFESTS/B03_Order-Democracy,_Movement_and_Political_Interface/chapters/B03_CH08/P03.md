# B03.CH08.P03 — Orthogonal axes: why OOA/OBA does not coincide with left/right

This paragraph formalizes the claim that **OOA/OBA** and **left/right** are **orthogonal axes**. Left/right is a coalition-and-identity axis that
bundles cultural preferences, moral frames, and instrument instincts. OOA/OBA is a **structural order axis** that tracks whether accumulation is
bounded, extraction is reduced, toll positions are neutralized, and measurability survives adversarial pressure. Because the axes measure different
things, each quadrant exists: *Left-OOA, Right-OOA, Left-OBA, Right-OBA*. The interface’s job is not to moralize quadrants, but to prevent **category
errors** (“left = good order” or “right = good order”) by translating rhetoric into rail deltas.

## Core principles

* **Orthogonality**: left/right labels do not predict order outcomes; order outcomes must be assessed directly.
* **Quadrant clarity**: each quadrant has stable failure modes and stable success conditions.
* **Anti-washing**: actors can adopt the opposing side’s vocabulary while preserving OOA mechanisms; quadrant analysis is the detection layer.
* **Mechanism constraints**: classification is derived from E, caps, R/B distribution, commons ΔG, ΔM channels, and auditability.

## B03.CH08.P03.S01 — Left-OOA: redistribution without E-reduction (asset-support reflex, exception politics)

**Left-OOA** describes positions that emphasize redistribution, public spending, or social protection while leaving **extraction mechanisms** intact.
The signature is a compensatory approach: pay the bottom while preserving toll positions at the top. This can improve short-run hardship metrics but
fails the OBA axis because the claim-stack continues to expand, fixed-cost burdens (L) keep rising, and capture channels remain open.

### Functional profile (Left-OOA)

* **Redistribution without toll neutralization**: transfers offset symptoms while housing, energy, health, education, or platform tolls remain.
* **Asset-support reflex**: crises are met with broad liquidity/guarantees that stabilize asset prices and claim values; extraction rebounds after relief.
* **Exception politics**: the system becomes a patchwork of exemptions, targeted subsidies, and discretionary carve-outs—creating insider pathways.
* **Opacity tolerance**: complexity is accepted as “necessary administration,” reducing auditability and enabling leakage.
* **R/B masking**: gains in net income can be cancelled by rising fixed burdens L; restruimte does not structurally improve.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A proposal **MUST** be classified as **Left-OOA** when it increases transfers or public outlays while (i) not specifying E-reduction mechanisms,
  (ii) expanding asset/claim support channels, or (iii) relying on exceptions and discretionary enforcement.
* If redistribution depends on ongoing subsidy of toll sectors rather than substitution/contestability/caps, the interface **MUST** flag **OOA rebound risk**.
* Use of “equity/justice” language **MUST NOT** be treated as evidence of order improvement without rail deltas and audit trails.

## B03.CH08.P03.S02 — Right-OOA: market rhetoric with lock-in/tolls and top-tail dominance

**Right-OOA** describes positions that emphasize markets, deregulation, and growth while tolerating (or actively producing) durable toll positions and
top-tail dominance. The signature is that “competition” is asserted rhetorically but not enforced structurally: barriers to entry, lock-in, and
concentration remain, and claim expansion proceeds behind a market vocabulary.

### Functional profile (Right-OOA)

* **Competition talk, contestability weak**: interoperability, portability, and anti-lock-in measures are absent; switching costs remain high.
* **Toll normalization**: essential sectors are treated as profit zones even when scarcity and gatekeeping make prices non-contestable.
* **Top-tail dominance**: accumulation is justified as incentive structure; caps are rejected or made non-binding, enabling capture and rule-shaping.
* **Privatized opacity**: measurement and allocation are outsourced to vendors; black-box metrics are used to resist public audit.
* **Externalization**: commons degradation and social costs are treated as “side effects,” then partially patched via charity or minimal safety nets.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A proposal **MUST** be classified as **Right-OOA** when it reduces constraints while (i) failing to neutralize toll positions, (ii) allowing
  concentration to increase, or (iii) replacing public auditability with private discretion.
* Claims of “free markets” **MUST** be rejected as sufficient unless contestability is demonstrated with concrete anti-lock-in and anti-capture rails.
* If a proposal lowers taxes/regulation but introduces proprietary enforcement or expands gatekeeping rights, the interface **MUST** classify it as OOA.

## B03.CH08.P03.S03 — Left-OBA: commons and caps with strict audit trails

**Left-OBA** describes positions that pursue social protection through **structural** measures aligned with OBA rails: commons substitution in essential
domains, binding caps to prevent capture, and strict auditability to prevent exception drift. The “left” signature here is a preference for commons
and universal baseline resilience—but crucially without trading away measurability.

### Functional profile (Left-OBA)

* **Commons substitution**: expand access to essentials via commons capacity under boundary rules (ΔG discipline), reducing dependency on toll sectors.
* **Binding caps as anti-capture**: caps (or equivalents) are treated as constitutional infrastructure, not as punitive symbolism.
* **R/B-first distribution**: baseline restruimte is protected structurally (not only via transfers) by reducing fixed-burden claim layers.
* **Audit strictness**: public logs, replicable measurement, and minimal discretion; programs are fail-closed when audit capacity is lost.
* **Exception minimization**: preference for simple, universal mechanisms over carve-outs; when exceptions exist, they are explicit and time-bounded.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A proposal **MUST** qualify as **Left-OBA** only if commons expansion and caps come with **replicable audit trails** and bypass-resistant design.
* “Public ownership” or “universal service” **MUST** be classified as **OBA-partial** if it lacks measurable E-reduction pathways or allows opaque procurement.
* If commons programs are financed through new opaque claim structures or non-transparent ΔM channels, classification **MUST** be downgraded.

## B03.CH08.P03.S04 — Right-OBA: market space without toll positions; caps as anti-monopoly infrastructure

**Right-OBA** describes positions that value decentralization and voluntary exchange but treat **toll positions** as the enemy of markets rather than
their expression. The “right” signature is preference for competitive space and plural ownership; the OBA signature is strict suppression of
lock-in, concentration, and captured rulemaking—using caps as **anti-monopoly infrastructure** and auditability as the condition for legitimacy.

### Functional profile (Right-OBA)

* **Market space without tolls**: essentials are kept contestable through anti-gatekeeping rules, interoperability mandates, and anti-lock-in standards.
* **Caps as constitutional anti-monopoly**: caps (and consolidation rules) prevent dominance that destroys contestability and converts markets into tolls.
* **Transparent rule-of-law enforcement**: minimal discretion, clear standards, predictable sanctions; no private black-box governance.
* **Commons where markets cannot exist**: when scarcity/essentiality makes contestability impossible, commons substitution is accepted as order necessity.
* **Anti-subsidy of extraction**: refusal to stabilize asset claims or rents through hidden guarantees; crisis policy targets order restoration, not claim preservation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A proposal **MUST** qualify as **Right-OBA** only if it pairs pro-market measures with **hard anti-toll mechanisms** (anti-lock-in, anti-capture,
  anti-monopoly) and accepts binding caps as infrastructure.
* Deregulation **MUST** be treated as OOA unless accompanied by explicit mechanisms that reduce extraction and prevent toll formation.
* If “freedom” is defined as freedom for toll-setters to gatekeep essentials, the interface **MUST** classify the position as OOA-consistent.
