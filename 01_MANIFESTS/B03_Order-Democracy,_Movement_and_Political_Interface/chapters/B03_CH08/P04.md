# B03.CH08.P04 — Mapping political positions: classification based on test axes

This paragraph defines the **mapping procedure** used by the interface layer to classify political positions and policy proposals without recourse to
party identity. The mapping takes an input claim (“we will fix X”) and outputs a **projection onto the OBA test axes**, a **control/benefit/block**
map, an **exception profile**, and an **evidence profile**. The procedure is designed for adversarial settings: it assumes rhetoric is optimized for
plausibility, and therefore treats the absence of mechanisms, logs, and rollback constraints as *order-relevant negatives* rather than mere omissions.

## Core principles

* **Projection discipline**: every claim is reduced to deltas on a fixed set of axes (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM, auditability).
* **Power surface mapping**: classify not only effects but enforcement feasibility (who can veto, who can bypass).
* **Exception minimalism**: exceptions are treated as structural risk; they require sunsets and rollback triggers.
* **Evidence-first**: a proposal is “real” only to the degree it is measurable, replicable, and contestable.

## B03.CH08.P04.S01 — Policy claim → projection onto test axes (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM)

The interface maps any policy claim into a **vector of axis deltas**. This replaces ideological interpretation with a standardized order grammar.

### Required test axes (minimum set)

* **E (extraction)**: does the proposal measurably reduce toll/claim extraction, or does it re-route/rename it?
* **R/B distribution**: does it structurally improve restruimte at the bottom (p10/p25/median), or merely shift gross/net while L rises?
* **Caps**: does it strengthen binding caps (or equivalents) and anti-bypass consolidation, or weaken them via carve-outs and form arbitrage?
* **ΔG (commons capacity)**: does commons access/capacity grow under boundary rules, or is commons substituted by rent-based access?
* **ΔM (channel discipline)**: does new fiscal/monetary capacity flow through auditable channels toward commons/resilience, or toward asset/claim support?
* **Auditability**: are definitions, logging, measurement, and restatement obligations explicit and enforceable?

### Projection output (standard fields)

For each axis, the interface outputs:

* **Direction**: {↑, ↓, ↔, unknown}
* **Mechanism**: one-sentence causal path (“how it changes the axis”)
* **Metric hook**: what is measured (data fields, definitions, period)
* **Uncertainty band**: what is unknown, and what would falsify the claim
* **Bypass risk**: top 1–3 known avoidance routes and the planned countermeasures

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every classified position/proposal **MUST** include an explicit projection for each axis (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM, auditability).
* If the mechanism cannot be stated, the axis **MUST** be marked **unknown** (not inferred from intention).
* If a proposal claims “OBA” outcomes while leaving any axis unaddressed, the interface **MUST** flag **incompleteness** and downgrade classification.
* A proposal that increases ΔM but lacks channel discipline defaults **MUST** be treated as **OOA-risk** until auditable allocation is specified.

## B03.CH08.P04.S02 — Control/benefit/block mapping: who loses/wins and who holds veto power

Order reforms fail less from “bad ideas” than from **veto surfaces**: actors who can block, dilute, or delay implementation. The interface therefore
requires a **control/benefit/block (C/B/B) mapping** that identifies: who holds decision power, who benefits economically, and who can block in practice
(de jure or de facto).

### Mapping dimensions

* **Control**: who sets the rules, sets the thresholds, defines categories, controls measurement, controls procurement, controls enforcement.
* **Benefit**: who receives rents, who loses rents, who gains contestability, who is compensated, who bears transition costs.
* **Block**: who can veto (formal), who can stall (procedural), who can litigate (legal), who can exit/capitalize (market), who can sabotage via opacity.

### Outputs (required)

* **Stakeholder roster**: impacted groups, including intermediaries and measurement vendors.
* **Veto list**: top veto actors/coalitions per axis (E, caps, ΔM, audit).
* **Capture vectors**: where rule-making or enforcement can be purchased (exceptions, procurement, lobbying, standards control).
* **Counter-instruments**: what prevents veto/capture (automatic triggers, independent audit, open logging, statutory clarity, sunset clauses).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every classified proposal **MUST** include a control/benefit/block map with explicit identification of veto power holders.
* If veto actors overlap with the primary extractors (E beneficiaries), the proposal **MUST** specify binding anti-capture and enforcement safeguards or be downgraded.
* The interface **MUST** treat “political feasibility” claims as non-evidence unless tied to explicit veto-neutralization mechanisms.

## B03.CH08.P04.S03 — Exception profile: carve-outs, sunsets, rollback requirements

Exceptions are the canonical pathway by which OBA regimes revert into OOA: carve-outs create insider lanes, expand discretion, and break comparability.
The interface therefore produces an **exception profile** and treats exception structure as a first-class determinant of classification.

### Exception profile fields

* **Carve-outs**: who is excluded, which sectors/vehicles are exempt, which thresholds create de facto exemptions.
* **Discretion points**: where officials can waive, delay, or negotiate compliance.
* **Sunset clauses**: expiry dates for exceptions, with automatic renewal barriers.
* **Rollback triggers**: objective conditions under which the policy auto-reverts or tightens (e.g., E not falling, audit failures, bypass rates rising).
* **Complexity budget**: administrative overhead and whether complexity itself becomes an opacity shield.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any exception **MUST** have: (i) explicit scope, (ii) justification, (iii) sunset date, and (iv) rollback requirements.
* Open-ended exceptions or discretionary waivers **MUST** downgrade classification (at minimum to **OBA-partial**) unless countered by strict auditability and automatic triggers.
* Threshold-based designs **MUST** be tested for “threshold tricks” (gaming, bunching, entity splitting); absence of such tests is a downgrade condition.

## B03.CH08.P04.S04 — Evidence profile: measurability, replicability, contestability

A proposal is only as real as its **evidence profile**. The interface therefore requires that any position claiming order improvements commits to
measurability (data exists), replicability (others can reproduce), and contestability (others can challenge and restate).

### Evidence profile dimensions

* **Measurability**: are required data fields defined, available, and linkable (lineage)? Are definitions public and versioned?
* **Replicability**: can an independent party recompute the classification and the axis deltas using the published rules and logs?
* **Contestability**: are there procedures for challenges, restatements, and error correction? Are audit trails open to scrutiny?
* **Adversarial robustness**: does the design anticipate evasion (entity splitting, off-balance structures, subcontracting opacity, synthetic exposure)?
* **Fail-closed behavior**: what happens when evidence quality degrades—does the policy tighten/scale back or does it proceed on trust?

### Evidence outputs (required)

* **Data dictionary references** (canonical IDs)
* **Logging requirements** (what must be logged, by whom, at what granularity)
* **Audit scope** (who audits, frequency, independence, publication)
* **Restatement rules** (how corrections propagate)
* **Uncertainty handling** (“unknown” discipline; conservative defaults)

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Proposals **MUST** be classified as **Unknown/Untestable** when measurability or replicability is absent.
* Claims of “expert assessment” or “model outputs” **MUST NOT** substitute for open measurement rules and logs.
* If contestability is restricted (proprietary metrics, sealed audits, no restatements), classification **MUST** be downgraded and washing risk flagged.
* Evidence commitments **MUST** be enforceable (legal/contractual obligation), not voluntary.
