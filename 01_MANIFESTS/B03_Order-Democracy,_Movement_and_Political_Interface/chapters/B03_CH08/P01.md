# B03.CH08.P01 — Purpose and delimitation: the interface as a translation and classification layer

This paragraph defines the **interface layer** of OBA-democracy: a **translation and classification layer** that converts political language
(identities, slogans, party frames, moral claims) into **order-relevant statements** that can be tested against OBA rails. The interface does
not “pick sides” inside a left/right spectrum; it **replaces that spectrum** as the primary analytic axis for order conflicts by re-framing
disputes as **OOA vs OBA** questions: *Does a position reproduce unbounded accumulation and opaque claim-stacks (OOA), or does it enforce bounded
accumulation with measurable reduction of extraction and auditable commons growth (OBA)?*

## Core principles

* **Translation over alignment**: map rhetoric to mechanisms, mechanisms to rails, rails to tests.
* **Classification over persuasion**: produce labels and evidence requirements, not party endorsements.
* **Fault-line clarity**: treat OOA/OBA as the relevant divide for order stability, regardless of left/right packaging.
* **Anti-washing discipline**: separate *naming* from *function*; disallow semantic laundering.

## B03.CH08.P01.S01 — Why left/right is insufficient for order conflicts

Left/right is a coalition shorthand, not an order instrument. It compresses diverse conflicts (distribution, culture, sovereignty, regulation,
market/state preferences) into an identity axis that is **non-diagnostic** for extraction dynamics. The same left-coded or right-coded program can
either (a) reduce extraction and expand contestability, or (b) entrench claim-stacks via carve-outs, opacity, and captured enforcement. When the
order problem is **structural** (accumulation feedback loops, rent capture, lock-in, black-box allocation), left/right labeling becomes a tool for
**misdirection**: it can block scrutiny of mechanisms by relocating disagreement into identity loyalty.

### Core principles

* **Non-diagnostic packaging**: ideological labels do not determine whether E (extraction) rises or falls.
* **Mechanism primacy**: order conflicts must be expressed as changes to measurable rails (E, caps, R/B, commons ΔG, ΔM allocation, auditability).
* **Identity-hijack risk**: left/right frames incentivize rhetorical winning over conformance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any analysis under this interface **MUST NOT** treat “left” or “right” as sufficient classification of order effects.
* Positions and proposals **MUST** be translated into mechanism-level claims before any evaluative label is assigned.
* Where the mechanism cannot be specified or measured, the output **MUST** be classified as **Unknown/Untestable** rather than inferred from ideology.
* The interface **MUST** surface trade-offs explicitly (e.g., distribution gains achieved through new opaque claim layers are not “order wins”).

## B03.CH08.P01.S02 — OOA/OBA as the fault line: definition and function

This interface adopts **OOA/OBA** as the primary fault line for order conflicts.

* **OOA (Order of Unbounded Accumulation)**: a regime where accumulation of wealth/claims/access/control can expand without binding caps and where
  extraction (E) is structurally reproducible through lock-ins, claim-stacks, opacity, and captured enforcement. OOA tolerates “growth” narratives
  even when **restruimte** (R/B) erodes and commons decay is externalized.
* **OBA (Order of Bounded Accumulation)**: a regime where accumulation is structurally bounded (caps), extraction is measurably reduced (E↓),
  baseline life-resilience is protected through improved **R/B** at the bottom of the distribution, commons are maintained and expanded under
  boundary rules (ΔG discipline), and monetary/fiscal channels (ΔM) are auditable and contestable.

Functionally, the interface does not claim moral superiority for a tribe; it claims **diagnostic sufficiency**: OOA/OBA classification predicts
whether a system tends toward capture and fragility or toward contestable, auditable stability.

### Core principles

* **Structural divide**: OOA/OBA is about feedback loops and enforceability, not about taste or identity.
* **Test-first definitions**: OOA/OBA labels are outputs of rails/tests, not starting assumptions.
* **Compatibility discipline**: “OBA-compatible” is a measurable claim that can be refused.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any position/proposal **MUST** be classifiable as one of: **OBA-compatible**, **OBA-partial**, **OOA-consistent**, **OBA-washing**, or **Unknown/Untestable**.
* The label **MUST** be derived from explicit rails impact statements (at minimum: E, caps, R/B distribution, commons ΔG, ΔM channel discipline, auditability).
* It is **FORBIDDEN** to assign “OBA” status based on stated intentions, moral language, or identity alignment without mechanism and evidence hooks.
* Where a proposal increases contestability and reduces extraction in one domain but introduces new opaque claim channels elsewhere, the interface **MUST**
  classify it as **OBA-partial** or **OOA-consistent** depending on net rail impact and bypass risk.

## B03.CH08.P01.S03 — Delimitation: no party-political recommendations, only test and language rules

The interface layer is explicitly **non-partisan** by design. It does not produce recommendations of the form “vote for X” or “support Y coalition.”
Its output is a **language regime** and a **test regime**: how claims must be stated, what evidence is required, and what labels are admissible.

This delimitation is not neutrality in the sense of “all views are equal.” It is neutrality in the sense of **procedure**: any actor can be assessed,
but only on the basis of **conformance** to rails and the quality of evidence.

### Core principles

* **Procedural strictness**: the interface constrains discourse by requiring mechanism-level claims.
* **Non-alignment**: it rejects the role of partisan guidance while enforcing hard classification.
* **Anti-personalization**: it evaluates proposals, structures, and measurable commitments—not personalities.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* The interface **MUST NOT** issue party-political endorsements, electoral recommendations, or coalition instructions.
* The interface **MUST** provide: (a) translation of claims into rails, (b) required evidence artifacts, (c) classification label.
* The interface **MUST** explicitly flag when a proposal’s success depends on discretionary enforcement capacity; such dependency **MUST** lower the classification status unless countered by auditable constraints.
* The interface **MUST** treat “rhetorical compliance” (adopting OBA vocabulary) as non-evidence and **MUST** apply washing detection rules.

## B03.CH08.P01.S04 — Output: classification scheme for positions, proposals, and rhetoric

The interface outputs a **classification scheme** applicable to (i) positions (what an actor stands for), (ii) proposals (what is to be implemented),
and (iii) rhetoric (how an actor frames and sells it). The scheme is designed to prevent semantic drift and to make “order claims” auditable.

### Classification labels (normative)

* **OBA-compatible**: mechanism-level commitments that (1) reduce E measurably, (2) enforce bypass-resistant caps or proven equivalents, (3) improve
  R/B at the bottom of the distribution, (4) maintain/expand commons under boundary rules (ΔG discipline), (5) allocate ΔM through auditable channels,
  and (6) meet auditability/replicability requirements.
* **OBA-partial**: meaningful movement on one or more rails, with unresolved bypass vectors, incomplete auditability, or trade-offs that may recreate E elsewhere.
* **OOA-consistent**: preserves or expands claim-stacks, opacity, lock-in, carve-outs, discretionary exceptions, or non-binding caps; tolerates R/B erosion.
* **OBA-washing**: adopts OBA language while mechanisms maintain OOA structure (e.g., non-binding caps, loopholes, proprietary measurement, exceptions for insiders).
* **Unknown/Untestable**: insufficient specificity or evidence; cannot translate into rail impacts.

### Translation outputs (required fields)

For any assessed object (position/proposal/rhetoric), the interface outputs:

* **Rail deltas**: explicit expected direction and mechanism for E, caps, R/B distribution, ΔG, ΔM, auditability.
* **Evidence plan**: what must be logged, how measurements are replicated, what uncertainty bands apply, what “unknown” triggers exist.
* **Bypass map**: known avoidance routes (vehikels, carve-outs, synthetic exposure, subcontracting opacity) and countermeasures.
* **Label + rationale**: classification label plus the minimum set of decisive reasons.

### Rhetoric detection flags (non-exhaustive)

* **Label-over-function**: “pro-OBA” claims without measurable rail deltas.
* **Exception inflation**: growth of discretionary carve-outs framed as pragmatism.
* **Opacity normalization**: “trust the experts/vendor” replacing replicable audit.
* **Identity diversion**: pushing left/right loyalty to avoid mechanism scrutiny.
* **Metric gaming**: cherry-picked KPIs that ignore R/B distribution, L growth, or off-balance extraction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every classification output **MUST** include rail deltas, evidence plan, bypass map, and label rationale.
* The label **MUST** be reversible: when evidence contradicts claims, reclassification is mandatory.
* The interface **MUST** treat “OBA-washing” as a first-class category, not as a rhetorical critique.
* It is **FORBIDDEN** to assign OBA-compatible status when auditability is not ensured or when caps are non-binding or structurally bypassable.
