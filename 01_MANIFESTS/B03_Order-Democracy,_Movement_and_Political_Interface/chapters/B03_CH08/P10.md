# B03.CH08.P10 — Evaluation: indicators for position shifts and regression in the political field

This paragraph defines a compact evaluation layer for tracking **position shifts** and **order regression** in the political field. The goal is not to
score personalities but to detect when rhetoric diverges from mechanisms and when institutions drift from OBA rails back into OOA patterns. The
evaluation layer is built around **directional indicators** (E, exception load, sanction rate, ΔG, ΔM testability), **legitimacy measurements**
(contestability, transparency, definition-set compliance), and a **public consistency regime** (changelog discipline and incident reviews).

## Core principles

* **Signals over narratives**: order drift is detected via observable signals, not via declarations.
* **Regression is measurable**: OOA has a characteristic signature (E↑, exceptions↑, sanctions↓, commons decay).
* **Stabilisation is structural**: OBA holds only if caps bind, R/B floors are stable, ΔM is testable, and auditability remains intact.
* **Consistency is public**: definition changes and enforcement failures must be logged, reviewed, and restated.

## B03.CH08.P10.S01 — OOA regression signals: E↑, exceptions↑, sanction rate↓, ΔG↓

OOA regression is not a single metric; it is a **pattern**. The interface treats the following as primary regression signals, especially when they
move together.

### Primary regression signals (canonical)

* **E↑ (extraction rising)**: toll margins widen; claim-stacks thicken; fixed burdens L increase relative to N; platform take-rates rise; scarcity rents persist.
* **Exceptions↑ (exception load rising)**: more carve-outs per rule; longer exception lifetimes; renewals without evidence; “temporary” holes become baseline.
* **Sanction rate↓ (enforcement weakening)**: fewer sanctions per detected breach; rising under-enforcement; negotiated compliance replacing rule-based penalties.
* **ΔG↓ (commons capacity/quality falling)**: maintenance backlogs grow; access narrows; commons substitution stalls; degradation is re-externalized.

### Secondary regression signals (supporting)

* **Reproducibility↓**: fewer outputs can be recomputed by outsiders; sealed audits increase.
* **Opacity↑**: procurement and subcontract chains expand without lineage; vendor metrics replace public rules.
* **Bypass rate↑**: entity splitting, form arbitrage, off-balance structures rise faster than enforcement adapts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If two or more primary regression signals move adversely for two consecutive reporting periods, the system/proposal **MUST** be flagged as **OOA-regressing**.
* Rising exceptions without proportional transparency and sanctions **MUST** be treated as capture risk and trigger mandatory review.
* Declining ΔG **MUST** be treated as an order breach when commons are part of the stated mechanism; it cannot be excused by rhetorical priorities.

## B03.CH08.P10.S02 — OBA stabilisation: E↓, binding caps, stable R/B lower bounds, ΔG↑, testable ΔM

OBA stabilisation is detected when extraction becomes harder to sustain and when daily-life resilience becomes structurally protected. The interface
treats the following as stabilisation signals.

### Stabilisation signals (canonical)

* **E↓ (extraction falling)**: measurable compression of toll margins; reduced take-rates; declining claim shares in essentials; shrinking lock-in indicators.
* **Binding caps**: caps demonstrably constrain top-tail accumulation; avoidance indicators fall; enforcement actions show consistency and predictability.
* **Stable R/B lower bounds**: p10/p25/median restruimte floors remain above α across cycles; L shares in essentials decline or stabilize.
* **ΔG↑ (commons growth/maintenance)**: commons capacity and quality improve; maintenance discipline holds; access expands within boundary rules.
* **Testable ΔM**: new fiscal/monetary capacity is channelled through auditable pathways; allocation can be reconstructed end-to-end; asset/claim support is bounded and justified.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* “OBA stabilised” status **MUST** require concurrent improvement in at least: E (↓), caps (binding), and one of (R/B floors stable, ΔG↑, ΔM testable).
* A drop in E achieved by shifting extraction into opaque channels **MUST NOT** count as stabilisation; auditability is a gating condition.
* If caps are announced but avoidance indicators rise, the system **MUST** be classified as **OBA-washing risk** until corrected.

## B03.CH08.P10.S03 — Legitimacy measurements: contestability, transparency, compliance with definition sets

Legitimacy here is not popularity; it is **procedural and epistemic integrity**. The interface defines legitimacy measurements that indicate whether
the political field remains compatible with OBA decision discipline.

### Legitimacy measurements (required)

* **Contestability**: the ability of affected parties to challenge classifications, demand restatements, and obtain remedy; presence of independent review.
* **Transparency**: public availability of definition sets, axis dashboards, exception ledgers, enforcement outcomes, and procurement lineage.
* **Definition-set compliance**: explicit version pinning of definitions used in policy; disciplined changelog; backward comparability or migration rules.
* **Audit integrity**: audit-of-auditors in place; conflicts disclosed; methodologies replicable; publication schedules met.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any institution claiming OBA compatibility **MUST** publish a legitimacy panel covering contestability, transparency, and definition-set compliance.
* Failure to publish definition versions or refusal of replication rights **MUST** trigger legitimacy downgrade.
* Where contestability is limited (sealed processes), decisions **MUST** be treated as non-OBA-compatible for classification purposes.

## B03.CH08.P10.S04 — Public consistency reporting: changelog discipline and incident reviews

OBA cannot hold without **public consistency**. This requires a reporting regime that makes drift visible: definition changes, exception changes,
audit failures, and enforcement incidents must be logged and reviewed in public.

### Public consistency regime (required components)

* **Changelog discipline**:

  * definition-set version changes with rationale
  * parameter changes (thresholds, α floors, cap levels) with impact projections
  * deprecations and migrations (what is no longer valid, what replaces it)
* **Incident reviews**:

  * audit failures (what failed, why, what changed)
  * major bypass discoveries (how it was done, how it is closed)
  * enforcement breakdowns (sanction collapse, selective enforcement)
  * crisis exceptions (what was suspended, what was restored, what persisted)
* **Restatement protocol**: corrected metrics and classifications propagate to prior dashboards and public summaries (not append-only footnotes).
* **Action ledger**: commitments made in reviews, deadlines, and completion status.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Changelogs and incident reviews **MUST** be published on a fixed schedule with permanent public access.
* Material incidents **MUST** trigger restatements and, where relevant, automatic correction mechanisms (tightening, rollback, scope reduction).
* If incident reviews are suppressed, delayed, or sanitized, classification **MUST** be downgraded and capture risk flagged.
* It is **FORBIDDEN** to claim continuity of OBA compatibility when definition sets have changed without published mappings and impact analysis.
