# B03.CH08.P06 — Coalition architecture along the fault line

This paragraph specifies how coalitions form and hold **along the OOA↔OBA fault line**. The interface treats coalition-building as an **order problem**:
actors align less around left/right identity than around (i) exposure to **E (extraction)**, (ii) dependence on **toll positions** and lock-in, (iii)
distributional position in **R/B**, and (iv) vulnerability to transition timing (who pays first, who benefits later). The aim is not to prescribe party
alliances, but to define **coalition objects** and **stability conditions** that keep reforms from collapsing into exception politics or asset-support reflexes.

## Core principles

* **Interest follows rails**: durable alignment tracks E-reduction, anti-lock-in, caps, commons access, and auditability—not ideological labels.
* **Timing is decisive**: many actors support OBA in principle but defect under short-term cost or status-loss pressure.
* **Elite fracture is a lever**: OBA transition requires splitting coalitions that currently profit from claim expansion and toll positions.
* **Stabilization anchors politics**: R/B lower bounds and continuity of essentials are the minimum conditions for long-run reform legitimacy.

## B03.CH08.P06.S01 — Core coalition: groups with direct interest in E-reduction / commons / anti-lock-in

A core OBA coalition forms among actors who experience extraction as a *daily constraint* and whose welfare improves directly when toll positions weaken.
This coalition is defined by **structural exposure**, not moral preference.

### Functional profile (core coalition)

* **High E exposure**: households and SMEs where fixed burdens (L) are dominated by toll sectors (housing, energy, mobility, health, education, platform fees).
* **Lock-in victims**: workers/consumers/small producers facing switching costs, closed ecosystems, licensing chokepoints, monopsony/oligopoly pricing.
* **Commons beneficiaries**: groups whose access improves via commons substitution (public options, open infrastructure, shared services under boundary rules).
* **Audit-aligned actors**: stakeholders who gain from transparent rules (independent professionals, competitive entrants, watchdog institutions), because opacity is a competitive disadvantage to them.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any coalition analysis under this chapter **MUST** identify the core coalition by **mechanism exposure** (E share, lock-in intensity, L burden) rather than identity labels.
* A reform package **MUST** include at least one early, tangible E-reduction mechanism for the core coalition (not only long-horizon promises), or coalition retention is assumed to fail.
* Commons expansions **MUST** be packaged with explicit boundary rules and maintenance obligations; “commons” without discipline is not admissible as coalition glue.

## B03.CH08.P06.S02 — Swing groups: timing asymmetry, housing/pensions, status-loss anxiety

Swing groups are actors whose material position or identity status is **ambiguous** under OBA transition. They often support order restoration but fear
being the ones who “pay first” or lose symbolic rank. Their defection risk is primarily driven by **timing asymmetry** and **asset/claim dependence**.

### Functional profile (swing groups)

* **Timing asymmetry**: groups facing immediate compliance costs (pricing discipline, interoperability mandates, tax-base consolidation) while benefits (lower L, higher R/B) appear later.
* **Housing and pension entanglement**: households whose perceived security is tied to asset valuations (home equity, pension fund performance) and who interpret any cap or rent compression as personal loss even when net welfare improves through lower toll burdens.
* **Status-loss anxiety**: middle strata whose identity is linked to “earned position” and who can be mobilized by narratives of moral devaluation, even if their objective R/B improves.
* **Local dependency**: regions/sectors dependent on a few dominant employers or platforms; fear of disruption pushes them toward “keep the gatekeeper stable” politics.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any OBA transition package **MUST** include explicit handling of timing asymmetry for swing groups: (i) transition sequencing, (ii) temporary buffers that do not entrench toll positions, and (iii) clear sunsets.
* Housing/pension exposure **MUST** be addressed with **function-over-asset-value** commitments: continuity of essentials and baseline security take priority over preservation of claim valuations.
* Communication **MUST** explicitly separate “status narratives” from rail deltas; if coalition support depends on identity reassurance rather than measurable R/B and L improvements, the package is classified as **washing-risk** at the political layer.

## B03.CH08.P06.S03 — Elite splitting: production vs finance; platform power vs competitive sectors

OOA persistence depends on elite coherence: when actors benefiting from claim expansion, leverage, and toll positions align with rule-making power, they
can block or dilute reforms. OBA therefore requires **elite splitting**: separating factions whose interests diverge under bounded accumulation.

### Functional profile (elite fracture lines)

* **Production vs finance**: productive sectors that suffer from rent burdens (real estate costs, platform fees, input monopolies, financing spreads) can align with E-reduction against purely financialized claim-maximizers.
* **Competitive business vs toll business**: firms that rely on contestable markets and open standards have incentives to support anti-lock-in, interoperability, and anti-monopoly caps.
* **Platform power vs ecosystem participants**: dependent complements (developers, merchants, content producers) often oppose gatekeeping once credible exit/portability exists.
* **National champions vs domestic plurality**: “strategic” rhetoric can mask toll consolidation; split occurs when competitive sectors recognize that champion protection becomes generalized extraction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any coalition strategy claiming OBA feasibility **MUST** present an explicit **block-surface map** of elite veto points (regulators, courts, standards bodies, procurement chains, data/measurement vendors).
* Reform design **MUST** include at least one mechanism that makes elite sabotage costly or visible (open logging, automatic triggers, independent audit, anti-carve-out constraints).
* Where a policy relies on “cooperation from incumbents” without enforceable auditability and sanctions, it **MUST** be classified as **OOA-susceptible** at the coalition layer.

## B03.CH08.P06.S04 — Stabilisation: R/B lower bounds and essentials continuity as coalition anchor

Coalitions hold when daily life becomes **predictable**. In OBA terms, predictability is not a mood; it is the combination of **R/B lower bounds** and
continuity of essentials under stress. Stabilization is therefore not an afterthought but the **coalition anchor** that prevents swing-group defection,
limits crisis opportunism, and blocks the asset-support reflex.

### Stabilization anchor (required components)

* **R/B lower bounds**: explicit floors for restruimte at the lower distribution (p10/p25/median reporting), enforced by reducing toll burdens and/or direct buffers that do not create permanent claim channels.
* **Essentials continuity**: operational guarantees that core services (housing access, energy, mobility, health, education) remain available during transition and shocks, preferably via commons capacity and contestable provisioning rules.
* **Fail-closed discipline**: when auditability or enforcement capacity weakens, scope tightens to what remains controllable rather than expanding discretion.
* **Anti-panic narrative structure**: communication ties stabilization to rails (“what happens to L and R/B, what happens to tolls”), not to partisan identity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any OBA-compatible political program **MUST** include an explicit stabilization module: R/B floors + essentials continuity + audit triggers.
* Stabilization measures **MUST NOT** be implemented via open-ended asset/claim support (guarantees that preserve toll positions); if used, classification is downgraded unless strictly time-bounded with rollback triggers.
* If a proposal cannot state how stabilization is measured and audited (R/B distribution, L components, service continuity metrics), it **MUST** be classified as **Unknown/Untestable** at the coalition layer.
