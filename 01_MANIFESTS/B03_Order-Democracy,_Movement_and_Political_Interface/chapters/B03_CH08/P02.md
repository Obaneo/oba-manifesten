# B03.CH08.P02 — Core axis: OOA versus OBA (order typology)

This paragraph defines **OOA vs OBA** as the *core analytic axis* for order conflicts and supplies a functional **order typology** that can be used
to classify systems, proposals, and political positions without relying on party identity or left/right packaging. The typology is not rhetorical.
It is constructed from **mechanism profiles**: how claims behave, how toll positions are created and defended, how susceptible the system is to capture,
and whether measurability and enforcement can survive contact with adversarial actors.

## Core principles

* **Functional typology**: classify by mechanism and enforceability, not by declared intent or ideology.
* **Extraction-first**: the decisive question is not “who gets what,” but “where is extraction produced and maintained.”
* **Contestability and measurability**: order stability requires the ability to detect, attribute, and neutralize extraction under adversarial pressure.
* **Fault-line objects**: caps, commons, ΔM channels, and auditability are the canonical conflict points in OOA↔OBA transitions.

## B03.CH08.P02.S01 — OOA: claim expansion, toll positions, capture susceptibility (functional profile)

**OOA (Order of Unbounded Accumulation)** is defined by the system’s permission structure: it allows the accumulation of **wealth, claims, and control**
to expand without binding limits, and it tolerates the creation of durable **toll positions** (gatekeeping nodes) that convert essential access into
recurring claims. The signature is not “markets” or “capitalism” as labels; it is a structural profile:

### Functional profile (OOA)

* **Claim expansion is self-reinforcing**: claims generate income; income purchases more claims; claims purchase influence; influence protects claims.
* **Toll positions proliferate**: chokepoints form around essentials (housing, energy, mobility, health, education, data/information, platforms),
  producing recurring extraction beyond functional costs.
* **Lock-in and dependency are monetized**: switching costs, interoperability barriers, and contractual asymmetries become primary value drivers.
* **Opacity is a feature, not a bug**: subcontracting chains, off-balance structures, proprietary metrics, and “complexity” are used as enforcement shields.
* **Capture susceptibility is high**: rule-writing, exemptions, auditing, and enforcement drift toward insiders, because accumulation buys procedural control.
* **Stability is purchased by postponement**: crises are managed via temporary relief while leaving toll positions intact, enabling extraction rebound.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A system/proposal **MUST** be classified as **OOA-consistent** when it preserves or enlarges toll positions, relies on non-binding limits, expands
  opaque claim stacks, or increases discretionary carve-outs that insiders can exploit.
* If improvements are claimed via growth or redistribution while leaving toll positions intact (L-growth unaddressed, E unmeasured), the interface **MUST**
  treat the proposal as OOA-consistent unless binding counter-rails are shown.

## B03.CH08.P02.S02 — OBA: bounded claims, measurability, binding caps, commons substitution (functional profile)

**OBA (Order of Bounded Accumulation)** is defined by a different permission structure: it binds accumulation, makes extraction measurable, and replaces
toll dependence with **commons substitution** where feasible. OBA is not “more state” or “less market.” It is a regime where any claim that behaves as
a toll must be either (a) competitively contested, (b) capped/regulated with auditable cost bases, or (c) substituted by commons access under boundary
rules and maintenance discipline.

### Functional profile (OBA)

* **Claims are bounded**: wealth and income accumulation face binding caps (or proven structural equivalents) that cannot be bypassed by legal form.
* **Extraction is measurable and targeted**: P/E/C decomposition exists in standards; E-reduction is a first-class objective and is audited.
* **Contestability is enforced**: lock-in is treated as an extraction vector; interoperability, portability, and exit rights are order requirements.
* **Auditability is non-optional**: definitions are public, logs are available, and measurement is replicable; black-box allocation is disallowed.
* **Commons substitution is strategic**: where toll positions sit on essentials, OBA grows commons capacity (ΔG discipline) to reduce dependency.
* **Fail-closed enforcement**: if measurability or enforcement capacity is lost, scope is scaled back to what remains auditable rather than “trust-based.”

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A system/proposal **MUST** be classified as **OBA-compatible** only if it provides binding constraints and auditable mechanisms that (i) reduce E,
  (ii) secure caps against bypass, (iii) improve R/B at the bottom, and (iv) protect commons capacity and access.
* If a proposal depends on discretionary enforcement, private metrics, or unverifiable modeling, it **MUST** be classified as **OBA-partial** or **Unknown**
  unless an auditable substitute exists.

## B03.CH08.P02.S03 — Fault-line objects: caps, commons, ΔM, auditability as conflict points

OOA↔OBA conflicts cluster around a small set of **fault-line objects** because these objects determine whether extraction can be structurally reproduced.

### Fault-line objects (canonical)

* **Caps (W-MAX / I-MAX or equivalents)**: where limits bite, how bases are defined, how consolidation works, and how avoidance is blocked.
* **Commons (ΔG discipline)**: what becomes open access under boundary rules, what maintenance obligations exist, and how commons prevent toll dependence.
* **ΔM channels (allocation interface)**: how monetary/fiscal capacity is injected and through which channels; whether it inflates asset claims or funds
  commons and lower-bound resilience.
* **Auditability**: who can measure, what logs exist, whether measurement is replicable, and whether restatements are mandatory when new facts emerge.

These are not “policy details.” They are structural choke points where OBA either holds or collapses back into OOA through exceptions, opacity, or bypass.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any serious proposal **MUST** state explicit positions on the fault-line objects (at minimum: caps, commons, ΔM channels, auditability).
* If a proposal is silent on one or more fault-line objects while claiming OBA outcomes, the interface **MUST** flag it as **washing risk** and classify as
  **OBA-partial** or **Unknown** pending evidence.
* A proposal that improves distribution but worsens auditability or expands opaque ΔM channels **MUST** be treated as OOA-consistent unless binding
  compensators exist.

## B03.CH08.P02.S04 — Minimal recognition rule: “where is the extraction, and who can block it?”

The minimal competence threshold for this interface is the **recognition rule**:

> **(1) Where is the extraction produced and booked?**
> **(2) Who has the blocking power that makes it enforceable?**

This rule prevents order debates from collapsing into ideology, intention, or moral accusation. Extraction (E) is not defined by dislike of profit;
it is defined by a **toll mechanism**: a claim that persists because an actor (or coalition) can block access, raise switching costs, or enforce
non-contestable terms. The “who can block it” clause forces identification of the **control surface**: legal rights, de facto market power,
platform governance, licensing, land scarcity, data lock-in, or regulatory capture.

### Core principles

* **Locate, then label**: no classification without locating the extraction site and the enforcement surface.
* **Block power is decisive**: extraction is sustained by the ability to deny access or impose terms; remove block power and E collapses.
* **Audit-first skepticism**: when extraction cannot be located or attributed, treat claims as untestable rather than assumed benign.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every classification output **MUST** explicitly answer both recognition questions (where is E; who can block).
* If either question cannot be answered with evidence hooks (data/logs/legal mapping), the result **MUST** be **Unknown/Untestable**.
* When “block power” is diffuse (e.g., oligopoly, standards control, network effects), the interface **MUST** map the coalition surface and identify
  the minimum intervention points required to restore contestability.
* It is **FORBIDDEN** to accept “competitive market” assertions as sufficient without mechanism-level proof of contestability (exit, interoperability,
  switching costs, and anti-capture constraints).
