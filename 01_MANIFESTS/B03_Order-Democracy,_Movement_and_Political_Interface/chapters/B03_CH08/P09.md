# B03.CH08.P09 — Institutional interface: translation into mandate and decision procedures

This paragraph defines the **institutional interface**: the layer that translates OBA language and tests into **mandates, decision procedures, and
governance constraints** that survive electoral cycles and adversarial pressure. The institutional interface exists because “good intentions” and
one-off reforms do not hold against capture. To be OBA-compatible, institutions must encode (i) **testable goals per pillar**, (ii) **hard stops**
when measurability or enforcement fails, (iii) **procedural guards** that prevent rule-writing and auditing from being captured, and (iv) **explicit
variant/fork labeling** to prevent semantic blending.

## Core principles

* **Mandate = rails + evidence**: mandates are written as testable pillar goals with measurement and correction duties.
* **Guardrails are constitutional**: separation of functions, audit-of-auditors, and disclosure rails are part of the order, not optional “good governance.”
* **Exception discipline is binding politics**: sunsets and rollback triggers convert promises into enforceable constraints.
* **Pluralism without drift**: variants are permitted only when explicitly labeled and mapped; blending is treated as washing.

## B03.CH08.P09.S01 — Mandate in order terms: testable goals per pillar + hard stops

An OBA mandate is not written as “increase growth” or “improve fairness.” It is written as a set of **testable goals per pillar** with explicit
measurement and **hard stops**. Each pillar’s goal is an observable claim: if it cannot be measured or audited, it cannot be the basis of authority.

### Mandatory mandate structure (pillars)

For each pillar, the mandate specifies:

* **Target** (direction + threshold): what must improve and by how much (with distributional requirements where relevant).
* **Mechanism scope**: which domains/channels are in scope (e.g., essentials toll sectors, cap base, commons domains, ΔM channel rules).
* **Measurement regime**: definition version, data fields, computation rule, frequency, uncertainty handling.
* **Audit regime**: independent verification, publication requirements, restatement rules.
* **Correction regime**: automatic triggers, rollback/tightening steps, escalation paths.

### Canonical pillars (minimum)

* **E**: measurable reduction of extraction/toll claims (function-over-label).
* **R/B**: improvement in restruimte distribution with explicit lower-bound floors.
* **Caps**: binding caps (or equivalents) with anti-bypass consolidation and enforcement metrics.
* **Commons/ΔG**: capacity/quality maintenance and expansion under boundary rules.
* **ΔM**: channel discipline; allocation must be auditable and contestable.
* **Auditability**: public definitions/logging; replicability; contestability; restatements.

### Hard stops (non-negotiable)

* **No measurement → no mandate expansion**: if data/logs are missing, scope is reduced to auditable subset.
* **Audit failure → automatic correction**: failed audits trigger rollback/tightening, not discretionary “review.”
* **Opacity trigger**: introduction of black-box scoring or sealed allocation is treated as mandate breach.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any institutional mandate claiming OBA compatibility **MUST** be expressible as testable goals on each canonical pillar.
* Mandates **MUST** include at least one hard stop per pillar (what breaks authority when evidence fails).
* Where uncertainty is high, mandates **MUST** adopt conservative defaults and “unknown” classification rather than optimistic interpretation.

## B03.CH08.P09.S02 — Procedural guards: separation of functions, audit of auditors, disclosure rails

Institutional power is where OBA can be quietly neutralised. The institutional interface therefore requires **procedural guards**: structural separation,
meta-audit, and disclosure rails that prevent capture through definition control, enforcement discretion, or information monopoly.

### Required procedural guards (minimum set)

* **Separation of functions**:

  * rule definition / standard-setting
  * implementation / allocation
  * enforcement / sanctions
  * measurement / reporting
  * auditing / verification
    No single actor may control more than one critical function without explicit counter-rails.
* **Audit of auditors**: auditors are themselves audited for independence, methodology integrity, and conflict of interest; rotation and disclosure rules apply.
* **Disclosure rails**: mandatory publication of definition versions, rule changes, exception counts, enforcement outcomes, and material procurement/contract data.
* **Restatement protocol**: correction of errors propagates through dashboards and public summaries; “append-only corrections” are insufficient.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any institution operating under an OBA mandate **MUST** demonstrate separation of functions with explicit role boundaries and conflict rules.
* Auditors **MUST** be independently auditable; sealed methodologies are non-compliant for order claims.
* Disclosure rails **MUST** be legally binding and time-bounded (reporting frequency); voluntary transparency is not sufficient.
* If disclosure rails are weakened (delayed reporting, restricted access, proprietary metrics), the mandate status **MUST** be downgraded and breach flagged.

## B03.CH08.P09.S03 — Exception discipline: sunsets/rollback as a political binding mechanism

Exceptions are where political promises go to die. OBA-compatible institutions therefore treat exception discipline as a **binding mechanism**: it makes
capture visible and reversible by forcing “temporary” deviations to expire unless actively re-justified under audit.

### Exception discipline (required)

* **Explicit scope**: what is exempt, who is exempt, and what rail is affected.
* **Sunset date**: fixed expiry; renewal requires affirmative action and updated evidence.
* **Rollback triggers**: objective thresholds that auto-terminate or tighten the exception (e.g., bypass rate, leakage, E not falling, audit failure).
* **Exception ledger**: public registry of exceptions, take-up, renewals, and measured impacts on axes.
* **Complexity budget**: maximum permitted exception complexity; beyond it, the policy must be redesigned or narrowed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any exception affecting caps, ΔM channels, auditability, or commons access **MUST** be sunsetted and tied to rollback triggers.
* Renewal without updated audit evidence **MUST** be treated as capture signal and trigger downgrade of institutional compatibility.
* High exception ratio (exceptions per rule), repeated renewals, or low sanction rates **MUST** prompt mandatory review and potential hard stop invocation.

## B03.CH08.P09.S04 — Fork interface: explicitly labeling variants instead of semantic blending

Pluralism is compatible with OBA only if semantic integrity is maintained. Institutions therefore require a **fork interface**: a formal process for
labeling and managing variants, rather than blending definitions until “OBA” means whatever the incumbent needs.

### Fork interface components (required)

* **Variant/fork classification**: determine whether a deviation is a variant (parametric within rails), extension (additive), or fork (breaks rails).
* **Labeling rules**: reserved name usage (“OBA”, “OBA-compatible”, “OBA-inspired”) with public disclosure of deviations.
* **Compatibility scorecard**: explicit pass/fail (or graded) results on the canonical pillars, with evidence references.
* **Registry and version pinning**: unique IDs for variants/forks, dates, rationale, scope, and mapping to canonical definition sets.
* **Bridging and migration**: mappings that allow comparison and migration back to compatible rails where possible.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any institutional deviation from canonical definitions **MUST** be labeled and registered; semantic blending is **FORBIDDEN**.
* Institutions **MUST** publish a compatibility scorecard for any variant/fork they adopt, including auditability and exception profiles.
* Use of “OBA” branding without disclosure of deviations **MUST** be classified as **OBA-washing** at the institutional level.
* If a fork breaks measurability/auditability/contestability, it **MUST** be treated as non-compatible, regardless of rhetorical justification.
