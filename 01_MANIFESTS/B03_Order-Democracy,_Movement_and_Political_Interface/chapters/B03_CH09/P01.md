# B03.CH09.P01 — Purpose and delimitation: representation as a mechanism to make order criteria governable

This paragraph defines **representation and participation** as **order mechanisms**: they convert distributed experience, conflict, and local knowledge
into *governable* decisions under OBA rails. Representation is not treated as a moral ideal (“let people speak”), but as a **control surface** that can
either (i) bind institutions to measurable order criteria (E↓, caps integrity, R/B floors, commons ΔG discipline, testable ΔM, auditability), or (ii)
expand discretion and open capture lanes. The chapter therefore starts by fixing the purpose and delimitation: **participation must be role-bound,
authority-bound, and evidence-bound**, and it must include contestable appeal rails and correction paths.

## Core principles

* **Representation is an execution interface**: it is the mechanism by which order criteria become operational decisions.
* **Participation is not a virtue**: it is a design choice that must be evaluated by capture risk and auditability.
* **Roles precede voice**: without explicit role definitions, “participation” becomes a discretionary gateway for organised interests.
* **Evidence and appeal are mandatory**: legitimacy under OBA requires measurable inputs, contestability, and correction mechanisms.

## B03.CH09.P01.S01 — Why “participation” without role and authority definitions facilitates capture

“Participation” is routinely weaponised. When participation is defined as *anyone can influence decisions* without specifying (a) **who participates**
(role), (b) **what they are allowed to decide** (authority), and (c) **what evidence is required** (inputs), the system creates an open channel that
is dominated by high-capacity actors: organised lobbies, incumbents, professional gatekeepers, and well-resourced NGOs. The result is not democracy;
it is **procedural capture**.

Capture occurs through predictable mechanisms:

* **Agenda capture**: high-capacity actors set what is discussable and what is excluded, using attention, media access, and institutional proximity.
* **Process capture**: participation becomes a negotiation arena; decisions shift from rule-based to case-by-case bargaining and exception granting.
* **Measurement capture**: actors shape what is measured (and what is not), substituting proprietary metrics or selective KPIs for OBA rail deltas.
* **Fatigue capture**: broad participation demands time; over time only professional participants remain, turning “voice” into an occupational niche.

Under OBA, this is not an abstract risk. Participation without role/authority definitions directly threatens **caps**, **auditability**, and **exception
discipline**: the exact surfaces where OBA collapses back into OOA via carve-outs and opacity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Participation channels **MUST** be defined by explicit **role categories** and **authority scopes**; open-ended “stakeholder participation” is non-compliant.
* Any participation input that can affect caps, ΔM channels, commons access, or audit rules **MUST** be logged, attributable, and contestable.
* Participation designs **MUST** include anti-capture measures: representative balancing, rotation, transparency of interests, and limits on repeated influence.
* If role/authority are not specified, the participation mechanism **MUST** be classified as **capture-prone** and cannot be used to claim OBA legitimacy.

## B03.CH09.P01.S02 — Delimitation: not a constitutional handbook, but role structures and decision interfaces

This chapter is not a general theory of the state and not a constitutional handbook. It does not prescribe party systems, electoral formulas, or
complete legal frameworks. Its scope is narrower and more operational:

* **Role structures**: which actor positions exist in OBA-democracy (citizen, worker, commons member/user, state custodian, intermediary).
* **Decision interfaces**: where these roles enter decisions (mandate granting, parameter setting, operational governance, oversight, appeal).
* **Authority separation**: which domains are constitutive (rails), parametric (within rails), and operational (execution within budgets and standards).
* **Evidence rails**: what must be measurable and replicable to treat a participation outcome as legitimate.
* **Appeal and correction**: how errors, bias, and capture are detected and reversed.

The delimitation matters because “participation” debates often drift into total constitutional redesign. OBA needs a portable, testable interface layer
that can be embedded in different jurisdictions while keeping semantic integrity and anti-capture constraints.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* The chapter’s outputs **MUST** be implementable as modular interfaces (role definitions, decision-domain rules, evidence/appeal rails).
* The chapter **MUST NOT** be read as prescribing a single constitutional form; it prescribes constraints that any form must satisfy to remain OBA-compatible.
* Any institutional design that expands discretionary power without auditability **MUST** be treated as incompatible regardless of participatory rhetoric.

## B03.CH09.P01.S03 — Relation to commons governance (B03.CH04) and mandate (B03.CH03)

Representation and participation are not standalone. In OBA they attach to two upstream structures:

1. **Mandate and re-authorisation (B03.CH03)**
   Citizens, as sovereign voters, grant and renew authority through mandates that must be written in **order terms** (testable pillar goals, hard stops,
   audit duties). Participation mechanisms cannot override mandates; they operate inside them. Where participation outputs conflict with mandate rails,
   the mandate prevails unless a constitutive change procedure is invoked.

2. **Commons governance (B03.CH04)**
   Commons are governed through boundary rules, stewardship duties, and contestability. Commons membership and user feedback are not generic “consultation”;
   they are a governance channel with rights and duties. The participation architecture in this chapter therefore imports commons governance constraints:
   access rules, maintenance obligations, complaint/appeal, and sanctions for gating or capture attempts.

Together, these interfaces ensure that participation does not dissolve into free-floating opinion. It becomes a structured input to mandate execution
and commons stewardship, with explicit authority and evidence constraints.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Participation outputs **MUST** be mappable to the mandate layer (what mandate goal/pillar they affect) and to the commons governance layer where relevant.
* Participation mechanisms **MUST NOT** alter constitutive rails (definitions, caps integrity, auditability requirements) without explicit constitutive procedures.
* Commons-related participation **MUST** include membership criteria, stewardship duties, and complaint/appeal rails consistent with B03.CH04.

## B03.CH09.P01.S04 — Output: participation models + role interfaces + evidence and appeal rails

The output of this chapter is a set of **participation models** and **role interfaces** that can be used to design institutions without semantic drift.
These outputs are evaluated by their ability to:

* **Bind decisions to rails**: participation must strengthen E-reduction, caps integrity, R/B floors, commons ΔG discipline, testable ΔM, auditability.
* **Minimise capture**: participation must not become an exception factory or an access privilege for high-capacity groups.
* **Maintain contestability**: decisions must be challengeable; measurement must be replicable; corrections must restate outcomes.
* **Separate decision domains**: constitutive vs parametric vs operational vs emergency decisions must have distinct procedures and authority requirements.

### Output objects (normative)

* **Participation models** (architectures): electoral, corporatist/professional, sortition panels, multi-stakeholder commons boards, hybrid dual-key forms.
* **Role interfaces**: defined rights and duties for citizen, worker, commons member/user, state custodian, and intermediaries.
* **Evidence rails**: logging, definition-set pinning, replicable measurement, publication requirements.
* **Appeal rails**: objection windows, independent review, escalation triggers, rollback requirements, sanction paths for abuse/capture.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any participation model claiming OBA legitimacy **MUST** specify: roles, authority scope, evidence rails, and appeal/correction mechanisms.
* Participation outputs **MUST** be logged (decision log + rationale + definition versions) and must support independent replication where feasible.
* If appeal rails are absent or toothless (no remedy, no rollback, no sanctions), the participation model **MUST** be classified as façade participation.
