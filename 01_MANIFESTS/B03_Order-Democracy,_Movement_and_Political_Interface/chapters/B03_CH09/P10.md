# B03.CH09.P10 — Governance protocols: formal processes for decision and correction

This paragraph defines **governance protocols**: the formal process layer that makes representation and participation operational and auditable. In OBA,
protocols are not bureaucracy for its own sake; they are **anti-capture and anti-regression mechanisms**. Without protocols, decisions become
non-reconstructible, consultation becomes theatre, escalation becomes discretionary, rollback becomes politically impossible, and evaluation becomes
narrative. Therefore OBA requires standardized processes for: decision logging, consultation handling, escalation, rollback/exitability, and periodic
re-authorisation linked to the democratic consistency test.

## Core principles

* **Every decision must be reconstructible**: without logs and definition versions, governance cannot be audited or corrected.
* **Consultation must have handling rules**: inputs must be processed transparently, not harvested selectively.
* **Escalation must be pre-committed**: triggers and timelines prevent suppressing bad news.
* **Rollback must be executable**: technical and contractual exitability is part of legitimacy.
* **Evaluation closes the loop**: re-authorisation links governance to evidence and to B03.CH12 drift detection.

## B03.CH09.P10.S01 — Decision log: rationale, data inputs, definition set, versions

A decision log is the minimum unit of accountable governance. It prevents “policy by vibe” and enables restatement when assumptions or data are wrong.
In OBA, the log is not optional metadata; it is the mechanism that binds a decision to rails.

### Required fields (minimum decision log schema)

* **Decision ID**: unique identifier; domain (constitutive/parametric/operational/emergency/fork).
* **Authority chain**: who decided, under which mandate clause, with which required approvals (dual-key, etc.).
* **Rationale**: mechanism statement in rail terms (expected deltas on E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM, auditability).
* **Data inputs**: datasets used, collection dates, coverage, known gaps, uncertainty bands.
* **Definition set**: canonical definition-set ID and version pinned to the decision.
* **Alternatives considered**: options, trade-offs, and why rejected (axis-based).
* **Risk and bypass analysis**: predicted evasion/capture routes and planned counter-rails.
* **Evaluation triggers**: what signals will trigger review, tightening, rollback, or restatement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* All decisions affecting caps, ΔM channels, commons access, auditability, or sanctions **MUST** have a complete decision log with pinned definitions.
* Logs **MUST** be publishable in privacy-safe form; decisions that cannot be logged publicly are presumptively non-compatible unless explicitly justified.
* If a decision is made without a log, subsequent evaluation **MUST** classify it as non-auditable and demand correction.

## B03.CH09.P10.S02 — Consultation: timelines, representative sampling, public comment handling

Consultation is a channel that often degenerates into performative “stakeholder engagement.” OBA requires a consultation protocol that constrains capture
and preserves evidence: inputs are collected, processed, and responded to under transparent rules.

### Consultation protocol components (minimum)

* **Timelines**: fixed windows; early publication of proposals; minimum time for response; emergency consultation rules distinct and sunsetted.
* **Representative sampling**: where panels are used, apply stratified sampling rather than self-selection; disclose sampling frame.
* **Comment intake and taxonomy**: standard categories tied to axes (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM, auditability) and to implementation risks.
* **Handling and response rules**: publish a comment digest; show which comments changed the proposal and why; log rejections with rationale.
* **Conflict disclosure**: commenters representing organisations disclose funding/interest; submissions are attributable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Consultation **MUST** include a published handling report: intake counts, category distribution, and disposition (accepted/partially/ rejected).
* If consultation outcomes are not traceable to changes (or explicit non-changes), it is classified as tokenistic.
* High-risk domains (caps, auditability, procurement, ΔM allocation) **MUST** use representative mechanisms and cannot rely solely on open comment volume.

## B03.CH09.P10.S03 — Escalation: triggers, timelines, authority chain

Escalation protocols prevent the suppression of failures. In OBA, escalation is pre-committed: when specified signals occur, review and corrective action
must start automatically, regardless of political convenience.

### Escalation triggers (canonical)

* **E regression**: extraction proxies rise beyond tolerance bands.
* **Exception stacking**: exceptions increase, renewals occur without evidence, or exception ratio crosses threshold.
* **Sanction collapse**: enforcement actions fall relative to detected breaches; selective enforcement signals rise.
* **ΔG degradation**: maintenance backlog grows; access/quality metrics fall.
* **Auditability breach**: missing logs, sealed methodologies, vendor obstruction, or replication failure.
* **Abuse/capture signals**: whistleblower reports, procurement anomalies, threshold gaming indicators.

### Escalation protocol fields (required)

* **Trigger definition**: metric + threshold + time window; uncertainty handling.
* **Timeline**: maximum time to acknowledge, investigate, publish interim findings, and decide correction.
* **Authority chain**: who must act at each step; when independent auditors/ombuds are activated.
* **Publication duty**: escalation notice and outcomes must be logged and disclosed (privacy-safe).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Systems **MUST** publish escalation triggers and timelines in advance; ad hoc escalation is non-compliant.
* Trigger activation **MUST** be logged and visible; silent suppression is a legitimacy breach.
* If the authority chain can veto escalation without public rationale and external review, the protocol is capture-prone.

## B03.CH09.P10.S04 — Rollback: conditions, technical/contractual exitability

Rollback is the ability to reverse or tighten policies when evidence contradicts assumptions or when capture is detected. Without rollback, errors
become path-dependent infrastructure. OBA therefore treats rollback as both **procedural** and **technical/contractual** capability.

### Rollback conditions (minimum)

* **Failure to meet rail targets**: E not falling; R/B floors breached; ΔG declining; caps avoidance rising.
* **Audit failure**: replication breaks; logs missing; auditors compromised; vendor blocks access.
* **Capture indicators**: exception stacking; procurement anomalies; selective enforcement patterns.
* **Crisis expiry**: emergency measures must expire or be re-authorised with evidence.

### Exitability requirements (minimum)

* **Technical exitability**: data portability, open interfaces where feasible, ability to switch vendors without service collapse.
* **Contractual exitability**: termination clauses, escrow/continuity, audit access rights, penalties for obstruction.
* **Operational contingency**: fallback modes that preserve essentials continuity during rollbacks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-risk policies and systems **MUST** have pre-declared rollback conditions and an executable rollback plan.
* Vendor-dependent systems **MUST** include exitability clauses; non-exitable contracts are a structural capture vector.
* When rollback triggers fire, failure to act **MUST** be treated as mandate breach and logged as an incident.

## B03.CH09.P10.S05 — Evaluation: periodic re-authorisation and linkage to the B03.CH12 test

Evaluation closes the governance loop: it connects participation and execution back to citizen authorisation. OBA evaluation is not a press release; it is
a periodic, evidence-based re-authorisation process linked to the **B03.CH12 democratic consistency test** (drift detection, capture indicators, and
compatibility status).

### Evaluation outputs (minimum)

* **Pillar performance report**: E, R/B distribution floors, caps compliance, ΔG metrics, ΔM testability, auditability status.
* **Legitimacy panel**: contestability, transparency, definition-set compliance, incident review completion, correction follow-through.
* **Exception and enforcement report**: exception ratio, renewals, sanction rate, selective enforcement signals.
* **Fork/variant registry update**: any deviations labeled, scored, and mapped to canonical definitions.
* **Incident review ledger**: what failed, what was corrected, what remains open, and by when.

### Re-authorisation discipline

* Evaluation feeds citizen re-authorisation: mandates are renewed, adjusted parametrically, or withdrawn based on evidence.
* Where performance is unknown (data missing), classification defaults conservative; authority narrows to auditable scope (fail-closed).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Institutions **MUST** run periodic evaluations on a fixed schedule and publish the full evidence bundle (privacy-safe).
* Evaluations **MUST** explicitly reference B03.CH12 criteria and output a compatibility/drift status.
* If evaluation is delayed, incomplete, or non-replicable, legitimacy is downgraded and re-authorisation cannot proceed without corrective action.
