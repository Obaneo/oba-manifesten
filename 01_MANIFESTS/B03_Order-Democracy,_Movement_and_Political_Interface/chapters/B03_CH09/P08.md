# B03.CH09.P08 — Commons membership: rights and duties

This paragraph specifies **commons membership** as a role with explicit **rights and duties**. In OBA, “commons” is not a romantic label; it is a
governed access regime designed to substitute dependence on toll positions. Therefore membership must be defined in a way that preserves:
(i) **open access where feasible**, (ii) **boundary rules** that prevent depletion and gating, (iii) **representation structures** that block capture by
high-capacity users or operators, (iv) **stewardship duties** that keep ΔG maintained, and (v) **contestable complaint and sanction rails** that keep
the commons from becoming a private club or a decaying public good.

## Core principles

* **Membership is an interface contract**: rights are granted in exchange for compliance with boundary rules and stewardship duties.
* **Open access is conditional**: openness requires explicit boundary discipline; otherwise commons degrade or are captured.
* **Influence must be capped**: governance must prevent dominance by heavy users, incumbents, or organised blocs.
* **Contestability is legitimacy**: exclusion, sanctions, and boundary rule changes must be appealable with evidence.
* **Sanctions protect openness**: sanctions exist to keep access fair and to block gating and capture attempts.

## B03.CH09.P08.S01 — Membership criteria: open access vs contribution; boundary rules explicit

Membership criteria define who can access a commons and under what conditions. OBA distinguishes two base patterns:

* **Open access**: any eligible person can use the commons under fair-use and anti-abuse rules.
* **Contribution-linked access**: access requires contribution (time, maintenance, fees at cost, compliance tasks) where scarcity or upkeep demands it.

Both patterns are compatible with OBA only if the boundary rules are explicit, auditable, and non-discriminatory in effect.

### Required membership fields (minimum)

* **Eligibility criteria**: who qualifies (jurisdiction, residency, need, usage class) and what is explicitly *not* required.
* **Access conditions**: fair-use rules, capacity limits, booking/queue rules, and anti-gating rules.
* **Contribution obligations** (if any): what counts as contribution, proportionality, exemptions, and how contributions are verified.
* **Boundary rules**: what behaviors are prohibited (abuse, resale, hoarding, exclusion), and how violations are detected.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Membership criteria **MUST** be explicit, public, and versioned; informal gatekeeping is non-compliant.
* Contribution requirements **MUST** be proportional and must not recreate toll extraction; “contribution” cannot be used as a disguised price gate.
* Boundary rules **MUST** include measurable triggers and enforcement pathways; “community norms” alone is insufficient.

## B03.CH09.P08.S02 — Voting rights and representation: weightings, caps on influence, rotation

Commons governance fails when voting rights track capacity rather than membership: heavy users, operators, professionals, or organised interests can
dominate decisions and quietly introduce gating or exceptions. OBA therefore requires governance designs that cap influence and preserve contestability.

### Governance design primitives

* **Representation structure**: direct member vote, delegated representatives, or multi-stakeholder boards (members/users, operators, auditors, public).
* **Weighting rules**: if weights exist, they must be justified by role (e.g., stewardship responsibility), not by wealth or usage volume.
* **Caps on influence**: limits on vote concentration, term limits, and restrictions on bloc voting where it creates domination.
* **Rotation**: mandatory rotation for representatives and board members to prevent representational monopoly.
* **Conflict-of-interest rules**: disclosure, recusal, and cooling-off for operator and vendor-linked representatives.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Governance **MUST** include caps on influence and rotation rules; indefinite tenure or cumulative dominance is capture-prone.
* Operators/vendors **MUST NOT** control a majority of governance votes; if operational expertise is needed, it must be counterbalanced by independent audit.
* All governance bodies **MUST** publish decision logs, voting records (where appropriate), and COI disclosures.

## B03.CH09.P08.S03 — Stewardship duties: maintenance, contribution, compliance with rules

Commons substitution only works if the commons is maintained. OBA therefore treats stewardship not as volunteerism but as a structured duty set linked to
ΔG discipline: upkeep, responsible use, and compliance with boundary rules.

### Stewardship duty categories (minimum)

* **Maintenance duty**: obligations for upkeep funding/planning, member contributions where applicable, and transparency of backlog/quality metrics.
* **Contribution duty**: where contribution-linked access exists, duties must be defined, verifiable, and non-extractive.
* **Compliance duty**: adherence to boundary rules; non-abuse, non-resale, and non-gating obligations.
* **Reporting duty**: members/users have a right (and in some models a duty) to report degradation, abuse, and capture attempts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every commons **MUST** publish a maintenance plan and ΔG metrics (capacity, quality, backlog) with review cycles.
* Where member contribution is required, the system **MUST** provide exemptions/support to prevent exclusion of low-capacity users.
* Stewardship duties **MUST** be enforceable and appealable; informal social enforcement alone is insufficient.

## B03.CH09.P08.S04 — Complaints and appeal: contestability with evidence (logs, metrics)

Commons legitimacy depends on contestability: users must be able to challenge exclusion, gating, arbitrary decisions, and maintenance neglect. OBA
therefore requires complaint and appeal rails that are evidence-bound and outcome-capable (real remedies).

### Complaint/appeal stack (minimum)

* **Complaint intake**: accessible channels (digital + non-digital), clear categories, acknowledgement and timelines.
* **Evidence linkage**: ability to reference logs, metrics, queue records, access decisions, maintenance schedules, and procurement data where relevant.
* **Independent review**: an ombuds/audit function not controlled by operators or dominant member blocs.
* **Remedy set**: reversal of wrongful exclusion, correction of boundary rules, sanctions for gating, mandated maintenance actions, restatements.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every commons governance regime **MUST** include complaint and appeal routes with independent review and published outcomes.
* Where logs/metrics are required for contestability, refusal to provide them **MUST** be treated as an auditability breach.
* Appeals **MUST** be usable by low-capacity users (support provisions); otherwise contestability is de facto restricted.

## B03.CH09.P08.S05 — Sanctions: for abuse, gating, capture attempts, maintenance neglect

Sanctions protect openness by making abuse and capture costly. Without sanctions, boundary rules are symbolic and heavy users or operators can create
private toll-like control within the commons. Sanctions must be proportional, evidence-bound, and appealable.

### Sanction categories (canonical)

* **Abuse sanctions**: for hoarding, sabotage, resale, fraud, harassment, or repeated boundary violations.
* **Gating sanctions**: for exclusionary practices, queue manipulation, preferential access sales, or informal gatekeeping networks.
* **Capture sanctions**: for undisclosed conflicts, vote manipulation, procurement corruption, exception stacking, or intimidation of whistleblowers.
* **Maintenance neglect sanctions**: for failure to meet maintenance obligations, backlog concealment, or falsified quality reporting.

### Sanction design requirements

* **Due process**: notice, evidence disclosure, hearing/response opportunity, appeal route.
* **Proportionality**: graduated sanctions; protect essentials access where possible while stopping abuse.
* **Transparency**: publication of anonymized sanction statistics, categories, and outcomes to detect selective enforcement.
* **Escalation**: repeated or systemic abuse triggers governance intervention (board replacement, external administration, tightened rules).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Commons regimes **MUST** specify sanction ladders and enforcement authority; “community moderation” without due process is non-compliant.
* If sanction rate collapses while abuse indicators rise, an incident review and governance intervention **MUST** be triggered.
* Sanctions **MUST** be appealable and logged; secret or selective enforcement is incompatible with OBA legitimacy.
