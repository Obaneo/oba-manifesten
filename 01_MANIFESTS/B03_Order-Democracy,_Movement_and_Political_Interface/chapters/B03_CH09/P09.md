# B03.CH09.P09 — Role of the state: execution without paternalistic or authoritarian drift

This paragraph specifies the **state role** under OBA as **order custodian**: an actor responsible for executing mandates, enforcing rails, maintaining
auditability, and managing exceptions—without expanding into paternalistic control or authoritarian discretion. The state is treated neither as a
moral savior nor as an inherent threat; it is a functional node that becomes dangerous precisely when its authority is **unbounded**, **opaque**, or
**non-contestable**. Therefore, OBA-state design is defined by **separation of functions**, **transparency and restatements**, **privacy-proportionality
constraints**, **bounded emergency regimes**, and **anti-capture procurement and vendor exitability**.

## Core principles

* **Custodianship, not ownership**: the state executes and enforces order criteria; it does not own society’s choices beyond the mandate.
* **Power must be decomposed**: execution, oversight, audit, and adjudication must not collapse into one chain.
* **Transparency is enforcement infrastructure**: definition sets, dashboards, registers, and restatements make drift visible and correctable.
* **Privacy is a rail**: data collection must be minimal and linkages must be explicitly prohibited.
* **Capture is expected**: procurement and vendor dependency are primary infiltration routes; exitability and audit-of-auditors are mandatory.

## B03.CH09.P09.S01 — Separation of functions: execution vs oversight vs audit vs judicial review

The main pathway from “state capacity” to authoritarian drift is **function fusion**: when the same chain that executes policy also defines metrics,
audits itself, and adjudicates disputes. OBA requires a decomposition of state power into separable roles with explicit boundaries and mutual checks.

### Required function separation (minimum)

* **Execution**: delivery, operations, procurement, staffing, service provision.
* **Oversight**: mandate monitoring, performance governance, escalation triggers, board interventions.
* **Measurement/reporting**: data production under definition sets; publication schedules; reproducibility support.
* **Audit/verification**: independent verification, audit rights enforcement, incident reviews, restatement requirements.
* **Judicial/appeal review**: adjudication of disputes, rights protection, remedies for wrongful exclusion/sanction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* No single institution or chain of command **MUST** control both execution and audit of the same domain without an independent counter-rail.
* Oversight bodies **MUST** have authority to trigger audits and enforce restatements, not merely to “recommend.”
* Appeal and judicial review **MUST** be accessible and empowered to order remedies, not only to validate process.
* If separation is weakened (merged functions, subordinated auditors, limited review), the system **MUST** be flagged as drift-risk and downgraded in legitimacy.

## B03.CH09.P09.S02 — Transparency: definition sets, dashboards, registers, restatements

OBA-state legitimacy depends on making state action **legible and replicable**. Transparency is not “open government” branding; it is the mechanism that
prevents silent re-routing of ΔM, exception stacking, and selective enforcement. The state must therefore operate under explicit publication rails.

### Mandatory transparency artefacts (minimum)

* **Definition sets**: public, versioned, and pinned to decisions; changes require changelogs and bridging notes.
* **Dashboards**: E proxies, R/B distribution, caps compliance, ΔG metrics, ΔM channel ledgers, exception ratio, sanction rate.
* **Registers**: exceptions registry (scope/sunset/renewals), procurement register (vendors/contracts), enforcement actions register (privacy-safe aggregation).
* **Restatements**: mandatory correction protocol when data/errors materially affect classifications or decisions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Decisions affecting caps, ΔM channels, commons access, or auditability **MUST** cite definition-set versions and be reconstructible from published logs.
* Dashboards and registers **MUST** be published on fixed schedules; discretionary publication is non-compliant.
* Errors **MUST** trigger restatements that propagate to downstream summaries; append-only “corrections” without propagation are insufficient.
* Refusal to publish methodologies or reliance on proprietary metrics for core order claims **MUST** trigger downgrade and washing-risk flags.

## B03.CH09.P09.S03 — Proportionality/privacy: data minimalism and prohibited linkages

State capacity often expands via data linkage: “to enforce X, we must connect everything.” OBA rejects this default. **Privacy is a rail**, not a
tradeable convenience. The state’s information regime must be minimal, purpose-bound, and explicitly constrained against cross-domain linkage that
creates surveillance power or political weaponization capacity.

### Privacy/proportionality primitives (required)

* **Data minimalism**: collect only what is necessary for the tested function; prefer aggregation and sampling over individual-level capture.
* **Purpose binding**: explicit scope of use; re-use requires renewed authorisation and impact analysis.
* **Prohibited linkages**: explicit lists of forbidden joins (e.g., service access logs ↔ policing/intelligence; welfare ↔ political profiling; health ↔ enforcement beyond narrow fraud proof).
* **Separation of identifiers**: technical and legal constraints that prevent silent cross-database correlation.
* **Audit of access**: access logs, permissioning, and publication of access statistics (privacy-safe).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any data collection for OBA enforcement **MUST** specify: purpose, fields, retention, linkage prohibitions, and audit access rules.
* Cross-domain linkage **MUST** be forbidden by default and allowed only via special procedure with independent review and strict sunset/rollback.
* If the state expands data linkage without explicit prohibitions and audits, the system **MUST** be treated as authoritarian-drift risk and downgraded.

## B03.CH09.P09.S04 — Crisis role: emergency regime with clear exit and ex post audits

Crises compress time and widen discretion. Historically, crisis governance becomes the route for permanent surveillance expansion, procurement opacity,
and exception stacking. OBA allows emergency action only through a **bounded emergency regime** that maintains auditability and forces exit.

### Emergency regime requirements (minimum)

* **Clear scope**: what powers are activated, for which domains, with what objectives.
* **Sunset by default**: hard expiry dates; renewals require affirmative re-authorisation and updated evidence.
* **Rollback triggers**: objective conditions that terminate emergency measures early or tighten them if abused.
* **Ex post audits**: mandatory incident review, publication of procurement and allocation logs, and restatements for material errors.
* **No hidden rail breaks**: auditability, disclosure rails, and appeal rights remain active unless explicitly suspended under special procedure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Emergency measures **MUST** include sunsets, rollback triggers, and ex post audits; absence is immediate downgrade.
* Emergency procurement **MUST** still satisfy minimum traceability and audit access; “speed” does not justify sealed contracting.
* If emergency regimes are repeatedly renewed without evidence, the system **MUST** be flagged for exception stacking and legitimacy regression.

## B03.CH09.P09.S05 — Anti-capture: procurement rails, vendor exitability, audit of auditors

Capture typically enters the state through **procurement** and **vendor dependence**: contracts become non-exitable, measurement becomes proprietary,
and auditing becomes attestational. OBA treats procurement design as a primary order rail, requiring structural exitability and independent meta-audit.

### Anti-capture mechanisms (minimum)

* **Procurement rails**: open criteria, conflict disclosure, competitive access, prohibition of bundled lock-in contracts, publication of key terms.
* **Vendor exitability**: data portability, open interfaces/standards where feasible, escrow/continuity clauses, and rights to replace vendors without service collapse.
* **Audit access clauses**: contracts must permit independent audit access to relevant systems/data for rail verification.
* **Audit of auditors**: auditors’ independence, funding, methodology, and conflicts are audited; rotation and publication are enforced.
* **Sanction discipline**: meaningful penalties for nondisclosure, procurement corruption, vendor obstruction of audits, and metric laundering.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any vendor involved in measurement, allocation, enforcement tooling, or audit infrastructure **MUST** be subject to exitability and independent audit rights.
* Proprietary “black-box” systems that determine eligibility, pricing, sanctions, or allocation **MUST** be treated as non-compatible unless fully contestable and replicable.
* Auditors **MUST** be independently auditable; if auditors cannot be audited, the regime is structurally capture-prone.
* Procurement opacity or non-exitable vendor dependence **MUST** trigger incident review and potential governance intervention.
