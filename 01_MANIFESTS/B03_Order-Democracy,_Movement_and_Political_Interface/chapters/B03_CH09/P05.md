# B03.CH09.P05 — Decision domains: what citizens/workers/commons members do and do not decide

This paragraph defines **decision domains**: the separation of what different roles may decide, and under which procedures. The purpose is to prevent
a recurring failure mode where “participation” becomes either (i) **total discretion** (everything negotiable → capture), or (ii) **total theatre**
(nothing decided → cynicism). OBA requires domain separation because order criteria are only governable when constitutive rails are protected, parametric
choices are contestable, operational execution is competent, and emergencies do not become permanent exceptions.

## Core principles

* **Domain separation is anti-capture infrastructure**: it limits what can be bargained away and where exceptions can be inserted.
* **Authority follows reversibility**: the more irreversible the decision, the stronger the procedure and legitimacy requirement.
* **Evidence discipline scales with stakes**: high-impact domains require stricter measurement, audit, and rollback triggers.
* **Fork clarity**: deviations must be explicit variants/forks, not semantic blending.

## B03.CH09.P05.S01 — Constitutive: only via special procedures (supermajority/dual-key)

**Constitutive decisions** change the rails themselves: definitions, cap bases, auditability requirements, mandate structure, and the rights/duties that
make commons governable. These decisions determine what “OBA” means in enforceable terms; therefore they must be rare, slow, and protected from
coalition opportunism.

### Constitutive scope (non-exhaustive)

* Definition-set changes that affect E measurement, cap bases, consolidation rules, or auditability.
* Changes to binding cap structure (W-MAX/I-MAX equivalents), anti-bypass equivalence doctrines.
* Changes to disclosure rails, audit rights, restatement protocols, or contestability mechanisms.
* Changes to commons boundary rule authority and sanction frameworks.

### Procedure profile (required)

* **Special procedure**: supermajority and/or dual-key approval; time delay; public publication and consultation.
* **Independent evaluation**: external audit of projected impacts and bypass risks; incident history review.
* **Compatibility scorecard**: explicit mapping of what rails change and why; version pinning and migration rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Constitutive decisions **MUST** use special procedures (supermajority and/or dual-key) and **MUST NOT** be made through routine executive discretion.
* Constitutive changes **MUST** include public changelogs, definition-set versioning, and migration/bridging notes.
* If constitutive rails are changed without explicit procedure, the system **MUST** be flagged as **illegitimate drift** (washing-risk at minimum).

## B03.CH09.P05.S02 — Parametric: rates/thresholds within rails, priorities, timing

**Parametric decisions** set adjustable parameters within protected rails: cap levels, thresholds, schedules, priorities, and transition timing. These
decisions are politically meaningful and legitimately contested, but they must remain **inside** the constitutive constraints (no stealth rail change).

### Parametric scope (non-exhaustive)

* Cap rates/levels and phase-in schedules (within fixed base and consolidation rules).
* Thresholds for commons eligibility, contribution requirements, or pricing bands (within boundary rules).
* Transition sequencing and prioritisation across sectors (e.g., which toll sectors are neutralised first).
* Budget prioritisation within mandated pillars (ΔG maintenance vs expansion; enforcement capacity allocation).

### Procedure profile (required)

* **Axis projection**: explicit projected deltas on E, R/B distribution, ΔG, ΔM channel discipline, auditability.
* **Gaming tests**: threshold/bunching analysis; entity splitting risk assessment.
* **Time-bound review**: scheduled evaluation checkpoints with rollback/tightening triggers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Parametric choices **MUST** be expressed as changes to explicit parameters, not as semantic reinterpretations of definitions.
* Any parametric threshold **MUST** include anti-gaming tests and a revision plan when gaming signals rise.
* Parametric decisions **MUST** be reversible through pre-declared review cycles; “set and forget” is non-compliant in adversarial domains.

## B03.CH09.P05.S03 — Operational: service levels, project selection, maintenance planning within budget rails

**Operational decisions** concern execution: service delivery, project selection, maintenance planning, staffing, procurement, and day-to-day governance
within budgets and standards. Operational discretion is necessary for competence, but it is also the primary entry point for vendor capture and opacity.
Therefore OBA constrains operations with logging, procurement rails, and audit-of-auditors.

### Operational scope (non-exhaustive)

* Commons service levels (uptime, coverage, quality), maintenance schedules, queue management.
* Project portfolio selection within a commons or public infrastructure program.
* Procurement choices (vendors, contracts), implementation sequencing, staffing and training.
* Enforcement operations: inspections, compliance processes, sanction execution.

### Procedure profile (required)

* **Decision log**: rationale, data inputs, definition versions, procurement references.
* **Procurement discipline**: open criteria, conflict disclosure, exitability requirements, audit access clauses.
* **Monitoring hooks**: operational KPIs tied to ΔG quality and access, and to anti-gating metrics.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Operational decisions **MUST** be logged and auditable; procurement opacity is non-compatible with OBA claims.
* Vendor or operator lock-in **MUST** be treated as an order risk; contracts require exitability and audit rights.
* Operational discretion **MUST NOT** be used to create de facto exceptions (informal waivers, selective enforcement); such patterns trigger incident reviews.

## B03.CH09.P05.S04 — Emergency decision: temporary powers with sunset/rollback and evidence discipline

**Emergency decisions** allow temporary deviations from normal process under shocks (supply crisis, war, pandemics, systemic failure). OBA permits
emergency authority only as a strictly bounded module because emergencies are the historical pathway for permanent exception stacking and asset-support
reflexes.

### Emergency scope (non-exhaustive)

* Temporary allocation powers to maintain essentials continuity.
* Temporary procurement acceleration with constrained vendor risk.
* Temporary price/usage rules to prevent scarcity profiteering.
* Temporary restrictions on specific behaviours that threaten commons integrity.

### Procedure profile (required)

* **Sunset by default**: hard expiry dates; renewal requires affirmative re-authorisation and evidence.
* **Rollback triggers**: objective conditions that terminate emergency measures early or tighten them if they are abused.
* **Ex post audit**: mandatory after-action review, publication of logs, restatements, and accountability measures.
* **No-rail breach**: auditability, definition-set pinning, and disclosure rails remain in force unless explicitly suspended under special procedure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Emergency powers **MUST** include sunsets, rollback triggers, and ex post audits; absence is immediate downgrade and capture-risk flag.
* Emergency ΔM expansion **MUST** be channel-testable; asset/claim support without toll neutralisation is classified as OOA-consistent.
* Emergency measures that degrade auditability or expand secrecy **MUST** be treated as high-risk and require independent oversight.

## B03.CH09.P05.S05 — Fork decision: explicit variant choice with name label and compatibility status

**Fork decisions** occur when an institution or jurisdiction chooses to deviate from canonical OBA rails or parameter sets. These decisions are
politically tempting because they allow coalition accommodation; they are also the primary vector for semantic drift (“OBA means whatever we do”).
OBA therefore requires a formal fork interface: variants must be explicit, named, registered, and scored.

### Fork decision scope (non-exhaustive)

* Adoption of alternative cap parameters or different commons boundary rules.
* Alternative ΔM channel designs (allocation mechanisms).
* Altered auditability regimes (e.g., different disclosure cadence or audit structure).
* Jurisdictional adaptations due to legal constraints.

### Procedure profile (required)

* **Classification**: variant vs extension vs fork (constitutive break).
* **Name label**: “OBA-compatible”, “OBA-partial”, “OBA-inspired”, or “non-compatible” as applicable.
* **Compatibility scorecard**: pass/fail (or graded) results on E, caps, R/B floors, ΔG discipline, ΔM testability, auditability.
* **Registry entry**: unique ID, scope, date, rationale, and bridging/migration notes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any deviation from canonical rails **MUST** be explicitly labeled and registered; semantic blending is **FORBIDDEN**.
* Fork decisions affecting measurability/auditability/contestability **MUST** be classified as non-compatible regardless of intent.
* Public communication **MUST** use the correct label; mislabeling triggers **washing** classification and correction requirements.
