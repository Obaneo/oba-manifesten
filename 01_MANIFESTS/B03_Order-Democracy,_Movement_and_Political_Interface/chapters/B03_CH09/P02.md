# B03.CH09.P02 — Actor roles: citizens, workers, commons members, and the state as distinct positions

This paragraph defines the **actor roles** used throughout OBA-democratic design. The purpose is to prevent a common failure mode: treating
“the people”, “the economy”, “civil society”, and “the state” as vague moral categories. In OBA, each role is a **position with specific authority,
exposure, duties, and evidence interfaces**. The same human can hold multiple roles (e.g., citizen + worker + commons user), but the roles must remain
conceptually distinct because they bind different decision rights and different accountability rails.

## Core principles

* **Roles are control surfaces**: they define who can grant authority, who can execute, who can monitor, and who can appeal.
* **Authority is domain-bound**: roles do not decide everything; they interface with constitutive, parametric, operational, and emergency domains.
* **Exposure shapes legitimacy**: roles capture different exposure to E and different stakes in R/B, commons quality, and ΔM allocation.
* **Evidence binding**: each role’s participation must be expressible in logs, metrics, and contestable procedures.

## B03.CH09.P02.S01 — Citizen as sovereign voter: mandate granting and re-authorisation

The **citizen** role is the *sovereign authoriser*. The citizen’s distinctive power is not day-to-day management, but the power to **grant, renew, and
withdraw mandates**. In OBA terms, citizenship is the role that binds institutions to **order criteria** over time by periodically re-authorising
authority under testable commitments.

### Functional profile (citizen role)

* **Mandate granting**: authorises institutions to pursue explicit order goals (pillars) and to use specified instruments within rails.
* **Re-authorisation**: renews authority based on evidence (dashboards, audit outcomes, incident reviews), not on narrative alone.
* **Constitutive constraint**: participates in changes to constitutive rails only through special procedures (supermajority/dual-key), not via routine politics.
* **Legitimacy oversight**: demands transparency, definition-set compliance, and contestability as a condition of continued authority.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Citizen-facing mandates **MUST** be expressed in order terms (pillar goals + hard stops + audit obligations), not in generic promises.
* Re-authorisation **MUST** be evidence-linked: institutions must publish the minimum evidence bundle needed for citizens to evaluate compliance.
* Citizens **MUST NOT** be asked to decide operational details without role separation; doing so is a capture and fatigue vector.

## B03.CH09.P02.S02 — Worker as economic actor: exposure to E and R/B; leverage through labour

The **worker** role is the actor position defined by economic dependence on wages/time and by direct exposure to extraction through **fixed burdens (L)**
and **restruimte (R/B)**. Workers also possess a distinctive leverage channel: **labour power** (collective bargaining, withdrawal of labour, shop-floor
knowledge) which can function as an order-corrective instrument when aligned with OBA rails.

### Functional profile (worker role)

* **High exposure**: workers typically bear a large share of toll-sector burdens (housing, energy, mobility, platform fees) relative to income.
* **Order-sensing**: workers observe fee-stacks, compliance sabotage, subcontract opacity, and real-world service failures earlier than macro dashboards do.
* **Leverage channel**: bargaining and collective action can enforce anti-extraction discipline when institutions drift or fail to sanction.
* **Transition risk**: workers can face timing asymmetry (costs now, benefits later); stabilisation modules (R/B floors, essentials continuity) are decisive.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Participation mechanisms for workers **MUST** include channels for monitoring and anomaly reporting with anti-retaliation protections.
* Worker input **MUST** be treated as evidence-bearing (logs, incident reports), not only as preference aggregation.
* Worker leverage mechanisms **MUST NOT** be neutralised through outsourcing opacity; if subcontract chains block attribution, auditability is breached.

## B03.CH09.P02.S03 — Commons member/user: access, quality feedback, stewardship duties

The **commons member/user** role is defined by **rights of access** and **duties of stewardship** within commons governance. Unlike “citizen voice,”
commons participation is not primarily about broad mandate; it is about maintaining and improving **commons capacity and quality (ΔG discipline)** and
enforcing boundary rules that prevent gating, abuse, and capture.

### Functional profile (commons member/user role)

* **Access rights**: entitlement to use the commons under boundary rules (eligibility, fair-use, contribution obligations where applicable).
* **Quality feedback**: structured reporting on service quality, maintenance backlogs, degradation, and exclusion patterns.
* **Stewardship duties**: compliance with rules; contribution to maintenance where specified; participation in governance when required by the model.
* **Contestability interface**: ability to file complaints, demand audits, and appeal governance decisions using evidence (logs/metrics).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Commons participation **MUST** specify membership criteria, boundary rules, and stewardship duties; “open for all” without rule discipline is non-compliant.
* Commons feedback **MUST** be routable into governance decisions and incident reviews; otherwise participation is tokenistic.
* Commons governance **MUST** include sanctions for gating/capture attempts and remedies for wrongful exclusion.

## B03.CH09.P02.S04 — State as order custodian: execution, enforcement, audit, exception management

The **state** role is defined as **order custodian**: it executes mandates, enforces rails, maintains auditability, and manages exceptions under strict
discipline. The state is not treated as a moral actor (“benevolent planner”) nor as an enemy (“intruder”), but as a functional necessity whenever
order requires **binding enforcement**, **public audit**, and **non-private legitimacy**.

### Functional profile (state role)

* **Execution**: implements policies and services within mandate constraints and budget rails; manages procurement and delivery.
* **Enforcement**: applies sanctions, closes bypass routes, prevents toll position entrenchment, and maintains contestability.
* **Audit and disclosure**: publishes definition sets, dashboards, ledgers, and restatements; enables independent replication.
* **Exception management**: authorises exceptions only with sunsets, rollback triggers, and public registries; prevents exception stacking.
* **Anti-authoritarian constraint**: must operate under separation of functions, judicial review, privacy minimalism, and prohibited linkages.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* The state **MUST** separate execution, enforcement, measurement, and audit functions; concentration of these functions triggers capture risk.
* Any use of discretionary power **MUST** be bounded by logs, public criteria, and appeal rights; otherwise it is incompatible with OBA legitimacy.
* Exception authority **MUST** be coupled to mandatory sunsets, rollback triggers, and publication of take-up and leakage.

## B03.CH09.P02.S05 — Intermediaries: unions, professional bodies, cooperatives, civil-society organisations

**Intermediaries** are organisations that aggregate participation, expertise, and bargaining power between individuals and institutions. They are not
automatically legitimate: they can reduce transaction costs and amplify weak voices, but they are also prime **capture vehicles**. OBA therefore treats
intermediaries as *licensed interfaces* with explicit accountability and rotation rules.

### Functional profile (intermediary role)

* **Aggregation**: represent dispersed members (workers, users, professionals) in negotiations and governance bodies.
* **Standards and integrity**: professional bodies can supply competence, ethics, and peer enforcement in execution/audit functions.
* **Cooperative provisioning**: cooperatives can operate commons-like services with user governance, reducing reliance on toll firms.
* **Watchdog function**: civil-society organisations can monitor, litigate, and publish analysis—if evidence and funding transparency exist.

### Capture risks (canonical)

* **Representational monopoly**: leadership ossifies; member voice is replaced by insider bargaining.
* **Funding capture**: intermediaries become dependent on state grants or corporate sponsorship and soften scrutiny.
* **Gatekeeping**: professional bodies use licensing to create toll positions rather than to protect integrity.
* **Opaque bargaining**: deals are struck without logs or member ratification, generating exception politics.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Intermediaries participating in OBA governance **MUST** disclose funding, conflicts of interest, and decision procedures; opaque intermediaries are non-compliant.
* Representation **MUST** include rotation or re-authorisation mechanisms to prevent entrenched leadership.
* Where intermediaries influence caps, procurement, audit standards, or commons access, their actions **MUST** be logged and contestable, with appeal rails for members and affected outsiders.
