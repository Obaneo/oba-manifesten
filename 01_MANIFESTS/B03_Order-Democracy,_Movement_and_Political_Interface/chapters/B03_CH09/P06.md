# B03.CH09.P06 — Legitimacy and fairness: inclusion, non-discrimination, and protection of minorities

This paragraph defines **legitimacy and fairness** as functional requirements for OBA-compatible representation and participation. In OBA, legitimacy is
not reduced to popularity or formal voting; it requires (i) **accessible participation**, (ii) **representative balance** that prevents domination by
high-capacity actors, (iii) **minority protection rails** that block selective enforcement and scapegoating, (iv) **generational rails** that prevent
maintenance neglect and future externalization, and (v) **conflict-of-interest regimes** that make capture visible and reversible. These are not
“ethical add-ons.” They are anti-capture and anti-regression infrastructure.

## Core principles

* **Legitimacy is procedural + epistemic**: decisions must be contestable, evidence-bound, and non-discriminatory in effect.
* **Inclusion is an enforcement condition**: if low-capacity groups cannot participate, organised interests gain de facto veto power.
* **Minority protection prevents regression**: without rails, crises and polarisation reintroduce OOA reflexes through selective enforcement.
* **Future duties are part of order**: commons and infrastructure require maintenance obligations that cannot be traded away for short-run gain.
* **Conflict discipline is structural**: undisclosed conflicts convert participation into covert procurement and exception politics.

## B03.CH09.P06.S01 — Accessibility: language, time, digital access, support

Accessibility determines who can use participation channels. Without explicit accessibility design, participation is biased toward those with time,
education, language fluency, and digital capability—i.e., high-capacity groups. OBA treats accessibility as a legitimacy precondition because it
constrains capture.

### Accessibility dimensions (minimum)

* **Language**: multi-language support where needed; plain-language summaries; defined term glossaries pinned to definition sets.
* **Time**: meeting times that do not systematically exclude shift workers and caregivers; asynchronous participation options.
* **Digital access**: low-bandwidth and non-digital pathways; public access points; accessibility compliance (disability support).
* **Support**: facilitation, explanation of axes/templates, legal aid for appeals, and anti-retaliation protections for whistleblowers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Participation channels **MUST** publish accessibility provisions (language, time windows, digital/non-digital routes, support services).
* If a channel cannot be used without specialised expertise, it **MUST** provide structured assistance or be classified as high-capacity biased.
* Appeals and complaint channels **MUST** include support measures; otherwise contestability is illusory.

## B03.CH09.P06.S02 — Representative balance: prevent over-representation of high-capacity groups

Even with open access, high-capacity groups can dominate by volume, expertise posture, organisational coordination, and repeated presence. OBA therefore
requires representative balancing: mechanisms that keep participation channels from becoming professionalised capture corridors.

### Balancing mechanisms (canonical)

* **Sampling/stratification**: for panels and consultations, use stratified sampling (income, region, age, work patterns) rather than self-selection.
* **Caps on repeated influence**: limits on consecutive terms, meeting attendance dominance, and committee stacking.
* **Transparency of interests**: mandatory disclosure of affiliations, funding, and represented constituencies.
* **Weighting corrections**: where self-selection remains, apply corrective weighting in input aggregation (documented and auditable).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-impact participation bodies **MUST** include balancing mechanisms (sampling or rotation) to prevent representational monopoly.
* Any body dominated by repeat participants without rotation **MUST** be flagged as capture-risk and subject to redesign.
* Interest and funding disclosures **MUST** be mandatory; anonymous organisational influence is non-compliant.

## B03.CH09.P06.S03 — Minority rails: vetoes, ombuds functions, anti-selective enforcement

Minority protection is not only about moral fairness; it is about preventing **selective enforcement** and scapegoating cycles that degrade rule-of-law
and reopen OOA dynamics. OBA requires minority rails that (i) provide remedies against discrimination, (ii) block arbitrary exclusion from commons, and
(iii) prevent enforcement from being weaponised.

### Minority protection instruments (minimum)

* **Ombuds functions**: independent ombuds with authority to investigate, publish findings, and trigger corrective action.
* **Veto/dual-key in sensitive domains**: for decisions that directly restrict rights or access, require higher thresholds or dual-key concurrence.
* **Anti-selective enforcement**: audit trails for enforcement actions (who is targeted, why), with bias detection and incident review triggers.
* **Appeal rights**: fast-track appeal routes for exclusion decisions and sanctions that affect essentials.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Systems **MUST** provide an independent ombuds or equivalent remedy institution with publication rights.
* Enforcement actions **MUST** be logged with attributable rationale and be reviewable for discrimination patterns.
* Where minority rails are absent, legitimacy claims **MUST** be downgraded because drift to selective enforcement is predictable under crisis pressure.

## B03.CH09.P06.S04 — Generational rails: maintenance obligations, future test, sunset discipline

OOA frequently externalizes to the future: underfund maintenance, run down commons, and push liabilities forward while preserving present claim values.
OBA therefore embeds generational rails into participation and representation: current decisions must carry **maintenance duties** and pass a **future
test**.

### Generational rail components (minimum)

* **Maintenance obligations**: mandatory funding and planning for upkeep of commons and infrastructure (ΔG discipline) with transparent backlog reporting.
* **Future test**: any major decision must include a forward impact projection on ΔG capacity, R/B floors, and cap enforceability under plausible shocks.
* **Sunset discipline**: exceptional measures expire unless renewed with updated evidence; prevents permanent drift based on one-time crises.
* **No hidden liabilities**: contingent claims and off-balance obligations must be disclosed and mapped to ΔM and cap frameworks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Commons governance and public infrastructure decisions **MUST** include maintenance plans and backlog metrics; neglect triggers incident reviews.
* Major policy changes **MUST** include a future test statement (assumptions, uncertainty, and falsification conditions).
* Emergency and exception measures **MUST** be sunsetted with rollback triggers; indefinite “temporary” regimes are non-compliant.

## B03.CH09.P06.S05 — Conflict-of-interest regimes: disclosure, cooling-off, rotation

Participation and representation are systematically corrupted through conflicts of interest: procurement influence, revolving doors, hidden funding,
and vendor dependence. OBA therefore requires explicit conflict-of-interest (COI) regimes to prevent capture and to preserve auditability.

### COI regime components (minimum)

* **Disclosure**: affiliations, funding sources, prior employment, relevant holdings, and procurement relationships.
* **Cooling-off**: time-bound prohibitions on moving between regulator/auditor roles and regulated/vendor roles in the same domain.
* **Rotation**: limits on tenure in high-influence roles; committee rotation to prevent network capture.
* **Recusal rules**: mandatory abstention in decisions where conflict exists; logged and auditable.
* **Sanctions**: penalties for nondisclosure, false disclosure, and backchannel influence.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* All decision-makers and high-influence participants **MUST** comply with COI disclosure and recusal regimes; voluntary disclosure is insufficient.
* Cooling-off and rotation rules **MUST** apply to auditors and procurement officials as well as politicians.
* COI violations **MUST** trigger incident reviews and, where decisions were affected, restatements and potential rollback.
