# B03.CH09.P04 — Participation channels: where input is produced and how it is processed

This paragraph defines **participation channels** as *input pipelines*: places where signals from citizens, workers, commons members, and intermediaries
are produced, structured, and converted into decisions under mandate and rails. In OBA, participation is not “voice” in general. It is **typed input**
with (i) a source role, (ii) an authority target (agenda / design / monitoring / correction), (iii) evidence requirements, and (iv) a defined processing
path with logging and appeal. Channels that do not specify processing become **noise amplifiers** and capture surfaces: they reward attention rather than
truth, and bargaining rather than rail conformance.

## Core principles

* **Typed channels**: every input has a role source, a domain target, and an evidence standard.
* **Processing is governance**: who triages inputs, how they are logged, and how they trigger decisions is the real power surface.
* **Contestability is non-optional**: inputs and outcomes must be challengeable; corrections must propagate via restatements.
* **Deliberation is axis-bound**: disagreements are weighed within test axes (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM, auditability), not resolved by slogans.

## B03.CH09.P04.S01 — Agenda-setting: problem selection via citizen initiatives and commons feedback

Agenda-setting is the upstream choke point: what is not placed on the agenda cannot be measured, capped, or corrected. OBA-compatible agenda-setting
therefore requires two complementary channels:

1. **Citizen initiatives**: mechanisms for citizens to force consideration of order-relevant problems without relying on party gatekeeping.
2. **Commons feedback**: structured signals from commons users/operators about access failures, quality decay, and boundary-rule breaches.

### Functional profile (agenda-setting channel)

* **Input types**: problem statements, incident bundles, service failure reports, toll/lock-in observations, exception stacking signals.
* **Pre-filter rule**: agenda proposals must be expressible as an OBA question (“where is extraction and who blocks it?”) with a minimum evidence hook.
* **Processing path**: intake → triage → publication → response obligation → decision on investigation or rejection with rationale.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Citizen initiatives **MUST** trigger an institutional response obligation: investigate, reject with evidence-based rationale, or schedule deliberation.
* Commons feedback **MUST** be routable into maintenance planning and boundary-rule review; otherwise it becomes performative.
* Agenda triage **MUST** publish acceptance/rejection rationales and must be auditable; secret agenda gatekeeping is non-compliant.
* Agenda items impacting caps, ΔM channels, or auditability **MUST** be flagged as high-risk and require independent review.

## B03.CH09.P04.S02 — Co-design: participatory design cycles for commons services and boundary rules

Co-design is the channel where participation legitimately enters **operational architecture**: service design, boundary rules, access criteria, and
maintenance protocols. In OBA, co-design is valuable because it surfaces real-world constraints and avoids “paper order.” But it is also a capture
surface: design workshops can become procurement theatre or operator self-justification. Therefore co-design must be **cycle-based**, evidence-bound,
and reversible.

### Functional profile (co-design channel)

* **Cycle stages**: (1) problem definition, (2) option generation, (3) impact projection on axes, (4) pilot, (5) measurement, (6) revision or rollback.
* **Participants**: users/members, operators/maintainers, frontline workers, independent auditors, and a mandate custodian.
* **Outputs**: boundary rules, service levels, maintenance plan, cost-base disclosure (where relevant), and an audit-ready logging schema.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Co-design outputs **MUST** include explicit axis projections (E, R/B, ΔG, auditability) and must define how results will be measured in pilots.
* Boundary rules **MUST** include complaint/appeal routes, sanctions for abuse, and maintenance obligations; “community norms” is insufficient.
* Any procurement-linked co-design **MUST** publish conflicts, vendor roles, and decision logs; otherwise it is classified as capture-prone.
* Co-design must be **reversible**: pilots require rollback conditions and restatement duties when evidence contradicts assumptions.

## B03.CH09.P04.S03 — Monitoring: public scorecards, complaint channels, anomaly reporting

Monitoring channels convert lived experience into **detectable signals** of drift and failure. Under OBA, monitoring is not optional “feedback”; it is a
pillar of enforcement because extraction and sabotage are adaptive. Monitoring must therefore combine **public scorecards** (macro visibility) with
**complaint and anomaly channels** (micro signals).

### Monitoring instruments (minimum set)

* **Public scorecards**: E proxies, R/B distribution panels, exception ratio, sanction rate, ΔG maintenance backlog and quality metrics, ΔM channel ledger.
* **Complaint channels**: accessible reporting for wrongful exclusion, gating, price abuse, fee-stacks, service denial, and corruption signals.
* **Anomaly reporting**: structured intake for outliers (bunching near thresholds, sudden margin spikes, unexplained vendor costs, audit access refusals).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Monitoring outputs **MUST** be public by default (privacy-safe aggregation) and pinned to definition-set versions.
* Complaint handling **MUST** have timelines, acknowledgement, and remedy pathways; “receive and ignore” is non-compliant.
* Anomaly detection **MUST** have escalation triggers that can activate audits or rollback; otherwise monitoring degenerates into venting.
* If monitoring indicates rising exceptions or falling sanctions, an incident review is **mandatory**, not discretionary.

## B03.CH09.P04.S04 — Correction: objection/appeal, recall, governance interventions, rollback requirements

Correction channels are where participation becomes **real power**. Without correction, participation is only consultation. In OBA, correction must be
able to (i) challenge classifications and decisions, (ii) demand restatements when evidence changes, (iii) trigger enforcement, and (iv) roll back
failed interventions. Correction mechanisms must be designed to avoid two extremes: capture by litigious elites and denial of remedy to low-capacity
actors.

### Correction instruments (canonical)

* **Objection and appeal**: formal contestation with evidence, independent review, and published rationales.
* **Recall and re-authorisation**: mandate-level correction (political accountability) tied to compliance evidence and incident histories.
* **Governance interventions**: replacing captured boards, imposing external administrators, tightening boundary rules, freezing exemptions.
* **Rollback requirements**: automatic reversal or tightening when triggers fire (E not falling, audit failure, bypass rising, ΔG declining).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Every participation domain **MUST** include an appeal route with an independent reviewer and a defined remedy set.
* Correction processes **MUST** be evidence-bound and must issue restatements that propagate to public dashboards.
* Rollback triggers **MUST** exist for high-risk domains (caps, ΔM channels, commons access, auditability); discretionary rollback is insufficient.
* Access to correction **MUST** be protected (support, legal aid, anti-retaliation); otherwise correction is a privilege of high-capacity actors.

## B03.CH09.P04.S05 — Deliberation: structured weighing within test axes, not via slogans

Deliberation is the channel where conflicting interests are weighed. Under OBA, deliberation is constrained by a rule: **arguments must be expressible
as axis impacts**. This prevents identity frames from substituting for mechanism analysis and allows disagreements to be documented as trade-offs rather
than as moral warfare.

### Deliberation discipline (required)

* **Axis framing**: every option is presented with projected deltas on E, R/B distribution, caps integrity, ΔG, ΔM, and auditability.
* **Evidence tiers**: separate what is measured, what is modeled, and what is unknown; apply conservative defaults where stakes are high.
* **Trade-off explicitness**: state who bears costs and who gains, and whether costs are temporary or structural (timing asymmetry).
* **Bypass analysis**: for each option, specify likely evasion/capture routes and the counter-rails.
* **Decision log**: record rationale, definition versions, dissent notes, and planned evaluation triggers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Deliberative bodies **MUST** use axis-bound templates; decisions justified only by slogans or identity claims are non-compliant.
* Where evidence is insufficient, deliberation **MUST** output “unknown” and prescribe measurement/pilot, not rhetorical certainty.
* Dissent **MUST** be logged in mechanism terms (which axis, which assumption), enabling future incident review and restatement.
* If deliberation bypasses auditability (sealed briefings, proprietary models), the resulting decision **MUST** be downgraded in legitimacy classification.
