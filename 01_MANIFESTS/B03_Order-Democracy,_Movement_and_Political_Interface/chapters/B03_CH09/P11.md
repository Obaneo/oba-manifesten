# B03.CH09.P11 — Failure modes: how representation/participation goes wrong

This paragraph defines the canonical **failure modes** of representation and participation under adversarial politics, and the minimum **correction
mechanisms** required to keep an OBA-compatible system from degenerating into theatre, capture, fatigue collapse, or polarised incident warfare. The
core claim is structural: participation is not self-legitimising. It fails in predictable ways unless channels are role-bound, evidence-bound,
auditable, and equipped with escalation and rollback.

## Core principles

* **Participation fails by design, not by accident**: failure modes are predictable and should be pre-encoded as tests and triggers.
* **Neutralisation dominates**: the main risk is not open rejection of participation but façade forms that preserve OOA mechanisms.
* **Quality requires throughput discipline**: excessive participation load collapses into professionalisation or apathy.
* **Polarisation is an order attack vector**: incident politics is used to justify exceptions, selective enforcement, and regression to the OOA reflex.
* **Correction must be executable**: redesign, rotation, audit, and sanctions are not “lessons learned” but binding interventions.

## B03.CH09.P11.S01 — Tokenism: consultation without influence; façade participation

**Tokenism** is consultation as ritual: inputs are collected, but authority and outcomes do not change. Tokenism is worse than no participation because
it trains cynicism and shifts accountability away from institutions (“we consulted”). It also functions as a shield against later critique.

### Functional profile (tokenism)

* **No authority mapping**: participants do not know what they can influence, and decision-makers are not bound to respond.
* **No handling trace**: comments disappear; there is no disposition report (accepted/rejected/why).
* **No remedy**: complaints and appeals exist but cannot alter decisions or trigger audits/rollback.
* **Narrative substitution**: engagement metrics (number of meetings) replace rail deltas and evidence.
* **Selective spotlighting**: organisers highlight supportive input while ignoring dissent, without disclosure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any participation channel **MUST** specify authority scope and must publish a handling report; otherwise it is classified as tokenistic.
* If consultation does not affect decisions (or produce explicit, logged non-changes), it **MUST** be flagged as façade participation.
* Complaint/appeal channels without remedies **MUST** be redesigned or removed; they are legitimacy laundering.

## B03.CH09.P11.S02 — Capture: organised interest groups dominate participation channels

**Capture** occurs when organised interests dominate agenda-setting, deliberation, or governance, converting participation into exception politics and
toll preservation. Capture is not rare; it is the default tendency when channels are open-ended and when time/attention are scarce.

### Capture mechanisms (canonical)

* **Volume dominance**: coordinated submissions overwhelm open consultations; self-selection becomes representational monopoly.
* **Expert posture**: insiders control framing and “technical” standards; proprietary metrics block contestability.
* **Funding leverage**: intermediaries become dependent on sponsors or state grants and soften scrutiny.
* **Procurement linkage**: participation bodies shape contracts and vendor selection, creating covert rent channels.
* **Gatekeeping**: councils/professional bodies restrict entry, becoming toll positions themselves.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-impact participation bodies **MUST** use representative balancing (sampling/rotation) and mandatory COI disclosure.
* Any participation output affecting caps, procurement, ΔM channels, or audit rules **MUST** be logged, audited, and contestable; closed bargaining is non-compliant.
* If exception ratio rises while enforcement weakens, capture is presumed until disproven; incident review is mandatory.

## B03.CH09.P11.S03 — Fatigue: participation overload and declining quality

**Fatigue** is a structural failure mode: when participation demands are too high, quality declines and participation becomes either (i) dominated by
professional participants, or (ii) abandoned by low-capacity citizens and workers. Fatigue is a capture accelerator because it narrows the participant
base.

### Fatigue mechanisms (canonical)

* **Process inflation**: too many meetings, too many documents, too many channels → attention collapses.
* **Cognitive overload**: decisions are not axis-structured; participants must interpret jargon and hidden definitions.
* **Asymmetric cost**: time costs fall on workers and caregivers; benefits accrue to organised actors.
* **Learned helplessness**: inputs are ignored (tokenism), causing drop-off and cynicism.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Participation systems **MUST** include throughput discipline: bounded cycles, clear templates, and domain separation (not everything for everyone).
* Deliberation **MUST** be axis-structured with plain-language support; complexity fog is non-compliant.
* Where fatigue indicators rise (drop-out rates, repeat participant dominance), redesign and rotation interventions are mandatory.

## B03.CH09.P11.S04 — Polarisation: incident politics, scapegoating, regression to the OOA reflex

**Polarisation** converts order conflict into identity warfare and incident cycles. In such environments, actors use sensational incidents to justify
exceptions, selective enforcement, surveillance expansion, and asset-support reflexes—i.e., regression to OOA mechanisms under crisis framing. The
political field becomes unable to deliberate in axis terms; slogans replace measurable trade-offs.

### Polarisation mechanisms (canonical)

* **Incident politics**: rare events dominate agenda; systematic extraction and maintenance neglect become invisible.
* **Scapegoating**: minorities or outgroups are blamed for degradation that is structurally produced (toll extraction, austerity, capture).
* **Emergency permanence**: crises are invoked to suspend auditability and sunset discipline; exceptions stack.
* **Selective enforcement**: rules are applied unevenly; legitimacy collapses; compliance becomes factional.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Deliberation bodies **MUST** use axis-bound templates and must publish evidence bundles; narrative-only governance is non-compliant.
* Emergency measures **MUST** be sunsetted with rollback triggers and ex post audits; repeated renewals trigger regression classification.
* If enforcement becomes selective (bias signals, sanction collapse), governance intervention and independent review are mandatory.

## B03.CH09.P11.S05 — Correction mechanisms: redesign of channels, rotation, audit, sanctions

Correction is not optional. OBA requires that failure modes trigger **pre-specified interventions**. Correction mechanisms must be capable of changing
authority, not only issuing recommendations.

### Correction mechanisms (minimum set)

* **Channel redesign**: re-define authority scope, evidence standards, processing paths; remove open-ended “stakeholder” gates.
* **Rotation and re-authorisation**: term limits, re-election/re-selection rules, randomisation/sampling, leadership replacement.
* **Audit escalation**: audit-of-auditors, replication demands, disclosure enforcement, incident reviews with restatement duties.
* **Sanctions**: penalties for nondisclosure, procurement corruption, gating, intimidation, and obstruction of audits/appeals.
* **Rollback**: reversal or tightening when triggers fire; restore baseline rails and close bypass routes.

### Trigger discipline (required)

* Tokenism indicators → redesign or removal of channels; publish handling and remedy rules.
* Capture indicators (exceptions↑, sanctions↓, opacity↑) → governance intervention + independent audit.
* Fatigue indicators → reduce load, improve templates/support, introduce sampling and rotation.
* Polarisation incidents → enforce axis deliberation, tighten emergency discipline, and protect minority rails.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Systems **MUST** publish correction triggers and intervention playbooks; ad hoc “learning” is insufficient.
* Correction mechanisms **MUST** include the power to change structures (membership, authority, contracts), not only to advise.
* If correction is blocked or repeatedly delayed, the participation regime **MUST** be classified as non-legitimate and drift-risk under OBA.
