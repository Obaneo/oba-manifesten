# B03.CH09.P07 — Labour and worker interface: participation via economic power and institutions

This paragraph defines the **labour/worker interface**: the set of participation channels through which workers influence order outcomes, not primarily
through opinion but through **economic power**, **institutional representation**, and **frontline evidence**. In OBA, labour participation is essential
because (i) workers are structurally exposed to E and R/B compression, (ii) workers are early detectors of fee-stacks and compliance sabotage, and (iii)
labour institutions can counterbalance toll positions and captured governance—if they are themselves constrained by transparency, anti-capture rules,
and evidence rails.

## Core principles

* **Labour power is a governance instrument**: bargaining and collective action can enforce anti-extraction rails when formal enforcement drifts.
* **Frontline evidence is first-class**: shop-floor signals are treated as audit inputs, not anecdotes.
* **Protection is precondition**: without anti-retaliation and legal aid, monitoring channels collapse into silence.
* **Coalition stability is material**: swing-group retention depends on R/B floors and continuity of essentials, not on identity narratives.

## B03.CH09.P07.S01 — Unions/collectives: bargaining, monitoring, abuse detection

Unions and worker collectives are intermediaries that can convert dispersed labour exposure into enforceable constraints. In OBA they have two primary
functions: **bargaining** (distribution and conditions) and **monitoring/abuse detection** (order integrity).

### Functional profile (union/collective interface)

* **Bargaining**: wage floors, scheduling, training, safety, and—critically—constraints on fee-stacks and contract forms that shift risk downward.
* **Monitoring**: systematic detection of evasion patterns: unpaid overtime, bogus self-employment, subcontract opacity, algorithmic wage suppression.
* **Abuse detection**: reporting of selective enforcement, retaliation, procurement corruption, and “exceptions by practice” (informal waivers).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Collective bargaining domains **MUST** explicitly include anti-extraction surfaces where relevant: fee-stacks, contracting structures, and lock-in terms.
* Monitoring outputs from unions/collectives **MUST** be admissible evidence: logged, attributable, and routable into audits and incident reviews.
* Union/collective governance **MUST** meet intermediary integrity rules: funding disclosure, internal democracy, and rotation; otherwise capture risk is reintroduced.

## B03.CH09.P07.S02 — Professional groups in execution/audit: professional standards and integrity

Professional groups (inspectors, engineers, clinicians, auditors, educators, maintainers) occupy execution and audit roles where competence and ethics
determine whether OBA rails hold. They can strengthen integrity, but they can also become licensing gatekeepers or cartel councils. OBA therefore treats
professional participation as **standards + accountability** rather than as deference.

### Functional profile (professional integrity interface)

* **Standards setting**: codify measurable performance and integrity standards for execution and audit functions.
* **Peer enforcement**: professional discipline mechanisms that sanction malpractice and protect whistleblowers.
* **Competence pipeline**: training, certification, and continuing education tied to OBA requirements (auditability, disclosure, anti-capture practices).

### Capture risks (canonical)

* **Gatekeeping**: licensing used to restrict entry and create toll positions.
* **Guild capture**: standards optimized for incumbents, not for public interest and auditability.
* **Opaque expertise**: “trust the experts” used to block replication and contestability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Professional standards used in execution/audit **MUST** be public, versioned, and contestable; proprietary standards are non-compliant for order claims.
* Certification regimes **MUST** include anti-gatekeeping constraints (transparent criteria, appeal routes, proportional requirements).
* Professional bodies **MUST** disclose conflicts and funding; where they influence procurement or audit, audit-of-auditors applies.

## B03.CH09.P07.S03 — Shop-floor feedback: service failures, fee-stacks, compliance sabotage signals

Shop-floor and frontline feedback is the highest-frequency sensor network available to an OBA system. It reveals the difference between **paper
compliance** and **functional reality**. OBA formalises shop-floor feedback as a structured, logged input stream.

### Canonical shop-floor signal types

* **Service failures**: recurring outages, understaffing, maintenance backlog evidence, rationing-by-queue, hidden gating practices.
* **Fee-stacks**: layers of fees, subcontract margins, platform take-rates, mandatory intermediaries, “administration” costs that function as tolls.
* **Compliance sabotage**: manipulated reporting, pressure to misclassify work, concealment of incidents, “don’t log this” practices.
* **Threshold gaming**: entity splitting, hours manipulation, classification tricks to avoid caps or obligations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Systems **MUST** provide protected channels for shop-floor reporting with standardized categories and linkage to audit/incident review workflows.
* Reports **MUST** be triaged with published timelines and outcomes; non-response converts feedback into cynicism and increases sabotage incentives.
* Where shop-floor signals contradict dashboards, an incident review **MUST** be triggered; dashboards do not override lived evidence without investigation.

## B03.CH09.P07.S04 — Protection: whistleblowers, anti-retaliation, legal assistance

Monitoring and abuse detection are impossible without protection. OBA treats **whistleblower protection** as core infrastructure because retaliation is
a rational strategy for extractive actors. Protection includes legal, economic, and procedural safeguards.

### Protection stack (minimum)

* **Anti-retaliation rules**: clear prohibitions, burden-shifting where appropriate, and meaningful penalties.
* **Confidential reporting**: secure channels; separation between intake and enforcement; data minimisation.
* **Legal assistance**: access to counsel, ombuds support, and representation for low-capacity workers.
* **Economic buffers**: temporary income support or job transition assistance for those who face retaliation or exit coercion.
* **Public accountability**: publication of anonymized statistics on reports, investigations, outcomes, and retaliation findings.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any system claiming OBA auditability **MUST** include a whistleblower protection regime with enforceable remedies.
* Reporting channels **MUST** be independent of the immediate management chain; otherwise capture and suppression are predictable.
* If retaliation is detected or sanction rates collapse, an incident review and governance intervention **MUST** be triggered.

## B03.CH09.P07.S05 — Coalition management: stabilising swing groups via R/B and essentials

Worker-aligned coalitions can fracture when transition costs are concentrated or when essentials continuity is threatened. OBA coalition management is
therefore anchored in **material stabilisation** rather than identity reassurance: keep R/B floors credible and essentials continuity operational.

### Stabilisation levers (canonical)

* **R/B lower bounds**: explicit floors for p10/p25/median restruimte; automatic stabilisers tied to measured L components.
* **Essentials continuity**: guarantees of access (housing, energy, mobility, health, education) during transition, preferably via commons capacity and anti-gating rules.
* **Timing symmetry**: sequencing that delivers early E-reduction benefits to core coalition groups while phasing in burdens with predictable schedules.
* **Anti-asset-support reflex**: avoid crisis packages that protect claim values while imposing austerity or scarcity on workers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any transition strategy involving labour participation **MUST** specify how swing-group costs are buffered without entrenching toll positions.
* Stabilisation measures **MUST** be measurable and audited; rhetorical “no one left behind” without R/B floors is non-compliant.
* If a policy stabilises assets/claims but allows essentials to degrade or R/B floors to fall, it **MUST** be classified as OOA-consistent in coalition effect.
