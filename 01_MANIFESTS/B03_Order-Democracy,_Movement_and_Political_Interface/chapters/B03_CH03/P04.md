# B03.CH03.P04 — Mandate granting: mechanisms and thresholds

OBA treats mandate granting as **rail-setting**, not as a symbolic act. Different decision classes require different mandate
channels and thresholds, because ordinary electoral logic is structurally vulnerable to short-term incentives, ambiguity capture,
and majority drift. This paragraph specifies the **minimum mandate mechanisms** and **threshold rules** that keep constitutive
rails stable while leaving legitimate political space for parametric and discretionary choices. The aim is to prevent “blank
cheques,” to preserve contestability, and to ensure that when society truly wants to change core rails, it does so explicitly and
traceably.

---

## B03.CH03.P04.S01 — Elections: direction and priorities, not a blank cheque

Elections grant an **electoral mandate** for direction, sequencing, and priorities within existing rails. They do not, by
themselves, authorise changes to constitutive invariants (caps bindingness, auditability, commons boundary rules, ΔM
contestability). Elections are high-bandwidth for values and agendas, low-bandwidth for technical definitional stability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Electoral mandates MUST be interpreted as authorising **parametric** choices and prioritisation within rails, not constitutive
  redefinition.
- Any electoral programme that implies invariant changes MUST specify the required constitutive route (supermajority/dual-key/etc.).
- Post-election implementation MUST translate promises into testable order criteria (mandate text in E/caps/ΔG/RB/ΔM axes) before
  high-impact execution.

---

## B03.CH03.P04.S02 — Referenda/citizen conventions: deployable for constitutive choices

Constitutive changes require mechanisms that can carry **explicit, high-salience consent** and withstand capture via elite
intermediation. Referenda and citizen conventions (sortition-based) can be used to decide on constitutive rails, provided their
inputs, evidence standards, and decision framing remain contestable and non-manipulative.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Referenda/conventions MUST operate on a published definition set and MUST include evidence packs and counter-arguments under
  source-duty rules.
- The question framing MUST be stable, auditable, and linked to concrete rail deltas (what changes, what stays invariant).
- Outcomes MUST produce a versioned mandate artifact: decision → rail delta → implementation constraints → rollback/sunset rules if
  staged.

---

## B03.CH03.P04.S03 — Supermajority and dual-key: protection against cap erosion and drift

OBA assumes that majorities can form that externalise costs onto minorities and the future. Therefore constitutive decisions must
be protected by higher thresholds: **supermajority** (to reduce volatility and capture) and, where relevant, **dual-key** (two
distinct constituencies must agree) to prevent single-axis dominance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any change affecting caps bindingness, exception regime structure, audit access, or ΔM contestability MUST require supermajority
  or an equivalent special procedure.
- Dual-key MUST be used for changes that predictably burden minorities, future cohorts (maintenance/capex), or commons users (access
  gating).
- Threshold rules MUST be explicit ex ante and MUST not be modifiable by the same decision path they protect (anti-self-amendment).

---

## B03.CH03.P04.S04 — Commons representation: who speaks for users/maintenance

Commons governance requires representation that reflects **use**, **maintenance burden**, and **service continuity**, not only
general electorate preferences. The mandate question becomes: who has standing to decide boundary rules, access prioritisation under
scarcity, maintenance backlogs, and investment schedules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons decision bodies MUST specify who represents (a) users, (b) maintainers/operators, (c) auditors, and (d) public interest,
  with role separation and conflict-of-interest regimes.
- Representation mechanisms (membership, election, sortition, stakeholder seats) MUST be published and contestable, with rotation
  and recall options where appropriate.
- Maintenance obligations MUST have explicit trusteeship: no governance model is valid if it can systematically underfund or defer
  maintenance without triggering hard stops and escalation.

---

## B03.CH03.P04.S05 — Minority and generational rails: vetoes, ombuds functions, trusteeship

To prevent majoritarian drift and crisis scapegoating, OBA requires minority and generational protections that are procedural and
institutional—not discretionary goodwill. These rails ensure that those with low political capacity (minorities, future cohorts,
diffuse commons users) are not structurally outvoted into permanent loss.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Decisions with high minority impact MUST include a minority-protection mechanism (veto scope, ombuds review, or equivalent) with
  evidence access and publishable rationales.
- Long-horizon obligations (maintenance, capex, commons continuity) MUST be protected by generational trusteeship rules and
  enforcement triggers (backlog thresholds, funding ring-fences, sunset discipline).
- If these rails are bypassed via “temporary” exceptions or emergency routines, the deviation MUST be time-bounded, logged, and
  subject to mandatory ex post audit and potential rollback.
