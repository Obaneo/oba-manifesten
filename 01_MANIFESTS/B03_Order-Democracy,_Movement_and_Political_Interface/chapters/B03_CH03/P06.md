# B03.CH03.P06 — Mandate conflicts: when mandates clash and how to decide

OBA assumes that mandate conflicts are not edge cases but a normal condition of pluralistic governance. Conflicts arise when
different legitimacy sources (electoral majorities, constitutive constraints, commons governance, and emergency powers) produce
incompatible instructions. This paragraph defines a **decision hierarchy** and **conflict-resolution protocol** that preserves the
non-negotiables, keeps contestability intact, and prevents “conflict” from being used as a pretext for semantic drift or exception
stacking.

---

## B03.CH03.P06.S01 — Electoral mandate versus constitutive rails: priority and review

Electoral mandates can select priorities and parameters, but they cannot override constitutive rails without a constitutive
procedure. The central conflict pattern is “majority wants X” versus “X breaks invariants.”

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If an electoral mandate conflicts with constitutive rails (caps bindingness, auditability, commons openness, exception discipline),
  the constitutive rails MUST prevail by default.
- Any proposal to override or modify constitutive rails MUST be reclassified as a **constitutive mandate request** and routed
  through the required higher-threshold procedure (supermajority/special process), with full publication of the delta.
- Pending constitutive review, implementation MUST be fail-closed on the conflicting portion (no “temporary” bypass without an
  emergency mandate with sunset/rollback).

---

## B03.CH03.P06.S02 — Commons interests versus market interests: boundary rules and access

Commons decisions frequently clash with market actors seeking prioritisation, monetised access, or governance influence. The
resolution point is the commons’ constitutive commitments: access defaults, boundary rules, maintenance duties, and anti-toll gates.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Access to commons MUST remain open by default within explicit boundary rules; market preferences cannot introduce private toll
  gates (paid priority, bundling, lock-in fees) via procurement or operational rules.
- Where scarcity forces prioritisation, the permitted prioritisation criteria MUST be applied (essentials continuity, vulnerability,
  continuity, public value) and MUST remain contestable with evidence.
- Any market arrangement that makes exit or audit infeasible (lock-in, proprietary scoring, refusal of logs) MUST be treated as
  incompatible with commons governance and must be blocked or rolled back.

---

## B03.CH03.P06.S03 — National goals versus international obligations: compatibility and carve-outs

Mandates can conflict with treaty obligations, cross-border regimes, or shared infrastructures. OBA treats this as a compatibility
problem: either the combined constraint set is compatible, or the system must explicitly declare and label deviations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The conflict MUST be expressed as a compatibility matrix: which obligations constrain **E, caps, commons/ΔG, R/B, ΔM**, and how.
- If compliance with international obligations requires structural carve-outs that erode OBA invariants (e.g., permanent exceptions,
  black-box governance, audit denial), the deviation MUST be:
  - explicitly disclosed,
  - time-bounded where possible,
  - and routed through the fork/compatibility procedure (status label required).
- “Silent carve-outs” (unpublished exemptions, side letters, de facto non-enforcement) are prohibited and trigger escalation.

---

## B03.CH03.P06.S04 — Crisis versus rule of law: emergency mandate and exit conditions

Crises intensify conflict by compressing timelines and increasing political tolerance for overreach. OBA permits emergency action,
but only as a constrained mode: evidence discipline, non-negotiables preserved where possible, and automatic exit.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any crisis-based override MUST be backed by an **emergency mandate** with:
  - explicit scope,
  - a hard sunset,
  - rollback requirements,
  - and ex post audit obligations (including restatements if definitions changed).
- Emergency powers MUST NOT be used to introduce prohibited linkages (profiling/surveillance), permanent cap carve-outs, or black-box
  allocations; if such measures are claimed “necessary,” they must be treated as constitutive deviations and trigger fork handling.
- Where measurement continuity is impaired, the default is **hard-stop or pause** for affected allocations unless a narrow,
  time-bounded emergency pathway is defined and auditable.

---

## B03.CH03.P06.S05 — Fork decision: explicit choice when compatibility is absent

Some conflicts are not resolvable within the rails: different coalitions require different constitutive choices. OBA’s mechanism is
to force clarity: declare the incompatibility, label it, and prevent semantic laundering.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a conflict cannot be resolved without breaking a non-negotiable, the system MUST initiate a **fork decision**:
  - classify the deviation (variant / extension / fork),
  - assign a compatibility status label,
  - and publish the delta, rationale, and measurement/audit plan.
- Until a fork decision is concluded, the default treatment of the conflicting pathway is **non-conform** (no implied compatibility).
- Any public communication MUST use the correct label (“OBA-compatible”, “partially compatible”, “OBA-inspired”, “non-compatible”)
  consistent with the declared status; mislabeling triggers correction and sanctions under the governance regime.
