# B03.CH03.P02 — Mandate types: distinctions by order impact

OBA treats “mandate” as a **typed authorisation** with explicit scope, evidence duties, and escalation thresholds. The same
institutional action can be legitimate under one mandate type and illegitimate under another, depending on whether it stays
**within the rails** (parametric/discretionary) or alters **constitutive boundary conditions** (definitions, bindingness of caps,
contestability, auditability, commons boundary rules). This paragraph defines the minimum mandate taxonomy used throughout Book 3
to prevent “blank-cheque” governance, to block mandate laundering via technocracy/procurement, and to ensure that order-relevant
changes trigger the correct procedure (supermajority/dual-key, audit gates, re-authorisation, or fork labeling).

---

## B03.CH03.P02.S01 — Parametric mandate: within the rails (thresholds, rates, priorities)

A **parametric mandate** authorises choices that fill in **degrees of freedom explicitly permitted by the Kernel Spec** without
changing the definitions or weakening invariant constraints. Examples include setting thresholds within allowed ranges, choosing
rates/tariffs within cap-compatible corridors, prioritising among eligible projects, and pacing implementation—provided that the
result remains measurable, auditable, and contestable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Parametric decisions MUST cite the relevant **definition-set/version** and the permitted parameter range/corridor.
- Parametric mandates MUST NOT be used to introduce structural carve-outs, hidden exceptions, or “threshold tricks” that de facto
  unbind caps or gate commons access.
- If a parametric choice materially changes auditability/contestability (e.g., black-box scoring), it MUST be reclassified as
  constitutive or treated as non-conform.

---

## B03.CH03.P02.S02 — Constitutive mandate: change of core rails (supermajority/special procedure)

A **constitutive mandate** is required when a decision changes the **core rails**: canonical definitions, bindingness and
non-stackability of caps, commons boundary rules (including access defaults), auditability/replicability requirements, or the
structure of contestability (objection/appeal + independent audit). Because such changes redefine what counts as “OBA-conform,”
they require higher democratic thresholds (supermajority, dual-key, constitutional procedure, or equivalent).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any change to definitions, computation rules, logging requirements, audit access, exception regime architecture, or core
  contestability MUST be treated as constitutive.
- Constitutive changes MUST include an impact analysis, migration/bridging plan (if time series break), and explicit fork/label
  consequences if compatibility is affected.
- Constitutive changes MUST be fail-closed: no deployment before procedure completion and independent review.

---

## B03.CH03.P02.S03 — Commons mandate: stewardship, access, maintenance, conflict resolution

A **commons mandate** authorises stewardship governance over commons: access management within boundary rules, service minima,
maintenance planning, investment selection under budget rails, and conflict resolution procedures. It is neither “market” nor
“state command”; it is a governance layer with explicit anti-capture safeguards (role separation, rotation, disclosure, audit).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons governance MUST preserve the access default (open within boundary rules) and MUST implement maintenance-first discipline
  (minimum levels, backlog metrics, ring-fencing where applicable).
- Scarcity prioritisation MUST use explicit, contestable criteria; black-box allocation and non-appealable profiling are prohibited.
- Commons decisions MUST produce auditable decision logs and expose a complaint→review path with evidence access.

---

## B03.CH03.P02.S04 — ΔM mandate: allocation criteria, channel distribution, transparency requirements

A **ΔM mandate** authorises decisions about the allocation interface: how monetary space is distributed across channels, under what
criteria, with which transparency and contestability guarantees. Because ΔM decisions can silently recreate toll positions (via
intermediaries, opaque scoring, or vendor lock-in), they require strict measurability and public accountability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- ΔM mandates MUST specify channel distribution rules, prohibited channels, and the audit surface (logs, model/version pinning,
  reproducibility requirements).
- Allocation decisions MUST be contestable (appeal path + explainability sufficient for audit) and MUST not rely on proprietary
  black boxes that block replication.
- Any ΔM mechanism that increases extraction risk (fee-stacks, hidden take rates, collateralisation paths) MUST trigger escalation
  review and potential reclassification.

---

## B03.CH03.P02.S05 — Emergency mandate: temporary powers with sunset/rollback

An **emergency mandate** authorises temporary deviations needed to preserve essentials continuity under shock. It exists to prevent
crisis governance from becoming a permanent exception channel. Emergency powers are valid only with predefined sunsets, rollback
requirements, evidence discipline, and post-hoc audit.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every emergency measure MUST include: explicit scope, duration, sunset, rollback plan (technical + contractual), and minimum
  transparency (no measurement blackout).
- Emergency deviations MUST NOT become stackable or renewable by default; renewal MUST require elevated thresholds and justification.
- Any emergency action that weakens non-negotiables beyond the temporary window MUST be escalated as a constitutive change and may
  require fork labeling if compatibility breaks.
