# B03.CH03.P03 — Legitimacy criteria: when a decision is “legitimate” under OBA

Under OBA, legitimacy is not exhausted by electoral endorsement or formal competence. A decision is legitimate only if it meets a
**multi-criterion legitimacy test** that binds procedure, evidence, distributional constraints, institutional design, and
substantive compatibility with non-negotiables. This prevents mandate laundering (“we were elected”), technocratic laundering
(“it’s just technical”), and crisis laundering (“exceptional times”) from hollowing out caps, commons access, ΔM contestability,
and auditability. The criteria below are cumulative: failure on any pillar makes the decision **non-legitimate under OBA**, even if
it is politically popular.

---

## B03.CH03.P03.S01 — Procedural legitimacy: transparent steps, objection/appeal, audit

Procedural legitimacy requires that decisions follow **publicly defined steps**, produce traceable decision logs, and remain
contestable through objection/appeal and independent audit. The purpose is not ceremony but **attack-surface reduction**: opaque
procedures are where capture, exceptions, and semantic drift hide.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Decisions MUST have a published procedure path (inputs → decision → implementation → review) and MUST produce a decision log
  referencing the applicable definition-set/version.
- Affected parties MUST have access to an objection/appeal route with defined timelines and non-sabotage defaults.
- Independent audit access MUST be guaranteed (including forensic access where relevant), with findings publishable at least in
  aggregated form consistent with privacy rails.

---

## B03.CH03.P03.S02 — Epistemic legitimacy: evidence standards, measurability, replicability

Epistemic legitimacy requires that the decision’s claims are **testable**: the metrics exist, the methods are specified, the
uncertainty is bounded, and independent parties can replicate the evaluation. This blocks “policy by assertion” and prevents
measurement blackouts from becoming a governance tool.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every decision with order impact MUST specify evidence standards, measurement methods, and uncertainty bands; “unknown” states
  MUST be explicitly labeled and treated as risk.
- Core calculations MUST be replicable: open computation rules or equivalent reproducibility provisions; version pinning and
  changelog discipline are mandatory.
- If measurability/auditability cannot be achieved at the required level, the decision MUST trigger hard stops or downscoping until
  the evidence deficit is corrected (fail-closed).

---

## B03.CH03.P03.S03 — Distributive legitimacy: R/B lower bounds and protection of essentials

Distributive legitimacy requires that decisions respect **R/B constraints**: protect essentials continuity and enforce lower-bound
constraints for life-world buffers, avoiding burden-shifting onto vulnerable groups or future generations. OBA rejects distributive
justifications that rely on hidden extraction, cap erosion, or maintenance neglect.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Decisions MUST demonstrate compatibility with R/B lower bounds and essentials continuity (including maintenance implications).
- Burden concentration on minorities, low-capacity groups, or future cohorts MUST be explicitly assessed and bounded by rails
  (buffers, compensation, pacing, or redesign).
- Any policy that preserves elite extraction while shifting costs onto essentials users MUST be treated as illegitimate under OBA.

---

## B03.CH03.P03.S04 — Institutional legitimacy: separation of functions, anti-capture, rotation

Institutional legitimacy requires that the decision is produced and executed under an institutional architecture that is resistant
to capture: separation of roles, conflict-of-interest controls, rotation systems, and audit-of-auditors. The aim is to ensure that
legitimate procedures remain legitimate over time, not only at the moment of enactment.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Execution, oversight, audit, and adjudication MUST be separated; incompatible role mixing MUST be prohibited and enforceable.
- Conflicts of interest MUST be disclosed and managed (recusals, cooling-off, public registers where appropriate).
- Anti-capture mechanisms MUST exist and be active: rotation/term limits in key functions, procurement exitability, and a
  meta-audit path when audit integrity is challenged.

---

## B03.CH03.P03.S05 — Substantive legitimacy: compatibility with non-negotiables

Substantive legitimacy means the decision does not violate OBA’s **constitutive invariants**: binding caps without stackable
exceptions, measurable E-reduction (function-over-label), commons access within boundary rules with maintenance discipline, ΔM
allocations that remain contestable and non-black-box, and the transparency/auditability minimum. No amount of procedural or
electoral support can legitimise an invariant breach.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any decision that structurally erodes caps, enables black-box allocation, gates commons access via toll mechanisms, or breaks
  auditability/contestability MUST be classified as non-legitimate under OBA.
- If political actors insist on such changes, the decision MUST be handled through constitutive procedure and may require fork
  labeling and mandatory relabeling (compatibility boundary).
- Emergency deviations are only substantively legitimate if strictly temporary with hard sunsets/rollback and post-hoc audit.
