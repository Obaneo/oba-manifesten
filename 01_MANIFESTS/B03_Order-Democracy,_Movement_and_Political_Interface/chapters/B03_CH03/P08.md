# B03.CH03.P08 — Evaluation and re-authorisation: mandate as a repeatable contract

In OBA, a mandate is not a one-off permission slip. It is a **repeatable contract** with a defined baseline, measurable
deliverables in the test axes (E, R/B, caps, ΔG, ΔM), and built-in stop/adjust rules. Evaluation is therefore not “review after
the fact,” but an integral governance mechanism that prevents drift, forces restatements when the model changes, and triggers
re-authorisation when decisions cross from parametric tuning into constitutive deviation.

---

## B03.CH03.P08.S01 — Midterm checks: thresholds for adjustment or stop (hard stops)

Midterm checks exist to prevent “runaway implementation”: once damage accumulates (cap erosion, black-box allocation, exception
stacking), later elections cannot cheaply repair it. OBA requires predefined trigger thresholds and hard stops.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every mandate MUST define midterm review moments and pre-committed thresholds for:
  - adjustment (parameter change within rails),
  - suspension (hard stop), and/or
  - escalation (expanded audit mode / governance intervention).
- Hard stops MUST activate automatically under at least:
  - measurement uncertainty beyond declared bounds,
  - black-box allocation (non-auditable decision rules),
  - cap erosion (structural carve-outs or stacking exceptions),
  - definitional shift without changelog + bridging.
- Midterm checks MUST be evidence-led: results are computed against the pinned definition set and published methods.

---

## B03.CH03.P08.S02 — Ex post audits: effect on E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM and exception ratio

Ex post auditing evaluates the mandate as implemented (not as advertised) and ties outcomes back to the order invariants and
distributional legitimacy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Ex post audits MUST report effects on:
  - **E** (toll/claim extraction): direction, magnitude, and where extraction relocated,
  - **R/B**: distribution profiles (at minimum p10/p25/median) and essentials protection,
  - **Caps**: bindingness, carve-outs, near-cap clustering behaviour, enforcement integrity,
  - **ΔG/commons**: access, service minima, maintenance backlog metrics, gating signals,
  - **ΔM**: channel distribution, contestability, and prohibited-channel leakage.
- Audits MUST include the **exception profile** (ratio, renewals, selectivity, impact-weighting) as a first-class output.
- If data are incomplete, results MUST fail-closed: publish “unknowns” explicitly and trigger enhanced audit mode.

---

## B03.CH03.P08.S03 — Version control: parameter tuning without core break (traceable changelog)

Learning requires change; drift is change without traceability. OBA separates legitimate parameter tuning from constitutive
deviation via strict version control.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any change to parameters, thresholds, or operational rules MUST be recorded in a traceable changelog with:
  - rationale,
  - impact analysis on E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM,
  - effective date,
  - backward-compatibility notes where applicable.
- If a change alters definitions, measurability, auditability, contestability, or cap bindingness, it MUST be treated as
  **constitutive** and routed through the higher-threshold procedure (not “policy maintenance”).
- When versions change, historical series MUST be bridged or restated with transparent methodology (no silent breaks).

---

## B03.CH03.P08.S04 — Public accountability: incident reports and definition sets

Accountability is operational: citizens can only contest decisions if they can see what happened, under which rules, and with what
evidence. OBA requires standardized public outputs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Mandate execution MUST produce public reporting at minimum:
  - a scorecard against the test axes (with trends),
  - an exceptions register (impact-weighted),
  - a decisions log (inputs, rationale, version references),
  - incident reports for failures, drift events, or enforcement anomalies.
- All public reporting MUST reference the applicable **definition-set ID + version** used for computation and classification.
- Corrections MUST be published as restatements with a clear “what changed, why, and how to reproduce it” discipline.

---

## B03.CH03.P08.S05 — Re-authorisation: when a new mandate is required (constitutive vs parametric)

OBA treats legitimacy as conditional: if implementation crosses key boundaries, continued authority requires renewed consent under
the correct decision procedure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Re-authorisation MUST be triggered when:
  - implementation deviates from constitutive rails (caps bindingness, auditability, contestability, commons openness),
  - a definitional change is introduced (or required) to “make the policy work,”
  - emergency powers persist beyond their sunset logic or become normalised,
  - exception stacking becomes structural rather than temporary and bounded.
- Parametric changes within rails MAY proceed under ordinary political procedures, but MUST remain bounded by:
  - published thresholds,
  - non-stackable exceptions,
  - reproducible measurement and audit access.
- If compatibility cannot be maintained, the system MUST initiate fork classification and correct labeling (compatible/partial/non),
  rather than pretending continuity under the same mandate.
