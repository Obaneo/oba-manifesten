# B03.CH10.P10 — Correction mechanisms: intervention in capture incidents

This paragraph specifies the **correction layer** for capture incidents: a binding intervention system that restores contestability and reverses drift.
In OBA, capture is assumed to occur intermittently even under strong controls; therefore the system must be able to (i) detect, (ii) intervene, (iii)
rollback corrupted outputs, (iv) reconstitute institutions, and (v) record lessons without semantic drift. Correction is not “reform discourse”; it is
an operational ladder with authority, timelines, and version-controlled outcomes.

## Core principles

* **Correction must be outcome-capable**: it must change contracts, decisions, and personnel—not merely recommend.
* **Escalate by structure, not by outrage**: intervention triggers are signal-based and pre-committed.
* **Rollback is central**: leaving captured decisions in force preserves the benefit of capture.
* **Reconstitution restores legitimacy**: when institutions are compromised, personnel and governance structures must be reset.
* **Lessons must be versioned**: improvements must be traceable without redefining the core rails.

## B03.CH10.P10.S01 — Intervention ladder: audit escalation → governance reset → legal actions

OBA uses an intervention ladder that increases intrusiveness as evidence of capture strengthens. The ladder prevents both underreaction (softness) and
overreaction (authoritarian shortcuts) by specifying bounded steps.

### Intervention ladder (minimum)

1. **Audit escalation**

   * Trigger: indicator breach (exceptions↑, sanctions↓, reproducibility↓, vendor concentration↑, definition anomalies).
   * Actions: independent re-performance, audit-of-auditors, expanded sampling, procurement and exception review, publication of interim findings.
2. **Governance reset**

   * Trigger: audit confirms structural compromise (selective enforcement, obstructed audits, conflict patterns, systematic exception trading).
   * Actions: replace leadership, rotate teams, freeze high-risk decisions, appoint interim oversight, enforce transparency and registry corrections.
3. **Legal actions**

   * Trigger: illegality, fraud, bribery, deliberate obstruction, or repeated violations.
   * Actions: prosecution/referral, civil recovery, debarment, nullification proceedings, judicially supervised remedies.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Ladder steps **MUST** be pre-specified with authority chains and timelines; ad hoc escalation is capture-prone.
* Audit escalation **MUST** be independent; the compromised unit cannot lead its own investigation.
* Governance reset **MUST** include operational continuity plans to protect essentials and ΔG maintenance during intervention.

## B03.CH10.P10.S02 — Rollback: reversing decisions, contracts, and tooling lock-ins

Rollback removes the benefits of capture. If captured outputs remain, actors learn that capture is profitable even if later “criticised.”

### Rollback targets (canonical)

* **Decisions**: regulatory changes, enforcement priorities, exemptions, eligibility rules, sanctions, settlement agreements.
* **Contracts**: procurement awards, renewals, bundled suites, consulting engagements, data-sharing agreements.
* **Tooling/model lock-ins**: black-box scoring systems, proprietary audit pipelines, opaque dashboards, hidden parameter changes.
* **Definition/exception artefacts**: definition-set reversions, semantic freezes, cancellation of improper exceptions.

### Rollback requirements (minimum)

* **Last-known-good reversion**: revert to pinned, auditable baseline (definition set, rules, tooling) pending reconstruction.
* **Restatement**: recompute dashboards, classifications, and dependent decisions affected by the captured regime.
* **Exitability execution**: enact portability, escrow, and modular replacement; run shadow computation to verify deltas.
* **Compensation discipline**: where rollback harms third parties, remedies must be structured without reintroducing extraction (no asset-support reflex).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Capture-confirmed incidents **MUST** trigger rollback review; refusal to roll back is a legitimacy breach.
* Rollback **MUST** be executable by prior design (exitability clauses, version pinning); lack of rollback capacity is itself a structural failure requiring remediation.
* Restatements **MUST** propagate; partial rollback that leaves downstream artefacts unchanged is prohibited.

## B03.CH10.P10.S03 — Reconstitution: reappointment, rotation, independent review board

When capture compromises a unit, incremental fixes are often insufficient because relationships and norms persist. Reconstitution restores the possibility
of impartial enforcement.

### Reconstitution mechanisms (minimum)

* **Reappointment and removal**: replace conflicted leadership; suspend implicated actors pending review; rehire integrity capacity where lost.
* **Rotation reset**: rotate teams; reassign inspection/audit portfolios; re-randomise sampling assignments.
* **Independent review board**: temporary board with authority to supervise remediation, approve high-risk decisions, and publish findings.
* **Governance redesign**: adjust authority separation, COI rules, and escalation triggers if root cause was structural.
* **Capacity rebuild**: reduce consultant dependence; rebuild internal competence with documented runbooks and training.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Reconstitution **MUST** be time-bounded with explicit exit criteria; permanent emergency boards are prohibited.
* Independent boards **MUST** be auditable, conflict-disclosed, and rotation-bound; otherwise they become the next capture point.
* If staff churn or audit obstruction persists after reconstitution, escalation must proceed to stronger legal and procurement remedies.

## B03.CH10.P10.S04 — Sanctions: personal, institutional, vendor-level

Sanctions deter and correct by imposing costs proportional to the benefit of capture and by removing repeat offenders from the system. OBA requires a
multi-layer sanction ladder: individuals, institutions, and vendors all can be capture actors.

### Sanction ladder (minimum)

* **Personal sanctions**

  * Fines scaled to benefit, bans from office/lobbying, disqualification from procurement roles, criminal referral where applicable.
* **Institutional sanctions**

  * Budgetary penalties, leadership replacement, loss of delegated authority, mandatory external oversight, nullification of compromised decisions.
* **Vendor-level sanctions**

  * Contract termination, debarment, compulsory disclosure and remediation, public notice, enforced modular replacement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Sanctions **MUST** be logged in a public violation register linked to decisions/contracts; secrecy preserves reputational arbitrage.
* Repeat violations **MUST** escalate nonlinearly; linear fines are priced in as cost of business.
* If sanctions are not applied despite verified breaches (sanction rate collapse), the enforcement unit itself becomes subject to intervention.

## B03.CH10.P10.S05 — Lessons and version control: structural adjustments without core breach (traceable changelog)

Capture incidents should improve the system without enabling semantic drift. OBA requires that “lessons learned” become **version-controlled structural
adjustments**: new controls, revised thresholds, improved registries—while preserving canonical definitions and rails.

### Lessons-to-structure pipeline (minimum)

* **Incident review**: mechanism analysis (how capture entered), affected domains, and evidence bundle.
* **Control update proposal**: specific control changes (rotation rules, access rules, exitability clauses, trigger thresholds).
* **Version control**: changes are applied through definition-set versions, protocol updates, and governance runbooks with changelogs and bridging notes.
* **Backward compatibility**: where possible, preserve comparability; where not, publish migration and restatement plans.
* **Publication**: publish the change and rationale; update the early-warning set and escalation mapping.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* All anti-capture control changes **MUST** be traceable (changelog + version pinning) and must not redefine core rails to make past failure “disappear.”
* Where a lesson implies new metrics or indicators, the system **MUST** backfill and restate where feasible; otherwise trend claims are bounded.
* If “lessons” are used to expand discretion, reduce auditability, or normalise exceptions, they **MUST** be classified as regression (OOA drift).
