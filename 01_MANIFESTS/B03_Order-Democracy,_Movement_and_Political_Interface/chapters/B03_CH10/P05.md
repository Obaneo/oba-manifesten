# B03.CH10.P05 — Media and the information ecosystem: capture via narrative infrastructure

This paragraph specifies how **capture operates through narrative infrastructure**: the media and information ecosystem that determines what becomes
salient, what becomes credible, and what becomes actionable. In OBA, the information layer is not “soft”; it is a primary control surface because
order-democracy depends on shared definitions, measurable claims, and contestability. Capture in this domain rarely looks like censorship. It looks like
**agenda steering**, **definition shifting**, **metric substitution**, and **incident amplification** that forces exception regimes and protects toll
positions.

OBA therefore requires countermeasures that preserve epistemic integrity without becoming authoritarian: **open dashboards**, **replication tooling**,
**source-duty**, and **correction/restatement discipline**, especially under crisis conditions.

## Core principles

* **Narrative is an order instrument**: it determines which rails are politically enforceable.
* **Capture works by confusion, not just by lies**: ambiguity and redefinition undermine measurability.
* **Platforms are chokepoints**: distribution/ranking/moderation can silently shape the political field.
* **Countermeasures must be procedural**: transparency + replication + correction, not viewpoint policing.
* **Crisis is the takeover window**: blackouts, panic frames, and incident politics expand exceptions.

## B03.CH10.P05.S01 — Agenda-setting: think-tank stacks, expert panels, sponsored content

Agenda-setting capture determines what is discussable and what is ignored. It operates upstream of elections and policy text by manufacturing the
appearance of consensus or inevitability.

### Capture mechanisms (agenda infrastructure)

* **Think-tank stacks**: chains of institutes and “research partners” funded by interested sectors that supply ready-made narratives, metrics, and draft ideas.
* **Expert panels**: recurring commentator networks with undisclosed conflicts; “independent experts” who are functionally lobbyists.
* **Sponsored content**: paid narratives presented as journalism; native ads; sponsored podcasts; “studies” released via media partnerships.
* **Topic saturation**: repetitive framing that crowds out structural issues (E, caps, ΔG maintenance) in favour of symbolic disputes.
* **Gatekeeping by format**: privileging short formats that cannot carry definitions, evidence tiers, or uncertainty.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Policy-relevant “expert” content **MUST** disclose funding, affiliations, and material interests; nondisclosure is treated as a capture indicator.
* Media ecosystems that regularly influence binding policy text **MUST** maintain provenance disclosures (who funded what) in accessible form.
* Public institutions **MUST** not rely on undisclosed think-tank materials for definitions, metrics, or draft text without publishing lineage.

## B03.CH10.P05.S02 — Platform power: distribution, ranking, moderation as a political choke point

Digital platforms concentrate distribution. That concentration creates a political chokepoint: what is boosted, suppressed, demonetised, or de-ranked
shapes public belief and coalition formation. Capture here does not require overt coercion; it can occur through **incentive-aligned moderation** and
opaque ranking.

### Platform capture mechanisms (canonical)

* **Ranking opacity**: algorithmic curation changes visibility without explanation; actors cannot contest suppression or amplification.
* **Monetisation leverage**: demonetisation or policy violations used to discipline narratives indirectly.
* **Coordinated influence**: bot networks and microtargeting create artificial salience and wedge polarization.
* **Policy drift**: moderation rules shift during crises; exceptions expand without audit or rollback.
* **Vendor dependency**: state institutions become dependent on platform tooling (analytics, messaging) that shapes what they can do.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* For platforms functioning as de facto public forums, minimum **contestability** is required: explainability at category level, appeal routes, and rule change logs.
* Political advertising and amplification mechanisms **MUST** be logged with provenance; covert microtargeting is a capture surface.
* State reliance on platform analytics/moderation tooling **MUST** be constrained by exitability and audit rights; otherwise governance becomes platform-dependent.

## B03.CH10.P05.S03 — Disinformation/confusion: definition shifting, metric gaming, incident amplification

In OBA, the most damaging information attack is not falsehood but **semantic sabotage**: shifting definitions so that measurement and accountability
collapse. Confusion also enables exception politics: incidents are amplified to justify emergency measures and to distract from structural extraction.

### Confusion attack patterns (canonical)

* **Definition shifting**: “cap”, “commons”, “audit”, “extraction”, “competition” redefined to fit current interests (semantic washing).
* **Metric substitution**: swap real E-reduction or ΔG quality for proxy KPIs that can be gamed.
* **Uncertainty denial**: present contested model outputs as certainty; or weaponise uncertainty to block action (“nothing can be known”).
* **Incident amplification**: rare events drive policy; systematic drift becomes invisible; emergency powers normalise.
* **Edge-case spotlighting**: highlight fringe cases to delegitimise rails (e.g., “a fraud case” used to justify broad surveillance).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Public debate on OBA-relevant policy **MUST** be anchored to published definition sets; redefining terms without disclosure is classified as capture rhetoric.
* Metric changes **MUST** be versioned with restatements and backward comparability; otherwise trend reporting is invalid.
* Crisis-driven narratives **MUST** be paired with baseline statistics and drift indicators (exceptions, sanctions, ΔG backlog) to prevent incident capture.

## B03.CH10.P05.S04 — Countermeasures: open dashboards, replication tooling, source-duty

OBA countermeasures do not police viewpoints; they impose **procedural epistemic rails** that make claims auditable and corrections enforceable.

### Countermeasure set (minimum)

* **Open dashboards**: public, version-pinned indicators for E proxies, R/B floors, caps compliance, ΔG metrics, ΔM channel ledgers, exception ratio, sanction rate.
* **Replication tooling**: public calculation rules, reference implementations, reproducible datasets (privacy-safe), and independent run capability.
* **Source-duty**: a norm and policy requirement that policy-relevant claims cite data lineage, methods, and uncertainty; “trust me” claims are downgraded.
* **Restatement discipline**: when errors are discovered, corrections propagate through dashboards and downstream summaries.
* **Public incident reviews**: structured post-mortems that separate narrative from mechanism and that update definition sets where necessary.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Institutions **MUST** publish dashboards and replication artefacts in accessible formats; otherwise “transparency” remains non-operational.
* Source-duty **MUST** apply to official communications and advisory outputs used for policy; undisclosed sources are treated as non-credible.
* Corrections **MUST** be restatements with propagation; silent edits are prohibited.

## B03.CH10.P05.S05 — Crisis information: measurement continuity, anti-blackout discipline, corrections

Crises are when capture pressure peaks: urgency expands discretion and reduces scrutiny. The information regime must therefore preserve measurement and
contestability precisely when they are most inconvenient.

### Crisis information regime (required)

* **Measurement continuity**: maintain core indicators during crisis (E, exceptions, sanctions, ΔG backlog, ΔM flows) with explicit uncertainty bands.
* **Anti-blackout discipline**: prohibit suspension of dashboards and registers without special procedure; any suspension is time-limited and logged.
* **Emergency comms protocol**: distinguish known facts, estimates, and unknowns; publish assumptions; avoid certainty theatre.
* **Correction priority**: fast restatements for materially wrong claims; publish correction logs and impacts on decisions taken.
* **Narrative restraint**: avoid scapegoating and incident-only frames; include structural drift indicators in briefings.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Emergency regimes **MUST** include an information continuity plan; blackouts are treated as legitimacy breaches unless narrowly justified.
* If crisis communication repeatedly diverges from later evidence without restatements, the system **MUST** trigger audit escalation.
* Where crisis measures expand secrecy or platform dependence, sunset and ex post audit requirements **MUST** tighten rather than loosen.
