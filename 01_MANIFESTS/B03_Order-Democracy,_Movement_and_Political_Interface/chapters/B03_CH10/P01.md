# B03.CH10.P01 — Problem statement: capture as an endogenous drift force in order-democracy

This paragraph establishes **capture** as an **endogenous drift force** in order-democracy: not an external accident, but a continuous pressure that
emerges from incentive asymmetries between (i) actors who benefit from extraction and rule-bending, and (ii) the diffuse public that bears the costs.
In OBA terms, capture is the systematic tendency to re-open **OOA pathways**—through exceptions, opaque allocation, and definition manipulation—without
formally renouncing OBA language. The chapter therefore treats capture control as *core infrastructure*: if capture is not continuously constrained,
order-democracy regresses into symbolic compliance with preserved extraction.

## Core principles

* **Capture is structural**: it is produced by incentive gradients, not by “bad people.”
* **Drift is silent**: capture operates through small changes in definitions, exceptions, measurement, and procurement—rarely through open coups.
* **Order targets are capture targets**: wherever OBA binds extraction (caps, ΔM channel discipline, commons substitution), capture concentrates.
* **Anti-capture is not moralism**: it is a design layer with logs, rotation, sanctions, and exitability.

## B03.CH10.P01.S01 — Capture domains: definitions, audit, ΔM, commons, exception regimes

Capture seeks **control points**: places where small interventions produce large downstream effects. In OBA-compatible governance, the primary capture
domains are the following.

### Canonical capture domains (minimum set)

* **Definition control**: shifting meanings, thresholds, consolidation rules, and measurement bases so that extraction persists while metrics appear compliant.
* **Audit and verification**: weakening replication rights, subordinating auditors, introducing proprietary methods, and delaying restatements.
* **ΔM channels**: steering fiscal/credit/guarantee flows toward asset/claim support or insider allocation, while preserving opacity and discretion.
* **Commons governance**: gating access, underfunding maintenance, operator capture of boards, and converting commons into quasi-toll clubs.
* **Exception regimes**: carving holes, extending “temporary” waivers, and creating selective enforcement patterns that neutralise caps and rails.

### Why these domains are central

These domains share a property: they affect **measurability and enforceability**. Control definitions and audits, and you control what can be proven.
Control ΔM and exceptions, and you control where pressure relief is applied and who is protected. Control commons governance, and you can recreate toll
dependence through scarcity and gating.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any system claiming OBA compatibility **MUST** explicitly enumerate these capture domains and assign owners, logs, and escalation triggers per domain.
* Definition changes and exceptions **MUST** be treated as high-risk acts requiring registries, version pinning, and independent review.

## B03.CH10.P01.S02 — Why transparency alone is insufficient (incentive structure)

Transparency is necessary, but not sufficient, because the core problem is **incentive structure under asymmetric capacity**. If the gains from capture
are concentrated and the costs are diffuse, then even transparent systems drift unless they also impose **costs, constraints, and reversibility**.

### Why transparency fails on its own (canonical mechanisms)

* **Attention scarcity**: disclosure can be voluminous; high-capacity actors can exploit that most people cannot process it.
* **Complexity laundering**: compliance becomes a documentation game; opacity shifts from “hidden” to “uninterpretable.”
* **Selective enforcement**: even with visible rules, if sanction probability is low or selective, capture persists.
* **Metric gaming**: transparent metrics can be Goodharted; actors optimise for dashboards rather than for real E-reduction and ΔG maintenance.
* **Time asymmetry**: capture benefits occur now; harm appears later (maintenance decay, concentration, silent definition drift).

### What must complement transparency

* **Sanctions and bans** for prohibited practices and nondisclosure.
* **Rotation and randomisation** to reduce relational capture and case selection.
* **Exitability** (technical/contractual) to prevent vendor lock-in and audit dependence.
* **Independent replication** and mandatory restatements to break metric laundering.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Transparency without enforceable sanctions and rotation **MUST** be classified as insufficient for anti-capture claims.
* Any transparency regime **MUST** include “interpretability rails”: standardized formats, machine-readable registers, and replication tooling.

## B03.CH10.P01.S03 — Delimitation: anti-capture ≠ anti-pluralism; legitimate interests remain

Anti-capture is often misrepresented as anti-pluralism (“you want to silence stakeholders”). OBA rejects this framing. Interests and advocacy are
legitimate; capture is the covert conversion of influence into **un-auditable control over rules, measurement, and enforcement**.

### Legitimate pluralism (allowed)

* Advocacy, lobbying, campaigning, and critique—**under disclosure and access rules**.
* Competing policy proposals—**expressed in rail terms with evidence**.
* Civil-society organisation and union activity—**with funding transparency and internal accountability**.
* Expert input—**when contestable and replicable**.

### Capture (prohibited)

* Hidden funding and astroturfing.
* Revolving-door consultancy stacks that bypass cooling-off.
* Proprietary black-box metrics used for core decisions.
* Exception trading and selective enforcement.
* Vendor lock-in that makes audit and computation dependent on a single supplier.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Anti-capture measures **MUST** be framed as *procedural constraints*, not as suppression of viewpoint diversity.
* Any restriction on participation **MUST** be justified as necessary to preserve contestability, auditability, and equal access—not as ideological filtering.

## B03.CH10.P01.S04 — Output: minimum anti-capture set + measurable detection signals

The output of this chapter is a **minimum anti-capture set** plus a **measurable detection and escalation layer**. The goal is operational: a system
must be able to say, at any time, whether capture pressure is increasing and what interventions are triggered.

### Output A — Minimum anti-capture set (normative modules)

* **Lobby register + access hygiene**: registration, publication of inputs, agenda disclosure, cooling-off for advisers, sanctions.
* **Funding firewall**: donor disclosure, caps, bundling bans, in-kind transparency, public matching options, audit and penalties.
* **Revolving-door controls**: role taxonomy, cooling-off, conflict registers, recusals, nullification rules for conflicted decisions.
* **Media/epistemic countermeasures**: source-duty, replication tooling, open dashboards, correction/restatement discipline.
* **Rotation/randomisation/redundancy**: term limits, audit-of-auditors, random case assignment, parallel implementations.
* **Vendor exitability**: open standards, portability, escrow, shadow computation, supplier diversity requirements.
* **Definition/exception protection**: changelog discipline, semantic freeze mechanisms, hard stops, re-authorisation gates.

### Output B — Measurable detection signals (minimum indicator suite)

* **Institutional**: sanction rate↓, enforcement latency↑, unexplained staff churn, audit scope shrinkage.
* **Governance**: exceptions↑, renewals↑, reproducibility↓, vendor concentration↑, blocked audit access incidents.
* **Economic**: E stabilisation/↑, fee-stacks↑, concentration↑, R/B floor stress.
* **Narrative**: uniform talking points, source poverty, definition shifting in public discourse, metric substitution.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Systems claiming OBA-compatible anti-capture **MUST** implement the minimum set and publish the indicator suite on a fixed schedule.
* Detection signals **MUST** be linked to escalation protocols with timelines and authority chains; “monitoring” without intervention triggers is non-compliant.
* Where indicators are missing or non-replicable, governance scope **MUST** fail-closed to the auditable subset until corrected.
