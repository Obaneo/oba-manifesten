# B03.CH10.P03 — Funding: political funding and civil-society funding without blackmail

This paragraph specifies funding rules that prevent **financial capture** of political parties, candidates, advocacy groups, and civil-society
organisations. In OBA, funding is a primary capture channel because it converts concentrated resources into **agenda control**, **definition drift**,
**exception trading**, and **enforcement softness**—often without any illegal act. The target is not to abolish pluralism, but to prevent **blackmail
structures**: situations where actors must align with funders to survive, and where public policy becomes a byproduct of donor portfolios.

## Core principles

* **Money is access + agenda + endurance**: funding shapes what can be said, pursued, and sustained over time.
* **Disclosure must resolve control**: it must identify who ultimately controls money flows, not merely front entities.
* **Limits must prevent dominance**: caps and bundling bans prevent a few actors from becoming structural veto-holders.
* **Public matching can re-balance**: public funding is a tool to reduce dependency on concentrated private money.
* **Firewalls prevent laundering**: sector money must not be repackaged through intermediaries to evade limits.
* **Audit and sanctions make rules real**: without enforcement, funding regimes become symbolic.

## B03.CH10.P03.S01 — Donor disclosure: thresholds, ultimate beneficial control of donations

Disclosure is not satisfied by naming an intermediary. It must resolve **ultimate beneficial control** (who is behind the money) and prevent donor
laundering through shells, associations, or layered foundations.

### Disclosure requirements (minimum)

* **Thresholds**: disclose donors above defined thresholds (with aggregation rules to prevent splitting).
* **Ultimate control**: identify the natural persons or controlling entities behind donations routed through companies, trusts, foundations, or PAC-like vehicles.
* **Donation taxonomy**: separate direct cash, in-kind support, services, data, media buys, staff secondments, and “research” sponsorship.
* **Timing disclosure**: disclose within short windows to prevent elections from being run on undisclosed money with post-hoc transparency.
* **Recipient disclosure**: disclose both donor and recipient sides, enabling cross-checks (donor ledgers ↔ recipient ledgers).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Disclosure **MUST** include aggregation rules (anti-splitting) and must identify ultimate controlling parties for indirect donations.
* Anonymous or untraceable donations above de minimis **MUST** be prohibited; “unknown donor” is a capture vector.
* Donor data **MUST** be published in machine-readable form and linked to the lobby register where relevant.

## B03.CH10.P03.S02 — Limits: caps on contributions, bundling bans, in-kind transparency

Transparency does not prevent dominance when actors can legally outspend the public sphere. OBA therefore requires **limits** that cap influence and
block bundling strategies that circumvent individual caps.

### Limit instruments (minimum)

* **Contribution caps**: per donor, per period, per recipient; include cumulative caps across affiliated entities.
* **Bundling bans**: prohibit bundlers from collecting and routing multiple donations as a single influence channel; require disclosure and/or hard limits.
* **In-kind transparency**: treat in-kind contributions as donations at fair-value valuation (media, staff time, analytics tooling, legal services).
* **Third-party spend rules**: regulate independent expenditures that are functionally coordinated; coordination tests must exist.
* **Foreign influence constraints**: prohibit or strictly constrain cross-border money and indirect funding via controlled entities.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Caps **MUST** apply to both cash and in-kind; exempting in-kind produces immediate capture via “free services.”
* Bundling and coordination evasion **MUST** be explicitly prohibited with investigative authority.
* If limits exist only formally but are bypassable through affiliates or in-kind channels, the regime is non-compliant by construction.

## B03.CH10.P03.S03 — Public matching: public funding as an anti-capture instrument

Public funding is treated as a **structural anti-capture instrument**: it can reduce dependency on concentrated donors by amplifying small-donor support
and by providing baseline operational capacity to parties and legitimate civil society.

### Public matching design primitives

* **Small-donor matching**: multiply small contributions to make broad support economically meaningful.
* **Baseline grants**: provide minimum capacity for qualifying organisations to operate without donor blackmail.
* **Qualification rails**: eligibility based on transparent criteria (membership counts, vote share, compliance history), with anti-fraud controls.
* **Spending transparency**: public funds require stricter accounting, publication of spend categories, and audit access.

### Risks and constraints

* **State capture of pluralism**: public funding can be weaponised by incumbents via eligibility manipulation.
* **Bureaucratic gatekeeping**: compliance burdens can exclude new entrants and low-capacity movements.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Public matching regimes **MUST** be rule-based, auditable, and resistant to incumbent manipulation (stable qualification criteria + independent review).
* Public funding **MUST** not substitute for donor disclosure and limits; it complements them.
* Misuse of public funds **MUST** trigger sanctions, repayment, and potential loss of eligibility.

## B03.CH10.P03.S04 — Firewall against sector funding: prohibited repackaging and intermediaries

Capture persists when sector money is laundered through intermediaries: foundations, think tanks, trade associations, “citizen groups,” or consultancy
networks that obscure origin and intent. OBA therefore requires explicit **firewalls** against repackaging.

### Firewall rules (minimum)

* **Look-through rules**: treat donations as originating from the ultimate controlling sector actor if routed through controlled intermediaries.
* **Prohibited repackaging**: ban pass-through entities whose primary function is political influence without donor traceability.
* **Intermediary disclosure**: require intermediaries to disclose major funders, governance control, and project-level funding for political work.
* **Sector-risk classification**: identify high-capture-risk sectors (toll sectors, procurement-heavy industries, regulated monopolies) requiring stricter scrutiny.
* **Ban on contingent funding**: prohibit “donations” conditional on policy outcomes, appointments, or procurement advantages.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Funding rules **MUST** include look-through and anti-pass-through provisions; otherwise disclosure and caps are trivially evaded.
* Think-tank and advocacy funding used in policy influence **MUST** be disclosed at project level where it shapes definitions, metrics, or draft text.
* If sector funding is structurally hidden, policy outputs **MUST** be treated as high capture-risk and subject to nullification review.

## B03.CH10.P03.S05 — Audit: periodic independent review and sanctioning for violations

Funding rules require continuous audit because evasion is adaptive. OBA therefore requires periodic, independent review with real sanction power and with
the ability to correct outcomes (not just punish after the fact).

### Audit regime components (minimum)

* **Periodic audits**: scheduled audits of parties, candidates, and qualifying civil-society organisations; risk-based deep dives for anomalies.
* **Cross-ledger reconciliation**: donor-side declarations reconciled against recipient-side ledgers; third-party spend reconciliation.
* **Anomaly detection**: threshold bunching, affiliate splitting, suspicious in-kind valuations, sudden spend spikes, repeat intermediary use.
* **Publication**: privacy-safe publication of audit outcomes, sanctions, and corrected accounts (restatements).
* **Sanction ladder**: fines, loss of eligibility, bans, and—where necessary—criminal referral; repeat violations escalate.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Funding regimes **MUST** include independent audit authority with access to underlying records, not merely summary reports.
* Audit outcomes **MUST** produce restatements and be linked to the violation register; “quiet settlements” are non-compliant.
* If sanction rates fall while anomalies rise, escalation protocols **MUST** trigger audit-of-auditors and governance intervention.
