# B03.CH10.P06 — Rotation systems and terms: structural anti-capture

This paragraph specifies **rotation** as structural anti-capture infrastructure. Capture is largely relational: repeated interaction between the same
people, vendors, and regulated entities produces reciprocity, dependency, selective enforcement, and norm drift—even without corruption. Rotation
reduces relational entanglement, while **randomisation** prevents adversarial pre-positioning, and **institutional redundancy** creates contestability
by ensuring that no single team, model, or implementation becomes the sole gatekeeper of truth.

Rotation must be balanced with **continuity**: knowledge and competence must persist despite turnover. Therefore OBA requires runbooks, documentation,
version pinning, and second-line assurance to prevent rotation from degrading capacity.

## Core principles

* **Capture is relational**: repeated contact creates soft capture; rotation breaks the channel.
* **Randomisation defeats targeting**: adversaries cannot reliably predict who will inspect or review them.
* **Redundancy creates contestability**: parallel teams/implementations reduce single-point manipulation.
* **Continuity is non-negotiable**: without documentation, rotation becomes capacity collapse and forces consultant dependency (a capture route).

## B03.CH10.P06.S01 — Term limits: key positions in audit/oversight/commons governance

Term limits prevent long-lived fiefdoms and reduce the ability to build entrenched patronage networks. They are most critical where authority includes
audit scope, sanctions, procurement influence, or commons boundary rules.

### Term-limit targets (minimum)

* **Audit leadership**: chief auditors, lead verifiers, audit program directors.
* **Oversight bodies**: chairs and key members of oversight committees and regulators with enforcement authority.
* **Commons governance**: board chairs, operator representatives, high-influence stewards, procurement approvers.
* **Procurement gate roles**: those who approve contracts, tooling, and vendor renewals in high-risk domains.

### Design constraints

* **Staggered terms**: prevent simultaneous turnover that collapses institutional memory.
* **Cooling-off for influence**: term limits must pair with post-role restrictions to prevent “rotate out, lobby back in.”
* **Role re-entry rules**: limits on cycling between adjacent bodies (audit ↔ oversight ↔ commons board) without time separation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-risk governance roles **MUST** have term limits and re-entry constraints; indefinite tenure is a capture indicator.
* Term limits **MUST** be enforced by registry and cannot be overridden by ad hoc “exception for continuity” without independent review and sunset.

## B03.CH10.P06.S02 — Team rotation: audit of auditors, cross-review, second-line assurance

Capture can occur at team level even if individuals rotate. OBA therefore requires **team rotation** and **cross-review** mechanisms that treat audit and
oversight as multi-layer systems.

### Team rotation mechanisms (minimum)

* **Audit-of-auditors**: periodic meta-audit of audit teams’ methods, sampling, enforcement choices, and conflict patterns.
* **Cross-review**: independent teams re-run or review a subset of audits and decisions to detect bias and softness.
* **Second-line assurance**: a separate assurance function verifies that controls are operating (like internal control validation, but independent and auditable).
* **Peer rotation**: rotate reviewers across domains to reduce domain-specific capture (without sacrificing competence—see continuity section).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Audit functions **MUST** be subject to audit-of-auditors; self-attestation is non-compliant.
* Cross-review **MUST** include re-performance (not only desk review) for a subset of cases, especially where sanctions or procurement outcomes are high stakes.
* If audit outcomes show systematic softness (sanctions↓, latency↑, exceptions↑), team rotation escalation is mandatory.

## B03.CH10.P06.S03 — Randomisation: sampling of cases, assignment of inspections

Randomisation converts enforcement from predictable theatre into adversarially robust inspection. If targets can predict inspections, they adapt:
they window-dress compliance around scheduled checks and route violations into uninspected channels.

### Randomisation domains (canonical)

* **Case sampling**: randomly select cases for deep audit among all eligible cases, with risk-based stratification.
* **Inspector assignment**: random assignment of inspectors/teams to targets to reduce relational entanglement and bribery targeting.
* **Spot checks**: unannounced inspections within defined legal boundaries, particularly in procurement and commons gating contexts.
* **Document selection**: random selection of contracts, exemptions, and amendments for lineage verification.

### Design constraints

* **Seeded randomness with audit trail**: random processes must be reproducible (logged seeds, method published) to prevent “randomness laundering.”
* **Stratified risk overlays**: combine random sampling with risk scoring to avoid missing rare but high-impact capture patterns.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Enforcement regimes **MUST** include randomised sampling and assignment with logged, reproducible methods.
* Any non-random override (manual selection) **MUST** be logged with rationale; otherwise selection becomes a capture surface.
* If inspection outcomes cluster suspiciously (few findings despite rising indicators), the randomisation system must be audited for tampering.

## B03.CH10.P06.S04 — Institutional redundancy: parallel teams/implementations for contestability

Redundancy is not waste; it is a contestability mechanism. Where a single team or vendor controls measurement, audit, or allocation tooling, capture can
occur by quietly shifting methods or parameters. Parallelism creates an independent baseline.

### Redundancy mechanisms (minimum)

* **Parallel teams**: independent teams produce measurements or audit results for the same domain periodically.
* **Second implementation**: a separate implementation of critical calculation rules (“shadow computation”) that can detect drift or manipulation.
* **Independent datasets**: alternative data sources or collection pathways where feasible (triangulation).
* **Institutional backstops**: an independent review board empowered to commission alternative analyses and publish results.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Critical rails (caps compliance, ΔM allocation rules, auditability metrics) **MUST** have redundancy: at least one independent re-computation path.
* Where redundancy is infeasible, the system **MUST** justify why and must tighten other controls (randomisation, stronger audit access, stricter vendor exitability).
* If vendor or team concentration rises, redundancy requirements must tighten (escalation rule).

## B03.CH10.P06.S05 — Continuity: knowledge retention despite rotation (documentation, runbooks)

Rotation without continuity causes capacity collapse and forces dependence on consultants or vendors—an indirect capture route. OBA therefore requires
explicit continuity infrastructure that preserves competence and institutional memory.

### Continuity infrastructure (minimum)

* **Runbooks**: step-by-step operational procedures for audits, inspections, escalation, and restatements.
* **Documentation**: decision schemas, definition-set mappings, sampling methods, tooling specs, and known failure modes.
* **Version pinning**: all methods and tools tied to versioned definition sets and changelogs; reproducibility is mandatory.
* **Training pipeline**: onboarding and recurring training for rotated staff; certification where appropriate.
* **Knowledge transfer windows**: staggered transitions with overlap, documented handovers, and post-handover review.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Rotation regimes **MUST** include runbooks and versioned documentation; undocumented practice is non-auditable and capture-prone.
* If turnover causes reliance on external consultants for core governance functions, the system **MUST** be flagged as capture-risk and corrected (capacity rebuild + exitability).
* Continuity controls themselves **MUST** be auditable; “tribal knowledge” is prohibited in high-risk domains.
