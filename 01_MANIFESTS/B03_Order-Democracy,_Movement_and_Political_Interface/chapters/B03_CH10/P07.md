# B03.CH10.P07 — Vendor lock-in as capture: tooling, models, and black-box audits

This paragraph specifies **vendor lock-in** as a capture mechanism in modern governance: when critical functions (measurement, allocation, eligibility,
pricing, enforcement, audit) depend on proprietary tooling or models controlled by a small set of suppliers, the vendor becomes a de facto co-sovereign.
Lock-in undermines contestability and enables silent drift through opaque parameter changes, proprietary “risk scores,” and audit regimes that cannot be
reproduced.

OBA therefore requires: (i) **open standards** for data, interfaces, and calculation rules, (ii) **exitability** as a contractual and technical
property, (iii) strict **reproducibility** with version pinning and independent runs, (iv) **concentration control** to avoid single points of failure,
and (v) **sanctions** that can unwind lock-in and penalise obstruction.

## Core principles

* **Dependence is power**: if the state cannot compute or audit without a vendor, governance authority has moved.
* **Black boxes break contestability**: what cannot be replicated cannot be governed democratically.
* **Exitability is a rail**: portability and replacement capability are required, not “nice-to-have.”
* **Redundancy beats monopoly**: parallel computation and supplier diversity reduce capture surface.
* **Contracts must encode contestability**: audit rights, change control, and transparency are contractual obligations.

## B03.CH10.P07.S01 — Open standards: data, interfaces, calculation rules, model cards

Open standards prevent proprietary chokepoints and enable independent verification. “Open” here does not require open-sourcing everything; it requires
that the **governing logic and interfaces** are not monopolised.

### Open standard components (minimum)

* **Data schemas**: documented formats, field definitions, and semantics aligned with definition sets; backward compatibility rules.
* **Interfaces/APIs**: published interface contracts enabling multiple implementations and portability.
* **Calculation rules**: public formulas and aggregation rules for core order metrics (caps compliance, E proxies, ΔM classifications, eligibility criteria).
* **Model cards** (where models are used): purpose, training/fit scope, inputs/outputs, known failure modes, bias/robustness tests, update policy, and audit hooks.
* **Change control**: any standard change must be versioned, logged, and accompanied by migration notes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any tool/model influencing eligibility, allocation, pricing, sanctions, or audit outcomes **MUST** have published calculation rules and interface contracts.
* “Proprietary logic” for core order functions **MUST** be classified as non-compatible unless independently replicable and contestable.
* Standards **MUST** be pinned to definition-set versions; semantic drift in data fields is prohibited.

## B03.CH10.P07.S02 — Exitability: portability, escrow, second implementation, shadow computation

Exitability is the ability to replace a vendor without losing operational capacity, data integrity, or auditability. Without exitability, procurement is
not competitive; it is a long-term dependency contract that becomes a capture channel.

### Exitability primitives (minimum)

* **Portability**: data export in open formats; documented extraction processes; no “hostage” data structures.
* **Escrow/continuity**: escrow of critical code/configuration where necessary; continuity plans for service operation during transition.
* **Second implementation**: at least one alternative implementation path for critical computations (reference implementation, independent vendor, or internal build).
* **Shadow computation**: independent re-computation of key outputs (caps status, eligibility decisions, allocation outcomes) to detect silent drift.
* **Transition testing**: periodic “exit drills” to verify that replacement is feasible (like disaster recovery tests).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-risk systems **MUST** include contractual portability and verified export rights; “we can export” without proof is non-compliant.
* Exitability **MUST** be tested periodically; untested exitability is fiction.
* If shadow computation reveals unexplained deltas, incident review and vendor change control escalation are mandatory.

## B03.CH10.P07.S03 — Reproducibility: version pinning, changelog discipline, independent runs

Reproducibility is the condition for contestability. If outputs cannot be reproduced by independent actors, the system becomes authority-by-assertion.
OBA therefore requires strict version discipline across data, code, models, and definitions.

### Reproducibility stack (minimum)

* **Version pinning**: every run cites definition-set version, code/version hash, model version, and dataset snapshot IDs.
* **Changelog discipline**: changes to logic, thresholds, features, or parameters must be logged with rationale and impact notes.
* **Independent runs**: third parties (auditors, oversight bodies) can execute the computation on the same inputs and reproduce outputs.
* **Restatements**: when errors are found, corrected outputs propagate to dashboards and decisions (with traceable deltas).
* **Deterministic modes**: where possible, deterministic computation paths for audit and legal defensibility (stochastic elements logged and bounded).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any vendor system producing binding outputs **MUST** support independent reproduction and provide artefacts needed for re-runs.
* Changes affecting outputs **MUST** be announced and logged before deployment (except emergencies with strict sunset and ex post audit).
* If reproducibility falls (cannot re-run, missing artefacts, undocumented changes), the system **MUST** fail-closed to an auditable subset until corrected.

## B03.CH10.P07.S04 — Concentration control: supplier diversity, single points of failure

Even with good contracts, concentration increases systemic risk and capture leverage. A dominant supplier can shape standards, pricing, and compliance
interpretations, and can threaten continuity to resist sanction or termination.

### Concentration controls (minimum)

* **Supplier diversity**: avoid single-vendor dependence in critical rails (audit tooling, allocation engines, identity, payments, dashboards).
* **Modular procurement**: split contracts into replaceable modules with published interfaces; avoid bundled “suite” lock-in.
* **Second source requirement**: maintain at least one alternative supplier or internal fallback for critical functions.
* **Single point of failure audits**: periodic review of concentration metrics and dependency graphs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Critical order functions **MUST** avoid single-vendor lock-in; where unavoidable, justification and tightened controls are required (shadow computation + stronger audit rights).
* Bundled contracts that eliminate competition **MUST** be treated as capture-risk and require special approval with published rationale.
* If supplier concentration rises beyond thresholds, escalation must trigger re-tendering or modularisation plans.

## B03.CH10.P07.S05 — Sanctions: contract termination, bans, re-tendering, public notice

Vendor capture cannot be deterred without credible sanctions. Sanctions must be capable of restoring contestability, not merely imposing costs while
dependency remains.

### Vendor sanction ladder (minimum)

* **Corrective orders**: mandated documentation, audit access, reproducibility fixes, and change control remediation with deadlines.
* **Financial penalties**: service credits and fines tied to obstruction, undisclosed changes, and audit failures.
* **Contract termination**: termination rights triggered by audit obstruction, non-reproducibility, security/privacy violations, or repeated noncompliance.
* **Bans/debarment**: exclusion from future tenders for severe or repeated violations (black-box laundering, corruption, obstruction).
* **Re-tendering and modular replacement**: structured transition plan to replace components, not only vendors.
* **Public notice**: publish sanction decisions, reasons, and remediation steps; secrecy protects repeat offenders.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Contracts for critical rails **MUST** include termination and audit-access clauses with enforceable timelines.
* Sanction outcomes **MUST** be public (privacy-safe) and logged in the violation register; secret settlements are non-compliant.
* If a vendor is sanctioned but remains irreplaceable, the system **MUST** treat this as a structural failure and trigger an exitability remediation program (fail-closed where necessary).
