# B03.CH10.P04 — Revolving doors: role switching and conflicts of interest

This paragraph specifies the **revolving-door** and **conflict-of-interest (COI)** regime required for OBA-compatible governance. Revolving doors are
treated as a capture pathway that converts institutional proximity into private advantage: rule-writing, procurement, audit scope, and enforcement
decisions are biased (or appear biased) when decision-makers can later monetise their inside position. The risk is amplified by consultancy networks:
they act as “soft landings” that circumvent formal cooling-off rules and launder influence as technical advice.

OBA therefore requires: (i) a clear **role taxonomy**, (ii) **cooling-off** rules calibrated by risk, including indirect consultancy infiltration, (iii)
public **disclosure** and conflict registers with recusal rules, (iv) **incentive design** to reduce the push factors that drive exits into the sector,
and (v) **enforcement** capable of sanctioning actors and correcting outcomes (including nullification where conflict corrupted decisions).

## Core principles

* **Perceived conflict is operational conflict**: legitimacy collapses when decisions appear purchasable ex post.
* **Role switching is a power transfer**: it imports insider knowledge and relationships into private control channels.
* **Consultancy is the bypass route**: without look-through rules, cooling-off collapses into contractor laundering.
* **Enforcement must correct outcomes**: penalties alone are insufficient if conflicted decisions remain in force.

## B03.CH10.P04.S01 — Role definitions: regulator, operator, auditor, vendor, lobbyist, sector lead

A revolving-door regime requires **role clarity**. OBA uses a functional taxonomy that is stable across institutions and jurisdictions.

### Role taxonomy (minimum)

* **Regulator**: defines binding rules, thresholds, definitions, licensing/eligibility, and enforcement standards.
* **Operator**: delivers services or runs systems subject to regulation (public or private), including commons operators.
* **Auditor/verifier**: verifies compliance, measurements, and outcomes; holds replication and restatement authority.
* **Vendor/supplier**: provides tooling, models, systems, data services, or operational capacity that affects allocation, eligibility, pricing, or audit.
* **Lobbyist/advocacy agent**: communicates to influence policy, regulation, procurement, definitions, or enforcement.
* **Sector lead**: senior role in a regulated or high-capture-risk sector; includes board memberships and “advisory” posts.

### High-risk adjacency rules

* Regulator ↔ operator/vendor transitions are high-risk when the domain includes procurement, audit rules, caps, ΔM allocation, or exception regimes.
* Auditor ↔ auditee transitions are high-risk by default (audit independence is fragile).
* Adviser roles are treated as regulator-adjacent when they influence drafts, definitions, procurement specs, or enforcement priorities.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* All key positions **MUST** be classified into the taxonomy; unclassified “special adviser” roles are non-compliant.
* Role definitions **MUST** be applied by function, not by job title; title games are a standard evasion route.
* High-risk domains **MUST** maintain a publicly documented role map showing who occupies which role at any time.

## B03.CH10.P04.S02 — Cooling-off: terms per risk role, including consultancy infiltration

Cooling-off rules are time-bound constraints on post-office employment and contracting, designed to prevent monetisation of insider access and to reduce
incentives for “policy as CV building.” OBA requires **risk-calibrated** terms and **look-through** rules for consultancy bypass.

### Cooling-off design (minimum)

* **Risk tiers**: longer terms for roles with direct influence over definitions, procurement, audit scope, sanctions, ΔM allocation, or exceptions.
* **Scope**: bans cover employment, consultancy, board seats, and paid advisory positions in the affected sector/domain.
* **Look-through**: if a person joins a consultancy, and the consultancy serves the restricted sector/domain, the restriction still applies.
* **Pre-clearance**: an independent COI body reviews proposed role changes before commencement.
* **Compensation handling**: where strict cooling-off causes hardship, transitional compensation may be used to prevent covert circumvention (with public disclosure).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Cooling-off **MUST** cover consultancy and intermediary pathways; otherwise it is bypassable by construction.
* Pre-clearance decisions **MUST** be logged and publishable; silent approvals are capture surfaces.
* If a person violates cooling-off, any decisions they influenced in their former role **MUST** be reviewed for conflict impact (nullification review pathway).

## B03.CH10.P04.S03 — Disclosure: public declarations, conflict registers, recusals

Disclosure converts invisible conflicts into auditable facts. OBA requires **public declarations** and **conflict registers** that are specific enough to
detect capture patterns and to enforce recusals.

### Disclosure scope (minimum)

* **Financial interests**: holdings, options, beneficial ownership stakes, and material income streams relevant to regulated domains.
* **Role history**: prior employment/consultancies, board positions, and advisory roles in the past defined period.
* **Relationships**: close family/partner ties where they create procurement or enforcement conflicts (bounded to what is necessary and lawful).
* **Gifts/hospitality**: above de minimis thresholds, including sponsored travel, events, and services.
* **Negotiations**: ongoing job negotiations with sectors under one’s regulatory/audit authority (mandatory immediate disclosure).

### Recusal rules (required)

* **Mandatory recusal** in any decision where the declared interests intersect the decision domain (procurement, sanctions, licensing, audit scope, exceptions).
* **Recusal logging**: record the decision, the conflict basis, and who substituted authority.
* **Independent oversight**: a COI body can order recusals and investigate nondisclosure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Conflict registers **MUST** be public by default (privacy-safe redactions only) and machine-readable.
* Failure to disclose a material conflict **MUST** trigger sanctions and review of affected decisions.
* If recusals are discretionary or unlogged, the regime is non-compliant.

## B03.CH10.P04.S04 — Incentive design: pay/career paths to reduce exits to the sector

Revolving doors are driven not only by greed but by institutional incentive gradients: underpaid public roles, weak career progression, burnout, and
status structures that reward exit to private sector. OBA treats incentive design as anti-capture: reduce the structural push toward capture.

### Incentive levers (canonical)

* **Competitive compensation** for high-integrity roles (audit, enforcement, procurement), coupled to strict disclosure and cooling-off.
* **Career ladders** inside public institutions to avoid “promotion requires exit.”
* **Prestige and protection**: institutional shielding for auditors and enforcers facing retaliation, including legal support.
* **Integrity rewards**: recognition and progression tied to audit quality, restatement discipline, and enforcement consistency—not to political loyalty.
* **Secondment discipline**: if private-sector secondments are used, they must be logged, bounded, and conflict-managed; otherwise they are infiltration routes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-risk roles **MUST** have a retention and integrity plan; chronic churn in audit/enforcement is a capture indicator.
* Incentive improvements **MUST** not weaken constraints; higher pay is paired with stricter cooling-off and disclosure, not traded against them.
* If the system relies on continuous import of consultants to fill core capacity, it **MUST** be flagged as vendor/consultancy capture-risk.

## B03.CH10.P04.S05 — Enforcement: fines, bans, nullification of decisions in case of conflict

A revolving-door/COI regime is only real if violations are costly and if outcomes can be corrected. OBA therefore requires an enforcement stack that
includes personal sanctions, access bans, and the ability to review and potentially void conflicted decisions.

### Enforcement stack (minimum)

* **Fines**: scaled to benefit and to role seniority; repeat violations escalate nonlinearly.
* **Bans**: disqualification from certain public roles; bans from lobbying/contracting with the state for defined periods.
* **Contract remedies**: termination or re-tendering when procurement is corrupted by conflict.
* **Nullification review**: procedures to re-open and potentially void decisions where undisclosed conflicts materially affected outcomes.
* **Public violation register**: publish violations, sanctions, and linked decisions/texts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Violations **MUST** be logged in a public register and linked to relevant decisions, procurement, or regulatory texts.
* If a conflict is material and undisclosed, a nullification review **MUST** be triggered; “pay the fine and keep the decision” is insufficient.
* Enforcement bodies **MUST** be independent and auditable (audit-of-auditors); otherwise the regime becomes self-protecting and collapses.
