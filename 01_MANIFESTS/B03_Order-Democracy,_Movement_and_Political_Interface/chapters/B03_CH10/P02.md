# B03.CH10.P02 — Lobby architecture: disclosure, access, and “policy hygiene”

This paragraph specifies the **lobby architecture** required for OBA-compatible order-democracy. Lobbying is treated as legitimate pluralism **only**
when it is rendered **legible, contestable, and non-coercive**. Without binding disclosure and access rules, lobbying becomes a covert control layer
over definitions, exceptions, procurement, and enforcement. “Policy hygiene” is the discipline that prevents hidden inputs from entering binding text:
every influence path must have a trace, and every access advantage must be constrained.

## Core principles

* **Lobbying is allowed; covert control is not**: legitimacy requires traceability of who influences what, for whom, and with what resources.
* **Access is a power variable**: unequal access produces unequal rule-writing capacity; access rules are anti-capture infrastructure.
* **Text lineage is mandatory**: laws and regulations must be reconstructible; amendments without provenance are capture vectors.
* **Enforcement makes disclosure real**: without sanctions and bans, the register becomes theatre.

## B03.CH10.P02.S01 — Registration duty: who lobbies, for whom, with what budget and objective

A **lobby register** is the minimum instrument for making influence traceable. The purpose is not to shame interests but to prevent silent dominance and
to allow the public and auditors to reconstruct influence flows around caps, ΔM, commons, audit rules, and exception regimes.

### Registration scope (minimum)

* **Who**: any person or entity that communicates with decision-makers for the purpose of influencing policy outcomes (directly or via intermediaries).
* **For whom**: clients, sponsors, employers, affiliated organisations; ultimate control where relevant (UBC/UBO-like clarity).
* **What resources**: lobbying budget ranges, staff/contractors involved, in-kind contributions (research, events, media support).
* **Objective**: targeted policy domains and intended changes (e.g., “raise threshold”, “add exemption”, “alter audit scope”, “procurement specification”).
* **Targets**: institutions/roles contacted (ministers, advisers, regulators, oversight bodies, commons boards, auditors).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Registration **MUST** be mandatory for all material lobbying and advisory influence; voluntary registers are non-compliant.
* The register **MUST** include budget/resource disclosure and declared objectives; “general interest” entries are insufficient.
* Contact logs for high-risk domains (caps, ΔM allocation, auditability, exceptions, procurement) **MUST** be recorded and cross-referencable.
* Non-registered lobbying **MUST** be sanctionable, including invalidation of influenced text where provenance cannot be reconstructed.

## B03.CH10.P02.S02 — Publication of inputs: consultation submissions, amendments, text lineage

Capture often enters through **text insertion**: obscure amendments, “technical fixes,” and late-stage wording changes that alter definitions or add
exceptions. OBA therefore requires **publication of inputs** and **text lineage**: the full ancestry of binding text must be traceable.

### Publication artefacts (minimum)

* **Consultation submissions**: all submissions published in a standardized, searchable format, with identity and funding disclosures.
* **Amendment register**: every amendment proposal with author, rationale, affected definitions/thresholds, and expected axis impacts.
* **Text lineage (“diff chain”)**: version history of draft text, showing who changed what and when (including committee edits and executive drafting).
* **Impact notes**: for amendments that affect caps, ΔM channels, auditability, or exceptions, publish a structured impact note (E/RB/ΔG/ΔM/auditability).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Binding legal/regulatory text **MUST** have a public lineage trail; “final text only” publication is non-compliant.
* Late-stage amendments **MUST** be time-locked (minimum review window) unless emergency procedures apply with sunset and ex post audit.
* If provenance is missing for a material clause (definition shift, exemption, audit limitation), the clause **MUST** be flagged for nullification review.

## B03.CH10.P02.S03 — Access rules: equal access, agenda publication, cooling-off for advisers

Access is a scarce resource. If access is unregulated, high-capacity actors obtain de facto veto power and shape agendas before public scrutiny begins.
OBA requires access hygiene: equalised opportunity to be heard, and strict constraints on advisers who act as hidden policy routers.

### Access hygiene rules (minimum)

* **Agenda publication**: publish meeting agendas, attendees, and topic scope in advance (with narrow exceptions for security).
* **Equal access discipline**: if a regulator or ministry engages one side materially, it must provide symmetrical access opportunities to counter-positions.
* **Meeting logs**: public logs of meetings, calls, and briefings for key decision roles; include registered lobby IDs.
* **Cooling-off for advisers**: advisers and senior staff face cooling-off periods before representing sectors they regulated or advised on.
* **No shadow channels**: informal “taskforces”, advisory groups, and backchannel drafting must be registered and documented as governance bodies.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Agenda and meeting logs **MUST** be published on a fixed schedule and be linkable to the lobby register.
* Any advisory role in drafting definitions, caps, audit rules, procurement specs, or exception regimes **MUST** be disclosed with conflicts and prior affiliations.
* Cooling-off breaches **MUST** trigger sanctions and, where relevant, review/nullification of decisions influenced by conflicted advisers.

## B03.CH10.P02.S04 — Prohibited practices: hidden funding, astroturfing, revolving consultancy stacks

Some practices are incompatible with contestable governance because they create **non-attributable influence** or **structural conflicts** that cannot be
audited away. OBA therefore explicitly prohibits the following capture techniques.

### Prohibited practices (canonical)

* **Hidden funding**: undisclosed sponsorship of research, events, “citizen campaigns,” or policy proposals.
* **Astroturfing**: manufactured grassroots campaigns, front groups, or coordinated submission farms without disclosure.
* **Revolving consultancy stacks**: chains of consultancies used to bypass cooling-off, obscure clients, or launder influence through “independent experts.”
* **Pay-to-access**: donations or sponsorships that purchase meetings, invitations, or drafting proximity.
* **Ghost drafting**: external entities drafting binding text without published provenance and register linkage.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Prohibited practices **MUST** be explicitly defined in law/policy with investigative powers and meaningful penalties.
* Any detected astroturfing or hidden funding **MUST** trigger public correction, register updates, and review of affected texts/decisions.
* Consultancy arrangements in high-risk domains **MUST** disclose ultimate clients and objectives; refusal is non-compliant.

## B03.CH10.P02.S05 — Enforcement: sanctions, bans, public register of violations

Without enforcement, lobby hygiene becomes performative. OBA therefore requires an enforcement stack that makes violations costly and that preserves
system integrity by correcting outcomes, not only punishing actors.

### Enforcement stack (minimum)

* **Sanctions**: fines scaled to budget/benefit, criminal penalties where appropriate (fraud, bribery), and administrative penalties (loss of access).
* **Bans**: temporary or permanent bans from lobbying/access for repeat violators or severe cases (astroturfing, ghost drafting, pay-to-access).
* **Nullification review**: capacity to review and potentially void provisions or decisions produced under undisclosed conflicts or illegal influence.
* **Violation register**: public, searchable register of violations with outcomes, sanctions, and linked decision/text references.
* **Audit escalation**: mandatory incident reviews when patterns emerge (exception stacking, repeated late amendments, sanction collapse).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A public violation register **MUST** exist and be linked to the lobby register and text lineage system.
* Enforcement **MUST** include access bans and nullification review; fines alone are insufficient when benefits are structural.
* If sanction rate collapses or enforcement latency rises, escalation triggers **MUST** activate independent review (audit-of-auditors).
