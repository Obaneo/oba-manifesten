# B03.CH05.P10 — Financial and organisational independence: budget, staffing, and mandates

This paragraph defines **financial and organisational independence** as a prerequisite for institutional integrity in OBA.
Even perfectly written rules fail if the executing and auditing bodies are structurally dependent on the actors they regulate,
or if they are forced into fragility through budget shocks, staffing churn, or incentive structures that reward superficial
compliance over real enforcement.

Independence here is not “autonomy without control”. It is **designed non-capture**: stable mandates, predictable resources,
clear role boundaries, and internal control systems that make **auditability, contestability, and sanctioning** feasible under
political pressure, market pressure, and crisis pressure.

---

## B03.CH05.P10.S01 — Budget rails: predictability, counter-cyclicality, emergency buffers

Budgets determine whether enforcement is real or performative. Budget rails prevent institutions from being defunded precisely
when their work becomes inconvenient.

### Budget design (minimum)
- **Predictability**: multi-year funding horizons tied to mandate scope and caseload risk.
- **Counter-cyclicality**: surge capacity funding when crisis and fraud risk increase.
- **Emergency buffers**: ring-fenced funds for forensic audits, litigation defence, and rapid replacement of compromised tooling.
- **Earmarked integrity spend**: protected line items for audit systems, logging, replication tooling, and training.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Institutions performing execution/audit/oversight MUST have budget rules that cannot be easily weaponised through annual
  discretionary cuts in response to enforcement decisions.
- When early-warning indicators trigger enhanced audit mode, budget rails MUST allow automatic capacity expansion (within caps).

---

## B03.CH05.P10.S02 — Staffing: expertise profiles, training, rotation, integrity regimes

Institutions fail through capacity collapse as often as through formal capture.

### Staffing requirements (minimum)
- **Expertise profiles**: mixed teams (legal, economic/measurement, investigative, technical/tooling, domain specialists).
- **Training**: continuous training in definition sets, evidence standards, audit methods, and anti-avoidance patterns.
- **Rotation**: role rotation in high-risk functions (procurement, audit assignment, enforcement leadership) with continuity controls.
- **Integrity regimes**: disclosure rules, cooling-off, recusal protocols, and monitoring of revolving-door risk.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Critical functions MUST have redundancy: no single-person dependency for key classifications, audits, or sanction decisions.
- High-risk roles MUST be subject to stricter disclosure and cooling-off rules than general staff, including consultancy pathways.

---

## B03.CH05.P10.S03 — Compensation and incentive design: avoid perverse KPIs and gaming

Incentives shape behaviour; naïve KPIs invite Goodhart effects and selective enforcement.

### Incentive principles (minimum)
- **No throughput-only targets** (e.g., “number of cases closed”) without quality and fairness constraints.
- **Quality-weighted performance**: correctness, evidence completeness, appeal outcomes, and reproducibility success.
- **Anti-selectivity checks**: guard against “easy cases only” and politically convenient enforcement.
- **Retention incentives** for scarce expertise to reduce exit-to-sector pressure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Staff incentives MUST not reward behaviour that predictably increases exception stacking, reduces contestability, or lowers audit depth.
- Any performance system MUST be auditable: metric definitions, gaming risks, and correction rules are published internally and reviewable.

---

## B03.CH05.P10.S04 — Internal controls: segregation of duties, access control, incident response

Internal controls turn formal separation-of-functions into operational reality.

### Control system (minimum)
- **Segregation of duties**: no one person/team can define rules, execute decisions, and certify correctness.
- **Access control**: least privilege, logged access, and tamper-evident trails for data and tooling.
- **Change control**: versioned releases for rules/tooling; approvals separated from implementers; rollback-ready.
- **Incident response**: defined playbooks for corruption signals, tooling compromise, data leakage, and audit obstruction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any material change to measurement rules, registers, or enforcement tooling MUST be logged, versioned, reviewable, and reversible.
- Incident protocols MUST include mandatory public-facing reporting when integrity of governance outputs is affected (with privacy rails).

---

## B03.CH05.P10.S05 — External review: periodic international/academic replication where possible

Independence is strengthened by external replication and peer challenge—without outsourcing authority.

### External review modes (minimum)
- **Method audits**: review of definitions, sampling logic, and evidence standards.
- **Replication runs**: independent reproduction of key indicators and time series on equivalent evidence sets.
- **Comparative benchmarking**: cross-jurisdiction comparability using pinned definition sets and bridging rules.
- **Public challenge windows**: structured contestability for methodologies (not ad-hoc media fights).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Where privacy and security permit, institutions MUST enable periodic independent replication of headline indicators and consistency tests.
- External review MUST not create a new black box: reviewers must publish methods, limitations, and correction proposals under the same
  changelog discipline as the institution.
