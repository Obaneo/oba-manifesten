# B03.CH05.P08 — Exception regime: governance, sunsets, and rollback as an anti-drift mechanism

This paragraph defines the **exception regime** as a *controlled breach mechanism* inside order-democracy: a narrow,
time-bounded way to tolerate discontinuities (capacity gaps, crises, transitional constraints) **without** allowing
exceptions to become the main channel of drift, capture, or semantic erosion. Exceptions are treated as **first-class
governance objects** with mandatory registration, measurable impact, and enforced expiry. The default posture is **fail-closed**:
if an exception cannot be bounded, measured, audited, and reversed, it is non-conform and must not be granted.

The exception regime is not a “flexibility” narrative; it is a **self-binding device** that preserves the invariants by ensuring
that every deviation is (i) explicit, (ii) traceable, (iii) temporary, and (iv) rollback-capable.

---

## B03.CH05.P08.S01 — Definition of an exception: scope, duration, impact-weighted registration

An **exception** is a formally granted, time-limited deviation from a constitutive or procedural rule that would otherwise apply.

### Mandatory exception fields (minimum)
- **Scope**: which rule(s) are being deviated from (by canonical ID), and which actors/flows are covered.
- **Duration**: start date/time and end date/time (no “until further notice”).
- **Impact-weight**: quantified expected effect on: E, caps, commons access/ΔG, R/B distribution, ΔM channel integrity.
- **Rationale**: continuity constraint being addressed (capacity, legal transition, crisis), with evidence.
- **Substitution path**: what replaces the exception at expiry.
- **Rollback plan**: how reversal occurs if the exception is terminated early or expires.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every exception MUST be recorded in a **public exception register** with unique ID, scope, duration, and impact-weight.
- Exceptions without impact-weight and rollback plan MUST be treated as invalid (non-grantable).
- Exceptions MUST be expressed as *delta on the rules*, not as new semantics (“semantic exceptions” are prohibited).

---

## B03.CH05.P08.S02 — Award criteria: continuity, proportionality, substitution plan, exitability

Exceptions are only grantable when they protect essentials continuity while preserving the invariant rails.

### Award criteria (cumulative; all required)
- **Continuity necessity**: without the exception, an essential service or constitutive function fails.
- **Proportionality**: the deviation is the smallest scope/duration that resolves continuity.
- **Substitution plan**: a concrete plan exists to remove the need for the exception (capacity build, procurement exit, legal fix).
- **Exitability**: the system can return to baseline without lock-in (contracts, tooling, data formats, operational dependencies).
- **Contestability**: affected parties can object/appeal and see the evidence set (within privacy rails).
- **Anti-capture check**: no privileged carve-out for specific incumbents without open eligibility and justification.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Exceptions MUST be denied if they create or preserve **toll positions**, **commons gating**, **cap hollowing**, or **black-box
  allocation**.
- Any exception that depends on a single vendor or proprietary measurement path MUST include an exit plan and a second-source or
  shadow computation path, otherwise it is non-conform.

---

## B03.CH05.P08.S03 — Sunsets: automatic expiry, reauthorisation with a higher threshold

Sunsets are the core self-binding device: exceptions expire unless reauthorised under stricter rules.

### Sunset rules (minimum)
- **Automatic expiry** at end date/time; no renewal by inertia.
- **Renewal threshold escalation**: each renewal requires a higher decision threshold and stronger evidence burden than the grant.
- **Renewal cap**: maximum number of renewals or maximum cumulative duration must be specified ex ante (per exception class).
- **Public renewal dossier**: impact review, updated substitution plan, and failure analysis.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Renewals without demonstrated progress on the substitution path MUST be treated as non-conform.
- “Rolling temporary” exceptions (serial renewals without structural progress) MUST trigger escalation (audit mode + governance
  intervention).

---

## B03.CH05.P08.S04 — Rollback: technical/contractual reversal requirements, data and log retention

Rollback ensures exceptions do not become irreversible through procurement, data, or institutional entrenchment.

### Rollback minimum set
- **Technical reversibility**: configuration flags, feature gates, contract clauses, and migration procedures to restore baseline.
- **Contractual reversibility**: termination rights, escrow where relevant, portability of data/config, no punitive exit fees.
- **Data/log retention**: full logging of exception use, including inputs/outputs and decision traces sufficient for ex post audit.
- **Bridging/restatements**: if metrics are affected, define how time series are bridged and corrected.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any exception MUST specify a rollback owner, timeline, and verification method (audit step).
- Exceptions that require breaking evidence continuity (missing logs, blackouts) MUST be denied or must trigger a hard stop.

---

## B03.CH05.P08.S05 — Abuse detection: exception ratio, renewals, selectivity, sanction-rate drift

The system must treat exceptions as a measurable drift channel and monitor them continuously.

### Abuse indicators (minimum)
- **Exception ratio ↑**: share of cases/flows governed by exceptions exceeds a defined threshold.
- **Renewals ↑**: renewal frequency or cumulative duration rises without substitution progress.
- **Selectivity ↑**: exceptions disproportionately benefit specific actors/sectors without open eligibility.
- **Sanction-rate drift**: enforcement weakens when exception volume rises (signals capture or laundering).
- **Definition creep**: exception language begins to function as redefinition (“temporary semantics”).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Breaching exception thresholds MUST trigger **enhanced audit mode** and, where necessary, **hard stops** on affected allocation
  or enforcement actions until the exception surface is reduced to auditable bounds.
- Exception abuse MUST be sanctionable: repeated misuse or strategic exception stacking triggers escalation and potential
  institutional reconstitution.
