# B03.CH05.P07 — Sanctions architecture: escalation ladder and neutralisation of extraction

This paragraph defines the **sanctions architecture** as the binding tail of enforcement: the part that makes remedies durable,
prevents relapse, and raises the cost of circumvention. Sanctions are not an end in themselves; they exist to (i) **stop ongoing
extraction**, (ii) **neutralise past extraction** (remove gains and dismantle toll positions), (iii) **restore the rails** (caps,
commons access, measurability, contestability), and (iv) **create repeatable precedent** so the system cannot be re-litigated case
by case. The architecture must be graduated, auditable, and resistant to capture: sanctions must not depend on discretionary
bargains, narrative pressure, or unequal access to legal resources.

---

## B03.CH05.P07.S01 — Sanction types: administrative, civil, criminal (criteria per class)

Sanctions come in distinct classes with distinct thresholds, aligned to order impact and intent.

### Sanction classes (minimum set)
- **Administrative**: compliance orders, reporting duties, corrective measures, fines, licence conditions, access restrictions.
- **Civil**: restitution, damages, clawbacks/disgorgement, contract nullification, injunctions, fiduciary breach remedies.
- **Criminal**: reserved for intentional fraud, systematic concealment, coercion/extortion, corruption, sabotage of auditability,
  and repeat high-impact extraction patterns.

### Classification criteria (non-exhaustive)
- **Order impact**: size and persistence of E, cap erosion, lock-in creation, or commons gating.
- **Mens rea / intent proxies**: concealment, falsification, repeated violations after notice, audit interference.
- **Control/benefit/block**: whether actors had decisive control, captured benefit, or exercised veto power to maintain extraction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Each sanction decision MUST specify: sanction class, trigger rule(s), evidence set, and expected neutralisation effect.
- Mixing classes via “settlement ambiguity” MUST be constrained: if criminal thresholds are met, administrative closure alone is
  non-conform unless explicitly justified and reviewable.

---

## B03.CH05.P07.S02 — Escalation ladder: warning → fine → restriction → exclusion → dismantling

Sanctions must follow an explicit ladder to prevent both arbitrary harshness and captured leniency.

### Escalation ladder (canonical form)
1. **Warning / compliance order**: formal notice + deadline + mandatory remediation plan.
2. **Fine / financial penalty**: calibrated to remove incentives, not merely “priced-in”.
3. **Restriction**: activity limits, licence conditions, mandatory unbundling, escrow, independent monitoring.
4. **Exclusion**: procurement bans, licence suspension/revocation, disqualification from privileged channels.
5. **Dismantling**: structural separation, removal of toll gates, forced divestiture/unwind, nullification of extraction-enabling
   contracts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Escalation MUST be **triggered by defined conditions** (severity, persistence, concealment, recidivism), not discretion alone.
- “Pay-to-continue” outcomes are prohibited where they preserve toll positions; restrictions must be structural when extraction is
  structural.

---

## B03.CH05.P07.S03 — Economic neutralisation: fee-stack removal, windfall capture, disgorgement

Neutralisation is the core: sanctions must eliminate the economic surplus produced by prohibited channels.

### Neutralisation instruments
- **Fee-stack removal**: eliminate layered fees, forced bundles, priority tolls, lock-in penalties, and shadow charges.
- **Windfall capture**: reclaim abnormal gains from crisis rents, scarcity exploitation, exception abuse, and cap bypass.
- **Disgorgement / clawback**: recover benefits from misclassification, concealment, threshold gaming, and chain laundering.
- **Routing**: where legally feasible, captured funds should be routed to **commons ΔG restoration** and/or **R/B buffers**, not
  recycled into discretionary budgets without traceability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Monetary penalties MUST be sized so that expected value of circumvention is negative (including probability-of-detection
  adjustments where measurable).
- Neutralisation MUST target the mechanism (the toll position), not only outcomes (a one-off fine).

---

## B03.CH05.P07.S04 — Recidivism and patterning: stronger sanctions for structural circumvention/capture

Order systems fail when repeat offenders treat enforcement as a cost of doing business. Pattern detection must therefore harden
responses.

### Recidivism indicators (non-exhaustive)
- Repeated violations of the same rule class (relabeling, exception stacking, audit refusal).
- Corporate reorganisation to evade sanctions (phoenixing, affiliate rotation, SPV substitution).
- “Compliance theatre” patterns: cosmetic changes while functional extraction persists.
- Coordinated ecosystem behaviour: vendor stacks, consultancy loops, lobbying pressure coinciding with enforcement softening.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Recidivism MUST trigger step-ups on the ladder (restriction → exclusion → dismantling) with reduced tolerance for negotiated
  remedies.
- Structural circumvention attempts MUST be treated as aggravating factors and logged as capture signals.

---

## B03.CH05.P07.S05 — Publicity and precedent: sanctions register, rationale, repeatability of decisions

Sanctions must generate a public memory so that enforcement cannot be selectively applied or quietly reversed.

### Publication requirements
- **Sanctions register**: public record with unique IDs, dates, actors, sanction class, violated rule references, remedies,
  and status (active/closed/appealed).
- **Rationale publication**: concise explanation of qualification and why the sanction was chosen on the escalation ladder.
- **Precedent format**: structured output so similar cases produce similar outcomes; deviations require explicit justification.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Sanctions MUST be publicly visible by default, with narrow exceptions (e.g., privacy/security) that are themselves logged and
  reviewable.
- Precedent MUST be reusable: decisions MUST reference canonical rule IDs and definition-set versions so that later reviewers can
  reproduce the classification and sanction logic.
