# B03.CH05.P11 — Escalation and intervention: when institutions must correct themselves

This paragraph defines **self-correction** as a required capability of OBA institutions. In an order-democracy, failure is not
an anomaly; it is a predictable mode under pressure: capture attempts, crisis shortcuts, procurement shortcuts, and semantic drift
all push institutions away from enforceable rails. Therefore, institutions must include an explicit **escalation and intervention
architecture** that activates when early-warning indicators show integrity loss.

Escalation is not punishment; it is **fail-closed governance**. When auditability, reproducibility, or enforcement capacity falls,
institutions must not “continue as normal” while outputs degrade. They must shift into higher-assurance modes, trigger governance
resets, and—if necessary—declare incompatibility and force relabeling.

---

## B03.CH05.P11.S01 — Early warning for institutional failure: reproducibility↓, exceptions↑, sanction rate↓

Institutional failure is detectable before it becomes irreversible. The minimum early-warning set must track whether the system is
still capable of producing **contestable, auditable outputs**.

### Core signals (minimum)
- **Reproducibility↓**: independent reruns fail; results drift across identical inputs; version pinning breaks.
- **Exceptions↑**: rising exception ratio; renewals and “temporary” exemptions accumulate; carve-outs cluster in sensitive zones.
- **Sanction rate↓**: enforcement collapses relative to detected non-compliance; warnings replace remedies; repeated breaches persist.
- **Latency↑** (supporting): enforcement delays grow; audits become stale; backlogs normalise.
- **Audit obstruction** (supporting): refusals of access, missing logs, proprietary blocks, “cannot reproduce” claims.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Early-warning indicators MUST be defined with thresholds and MUST trigger mandatory escalation steps (not discretionary “reviews”).
- Indicators MUST be published as part of the institutional dashboard minimum (with privacy-preserving aggregation where needed).

---

## B03.CH05.P11.S02 — Intervention mechanisms: temporary takeover, reconstitution, re-governance

When early-warning thresholds are breached, the system must have **predefined intervention instruments** that restore integrity
without waiting for the same compromised actors to fix themselves.

### Intervention ladder (minimum)
- **Enhanced audit mode**: expanded scope, forensic access, parallel review teams, intensified sampling.
- **Temporary takeover**: limited-duration authority shift to an integrity body with narrow mandate (restore auditability/enforcement).
- **Reconstitution**: replacement/rotation of leadership and key roles; removal of conflicted persons; reset of procurement control.
- **Re-governance**: structural redesign of decision interfaces (segregation of duties, audit-of-audit, disclosure rails).
- **Tooling reset**: exit from vendor lock-in; second implementation; shadow computation until reproducibility is restored.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Intervention powers MUST be bounded in scope and time, and MUST be activated by objective triggers (not political convenience).
- Any intervention MUST preserve contestability and legal review; “integrity” cannot be used as a blanket override for rights.

---

## B03.CH05.P11.S03 — Crisis protocols: emergency power with evidence discipline and exit conditions

Crisis is the primary window for regression: shortcuts become permanent, exceptions stack, and black boxes are justified as
“speed”. Crisis protocols are therefore part of institutional design.

### Crisis protocol elements (minimum)
- **Emergency powers**: explicitly defined, narrow, and time-limited.
- **Evidence discipline**: mandatory logging; defined uncertainty bands; “unknown” allowed; no fabricated certainty.
- **Non-suspensive defaults**: prevent procedural sabotage, but keep appeal routes open.
- **Sunset + rollback**: automatic expiry; technical and contractual reversibility planned in advance.
- **Post-crisis audit**: ex post review of decisions, exceptions, and outcomes; restatements where measures were distorted.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Crisis actions MUST not introduce black-box allocation, prohibited linkages, or permanent carve-outs.
- If measurement continuity is lost, the system MUST fail-closed on sensitive outputs and activate hard stops where required.

---

## B03.CH05.P11.S04 — Public accountability: incident report, decision log, traceable changelog

Escalation and intervention are legitimate only if they are **traceable, reviewable, and publicly accountable**.

### Minimum public artifacts
- **Incident report**: what happened, detection signals, scope, affected decisions, and immediate containment.
- **Decision log**: rationale, inputs, authority basis, and interfaces used (including recusal/conflict disclosures).
- **Traceable changelog**: what changed (rules, tooling, thresholds), why, expected impact, and rollback plan.
- **Restatements**: if indicators or classifications are revised, publish bridging logic and timelines.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any integrity incident that affects governance outputs MUST be reported publicly at a minimum level of detail sufficient for
  independent scrutiny (subject to privacy and security rails).
- “Silent fixes” are prohibited: unlogged changes are treated as definition shift / black-box drift.

## B03.CH05.P11.S05 — Fork threshold: when institutional design formally becomes “no longer OBA-compatible”

Some institutional deviations are not “bugs”; they are a change of regime. This section defines when institutional design crosses
the boundary into **formal incompatibility** with OBA.

### Incompatibility triggers (non-exhaustive)
- **Structural black-box governance**: allocation/enforcement depends on non-auditable models or proprietary computation.
- **Persistent exception explosion**: carve-outs become normal form; sunsets fail; exceptions stack in cap-sensitive zones.
- **Contestability collapse**: appeals are blocked in practice; evidence access is denied; independent audit cannot operate.
- **Selective enforcement**: caps/enforcement applied discriminatorily; sanctioning becomes politically conditional.
- **Vendor lock-in as governance**: procurement choices create a single point of control that cannot be exited or replicated.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- When incompatibility triggers are met, the institution/jurisdiction MUST be classified as **not OBA-compatible** until restored.
- Name usage MUST follow the fork/compatibility rules: mandatory relabeling, explicit deviation disclosure, and publication of the
  recovery plan and re-entry conditions.
