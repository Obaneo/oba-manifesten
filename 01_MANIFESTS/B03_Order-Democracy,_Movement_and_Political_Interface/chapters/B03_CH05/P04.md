# B03.CH05.P04 — Transparency architecture: public definitions, registers, and dashboards

This paragraph defines the **transparency layer** as enforceable infrastructure: public definitions, mandatory registers, and a
dashboard minimum that makes OBA governance **observable**, **auditable**, and **contestable**. Transparency is not optional
communications; it is the mechanism that prevents semantic drift, exception laundering, black-box allocation, and institutional
capture. The core rule is: **no enforcement without a public definition-set reference**, and no legitimacy without **publicly
traceable decision lineage**. Transparency must be designed to survive adversarial conditions (gaming, capture attempts, crisis
pressure) while remaining compatible with privacy rails (data minimalism, controlled access, logged disclosure).

---

## B03.CH05.P04.S01 — Definition publication: concept framework, exclusions, reference budgets

A transparency architecture starts with a stable, referenceable **concept framework**: what terms mean, what is excluded, and
what baseline expectations apply. Without this, reporting becomes branding.

### Required published elements
- **Concept framework**: canonical terms used in governance outputs (classification, routing, exceptions, sanctions).
- **Exclusions and boundary statements**: what is explicitly *not* counted / not targeted / not permitted.
- **Reference budgets / baselines** where applicable: the baseline state against which changes are measured (including known
  limitations and uncertainty bands).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every published indicator, decision, or status label MUST reference the applicable **definition-set ID + version**.
- Any change to definitions, exclusions, or baseline construction MUST be logged as a **versioned change** with rationale and
  impact analysis.
- Where definitions are not yet settled, outputs MUST declare **unknown/uncertain** rather than silently substituting proxy
  meanings (fail-closed on ambiguity).

## B03.CH05.P04.S02 — Registers: exceptions register, sanctions register, beneficial control/exposure where relevant

Registers are the memory of the regime: they prevent quiet drift by making deviations and enforcement **countable** and
**reviewable**.

### Mandatory registers (minimum)
- **Exceptions register**: scope, duration, rationale, legal basis, affected populations, quantitative impact where possible,
  sunset date, renewal history, and rollback plan.
- **Sanctions register**: qualification basis, measure taken, appeal status/outcome, remedy executed (incl. neutralisation),
  repeat-offender linkage where lawful.
- **Beneficial control / exposure registers** (where relevant): ownership/control and exposure structure necessary to apply
  look-through, cap checks, and chain attribution—subject to strict privacy and access rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Exceptions MUST be **registered before effect** (or immediately after under emergency, with tight deadline) and MUST be
  non-stackable unless explicitly authorised as a fork-level deviation.
- Sanctions MUST be recorded with enough detail to permit **precedent consistency** and external scrutiny without doxxing.
- Registers MUST support **time-series analysis**: counts, renewals, selectivity, and enforcement latency.

## B03.CH05.P04.S03 — Dashboard minimum: E, R/B profiles, cap signals, ΔG/commons quality, ΔM distribution

The dashboard is the public interface for “how the order is doing.” It must cover the minimum set that detects regression even
when rhetoric is aligned.

### Dashboard minimum (high-level)
- **E (extraction)**: measurable proxies and/or direct measures of toll/claim extraction, plus decomposition where possible.
- **R/B profiles**: distribution summaries (e.g., p10/p25/median), essentials continuity indicators, and buffer stress signals.
- **Caps signals**: near-cap clustering, exception ratios affecting caps, concentration metrics aligned to cap objects.
- **ΔG / commons quality**: access, reliability, maintenance backlog, service minima compliance, gating signals.
- **ΔM distribution**: channel allocation shares, contestability indicators, prohibited-channel flags, black-box risk markers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Each dashboard metric MUST link to a published definition and method, and MUST declare uncertainty bands where material.
- Dashboards MUST be **comparable over time**; if comparability breaks, restatements/bridging must be published (see S04).
- Absence of a dashboard element MUST be treated as a governance failure unless explicitly declared “unknown” with remediation
  timelines.

## B03.CH05.P04.S04 — Reproducibility: open calculation rules, restatements, time-series bridging

Transparency without reproducibility is theatre. Reproducibility means a competent third party can re-run the calculation (or a
privacy-preserving equivalent) and reach the same outputs within declared tolerances.

### Reproducibility requirements
- **Open calculation rules**: formulas, classification rules, thresholds, and aggregation logic published and versioned.
- **Data lineage**: what inputs were used, how they were transformed, and what was excluded.
- **Restatements**: explicit corrections when errors or definition shifts are found.
- **Time-series bridging**: a documented mapping that preserves interpretability across version changes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any change that affects comparability MUST include a bridging note: what changed, why, and how to interpret old vs new series.
- Restatements MUST be timestamped, publicly logged, and linked to impacted dashboard metrics and register entries.
- Where full public replication is impossible due to privacy, a **controlled replication pathway** MUST exist (e.g., audited
  secure environments, escrowed computation, second-implementation checks).

## B03.CH05.P04.S05 — Privacy rails: aggregation/pseudonymisation and access control with logging

Transparency must not become surveillance. The privacy design goal is **maximum governance visibility with minimum personal data**.

### Privacy rails (minimum)
- **Aggregation-first** publication for dashboards and registers, with suppression rules to prevent re-identification.
- **Pseudonymisation** where entity-level continuity is necessary (e.g., recidivism, exception renewals) while avoiding direct
  personal exposure.
- **Access control** for sensitive evidence: tiered permissions, purpose limitation, and expiry.
- **Disclosure logging**: who accessed what, when, for what purpose, and under which authority.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Prohibited linkages (profiling/surveillance for order enforcement) MUST be enforced at the data architecture level.
- Any access to sensitive data MUST be logged and auditable; missing logs are a hard-stop condition for affected outputs.
- If privacy constraints prevent publishing a metric, the regime MUST publish (i) the reason, (ii) an alternative proxy, and
  (iii) a plan to restore observability without expanding surveillance.
