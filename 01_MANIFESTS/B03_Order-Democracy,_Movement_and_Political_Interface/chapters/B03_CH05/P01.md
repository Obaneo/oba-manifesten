# B03.CH05.P01 — Purpose and delimitation: institutional design as anti-capture infrastructure

This paragraph defines why **institutional design** is treated in Book 3 as **anti-capture infrastructure**: OBA’s invariants
(caps, commons, auditability, contestability, exception discipline) do not enforce themselves. They require **roles, interfaces,
and procedural rails** that turn normative constraints into repeatable behaviour under adversarial incentives. The chapter therefore
specifies the **minimum institutional set** and its operating constraints so that governance cannot be quietly hollowed out through
role mixing, black-box discretion, vendor lock-in, or exception normalisation.

---

## B03.CH05.P01.S01 — Why institutions operationalise “the law” (from norm to behaviour)

A law that cannot be executed, audited, or contested at the point of decision is functionally a narrative. Institutions are the
mechanism that converts **written constraints** into **observed practice**: who registers what, who decides, who logs, who audits,
who can object, and what happens when rules are breached.

### Core principles
- **From rule to interface**: every normative rail must map to an operational interface (decision log, register, audit trail).
- **Adversarial assumption**: actors will search for loopholes, latency, and discretion surfaces; institutions must be built for that.
- **Proof over proclamation**: “compliance” is established by evidence (logs, reproducible computations, registers), not by intent.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any OBA-relevant decision MUST be attributable to a **defined role** and MUST produce a **decision record** with inputs and rationale.
- Execution systems MUST produce **auditable traces** sufficient for independent replication and challenge.

---

## B03.CH05.P01.S02 — Delimitation vs Books 1/2: economic rules and transition instruments remain canonical there

This chapter does not redefine the economic content of OBA (Book 1) and does not supply a full transition playbook (Book 2). It
defines the institutional scaffolding required so that whatever economic and transition rules are adopted remain **stable, testable,
and non-capturable** in practice.

### In scope
- **Institutional roles** (execution, audit, oversight, judicial review) and separation rails.
- **Transparency surfaces** (definition sets, registers, dashboards) as enforceable outputs.
- **Audit/enforcement plumbing** (logging, reproducibility, exceptions, sanctions, escalation).

### Out of scope
- Sector-by-sector policy design, economic parameter calibration, and transition sequencing details beyond institutional interfaces.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Local policy modules MUST reference canonical economic definitions and tests from the Kernel/Book 1; they MUST NOT redefine them here.
- Transition instruments referenced in institutional procedures MUST remain consistent with Book 2 (e.g., rollback, sunsets, hard stops).

## B03.CH05.P01.S03 — Design requirements: separation of functions, evidence discipline, contestability

Institutional design is judged by whether it preserves OBA’s ability to detect, correct, and resist capture while maintaining
governability. The minimum requirement set is therefore **structural**, not aspirational.

### Core requirements
- **Separation of functions**: execution, audit, oversight, and judicial review must be organisationally and procedurally distinct.
- **Evidence discipline**: decisions must be reproducible from logged inputs under pinned definition sets and methods.
- **Contestability**: affected parties must have a workable objection/appeal route with access to evidence, without enabling sabotage.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Incompatible role combinations MUST be prohibited, with cooling-off and conflict-of-interest regimes.
- Affected parties MUST have access to the evidence needed to contest outcomes, subject to privacy-minimal access controls with logging.
- Where regression/capture risk is high, objection MUST be **non-suspensive by default**, with explicit, logged exceptions only.

## B03.CH05.P01.S04 — Failure modes: capture, latency, semantic drift, exception normalisation

Institutions fail in predictable patterns. Book 3 treats these patterns as **design targets**: if you can name the failure mode,
you can engineer rails against it.

### Key failure modes (non-exhaustive)
- **Capture**: controlled auditors, revolving-door staffing, vendor lock-in, selective enforcement.
- **Latency**: enforcement delays that turn rules into optional guidance.
- **Semantic drift**: quiet redefinition or “interpretation creep” that weakens invariants without declaring a fork.
- **Exception normalisation**: temporary carve-outs become permanent through renewals, stacking, or threshold tricks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Institutions MUST operate with **early-warning indicators** and escalation triggers (e.g., sanction rate↓, exceptions↑, reproducibility↓).
- Any definition shift or exception expansion MUST be recorded in a **changelog/register** and MUST be contestable; silent drift is non-conform.

## B03.CH05.P01.S05 — Output: minimum institutional set + interfaces + escalation ladders

The deliverable of CH05 is a **minimum viable governance stack**: roles, interfaces, and escalation ladders that keep OBA enforceable
under political and economic pressure.

### Outputs (chapter-level)
- **Minimum institutional set**: execution authority, independent audit, oversight function, judicial contestability.
- **Standard interfaces**: decision logs, registers (exceptions/sanctions), dashboards, evidence access routes.
- **Escalation ladders**: triggers → enhanced audit → corrective measures → rollback/hard stops → reconstitution where needed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any OBA-claiming jurisdiction or implementation MUST instantiate the minimum institutional set and publish the required interfaces.
- If the minimum set is not instantiated, or interfaces are non-auditable/closed, the implementation MUST be treated as **non-conform**
  until corrected (fail-closed).
