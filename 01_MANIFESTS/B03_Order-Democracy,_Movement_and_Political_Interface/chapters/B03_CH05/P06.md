# B03.CH05.P06 — Enforcement: detection → qualification → remedy (function over label)

This paragraph defines **enforcement** as the operational pipeline that turns measured signals into **binding outcomes**. In
order-democracy, enforcement is not “punishment-first”; it is **classification-first**: identify relevant behaviour, qualify it
against the canonical definitions, then apply remedies that **neutralise extraction**, restore compliance, and protect the rails.
The governing rule is **function over label**: legal form, branding, or declared intent cannot override the functional profile of
a practice or channel. Enforcement must therefore be evidence-driven, repeatable, contestable, and designed to resist
anti-avoidance patterns (relabeling, fragmentation, intermediaries, threshold games, and chain laundering).

---

## B03.CH05.P06.S01 — Detection sources: reporting, logs, whistleblowing, anomaly engines

Detection is a **multi-source intake** that minimises blind spots and reduces the ability to suppress signals.

### Detection sources (minimum set)
- **Mandatory reporting**: periodic disclosures and event-based notifications (exceptions, thresholds, restructures, vendor swaps).
- **Operational logs**: allocation logs, decision logs, access logs, calculation lineage, register updates (version-pinned).
- **Complaints and appeals**: citizen/user/worker submissions routed into case intake with tracking and response deadlines.
- **Whistleblowing**: protected channels, confidential intake, anti-retaliation enforcement, legal aid support where applicable.
- **Anomaly engines**: statistical and rule-based detectors for fee-stacks, concentration, margin spikes, churn anomalies,
  exception stacking, sanction-rate drift, and audit refusal patterns.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Detection pipelines MUST be designed for **non-suppression**: every intake item MUST receive an ID, timestamp, and handling
  status in a case log.
- Evidence lineage (source, method, transformation steps) MUST be recorded; “uncited signals” are not admissible for binding
  outcomes.
- Whistleblowing channels MUST include anti-retaliation rails; retaliation incidents MUST be treated as enforcement priorities.

---

## B03.CH05.P06.S02 — Qualification: requalification principles, look-through, chain attribution

Qualification is the decisive step: convert signals into **canonical classifications** under the definition set.

### Qualification principles
- **Function-over-label**: classify by functional effects (extraction, toll position, lock-in, control/benefit/block), not by
  contract name, corporate form, or public narrative.
- **Look-through**: pierce intermediaries, SPVs, bundling, netting, and “service wrappers” to find the underlying claim and
  routing.
- **Chain attribution**: allocate responsibility across platform–supplier–financier chains when extraction is structurally
  produced or enabled (shared causality, shared control, shared benefit).
- **Threshold integrity**: treat threshold games, fragmentation, and time-slicing as anti-avoidance patterns unless proven
  substantively justified.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Qualification decisions MUST reference: (i) applicable definition-set ID + version, (ii) the specific rules applied, and
  (iii) the evidence set sufficient for independent reproduction.
- If chain attribution cannot be resolved due to missing disclosures or inaccessible contracts, the case MUST escalate to
  forensic access (or apply fail-closed restrictions where the regime allows).

---

## B03.CH05.P06.S03 — Remedy instruments: corrections, clawbacks, routing to commons, licence interventions

Remedies must **neutralise extraction** and restore the intended rails; deterrence is a by-product, not the sole objective.

### Remedy toolkit (non-exhaustive)
- **Corrections**: reclassification, recalculation, restatement of indicators, correction of registers and affected decisions.
- **Clawbacks / disgorgement**: recover gains derived from prohibited channels, exception abuse, or misclassification.
- **Routing to commons**: redirect extracted value (where legally feasible) into commons maintenance/ΔG recovery or R/B buffers,
  rather than allowing private retention.
- **Contract/structure unwind**: voiding side letters, removing toll gates, unbundling forced bundles, eliminating fee stacks.
- **Licence interventions**: restrictions, conditions, suspension, or revocation for systemic circumvention or recidivism.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Remedies MUST be proportional to order impact and MUST prioritise **removing the extraction mechanism** over symbolic penalties.
- Remedy selection MUST be published with rationale and references to rules; secrecy-by-default is non-conform (except where
  narrowly justified and logged).

---

## B03.CH05.P06.S04 — Burden of proof and evidentiary standards: documentation duty, reversal where appropriate

To prevent “evidence starvation” as a strategy, the system must assign proof burdens where the relevant information is controlled.

### Evidence standards (minimum)
- **Documentation duty**: actors operating in high E-risk / cap-sensitive contexts MUST maintain records sufficient to support
  classification and audit (data lineage, contracts, side arrangements, routing logs).
- **Reasoned inference**: when full proof is unavailable, the regime may rely on structured inference from observable signals,
  but must mark uncertainty and escalate for verification.
- **Burden reversal (where appropriate)**: when an actor controls the evidence surface (contracts, algorithms, routing), failure
  to provide required evidence MAY shift the burden and justify restrictive interim measures.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Enforcement actions MUST specify evidentiary thresholds for each step (intake → qualification → remedy) and MUST record the
  uncertainty band where applicable.
- Missing required evidence MUST not be treated as exculpatory by default; it is a risk signal and MUST trigger escalation or
  fail-closed constraints per the regime’s rules.

---

## B03.CH05.P06.S05 — Objection/appeal: non-suspensive defaults where regression risk exists; exceptions explicit

Contestability is constitutive, but it must not become a sabotage channel. Appeals must exist without enabling delay tactics that
preserve extraction.

### Appeal architecture
- **Right to object/appeal** with access to evidence, methods, and the applied definition-set references.
- **Non-suspensive default** for high-regression-risk cases: remedies that stop ongoing extraction should normally continue while
  the appeal is heard, unless a specific suspension test is met.
- **Explicit suspension criteria**: narrow, evidence-based conditions (e.g., clear error + low order risk + reversible impact).
- **Timelines and finality**: deadlines for decisions; escalation paths; publication of outcomes and precedents.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Appeals MUST not block baseline enforcement where continuing would likely worsen E, erode caps, or create lock-in.
- Any granted suspension MUST be time-limited, logged, and justified; repeated suspensions for the same actor/pattern are a
  capture indicator.
- Appeal outcomes MUST feed back into rule clarification, restatements, and—where needed—anti-avoidance tightening.
