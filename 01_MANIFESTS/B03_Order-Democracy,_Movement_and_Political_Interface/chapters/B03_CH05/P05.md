# B03.CH05.P05 — Audit design: assurance, sampling, triggers, and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the **audit layer** as the enforcement backbone of order-democracy: it is the mechanism that turns
public definitions and dashboards into **credible constraints**. Audit is not a ceremonial review; it is a **repeatable assurance
system** that (i) verifies core indicators against the definition set, (ii) detects circumvention and drift, (iii) escalates under
anomalies, and (iv) forces **hard stops** when measurability, contestability, or cap integrity is compromised. The design goal is
to minimise capture and gaming by combining **baseline periodic assurance**, **risk-weighted sampling**, **trigger-based forensics**,
and **meta-audit** (audit of auditors) with rotation and independent replication.

---

## B03.CH05.P05.S01 — Baseline audit: periodic verification of core indicators and definition set

Baseline audits provide routine assurance that the regime is still measuring and classifying according to the **published canon**.

### Baseline scope (minimum)
- **Definition-set conformance**: verification that published indicators, registers, and decisions reference the correct
  definition-set ID + version and apply it correctly.
- **Core indicator verification**: E, R/B profiles, cap signals, ΔG/commons quality, ΔM channel distribution—checked against
  source data lineage and calculation rules.
- **Register integrity**: completeness and internal consistency of exceptions and sanctions registers (incl. renewal history,
  appeal outcomes, rollback completion).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Baseline audits MUST run on a fixed schedule and MUST be published as assurance statements with scope, methods, and limitations.
- If definition-set application cannot be verified, affected outputs MUST be labeled **uncertain** and treated as **non-conform**
  until corrected (fail-closed).

---

## B03.CH05.P05.S02 — Sampling versus full coverage: criteria based on E-risk and cap sensitivity

Audit coverage must be proportional to **order risk**. Full coverage is reserved for high-risk surfaces; sampling is used
elsewhere, but sampling must be defensible and resistant to selection bias.

### Coverage logic
- **Full coverage** where:
  - cap integrity is directly at stake (near-cap clustering, carve-outs, threshold changes),
  - prohibited channels / black-box allocation risk exists,
  - prior non-compliance or recidivism is detected,
  - concentration or single points of failure dominate.
- **Risk-weighted sampling** where:
  - exposure is distributed and measurability is strong,
  - controls are stable and independently replicated,
  - anomaly rates are low and contestability pathways function.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Sampling frames MUST be published (at least at the methodological level) and MUST be periodically validated against drift and
  gaming.
- Sampling MUST include **randomisation** and **stratification** on E-risk and cap sensitivity to prevent “hiding in the noise.”
- If sampling cannot credibly detect expected circumvention patterns, coverage MUST be expanded.

## B03.CH05.P05.S03 — Trigger-based audits: anomalies (fee stacks, concentration, margin spikes, relocations)

Trigger-based audits are the regime’s early-warning response: when patterns indicate extraction, laundering, or capture, audit
switches from assurance to **forensic mode**.

### Trigger classes (non-exhaustive)
- **Fee-stack anomalies**: unexplained growth in layered fees, commissions, bundling, or side payments.
- **Concentration spikes**: sudden increases in market share, vendor dependence, or network choke-point dominance.
- **Margin spikes / price wedges**: persistent spreads inconsistent with costs or competitive conditions.
- **Relocation and restructuring patterns**: sudden legal/entity shifts that correlate with cap avoidance or reporting gaps.
- **Register drift**: exceptions↑, renewals↑, selectivity↑, sanction rate↓, enforcement latency↑.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Trigger thresholds MUST be defined, versioned, and published (at least at the category/logic level).
- Trigger activation MUST generate an auditable case file: inputs, rationale, scope expansion, and decision outcomes.
- Failure to execute triggered audits within defined timelines MUST be treated as an institutional failure signal (capture risk).

---

## B03.CH05.P05.S04 — Hard stops: measurement uncertainty, black-box allocation, cap erosion, definition shift without changelog

Hard stops are the **fail-safe**: when the system cannot reliably measure or contest, it must stop doing the thing that depends on
that capability. Hard stops prevent “governance by faith.”

### Hard-stop conditions (minimum)
- **Measurement uncertainty** exceeds declared tolerances or cannot be bounded due to missing lineage, missing logs, or model drift.
- **Black-box allocation**: decisions or allocations rely on closed rules, unavailable evidence, or non-replicable computation.
- **Cap erosion**: structural carve-outs, exception stacking, threshold tricks, or enforcement collapse that undermines bindingness.
- **Definition shift without changelog**: changed semantics, proxies, or classification rules applied in practice without a
  published version change and bridging.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- When a hard-stop condition is met, the affected allocation/decision pipeline MUST be suspended or rolled back to an auditable
  scope until compliance is restored.
- Hard stops MUST trigger an escalation ladder: forensic audit, public incident report, corrective action plan, and—if structural—
  re-authorisation or fork classification.

---

## B03.CH05.P05.S05 — Audit of auditors: rotation, independent review, capture indicators

Auditors are themselves a capture target. Meta-audit ensures the assurance layer remains independent, competent, and non-collusive.

### Meta-audit mechanisms (minimum)
- **Rotation and term limits** for key audit roles; separation between auditors and audited entities/vendors.
- **Independent review**: second-line assurance, peer review, and where feasible parallel implementation checks.
- **Conflict-of-interest regimes**: disclosure, recusals, cooling-off, and public registers of conflicts.
- **Capture indicators**: staff churn patterns, suppressed findings, scope shrinkage, unexplained latency, repeat exemptions for
  the same actors, vendor dependence of audit tooling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Audit methodologies and tooling MUST be reproducible and version-pinned; “trust us” auditing is non-conform.
- Audit findings MUST be publishable at an appropriate level of abstraction; refusal to publish assurance outcomes is a red flag
  and MUST trigger independent review.
- If audit independence is compromised, the regime MUST reconstitute the audit function (rotation/reset) and review affected
  decisions retroactively.
