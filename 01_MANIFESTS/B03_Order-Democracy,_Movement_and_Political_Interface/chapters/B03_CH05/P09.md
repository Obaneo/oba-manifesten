# B03.CH05.P09 — Procurement and vendor governance: preventing lock-in and black-box institutions

This paragraph defines **procurement and vendor governance** as a constitutive safeguard: institutions cannot remain OBA-conform
if critical functions (measurement, allocation, enforcement, audit) are delegated to **non-exitable vendors** or **black-box
toolchains**. Procurement is treated as an **order surface**: it can silently reintroduce toll positions, reduce contestability,
and create capture through dependency, proprietary computation, or monopoly supply.

The core rule is: **no institutional function may depend on a vendor path that cannot be independently reproduced, audited, and
exited without breaking evidence continuity**. Vendors may supply components and services, but *the order must not be hosted inside
their opacity*.

---

## B03.CH05.P09.S01 — Open standards: data models, interfaces, calculation rules, auditability by design

Open standards are the anti-lock-in baseline: public institutions must be able to move, replicate, and verify critical processes.

### Standard surface (minimum)
- **Data models**: canonical schemas for registers, case files, logs, metrics, and exceptions.
- **Interfaces**: stable APIs and export/import formats for all critical flows (including bulk export).
- **Calculation rules**: published computation specifications for indicators, tests, and classifications.
- **Auditability by design**: mandatory logging points, trace IDs, and reproducible runs as part of architecture.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Procurement MUST require that all critical outputs are derivable from **documented inputs + published rules + retained logs**.
- Any vendor component that affects measurement, qualification, or allocation MUST expose the full **evidence surface** needed for
  independent audit (no “trust us” attestations).
- “Open interface, closed semantics” is non-conform: if semantics are proprietary or uninspectable, the component fails.

---

## B03.CH05.P09.S02 — Exitability: portability, escrow, second implementation, shadow computation

Exitability is treated as a first-class requirement, not an optional contract clause.

### Exitability package (minimum)
- **Portability**: data/config export in open formats, with integrity proofs and complete histories.
- **Escrow** (where applicable): source/config/model artifacts held under conditions that enable continuity under vendor failure.
- **Second implementation**: at least one alternative implementation path for critical functions (in-house or third party).
- **Shadow computation**: parallel computation that re-runs key outputs to detect drift, manipulation, or vendor errors.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Contracts MUST prohibit punitive exit fees, data hostage terms, or portability throttling.
- If a function is safety-critical (classification, caps, exceptions, allocation, audit), procurement MUST include either
  second implementation or shadow computation; otherwise the institution becomes vendor-dependent and non-conform.

---

## B03.CH05.P09.S03 — Model and tooling audit: reproducibility, version pinning, changelog discipline

Tooling can drift the order without changing any law; therefore tooling must be governed like a regulated subsystem.

### Audit requirements (minimum)
- **Reproducibility**: deterministic reruns on the same inputs produce the same outputs (within declared uncertainty rules).
- **Version pinning**: every decision references tool versions, rule sets, and dependencies.
- **Changelog discipline**: every change has rationale, impact analysis, and backward-compatibility statement.
- **Independent runs**: auditors (or independent teams) can run the same computation on equivalent evidence sets.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- “Auto-updating” toolchains that change outcomes without an explicit versioned change record are non-conform.
- Any model/tool used in governance MUST have audit artifacts sufficient to explain output lineage (inputs → transforms → outputs).
- If a change breaks comparability, a **bridging/restatement** method MUST be published; otherwise trend reporting is invalid.

---

## B03.CH05.P09.S04 — Concentration control: supplier concentration, single points of failure, capture risk

Even with open standards, concentration creates practical capture: procurement must avoid single chokepoints.

### Concentration controls (minimum)
- **Supplier diversity** for key layers (compute, registry, audit tooling, case management, analytics).
- **Single-point-of-failure mapping**: identify dependencies that can halt enforcement or measurement.
- **Chokepoint stress tests**: simulate vendor failure, licensing disputes, outage, and hostile withdrawal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Procurement MUST treat high concentration as a measurable risk and apply mitigation (multi-sourcing, modularity, portability drills).
- If vendor concentration crosses a defined threshold for a critical function, enhanced audit mode MUST be triggered and a reduction
  plan becomes mandatory.

---

## B03.CH05.P09.S05 — Sanctions for tooling non-compliance: breach, exclusion, re-tendering

Vendor governance must have teeth; otherwise procurement becomes a hidden exception regime.

### Sanction ladder (minimum)
- **Breach notice + cure window** (short, predefined) for auditability/portability failures.
- **Withholding/termination** for failure to deliver evidence surface, portability, or reproducibility.
- **Exclusion** from future tenders for repeated non-compliance or strategic obstruction.
- **Re-tendering** with emergency continuity measures (shadow computation, manual fallback) when integrity is threatened.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a vendor blocks audit access, refuses portability, or introduces black-box computation into a critical function, the institution
  MUST treat this as an integrity incident and MUST initiate contract rollback and replacement procedures.
- Public reporting MUST include vendor non-compliance incidents and the applied remedy (privacy-protective aggregation allowed).
