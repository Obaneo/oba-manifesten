# B03.CH11.P06 — Recovery procedures: rollback, re-authorisation, and reconstitution

This paragraph specifies the **recovery procedures** required when internal conflict has damaged governability, produced capture windows, or triggered
hard stops. Recovery is the act of restoring OBA rails after drift: undoing structural exceptions, unwinding lock-ins, re-establishing contestable
authority, repairing commons capacity, and repairing the historical record. The recovery set is deliberately **operational**: it must be executable
under pressure, time-bounded, and logged. “Recovery” is not narrative reconciliation; it is a controlled return to auditable order.

OBA defines five recovery instruments: **rollback**, **re-authorisation**, **reconstitution**, **repair**, and **restatements**. Together, they ensure
that conflicts do not permanently degrade the system into semantic washing or authoritarian shortcuts.

## Core principles

* **Remove the benefit of drift**: recovery must reverse captured outcomes, not merely acknowledge failure.
* **Restore contestability**: authority must be re-grounded in procedures and evidence, not in emergency discretion.
* **Protect continuity**: essentials and commons services must continue via auditable fallback modes.
* **Repair the record**: without restatements, drift remains embedded in public metrics and legitimacy claims.
* **Time-bound recovery**: recovery regimes must have exit criteria to prevent permanent emergency governance.

## B03.CH11.P06.S01 — Rollback: reversing exceptions, contract lock-ins, tooling changes

Rollback is the primary recovery instrument: it reverses decisions and structures that breached rails. It prevents capture from remaining profitable.

### Rollback targets (minimum)

* **Exceptions**: revoke improperly issued exemptions; halt renewals; restore baseline applicability of caps and boundary rules.
* **Contracts and lock-ins**: terminate or re-tender captured procurement; unwind bundled suites; enforce portability and exitability.
* **Tooling and models**: revert to last-known-good auditable version; remove black-box scoring; reinstate replication rights.
* **Governance decisions**: void or revise decisions made under conflicted authority; re-run allocation decisions under auditable procedures.

### Rollback method (required)

* **Baseline reversion**: revert to pinned definition set + pinned tooling version.
* **Scope-limited fallback**: operate only in auditable subset while rollback proceeds (fail-closed mode).
* **Evidence-linked**: each rollback item links to incident report, authority, and audit findings.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Confirmed rail breaches **MUST** trigger rollback review; “we keep the outcome” is non-compatible.
* Rollback actions **MUST** be logged with affected objects, dates, and migration steps; silent reversions are prohibited.
* If rollback is technically impossible due to vendor lock-in, that impossibility is a structural failure requiring immediate exitability remediation.

## B03.CH11.P06.S02 — Re-authorisation: new mandate for a constitutive deviation

Re-authorisation is required when a change is **constitutive**: it alters non-negotiables, redefines applicability, or materially changes the rails. OBA
prevents constitutive change by stealth (via exceptions or definitional drift) by requiring renewed authorisation under special procedure.

### Re-authorisation triggers (minimum)

* Persistent or large-scale exceptions that functionally change cap/commons/auditability rails.
* Definition-set changes that break comparability or alter the meaning of core tests.
* Emergency measures extended beyond allowed windows or normalised into baseline.
* Black-box allocation regimes proposed as permanent “efficiency” solutions.

### Re-authorisation requirements (minimum)

* **Explicit delta statement**: what rail changes, why, and how compatibility will be assessed.
* **Evidence pack**: impacts on E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM, uncertainty bands, failure mode analysis.
* **Special procedure**: supermajority/dual-key or equivalent higher threshold; cannot be achieved by routine majority only.
* **Sunset and review**: even after re-authorisation, include review dates and rollback triggers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any constitutive deviation **MUST** be re-authorised or rolled back; “temporary” renewals cannot substitute for mandate.
* Re-authorisation outcomes **MUST** be published with definition-set versioning and restatement obligations.
* If re-authorisation fails, the deviation is illegitimate and rollback is mandatory.

## B03.CH11.P06.S03 — Reconstitution: reappointment/rotation of bodies under capture

Reconstitution is required when institutions are compromised: audit units are soft, oversight is captured, procurement is locked, or enforcement is
selective. It restores integrity by changing personnel and structure, not merely issuing guidance.

### Reconstitution actions (minimum)

* **Leadership replacement**: remove conflicted leadership; appoint interim integrity leadership with bounded mandate.
* **Rotation reset**: rotate teams; re-randomise assignments; implement cross-review and audit-of-auditors.
* **Independence restoration**: separate execution/oversight/audit functions; enforce COI rules and cooling-off.
* **Independent review board**: temporary board to supervise remediation, approve high-risk decisions, and publish findings.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Reconstitution **MUST** include continuity plans; otherwise it creates service collapse and reopens vendor-dependence capture routes.
* Interim bodies **MUST** be time-limited with explicit exit criteria; permanent emergency governance is prohibited.
* If reconstitution does not restore sanction rate, latency, and reproducibility within defined windows, escalation must proceed to stronger legal remedies.

## B03.CH11.P06.S04 — Repair: restoration of commons ΔG and neutralisation of crisis rents

Recovery is incomplete if it restores rules but leaves the material environment degraded. Commons require ΔG maintenance and crisis rents must be
neutralised, otherwise the system remains politically unstable and vulnerable to scapegoating and exception pressure.

### Repair domains (minimum)

* **Commons ΔG restoration**: clear maintenance backlog; restore service quality; fund surge capacity where scarcity caused conflict escalation.
* **Neutralisation of crisis rents**: clawbacks, price controls within rails, temporary measures that remove toll profiteering without creating new permanent extraction.
* **Essentials continuity**: ensure housing/energy/care/transport continuity during recovery; avoid burden concentration.
* **Rebalancing**: temporary R/B buffers that protect swing groups while rails are restored (time-bounded and auditable).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Repair measures **MUST** be linked to measurable outcomes (ΔG backlog↓, service reliability↑, E proxies↓).
* Anti-asset-support discipline applies: repair cannot become incumbent protection that preserves extraction structures.
* If repair is skipped, legitimacy conflict persists and fork pressure rises; this is treated as governance failure.

## B03.CH11.P06.S05 — Restatements: correction of time series and definition-shift bridging

Restatements repair the public and institutional record. When definitions, methods, or tooling change (or were corrupted), past measurements and
classifications become incomparable. Without restatements, actors can rewrite history by changing semantics.

### Restatement requirements (minimum)

* **Time series correction**: recompute historical indicators under the corrected definition set where feasible.
* **Bridging notes**: document differences between old and new definitions; provide mapping or dual reporting where full restatement is impossible.
* **Provenance**: each restated series links to definition-set versions, code/model versions, and data snapshots.
* **Public disclosure**: publish what changed, why, and how prior conclusions are affected; corrections must propagate to dashboards and summaries.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any definitional or tooling change that affects core indicators **MUST** include restatement plan and publication.
* Silent edits are prohibited; corrections must be explicit, logged, and time-stamped.
* If restatement is impossible, trend claims **MUST** be bounded and the system must fail-closed on decisions requiring comparability until resolved.
