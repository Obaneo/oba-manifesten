# B03.CH11.P05 — Escalation architecture: conflict resolution without breaking order

This paragraph defines the **escalation architecture** for resolving internal conflict in OBA governance without breaking rails. The architecture is a
procedural ladder that routes disputes through progressively stronger forums while preserving two invariants: (i) **contestability** (decisions are
challengeable on evidence and procedure), and (ii) **continuity** (essentials and commons operation continue unless a hard-stop condition is met).
Without a pre-committed escalation ladder, conflict resolution degenerates into ad hoc power, procedural sabotage (stacking), selective enforcement, or
semantic drift—each a direct path to schism and fork pressure.

The ladder integrates evidence discipline (logs, definition sets, replication), non-suspensive defaults to prevent “appeal-as-veto,” hard stops under
governability loss, and public accountability via incident reporting and changelog/exception registries.

## Core principles

* **Procedure is anti-capture**: conflict resolution must not be improvized; it must be rule-bound and auditable.
* **Evidence is the shared substrate**: disputes are resolved by reference to pinned definitions, logs, and reproducible computation.
* **Continuity is default**: services and enforcement continue while disputes are processed (non-suspensive).
* **Hard stops protect rails**: when measurability/auditability collapses, the system must fail-closed to auditable scope.
* **Public accountability prevents laundering**: decisions, exceptions, and definition changes must be traceable and reviewable.

## B03.CH11.P05.S01 — Internal procedure: complaint → mediation → arbitration/tribunal → judicial review

OBA requires a standard internal procedure that is accessible, time-bounded, and authority-clear. The purpose is to convert conflict into a routable
case with a defined evidentiary basis and remedy scope.

### Escalation ladder (minimum)

1. **Complaint (intake)**

   * Register the dispute: claimant, affected domain (caps/ΔM/commons/RB/audit), alleged breach, desired remedy.
   * Create a case ID; attach relevant definition-set version and decision references.
2. **Mediation (facilitated resolution)**

   * For parametric and low-stakes interpretive conflicts: negotiate within rails using published options and impact notes.
   * Produce a written settlement with parameter deltas, monitoring plan, and review date.
3. **Arbitration / tribunal (binding adjudication)**

   * For interpretive, institutional, or high-stakes conflicts: decide classification, compliance, and remedies.
   * Tribunal has authority to order corrective action, rollback of specific decisions, or escalation to hard stops where governability fails.
4. **Judicial review (external legality + proportionality)**

   * Where rights, sanctions, procurement awards, or systemic rail breaches are alleged: courts/tribunals review legality, procedure, and evidence sufficiency.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Each stage **MUST** have maximum timelines and published authority scope; “indefinite review” is procedural sabotage.
* Remedies **MUST** include capability to correct outcomes (reclassify, rollback, restate), not only to issue opinions.
* Accessibility requirements (language, time, digital access) must be met; otherwise legitimacy conflict escalates by design.

## B03.CH11.P05.S02 — Evidence discipline: logs, definition sets, audit trails, replication

Evidence discipline is what makes conflict governable. OBA treats logs and definition sets as constitutive infrastructure: without them, conflict becomes
rhetorical and unresolvable.

### Evidence bundle (minimum)

* **Decision logs**: who decided, under which authority, using what inputs, at what time, with what rationale.
* **Definition set pinning**: the exact semantic version of definitions and tests used (caps base, classification rules, boundary rules).
* **Audit trails**: sampling method, findings, enforcement actions, sanction decisions, and exception usage.
* **Replication artefacts**: data snapshots, code/model versions, seeds for randomness, and the ability for independent re-runs.
* **Uncertainty disclosure**: where relevant, uncertainty bands and contingency rules used in decisions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any decision contested in escalation **MUST** be reproducible against pinned definitions and artefacts; otherwise hard-stop conditions are approached.
* Evidence must be publishable in privacy-safe form; “cannot disclose” claims must be narrow, logged, and reviewable.
* If the evidence bundle is incomplete, the system **MUST** default to fail-closed behaviour for the contested domain until reconstructed.

## B03.CH11.P05.S03 — Non-suspensive defaults: preventing sabotage via procedural stacking

Procedural stacking is the tactic of using endless appeals, consultations, or jurisdictional disputes to create de facto veto power. OBA counters this
with **non-suspensive defaults**: filing a complaint does not automatically halt enforcement or service delivery.

### Non-suspensive design (minimum)

* **Operations continue** by default while cases proceed, except where hard-stop conditions apply.
* **Time-boxed stays**: if interim harm is plausible, limited stays may be granted with strict timelines and evidentiary threshold.
* **Anti-forum shopping**: a single primary forum is designated per conflict type; parallel filings are consolidated.
* **Priority routing**: essential services and cap enforcement disputes get accelerated routing to prevent prolonged limbo.
* **Burden of proof rules**: those requesting suspension must show defined harms and provide alternative continuity plans.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Appeals **MUST NOT** function as automatic vetoes; otherwise organised actors can sabotage rails.
* Any granted suspension **MUST** be logged, time-limited, and paired with measurement continuity obligations.
* Repeated procedural delays trigger escalation: if delay patterns correlate with exceptions↑ or sanction rate↓, treat as capture-risk.

## B03.CH11.P05.S04 — Hard stops: stop conditions under measurement blackout, cap erosion, black-box allocation

Hard stops are fail-closed mechanisms that protect governability when core conditions are lost. They are not punitive; they are safety rails. When
measurement, auditability, or contestability fails, continuing normal operation produces unaccountable power.

### Hard-stop conditions (minimum)

* **Measurement blackout**: dashboards/registers suspended beyond allowed window; missing artefacts prevent replication.
* **Cap erosion**: exception ratio breaches thresholds; renewals become routine; near-cap clustering is unpoliced; enforcement softness persists.
* **Black-box allocation**: ΔM/commons allocation cannot be explained or contested; vendor models or secret criteria determine outcomes.
* **Audit obstruction**: denied access to records, tooling, or procurement lineages necessary for verification.
* **Definition drift without restatement**: semantic changes occur without version pinning and correction of time series.

### Hard-stop actions (required)

* **Fail-closed to auditable subset**: operate only on last-known-good definition set and verifiable metrics.
* **Freeze high-risk changes**: semantic freeze on definitions and exception renewals; procurement freeze on critical tooling where needed.
* **Trigger recovery**: mandate rollback/reconstitution/restatement procedures (P06) with independent oversight.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Hard stops **MUST** be automatic upon threshold breach; discretionary enforcement creates a new capture channel.
* Hard-stop activation **MUST** be publicly logged with scope, rationale, and exit criteria.
* Continuing full operation under black-box conditions is non-compatible with order-democracy claims.

## B03.CH11.P05.S05 — Public accountability: incident report, exception register, changelog

Public accountability ensures conflict resolution does not become internal laundering. Internal disputes that touch caps, exceptions, allocation, or
audit integrity must generate public artefacts sufficient for contestation and learning.

### Public artefacts (minimum)

* **Incident report**: description, affected rails, trigger signals, interim measures, investigation plan, and timelines.
* **Exception register updates**: exceptions used, scope, beneficiaries, sunset dates, renewal decisions, and evidence basis.
* **Changelog entries**: definition changes, parameter changes, tooling changes, and their impact notes; restatement status.
* **Decision publication**: tribunal outcomes, remedies ordered, and compliance follow-up.
* **Post-mortem**: mechanism analysis and lessons-to-structure updates (version-controlled).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Material incidents **MUST** produce publishable reports and registry updates; silence is a capture enabler.
* Changelog discipline **MUST** include restatements; otherwise accountability is cosmetic.
* If public reporting is systematically delayed or redacted beyond necessity, escalation must treat it as legitimacy breach and trigger independent review.
