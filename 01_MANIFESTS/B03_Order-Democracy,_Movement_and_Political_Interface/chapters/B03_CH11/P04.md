# B03.CH11.P04 — Schism and split dynamics: when conflict becomes a fork

This paragraph defines the dynamics by which internal conflict crosses the threshold into a **schism** and becomes a **fork** event. In OBA, not every
deep disagreement implies incompatibility. Fork pressure arises when conflict stops being a parametric/interpretive dispute and becomes an attack on
governability itself: exceptions become structural, sanctions stop, allocation becomes black-box, and definition control is captured. At that point,
the system has two options: restore rails through hard stops and recovery procedures, or formalise divergence via the **fork protocol** and relabeling.

The purpose of this paragraph is to (i) specify **trigger conditions**, (ii) describe coalition and elite split mechanics that amplify rupture, (iii)
explain why crises accelerate schisms, and (iv) bind the transition from “internal conflict” to “formal fork” through B03.CH07 compatibility rules.

## Core principles

* **Forks are about rails, not rhetoric**: incompatibility is functional (measurability/auditability/caps/commons), not tribal identity.
* **Schism is often procedural first**: the first rupture is usually in enforcement and auditability, not in speeches.
* **Crises compress time**: shocks increase discretion and create cover for exception normalisation.
* **Forking is a governance act**: when rails diverge, the name must diverge; semantic blending is washing.

## B03.CH11.P04.S01 — Triggers: exception explosion, sanction-rate collapse, black-box allocation

Fork dynamics begin when governability breaks in observable ways. The strongest triggers are those that remove contestability and enforceability.

### Trigger set (minimum)

* **Exception explosion**: exception ratio rises sharply; “temporary” renewals become routine; carve-outs concentrate among high-power actors.
* **Sanction-rate collapse**: verified breaches no longer produce sanctions; enforcement latency rises; selectivity becomes visible.
* **Black-box allocation**: ΔM or commons allocation decisions cannot be reproduced or contested; models or vendor systems become authoritative without replication.
* **Reproducibility failure**: definition sets/tooling change without version pinning; restatement gaps persist; independent runs fail.
* **Audit obstruction**: denied access to records, models, procurement specs, or decision logs; “confidentiality” becomes a systematic shield.

### Why these triggers matter

They are not “policy differences.” They are **infrastructure failures** that convert a democracy into authority-by-assertion. Once they persist, ordinary
deliberation cannot resolve the dispute because the shared evidentiary substrate is gone.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any trigger breach **MUST** activate hard stops and escalation (P05) within predefined timelines.
* If triggers persist beyond the maximum remediation window, the system **MUST** either restore rails or initiate fork classification and relabeling (S05).
* Trigger denial without counter-evidence is non-compliant; claims must be answered through logs, audits, and replication.

## B03.CH11.P04.S02 — Coalition rupture: swing groups lose, scapegoating, media capture

Even when technical triggers exist, schisms become politically irreversible when coalitions break. OBA’s stability depends on maintaining R/B floors and
essentials continuity; when these fail, swing groups become available to anti-rail narratives.

### Coalition rupture mechanisms (canonical)

* **Swing groups lose**: concentrated short-run burdens without buffers; perceived unfairness; benefits delayed or invisible.
* **Scapegoating**: failures are attributed to out-groups or “saboteurs” rather than to measurable mechanism faults; selective enforcement rises.
* **Incident politics**: edge cases become emblematic; exceptions are demanded to “solve” incidents; rails are softened.
* **Media capture**: definitional clarity is replaced by frames (“unworkable”, “capital flight”, “bureaucratic tyranny”); source poverty increases.
* **Participation legitimacy fracture**: groups feel excluded from decision procedures; procedural fairness is contested.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If coalition rupture correlates with rising exception ratio or enforcement softness, the system **MUST** treat it as a fork-risk incident and publish an incident report.
* Anti-scapegoat rails and procedural fairness reviews **MUST** activate (P07 + P05), with measurable commitments and timelines.
* Narrative claims **MUST** be answered with dashboards/logs and definition-set anchors, not counter-slogans.

## B03.CH11.P04.S03 — Elite splitting: production vs finance, platform power vs commons

Elite splits are a predictable internal fracture line in OBA because caps and commons challenge toll positions. Some elite segments can profit in an
OBA regime (competitive production, innovation without tolls); others depend on extraction (finance rent, gatekeeping platforms, monopoly scarcity).

### Elite split patterns (canonical)

* **Production vs finance**: productive capital accepts caps as anti-monopoly; financial/toll actors push for carve-outs, netting tricks, and shadow claims.
* **Platform power vs commons**: platforms resist interoperability and commons substitution; they prefer proprietary distribution and black-box allocation.
* **Regulated incumbents vs entrants**: incumbents demand protection; entrants demand open rails and contestable procurement.
* **Vendor elites**: contractors and consultancies resist exitability and replication; they benefit from black-box dependence.

### Schism accelerants

* Funding and lobbying amplify the anti-rail faction during crisis windows.
* Procurement and tooling become battlegrounds: capture attempts focus on standards and auditability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Elite split pressures **MUST** be made legible: publish lobbying/funding links, procurement lineages, and vendor dependency metrics.
* If the anti-rail faction succeeds in black-box allocation or exception normalisation, hard stops and rollback protocols **MUST** trigger.
* The system **MUST** prevent semantic blending: factions cannot preserve the OBA label while neutralising OBA rails.

## B03.CH11.P04.S04 — Crisis windows: shocks as accelerators of rupture

Crises compress time and expand discretion. They create legitimacy cover for measures that would be rejected in normal times: emergency powers,
exceptions, secrecy, and vendor dependency. This is why shocks are accelerators of schism and fork.

### Crisis acceleration mechanisms (canonical)

* **Emergency exception normalisation**: temporary waivers become baseline through renewals; sunsets fail.
* **Measurement blackout**: dashboards and registers pause; uncertainty is hidden; post-hoc narratives replace evidence.
* **Crisis rents**: toll profiteering expands; asset-support reflex returns as “stabilisation.”
* **Centralisation drift**: decision power consolidates; appeal routes weaken; procedural stacking blocks contestation.
* **Vendor lock-in**: rushed procurement creates dependence; audit rights are traded away for speed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Crisis regimes **MUST** tighten, not loosen, core rails: sunsets, ex post audit, restatements, and procurement exitability become stricter.
* Measurement continuity (minimum indicator suite) **MUST** be maintained; blackouts are legitimacy breaches unless narrowly justified and time-locked.
* If crisis measures persist without re-authorisation, the system **MUST** classify it as constitutive deviation and trigger fork protocol or rollback.

## B03.CH11.P04.S05 — Fork protocol: classification, name label, and compatibility status (link to B03.CH07)

When conflict becomes a fork, the critical mistake is to keep one shared label. Semantic blending converts incompatibility into confusion and enables
washing. Therefore, OBA requires that fork events be formalised through B03.CH07.

### Fork protocol linkage (required)

* **Classification**: determine whether the divergence is a variant (parametric within rails), extension (add-on), or fork (rail deviation).
* **Name label**: apply strict naming conventions (OBA-compatible / partial / non-compatible / inspired) and disclose which rails diverge.
* **Compatibility status**: run the compatibility test (caps binding, commons discipline, ΔM auditability, R/B floors, reproducibility).
* **Registry and lineage**: record the fork in a registry with scope, dates, rationale, impacted definitions, and migration/bridging notes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If non-negotiables are violated or auditability/contestability breaks, continued use of “OBA” without relabeling is prohibited (misrepresentation).
* Fork classification **MUST** be published with a scorecard and evidence; rhetorical self-declaration is insufficient.
* Where reconciliation is possible, rollback and re-authorisation protocols (P06) **MUST** be attempted before fork permanence is accepted—unless hard-stop conditions demand immediate separation.
