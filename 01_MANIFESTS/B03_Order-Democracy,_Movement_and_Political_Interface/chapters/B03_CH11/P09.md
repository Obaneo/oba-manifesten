# B03.CH11.P09 — Integration with the democratic consistency test (B03.CH12)

This paragraph specifies how internal conflict management integrates with the **democratic consistency test** (B03.CH12). Conflict is not only a social
phenomenon; it is a **diagnostic channel**: sustained conflict patterns reveal whether the system is drifting toward capture, losing contestability, or
breaking rails under pressure. B03.CH12 evaluates whether order-democracy remains consistent over time; this paragraph defines which conflict signals
feed into that evaluation, when intervention becomes mandatory, how documentation must be structured for auditability, how forks are mapped and labeled,
and how lessons are incorporated without redefining the core.

The integration rule is simple: **conflict is evidence**. It must be measured, logged, and treated as early warning when it correlates with rail-adjacent
integrity failures (exceptions, sanctions, reproducibility, black-box allocation).

## Core principles

* **Conflict is an integrity sensor**: patterns of disputes, delays, and exceptions predict drift earlier than outcomes alone.
* **Mandatory intervention thresholds**: escalation cannot be discretionary when leading indicators breach.
* **Documentation is legitimacy**: without traceable logs and changelogs, consistency cannot be assessed.
* **Fork clarity prevents washing**: when incompatibility exists, labels must split.
* **Learning must be versioned**: protocol adjustments must be traceable without retroactively changing what “OBA” means.

## B03.CH11.P09.S01 — Conflict indicators as early warning: exceptions↑, sanction rate↓, reproducibility↓

Certain conflict signals are not “debate noise” but early warnings of systemic integrity failure. B03.CH12 therefore ingests a minimum early-warning set
in which conflict metrics are paired with governance and enforcement indicators.

### Early-warning conflict-linked indicator set (minimum)

* **Exceptions↑** during contested policy cycles (conflict produces carve-outs rather than resolutions).
* **Sanction rate↓** during rising disputes (conflict becomes softness/selectivity rather than adjudication).
* **Reproducibility↓** in contested domains (measurement becomes uncheckable; black-box authority rises).
* **Enforcement latency↑** (conflict converts into delay-as-veto).
* **Appeal congestion patterns** (procedural stacking rises; forum shopping increases).
* **Narrative drift signals** (definition shifting in public discourse; “OBA” used for rail breaks).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* The early-warning set **MUST** be published and pinned to definition versions; trend claims without pinned semantics are invalid.
* Where conflict rises but integrity indicators remain stable and auditable, treat as normal politics; where integrity indicators move, treat as drift risk.
* If reproducibility is lost, the system **MUST** fail-closed and escalate, regardless of political claims of urgency.

## B03.CH11.P09.S02 — Thresholds for intervention: when escalation becomes mandatory

B03.CH12 requires explicit thresholds that convert warning into mandatory action. Without thresholds, intervention becomes political discretion, which is a
capture surface.

### Threshold types (required)

* **Level thresholds**: absolute maxima/minima (exception ratio ceiling; sanction rate floor; reproducibility pass/fail).
* **Rate-of-change thresholds**: abrupt shifts (exceptions spike; sanctions collapse; latency jumps).
* **Co-occurrence thresholds**: combinations that imply capture risk (exceptions↑ + sanction rate↓; reproducibility↓ + vendor concentration↑).
* **Persistence thresholds**: duration beyond which a deviation is structural rather than transient.

### Mandatory intervention mapping (minimum)

* Threshold breach → **P05 escalation ladder** activates with deadlines.
* Hard-stop conditions → **fail-closed mode** + **P06 recovery** starts immediately.
* Persistent breach → **fork evaluation** and compatibility labeling becomes mandatory (S04).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Thresholds **MUST** be published with triggers, authority chain, and timelines; “we decided not to intervene” is not acceptable without logged justification.
* If thresholds are repeatedly revised upward to avoid intervention, this is definition/exception capture and triggers semantic freeze review.
* Intervention must be auditable: each trigger produces a case ID, incident report, and post-mortem.

## B03.CH11.P09.S03 — Documentation: conflict logs, decisions, rollback actions (traceable changelog)

Democratic consistency is assessable only if conflict handling leaves a verifiable trail. This section binds documentation requirements that allow
B03.CH12 to evaluate drift versus correctable variance.

### Required documentation artefacts (minimum)

* **Conflict log**: case IDs, parties, domain (caps/ΔM/commons/RB/audit), conflict mode classification, timeline, and procedural stage.
* **Decision log**: authority basis, evidence bundle, definition-set version, rationale, remedy chosen, and review date.
* **Rollback ledger**: what was rolled back (exceptions/contracts/tooling/decisions), baseline restored, and verification results.
* **Restatement record**: what series were corrected, mapping notes, and propagation status to dashboards/summaries.
* **Changelog linkage**: every conflict incident links to relevant definition and protocol changes (if any).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Documentation **MUST** be versioned and cross-referenced; isolated PDFs and unlinked notes are non-auditable.
* Major conflicts **MUST** produce publishable incident reports and registry updates (exceptions/changelog).
* Missing logs or sealed evidence in rail-adjacent conflicts triggers fail-closed behaviour until integrity is restored.

## B03.CH11.P09.S04 — Fork mapping: when formal (in)compatibility is established

B03.CH12 requires that persistent internal conflict sometimes be reclassified as incompatibility. Fork mapping provides the bridge: it decides whether
divergence is a variant (within rails) or a fork (rail break), and labels it accordingly to prevent semantic washing.

### Fork mapping rules (minimum)

* **Trigger-based fork review**: repeated hard stops, exception normalisation, black-box allocation, or reproducibility failure forces fork evaluation.
* **Compatibility scoring**: run the B03.CH07 compatibility test (caps binding, commons discipline, ΔM auditability, R/B floors, replicability).
* **Name discipline**: label outcomes as compatible/partial/non-compatible/inspired; disclose deviating rails.
* **Registry entry**: create fork/variant IDs with scope, date, rationale, and bridging notes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If incompatibility is established, shared naming is prohibited; continued single-label usage is misrepresentation.
* Fork mapping **MUST** be evidence-backed and public; self-declaration is insufficient.
* If reconciliation is chosen, it must be via rollback/re-authorisation/reconstitution, not via rhetorical compromise.

## B03.CH11.P09.S05 — Lessons: adjustments without core breach in governance protocols

Conflict episodes should improve governance protocols. But “learning” is also a laundering opportunity: actors may redefine the rails to make failures
disappear. Therefore B03.CH12 requires lesson integration through version-controlled protocol updates without changing canonical meanings.

### Lessons-to-protocol pipeline (minimum)

* **Incident post-mortem**: mechanism analysis (where drift entered: exceptions, definitions, procurement, enforcement).
* **Control adjustment proposal**: specific changes (thresholds, rotation, access rules, documentation requirements).
* **Versioned update**: apply through protocol/runbook versions with changelog, bridging notes, and restatements where impacts are material.
* **Backtesting**: evaluate whether new controls would have flagged the incident earlier (simulation against past logs).
* **Public publication**: publish adjustments and link them to the incident record.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Protocol changes **MUST** not relax auditability, expand discretion, or normalise exceptions under the banner of “learning.”
* Where a lesson implies new indicators, they **MUST** be integrated into the early-warning set with thresholds and escalation mapping.
* Any attempted redefinition of core rails to avoid accountability triggers a semantic freeze review and compatibility re-assessment.
