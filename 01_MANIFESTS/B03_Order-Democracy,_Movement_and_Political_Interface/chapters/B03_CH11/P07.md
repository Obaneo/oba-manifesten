# B03.CH11.P07 — Internal safety rails: preventing authoritarian drift or regression during conflict

This paragraph specifies **internal safety rails** that must remain binding during periods of internal conflict. Conflict is a known vulnerability window:
actors will attempt to resolve uncertainty by expanding discretion, weakening contestability, accelerating surveillance, scapegoating minorities, and
locking governance into vendors and black-box allocation. Those moves often present as “stabilisation,” but functionally they are either **authoritarian
drift** (power without contestation) or **OOA regression** (reconstitution of toll positions and exceptions under crisis cover).

The purpose of this paragraph is to define what is **prohibited**, what must remain **auditable and contestable**, and which **judicial and procedural
checks** prevent emergency logic from becoming permanent governance.

## Core principles

* **Conflict is not a license**: rails tighten under pressure; they do not loosen.
* **Order enforcement ≠ control expansion**: governability is achieved via evidence and procedure, not surveillance or profiling.
* **Minority safety is a legitimacy rail**: scapegoating converts conflict into selective enforcement and schism dynamics.
* **Procurement is a capture surface**: “quick fixes” during conflict often become vendor lock-in and black-box authority.
* **Emergency must self-terminate**: sunsets, evidence standards, and judicial review prevent permanent exception regimes.

## B03.CH11.P07.S01 — Prohibited linkages: profiling/surveillance as “order enforcement”

During conflict, a common move is to treat contestation as disorder and to propose profiling, expanded surveillance, or cross-system data linkage as a
solution. In OBA, these are high-risk linkages because they convert governance from measurable rails to discretionary control.

### Prohibited linkages (minimum)

* **Profiling for compliance**: risk scoring of persons/groups for enforcement priority without contestable criteria and replicability.
* **Cross-domain linkage creep**: linking welfare, policing, immigration, health, education, banking, or platform identity systems for “order” purposes.
* **Permanent exceptional data access**: emergency data powers that lack strict sunset and ex post audit.
* **Black-box suspicion triggers**: enforcement triggered by opaque model outputs that cannot be reproduced or challenged.
* **Function creep**: data collected for service delivery repurposed for sanctioning or political targeting.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Profiling/surveillance expansion **MUST NOT** be used as a substitute for measurability and auditability.
* Any proposed data linkage **MUST** pass a strict necessity test, data-minimisation rule, and contestability requirements (replicable criteria + appeal).
* If conflict handling relies on non-contestable surveillance, the system is in authoritarian drift mode and **MUST** trigger hard-stop review and rollback.

## B03.CH11.P07.S02 — Anti-scapegoat rails: minority protection and anti-selective enforcement

Internal conflict often seeks resolution by identifying enemies rather than mechanisms. Scapegoating is not merely unethical; it is structurally
destabilising: it enables selective enforcement, exception trading, and legitimacy collapse.

### Anti-scapegoat rails (minimum)

* **Mechanism-first rule**: failures must be described in terms of rails, metrics, and procedures (exceptions, sanction softness, allocation opacity), not identities.
* **Selective enforcement tests**: sanction rates and inspection patterns must be monitored for group-based or region-based selectivity.
* **Minority protection functions**: ombuds/rights bodies with authority to pause discriminatory enforcement pathways and force evidentiary disclosure.
* **Incident discipline**: edge cases cannot justify broad category restrictions without baseline rates and drift indicators.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any conflict narrative that attributes systemic failures to protected groups **MUST** be treated as a schism accelerant and triggers procedural review.
* Enforcement statistics **MUST** be published in privacy-safe aggregate form sufficient to detect selectivity.
* If selective enforcement rises during conflict, escalation must proceed to institutional review and reconstitution (P06) rather than rhetorical management.

## B03.CH11.P07.S03 — Procurement rails: preventing conflict from turning into vendor lock-in

Conflict periods are procurement traps: urgency becomes the justification for bundled contracts, closed tooling, and “temporary” vendor dependence.
This is a direct capture route and often a one-way door.

### Procurement safety rails (minimum)

* **No emergency bypass of exitability**: portability, audit rights, and change-control clauses remain mandatory even under urgency.
* **Modular procurement**: avoid suites that collapse contestability; interfaces must remain open.
* **Second implementation / shadow computation**: independent computation paths for critical outputs during and after procurement transitions.
* **Transparency of text lineage**: procurement specs and evaluation rationale must be logged and reviewable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Conflict-related procurement **MUST** meet the vendor lock-in constraints (open standards, exitability, reproducibility); “speed” does not waive rails.
* If a vendor refuses audit access or reproducibility, procurement **MUST** fail-closed to an auditable alternative, even if less feature-rich.
* Any procurement executed under emergency rules **MUST** have a hard sunset and re-tender requirement with published review.

## B03.CH11.P07.S04 — Crisis communication: transparency minimum, no measurement blackouts

Conflict and crisis invite epistemic collapse: dashboards go dark, uncertainty is hidden, and narrative becomes the operating system. OBA requires
measurement continuity because without it, conflict becomes ungovernable and drift becomes deniable.

### Crisis communication minimum (required)

* **Measurement continuity**: keep core indicators running (exceptions, sanctions, reproducibility status, ΔM allocations, ΔG backlog) with uncertainty bands.
* **No silent edits**: corrections must be restatements with timestamps and propagation to dependent outputs.
* **Source-duty**: official claims must cite data lineage and definition-set versions.
* **Incident reporting**: conflict escalations that touch rails must produce publishable incident notes and timelines.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Measurement blackouts beyond narrowly defined windows are prohibited; they trigger hard stops (P05) and recovery procedures (P06).
* If officials communicate binding claims without sources, the claims are downgraded in legitimacy and must be corrected with replicable evidence.
* Crisis communications **MUST** avoid scapegoat framing and must report structural drift indicators alongside incident narratives.

## B03.CH11.P07.S05 — Judicial checks: proportionality, evidence standards, emergency sunset

Safety rails require enforceable external checks. Judicial review and proportionality standards prevent internal conflict from justifying permanent
rights erosion, selective enforcement, or opaque governance.

### Judicial safety rails (minimum)

* **Evidence standards**: coercive measures require disclosed evidentiary basis; black-box assertions are insufficient.
* **Proportionality**: measures must be the least intrusive option compatible with order goals; broad powers for narrow problems are prohibited.
* **Standing and access**: affected parties must have practical ability to challenge decisions and obtain relevant records (privacy-safe).
* **Emergency sunset**: emergency powers auto-expire; renewal requires affirmative re-authorisation with published evidence and restatements.
* **Nullification capability**: courts/tribunals must be able to void unlawfully issued exceptions, contracts, or enforcement actions and order rollback.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Emergency measures **MUST** be time-bounded with explicit exit criteria and ex post audits; “open-ended emergency” is non-compatible.
* If judicial review is blocked by secrecy, missing logs, or proprietary models, the regime must fail-closed to auditable operations until contestability is restored.
* Where judicial findings indicate rail breach or disproportionality, rollback and reconstitution are mandatory (P06), not optional.
