# B03.CH11.P08 — Stabilisation: mechanisms to structurally reduce conflict intensity

This paragraph specifies **stabilisation mechanisms** that reduce the intensity and frequency of internal conflict in OBA governance without loosening the
rails. Conflict cannot be eliminated; it can be made less explosive by reducing scarcity spikes, making allocations predictable, protecting swing groups
against timing asymmetry, maintaining contestability through redundancy, and enforcing a shared language discipline so disputes remain about mechanisms
and tests rather than identity and slogans.

Stabilisation is not appeasement. It is infrastructure: design choices that reduce the incentives for exception pressure, scapegoating, and procedural
sabotage, thereby lowering the probability that conflict escalates into schism and fork dynamics.

## Core principles

* **Predictability reduces warfare**: when rules and bands are stable, actors stop treating every cycle as an existential battle.
* **Capacity reduces scarcity conflict**: commons shortages convert normal politics into zero-sum rationing fights.
* **Buffers protect legitimacy**: swing groups under shocks determine whether rails survive or are softened via exceptions.
* **Redundancy prevents monopoly of truth**: parallelism keeps auditability and prevents capture during contentious periods.
* **Shared grammar prevents semantic drift**: test axes convert moral conflict into governable classification conflict.

## B03.CH11.P08.S01 — Predictable ΔM: fixed allocation bands and transparent criteria

ΔM allocation is a prime internal conflict source because it determines visible distribution. Stabilisation requires reducing discretionary swings and
making allocation constraints predictable.

### Predictability mechanisms (minimum)

* **Fixed allocation bands**: stable percentage bands for major domains (essentials continuity, commons ΔG maintenance, transition investment, buffers).
* **Transparent criteria**: published scoring/eligibility criteria for projects with clear axis mapping (E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM).
* **Rule-based sequencing**: pre-committed sequencing rules to reduce bargaining cycles and “winner takes all” moments.
* **Review cadence**: scheduled recalibration windows; outside windows, changes require special procedure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Major ΔM allocations **MUST** operate within published bands; ad hoc reallocations are exceptions and must be logged as such.
* Criteria **MUST** be auditable and replicable; black-box “strategic” allocation increases schism risk.
* If allocations repeatedly breach bands without re-authorisation, treat as governance drift and trigger escalation.

## B03.CH11.P08.S02 — Commons capacity: surge planning to limit scarcity conflict

Commons politics becomes explosive under scarcity. Stabilisation requires operational planning that prevents predictable capacity shocks from becoming
political schisms.

### Capacity stabilisers (minimum)

* **Surge planning**: predefined plans for demand spikes (winter energy peaks, care shortages, housing pressure, crisis migration).
* **Maintenance-first discipline (ΔG)**: protect maintenance budgets and backlog clearance; neglect creates failure cascades and scapegoating.
* **Rationing rules upfront**: publish prioritisation criteria and appeal routes before scarcity hits; avoid improvised gatekeeping.
* **Interoperability and substitution**: design commons services to allow substitution and load balancing across regions/providers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Commons operators **MUST** publish surge plans and prioritisation rules; improvisation under crisis invites selective enforcement.
* ΔG backlogs **MUST** be measured and reported; persistent backlog growth is a stabilisation failure.
* Scarcity-driven rule changes **MUST** be time-bounded and restated; otherwise scarcity becomes a permanent exception regime.

## B03.CH11.P08.S03 — R/B buffers: protecting swing groups under shocks and sequencing

Political stability depends on protecting swing groups from concentrated losses during transition. Without buffers, conflict escalates into backlash and
exception pressure that hollows out rails.

### Buffer mechanisms (minimum)

* **Lower-bound guarantees**: explicit R/B floors for essentials (housing, energy, care, mobility) during transition windows.
* **Shock absorbers**: automatic stabilisers triggered by macro shocks (unemployment spikes, energy price shocks) with strict sunsets.
* **Sequencing discipline**: implement cost-imposing measures only when alternatives and buffers are in place; avoid “burden first, benefit later” traps.
* **Targeted transition support**: time-limited support for affected workers/regions that avoids preserving extraction structures (no toll protection).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Buffer measures **MUST** be auditable, time-bounded, and designed to avoid E-preservation; otherwise they become asset-support reflex.
* If swing-group stress indicators rise without buffers, escalation must treat it as schism-risk and trigger stabilisation intervention.
* Buffer spending must be accounted for in ΔM with published bands; hidden buffers via exceptions are prohibited.

## B03.CH11.P08.S04 — Institutional redundancy: parallel audits and second implementations

Conflict intensifies when actors distrust the measurement and enforcement substrate. Redundancy keeps the system contestable when trust collapses.

### Redundancy stabilisers (minimum)

* **Parallel audits**: independent teams re-audit samples and publish deltas; audit-of-auditors is routine.
* **Second implementations**: shadow computation of key metrics and classification outputs (caps status, ΔM allocations, exception ratios).
* **Independent data pathways**: alternative data sources or verification channels for critical indicators.
* **Rotation + redundancy coupling**: rotate teams but preserve capacity through parallelism and runbooks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* High-stakes domains **MUST** have redundant audit/computation paths; monopoly measurement is a schism accelerator.
* If reproducibility declines, redundancy must increase (expanded shadow computation) until integrity is restored.
* Redundancy results **MUST** be logged and publishable; hidden parallel checks defeat legitimacy.

## B03.CH11.P08.S05 — Education and language discipline: test axes as a shared grammar

Stabilisation requires a shared grammar to prevent disputes from degenerating into slogans or identity wars. OBA’s test axes (E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM +
auditability) are that grammar: they allow people to disagree while still speaking in comparable terms.

### Language discipline mechanisms (minimum)

* **Axis-first communication**: proposals and critiques must be stated as changes in mechanisms and expected axis impacts.
* **Definition-set literacy**: key actors (media, councils, unions, commons boards) must have minimal training in the canonical terms and tests.
* **Anti-relabeling practice**: function-over-label checks in public communication; “OBA” cannot be used to describe OOA functions.
* **Public dashboards**: concrete flows, exceptions, sanctions, and reproducibility status as shared reference points.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Official debate and consultation **MUST** use the test axes and pinned definitions; otherwise politics reverts to semantic drift and washing.
* Education must be continuous: turnover without training recreates technocracy perception and legitimacy conflict.
* If discourse shifts toward definition-blending or slogan substitution, institutions must publish clarifications and restore axis-based reporting.
