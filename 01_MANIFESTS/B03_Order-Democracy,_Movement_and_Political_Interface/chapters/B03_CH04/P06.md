# B03.CH04.P06 — Financing and the ΔM interface: budgets, channels, and control

This paragraph specifies how commons-democracy connects to **ΔM** (monetary space) as the **allocation interface** for funding,
budgeting, and control. The goal is to ensure that financing strengthens **ΔG** (commons capacity and service guarantees) without
creating new toll positions, hidden claims, or black-box allocations. Funding must therefore be channelled through explicit,
auditable **ΔM channels**, governed by predictable budget rules, and protected against capture through procurement and exitability
rails. Commons financing is treated as a governance-critical surface: if it becomes opaque, collateralised, or privately gated, the
commons reverts to an OOA-like claim regime even if the label remains “public”.

---

## B03.CH04.P06.S01 — ΔM channels: allocation to commons versus other public goals

Commons financing must be expressed as explicit ΔM channel choices with measurable outcomes.

### Channel families (minimum)
- **Commons operations (OPEX)**: service continuity, staffing, preventive maintenance.
- **Commons lifecycle CAPEX**: renewal, replacement, resilience, capacity expansion.
- **Emergency buffers**: surge capacity and continuity under shocks.
- **Non-commons public goals**: other allocations that must remain distinct from commons funding to prevent cross-subsidy opacity.

### Channel discipline
- Each allocation MUST identify: channel ID, purpose class, recipient/control entity, conditions, and measurement link to ΔG.
- Commingling funds across channels MUST be treated as a risk factor and requires explicit disclosure and reconciliation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons allocations MUST be separable and auditable as ΔM channels (no lump-sum opacity).
- Allocation decisions MUST include a stated ΔG linkage (service minima, backlog reduction, coverage, reliability).

---

## B03.CH04.P06.S02 — Budget rules: predictability, counter-cyclicality, emergency buffers

Budget design must prevent cyclical underfunding and crisis-driven privatisation drift.

### Budget principles (minimum)
- **Predictability**: multi-year baselines that stabilise operations and maintenance cycles.
- **Counter-cyclicality**: automatic stabilisers where feasible (avoid austerity-induced decay).
- **Ring-fencing**: protected shares for maintenance and essentials continuity.
- **Emergency buffers**: predefined reserve logic with entry/exit conditions and audit trails.

### Exception governance
- Emergency reallocations require time-bounded authorisation, published rationale, and rollback plans.
- “Temporary” budget diversions must not become structural through renewals or stacking.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A published baseline + ruleset MUST exist for commons budgeting (including triggers and thresholds).
- If buffers are drawn down, replenishment rules MUST be explicit and enforced.

---

## B03.CH04.P06.S03 — External procurement: procurement rails, open standards, exitability

External procurement is a primary capture vector; it must be engineered for contestability and reversibility.

### Procurement rails (minimum)
- **Open standards** for data models, interfaces, logging, and calculation rules where relevant.
- **Auditability by design**: access to evidence, decision logs, and reproducible outputs.
- **Contestability**: competitive tendering by default; single-source exceptions tightly bounded.
- **Exitability**: portability, escrow where relevant, second-implementation pathways, and termination rights.

### Vendor concentration controls
- Track supplier concentration and single points of failure.
- Require mitigation plans when concentration thresholds are exceeded.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons procurement MUST prohibit black-box dependencies that prevent independent replication or audit.
- Contracts MUST include exit clauses and data/tooling portability sufficient to avoid lock-in.

---

## B03.CH04.P06.S04 — Claims on commons: ban on collateralisation/privatisation paths

Commons assets and revenue flows must not become collateral or a base for private claim stacking.

### Prohibited claim patterns (minimum)
- **Collateralisation** of commons assets or future service revenues for private financing structures.
- **Securitisation** or off-balance-sheet vehicles that create senior claims on commons continuity.
- **Paid priority** or bundling that recreates toll gates under funding pressure.
- **Vendor-financed lock-in** (build-now-pay-later structures with control rights or exclusivity).

### Allowed only under strict constraints (if ever)
- Time-bounded, explicitly authorised emergency financing with: transparency, capped claims, exitability, and mandatory rollback.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any financing instrument that creates senior claims on commons services MUST be treated as non-conform unless explicitly exempted
  under a crisis regime with hard sunsets and rollback.
- The default rule is **no private claim priority over commons continuity**.

## B03.CH04.P06.S05 — Public accountability: scorecards for ΔG and allocation outcomes

Commons financing must be accountable through public scorecards linking money (ΔM) to service reality (ΔG).

### Scorecard minimum (examples)
- ΔM allocations by channel and purpose class (with versioned definitions).
- ΔG outcomes: service minima attainment, backlog reduction, coverage, reliability, scarcity activations.
- Integrity metrics: exceptions, procurement concentration, audit findings, reproducibility status.

### Accountability mechanics
- Public reporting cadence and restatement rules for corrected data.
- Appeal/objection routes for stakeholders (users, workers, auditors) with evidence access.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Scorecards MUST be published with sufficient detail to reproduce headline claims.
- If scorecard integrity fails (missing data, unverifiable computation, suppressed logs), governance MUST escalate (audit mode and
  potential hard stops on discretionary allocations).
