# B03.CH04.P08 — Anti-capture and anti-façade: protecting commons-democracy

This paragraph defines the minimum **anti-capture** and **anti-façade** protections required for commons-democracy to remain a
real substitute for toll regimes rather than a rhetorical label. Commons governance is uniquely exposed to capture because it
controls access, prioritisation, procurement, and maintenance budgets—exactly the levers that can be converted into private
advantage through vendor lock-in, exception stacking, and procedural domination. The core threat is not only overt corruption but
**functional regression**: commons structures that keep the name while reintroducing toll gates, black-box allocation, or
selective enforcement. Anti-capture therefore focuses on (i) **detectable indicators**, (ii) **structural constraints** (rotation,
disclosure, exitability), and (iii) **credible interventions** that can requalify or reset governance when drift appears.

---

## B03.CH04.P08.S01 — Capture indicators: vendor concentration, exception explosion, sanction rate↓

Capture must be detectable with a minimum indicator set that is hard to game and anchored in observable operations.

### Minimum indicator set (non-exhaustive)
- **Vendor concentration↑**: rising single-supplier share, repeat sole-source procurement, shrinking supplier diversity.
- **Exitability↓**: portability failures, escrow absence, refusal to provide interfaces/logs, no second implementation.
- **Exception explosion↑**: growing exception ratio, renewals, and scope creep; “temporary” holes becoming permanent.
- **Sanction rate↓** (with stable or rising anomaly signals): enforcement collapse indicating selective non-application or capture.
- **Enforcement latency↑**: delays in response, backlog in complaints, slow incident closure under recurring patterns.
- **Reproducibility↓**: inability of independent parties to replicate allocation/priority outcomes from published rules + logs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons governance MUST publish an indicator dashboard minimum (aggregated where necessary) and MUST define thresholds that
  trigger escalation or hard stops.
- If indicators cannot be produced due to missing data/logging, this MUST be treated as an order breach (fail-closed) and
  escalated.

---

## B03.CH04.P08.S02 — Façade detection: closed access under the “commons” label; private tolls in language

Façade commons are regimes that adopt commons terminology while reproducing toll structures or exclusion in practice.

### Façade signatures (minimum)
- **Closed access** without explicit boundary rules, or boundary rules that are discretionary, opaque, or unappealable.
- **Paid priority**: “fast lanes”, premium tiers, bundling, membership gating, or indirect priority via vendor packages.
- **Toll language laundering**: “service fees” that function as extraction (take-rate behaviour) rather than cost recovery.
- **Black-box eligibility**: profiling, scoring, or ranking determining access/prioritisation without explainability or appeal.
- **Private collateralisation/claims** on commons assets or revenues that create de facto toll gates.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any commons-labelled system MUST publish boundary rules, scarcity protocols, and fee rules in a form that allows contestation.
- If toll mechanisms are present (paid priority, gating, lock-in), the system MUST be requalified as non-commons unless removed.

---

## B03.CH04.P08.S03 — Rotation and disclosure: conflicts of interest, revolving doors, procurement checks

Structural protections reduce capture probability and prevent long-term entrenchment.

### Minimum structural rails
- **Conflict-of-interest regimes**: mandatory disclosures, recusals, and public registers for decision-makers.
- **Cooling-off**: defined terms for high-risk role transitions (operator ↔ vendor ↔ auditor ↔ governance seat).
- **Rotation/term limits**: key positions in oversight, audit, and procurement cannot be held indefinitely.
- **Procurement hygiene**: publication of requirements, evaluation criteria, and contract lineage; bans on side letters.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No individual or entity MAY occupy simultaneously incompatible roles (e.g., vendor + decision authority; operator + auditor).
- Recurring procurement awards to the same vendor MUST require heightened justification, independent review, and exitability proof.

---

## B03.CH04.P08.S04 — Open calculation rules and external replication: third-party audit without privacy override

Contestability requires that allocation outcomes can be explained and independently reproduced without turning privacy into an
excuse for secrecy.

### Replication requirements
- **Open calculation rules**: the rules that determine eligibility, prioritisation, fees, and sanctions must be published in
  stable, versioned form.
- **Version pinning**: every decision must reference the rule version and definition set used.
- **Audit logs**: sufficient logging to reproduce outcomes and detect discrimination/gating without exposing unnecessary personal data.
- **Third-party replication**: independent parties must be able to run “shadow computation” on equivalent inputs (or privacy-safe
  representations) and compare outputs.

### Privacy rails (non-negotiable)
- Privacy protections MUST use minimisation, aggregation, pseudonymisation, and controlled access with logging—not black-boxing
  the rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If outcomes cannot be replicated because rules are proprietary or hidden, the mechanism MUST be treated as black-box and
  escalated to a hard stop (until corrected).
- Privacy arguments MUST NOT be used to block auditability; instead, privacy-safe replication pathways MUST be provided.

---

## B03.CH04.P08.S05 — Intervention mechanisms: requalification as market/private toll regime, re-governance, rollback

Anti-capture is not credible without enforceable interventions that can restore order.

### Minimum intervention ladder
1. **Enhanced audit mode**: forensic access, parallel replication, independent review board involvement.
2. **Operational rollback**: suspend or remove gating features, black-box scoring, or exception-driven carve-outs.
3. **Procurement reset**: contract termination where auditability/exitability obligations are breached; re-tender under open standards.
4. **Governance reconstitution**: rotation/recall of captured bodies; temporary trusteeship under defined timelines.
5. **Requalification**: if toll mechanisms persist, formally classify the regime as market/private toll (non-commons) and apply the
   corresponding enforcement/sanction path.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Intervention triggers and authorities MUST be pre-defined, and interventions MUST be time-bounded with public rationale.
- If a commons regime is requalified due to façade/toll behaviour, the “commons” label MUST be withdrawn and public communication
  MUST be corrected (anti-washing rule).
