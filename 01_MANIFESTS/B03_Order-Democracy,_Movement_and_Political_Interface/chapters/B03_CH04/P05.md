# B03.CH04.P05 — Maintenance and ΔG: stewardship as a democratic duty

This paragraph defines **maintenance** as a constitutive obligation of commons-democracy and ties it directly to **ΔG** (commons
capacity and service guarantees). Under OBA, commons are not “owned” benefits that can be run down for short-term relief; they are
a **public substitute for toll regimes** and therefore require explicit maintenance cycles, ring-fenced resources, quality
measurement, and enforceable accountability. Maintenance neglect is treated as a predictable failure mode that produces scarcity,
gating, and covert privatisation—so the system must detect neglect early and compel recovery.

---

## B03.CH04.P05.S01 — Maintenance cycles: minimum levels, backlog metric, ring-fencing

Maintenance must be governed as an explicit lifecycle process rather than an ad-hoc operational preference.

### Maintenance minimums (examples)
- Minimum inspection cadence and preventive maintenance schedules.
- Replacement cycles for critical components with known failure curves.
- Mandatory resilience checks for surge and crisis modes.

### Backlog and decay metrics (minimum set)
- **Backlog stock**: deferred maintenance quantified by scope, criticality, and age.
- **Backlog flow**: inflow vs outflow (new issues vs resolved issues).
- **Critical backlog ratio**: share of backlog above risk threshold.
- **Service impact linkage**: correlation between backlog and outages/quality degradation.

### Ring-fencing rules
- A defined share of budget MUST be ring-fenced for maintenance (OPEX and lifecycle CAPEX where relevant).
- Ring-fenced maintenance funds MUST NOT be diverted without a logged, time-bounded exception and replacement plan.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons operators MUST publish maintenance cycles and the backlog metric definition set.
- Maintenance ring-fencing MUST be enforced with non-stackable exceptions and explicit rollback if violated.
- Persistent backlog growth MUST trigger escalation (audit mode and intervention thresholds).

## B03.CH04.P05.S02 — Investment (capex): prioritisation, project selection, lifecycle accounting

Investment decisions must be driven by ΔG outcomes and lifecycle discipline, not by visible ribbon-cutting incentives.

### Prioritisation criteria (ordered families)
- **Restore and protect service minima** (essentials continuity).
- **Reduce structural scarcity** (capacity expansion where justified).
- **Lower lifecycle cost** (prevent repeated emergency repairs).
- **Resilience and redundancy** (avoid single points of failure).

### Project selection requirements
- Publish project candidates with expected ΔG impact, risk reduction, and maintenance implications.
- Use explicit decision rules and tie-breaks to avoid discretionary capture.
- Include a “do-nothing” baseline and uncertainty bands.

### Lifecycle accounting (minimum)
- Total cost of ownership, replacement and maintenance costs, and decommissioning.
- Asset register linkage: what is built must be maintainable, auditable, and tracked.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- CAPEX decisions MUST include lifecycle and maintenance consequences, not only upfront costs.
- Projects that increase future maintenance burden without funding/plan MUST be non-conform unless explicitly authorised with
  mitigation and ring-fencing.

## B03.CH04.P05.S03 — Quality measurement: coverage, reliability, uptime, service metrics

Commons legitimacy depends on measured service reality, not label claims.

### Minimum measurement families
- **Coverage**: who is served, where gaps exist, and whether access is effectively open.
- **Reliability**: failure rates, downtime incidents, mean time to repair.
- **Continuity**: queue times, rationing frequency, scarcity mode frequency/duration.
- **Equity/fairness proxies**: differential service outcomes across groups/regions (bounded and privacy-safe).
- **User experience signals**: complaint volumes, appeal outcomes, incident recurrence.

### Measurement discipline
- Versioned definitions and calculation rules.
- Baselines and time-series continuity with restatements when definitions change.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A dashboard minimum MUST exist and be public at appropriate aggregation levels.
- If measurement collapses (blackout, unverifiable metrics), decision powers MUST be constrained (hard-stop logic).

---

## B03.CH04.P05.S04 — Transparency: maintenance log, project changelog, incident reporting

Maintenance governance must be legible to outsiders to prevent façade commons and silent decay.

### Required transparency artifacts
- **Maintenance log**: planned vs executed work, deferrals, backlog changes, criticality tags.
- **Project changelog**: scope changes, cost revisions, delays, vendor changes, and rationale.
- **Incident reporting**: outages, service failures, root causes, mitigation, and recurrence prevention.

### Decision log linkage
- Every major maintenance deferral or reprioritisation must be logged with: reason, duration, expected impact, and replan date.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Logs MUST be timely, versioned, and accessible for audit and contestation.
- Missing logs or systematic opacity MUST trigger escalation and potential governance intervention.

## B03.CH04.P05.S05 — Sanctions for maintenance neglect: intervention, re-governance, recovery plan

Neglect must have consequences; otherwise the commons will drift back into scarcity and toll substitutes.

### Neglect indicators (examples)
- Persistent backlog growth beyond threshold bands.
- Repeated scarcity activations attributable to deferred maintenance.
- Rising outage rates with no corrective trend.
- Evidence of diversion of ring-fenced funds or hidden gating measures.

### Sanction and correction ladder (minimum)
1. **Corrective order**: mandatory maintenance plan, ring-fence enforcement, timeline and milestones.
2. **Enhanced audit mode**: forensic access to procurement, vendor performance, deferral rationales.
3. **Governance intervention**: reconstitution/rotation of responsible bodies; procurement reset if captured.
4. **Operational takeover (time-bounded)**: temporary administration to restore service minima.
5. **Recovery plan and verification**: measured ΔG restoration; post-mortem and prevention controls.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A recovery plan MUST be mandatory after neglect classification, with measurable milestones and public reporting.
- If recovery does not occur within defined timelines, escalation MUST continue automatically (non-suspensive defaults).
