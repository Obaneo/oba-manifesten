# B03.CH04.P02 — Constitutive principles: boundary rules, openness, and non-discrimination

This paragraph defines the **constitutive principles** of commons-democracy: the minimal rails that must hold regardless of
political majorities, operational pressure, or crisis narratives. The commons is only a commons if **access is default**, if
boundary rules are **explicit and rule-bound**, and if exclusion cannot be smuggled in through pricing, profiling, or informal
gatekeeping. These principles also impose governance duties: **transparency**, **auditability**, and **contestability** are not
nice-to-haves but the mechanism that keeps the commons from turning into a private toll regime or a closed administrative club.

---

## B03.CH04.P02.S01 — Access as default: open access within explicit boundary rules

Openness is the default posture of a commons. Access restrictions are permitted only where the commons must protect continuity,
safety, or capacity—and only via **explicit boundary rules** that are published, stable, and contestable.

### Boundary rules (required properties)
- **Explicit**: written rules; no “case-by-case discretion” as the default access regime.
- **Non-discretionary baseline**: routine access does not require approvals, sponsorship, or insider channels.
- **Capacity-aware**: scarcity is handled via a defined protocol (queues, prioritisation criteria, service minima).
- **Non-discriminatory**: protected classes and arbitrary proxies are prohibited; rule design must resist indirect exclusion.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST publish boundary rules and the scarcity protocol, including criteria, evidence inputs, and decision rights.
- Any access restriction not grounded in a published boundary rule MUST be treated as **non-conform** and must trigger correction.

---

## B03.CH04.P02.S02 — Ban on private toll gates: paid priority, bundling, lock-in fees

A commons cannot permit private toll gates to re-enter through “premium” lanes, bundling, membership monetisation, or switching
penalties. These mechanisms recreate extraction even when the service is nominally public.

### Prohibited toll-gate patterns (non-exhaustive)
- **Paid priority**: faster access or higher quality in exchange for payment or side contracts.
- **Bundling/tying**: forcing users to buy adjacent services to obtain core access.
- **Lock-in fees**: penalties for switching providers, moving jurisdictions, or exiting a vendor ecosystem.
- **Take-rate insertion**: intermediaries that skim a percentage from access or usage.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any paid-priority or bundling mechanism MUST be classified as a **toll gate** unless explicitly permitted under a narrowly
  defined, auditable cost-recovery exception with sunset and rollback.
- Contracts and procurement MUST prohibit vendor designs that implement toll gates via UI, APIs, or operational throttling.

## B03.CH04.P02.S03 — Quality and service minima: essentials continuity as the norm

Commons are defined not only by access rules but by **continuity**: essential services must meet minimum reliability and coverage
levels. Without service minima, “open access” becomes symbolic and scarcity becomes a covert exclusion tool.

### Service-minimum discipline
- **Essentials continuity**: defined minimum uptime/coverage/availability for the essential function.
- **Maintenance-first**: ΔG is protected via ring-fencing, backlog metrics, and lifecycle obligations.
- **Scarcity is exceptional**: scarcity protocols are for surge windows, not the normal operating state.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST define service minima and publish metrics that show compliance over time (including incident logs).
- Persistent violation of service minima MUST trigger escalation (enhanced audit, governance intervention, or requalification).

## B03.CH04.P02.S04 — Transparency: definitions, indicators, decision logs, auditability

Commons governance must be inspectable. Transparency is required so that boundary rules, scarcity decisions, and maintenance claims
can be challenged with evidence rather than political rhetoric.

### Transparency minimum
- **Definitions**: the canonical terms used (by reference to the Kernel Spec / Book 1).
- **Indicators**: access, quality, maintenance backlog, scarcity events, exception counts.
- **Decision logs**: who decided what, on what basis, under which rule version.
- **Auditability**: records and computation rules sufficient to reproduce key determinations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST publish a transparency pack: definition-set references, indicator schema, and decision-log format.
- Black-box allocation (no inspectable rules or logs) MUST be treated as **order-incompatible** and triggers a hard stop.

---

## B03.CH04.P02.S05 — Contestability: objection, appeal, and independent review

Democratic commons require **contestability**: users and affected parties must be able to challenge decisions, access denials,
scarcity priorities, and governance actions. Contestability is the safeguard against capture and quiet discrimination.

### Contestability rails
- **Objection**: a defined pathway to challenge a decision with evidence.
- **Appeal**: escalation to an independent body (tribunal/auditor/ombuds), not the original decision-maker.
- **Independent review**: audit access to logs and evidence; power to order correction and remediation.
- **Non-sabotage defaults**: procedures cannot be used to stall continuity or to normalise violations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST provide a contestability interface with timelines, evidence rights, and remedies.
- If users cannot access reasons, rules, and evidence sufficient to contest a decision, the decision regime MUST be treated as
  **non-conform** until corrected.
