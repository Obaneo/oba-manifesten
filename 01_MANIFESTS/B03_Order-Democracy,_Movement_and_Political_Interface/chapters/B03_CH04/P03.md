# B03.CH04.P03 — Governance architecture: roles, representation, and powers

This paragraph specifies the **governance architecture** of commons-democracy: the minimal role model, representation options,
and power boundaries required to keep commons governance **open, contestable, and non-capturable**. The core design principle is
that commons institutions must be able to **decide and operate**, but must not be able to **self-validate** without independent
audit and appeal. Governance is therefore defined as a set of **separated functions** with explicit mandates, terms, and conflict
rails—so “commons” cannot be used as a label for opaque discretionary control.

---

## B03.CH04.P03.S01 — Roles: users, managers, operators, auditors, overseers

Commons governance is defined by **distinct roles** that map to distinct responsibilities and evidence rights.

### Canonical roles
- **Users (members / beneficiaries)**: holders of access rights and the primary source of quality feedback; may initiate complaints.
- **Managers (governance executives)**: translate mandate into policies/rules within rails; propose budgets and investment plans.
- **Operators (service delivery)**: execute day-to-day provision, maintenance, incident response; produce operational logs.
- **Auditors (independent verification)**: test compliance, reproduce indicators, inspect logs, and publish findings.
- **Overseers (policy/mandate guardians)**: approve constitutive changes, handle exceptions, trigger escalations and interventions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST define these roles (or functional equivalents) explicitly and document their responsibilities, permissions,
  and evidence access.
- Any design where the same body is simultaneously **operator + auditor** or **manager + final reviewer** MUST be treated as
  non-conform unless separation is enforced via hard institutional rails.

---

## B03.CH04.P03.S02 — Representation models: membership, election, sortition, stakeholder seats

Representation determines who may speak and decide for users and stewards. OBA does not prescribe one model, but it constrains
models to remain **non-capturable**, **inclusive**, and **contestable**.

### Permitted representation models (examples)
- **Membership model**: members elect representatives; strong fit for local commons with clear user base.
- **Election model**: elected board with explicit mandate; must include strong conflict-of-interest rails.
- **Sortition / citizen panels**: lottery selection for constitutive and evaluative tasks; reduces professional capture.
- **Stakeholder seats**: designated seats for user cohorts, operators, auditors, and public interest; requires influence caps.

### Design rails (required)
- **Anti-dominance**: caps on influence (vote weights, seat limits, quorum rules) to prevent high-capacity capture.
- **Inclusion**: accessibility measures (time, language, digital access, support) to prevent representation bias.
- **Transparency**: membership/eligibility rules and selection methods must be published and stable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any representation model MUST publish its eligibility rules, selection method, and influence caps.
- If representation outcomes show persistent bias toward high-capacity groups without corrective rails, governance MUST be treated
  as drift-prone and require redesign.

## B03.CH04.P03.S03 — Powers: access, fees/rules (within rails), investments, sanctions

Commons governance must have real powers to function, but these powers must be constrained to prevent covert toll gates and
discretionary exclusion.

### Core power domains
- **Access governance**: boundary rules, scarcity protocol activation, prioritisation criteria (within constitutive rails).
- **Fees and rules**: cost-recovery rules where permitted; explicit ban on paid priority and toll-gate patterns.
- **Investment authority**: maintenance and capex planning, project selection, lifecycle budgeting, ring-fencing.
- **Sanctioning**: sanctions for misuse, abuse, and maintenance neglect; must be rule-bound and appealable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST publish a power map: which body can do what, under which rule version, and with which evidence and logging.
- Any power to deny access, set scarcity priorities, or impose sanctions MUST be accompanied by objection/appeal rights and
  auditable decision logs.

---

## B03.CH04.P03.S04 — Separation of functions: execution vs oversight vs audit

Separation prevents the commons from turning into an unreviewable administrative monopoly.

### Separation rails (minimum)
- **Execution (operators)** cannot set their own compliance criteria or close their own incident findings.
- **Oversight (overseers)** cannot perform audits of itself; oversight decisions must be logged and reviewable.
- **Audit (auditors)** must have independence, forensic access within scope, and publish findings without veto by managers/operators.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Operator and manager roles MUST be structurally separated from audit roles (incompatible roles).
- If audit access is blocked, computation rules are closed, or logs are missing, the system MUST fail closed: governance outputs
  become non-conform until auditability is restored.

---

## B03.CH04.P03.S05 — Mandate and terms: rotation, recall, conflict-of-interest regimes

Governance legitimacy requires explicit mandates, bounded terms, and enforceable integrity rails.

### Mandate and term discipline
- **Mandate definition**: scope, objectives, and rails (access default, service minima, no toll gates, transparency minimum).
- **Term limits and rotation**: key positions rotate to prevent capture and long-run dependency networks.
- **Recall and intervention**: mechanisms for removal under breach, and external intervention under capture indicators.
- **Conflict-of-interest regime**: disclosure, recusals, cooling-off, vendor relationship prohibitions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Governance bodies MUST publish mandates, term lengths, rotation rules, and recall triggers.
- Any material conflict (vendor ties, revolving-door patterns, undisclosed funding) MUST trigger recusals at minimum and may trigger
  escalation (enhanced audit, reconstitution, procurement reset) depending on severity.
