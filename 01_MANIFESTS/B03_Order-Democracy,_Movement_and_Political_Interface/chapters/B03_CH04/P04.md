# B03.CH04.P04 — Access and allocation: rules for scarcity and prioritisation

This paragraph defines the **access and allocation regime** for commons services under conditions of scarcity. The objective is
to ensure that rationing, prioritisation, and any access constraints remain **rule-bound**, **auditable**, and **contestable**,
and do not reintroduce toll extraction through covert pricing, paid priority, bundling, or profiling. Scarcity is treated as an
exceptional operating mode with explicit triggers, published criteria, decision logs, and a default preference for restoring
capacity (ΔG) rather than normalising exclusion.

---

## B03.CH04.P04.S01 — Scarcity protocol: when and how prioritisation is allowed

Prioritisation is permitted only when scarcity is real, measurable, and declared through a formal protocol.

### Scarcity triggers (examples)
- Capacity shortfall against published service minima (coverage/uptime/queue thresholds).
- Emergency events or surge conditions validated by operational metrics.
- Maintenance outages within pre-registered schedules.

### Protocol steps (minimum)
1. **Declare scarcity mode** with timestamp, scope (service/region), and metric trigger.
2. **Activate prioritisation rule set** (pre-published) and publish the rule version in the decision log.
3. **Apply time-bounded measures** with review cadence and exit criteria.
4. **Publish post-hoc review**: impact, complaints, appeal outcomes, and corrective actions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Scarcity mode MUST be explicit (no implicit rationing).
- Any scarcity activation MUST include: trigger metrics, scope, duration, rule version, and exit criteria.
- Scarcity measures MUST be reversible and MUST not silently become a permanent regime.

---

## B03.CH04.P04.S02 — Prioritisation criteria: essentials, vulnerability, continuity, public value

When scarcity is declared, prioritisation must be based on criteria that preserve **essentials continuity** and protect those who
carry disproportionate harm from exclusion.

### Permitted criteria (ordered families)
- **Essentials continuity**: access necessary to maintain baseline life-world stability (service minima).
- **Vulnerability**: risk of severe harm; inability to substitute; disability/health/safety dependence where applicable.
- **Continuity and prevention**: preventing cascading failures (e.g., critical infrastructure dependencies).
- **Public value**: allocations that prevent systemic harm or preserve shared functioning (bounded and defined).

### Forbidden criteria (examples)
- Ability-to-pay, paid priority, membership tiers that function as exclusion, or discretionary favoritism.
- Political loyalty tests, ideological filters, or any non-relevant identity proxy.
- “Convenience optimisation” if it displaces essentials continuity and vulnerability protection.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST publish the prioritisation criteria, including tie-break rules and an explanation of how criteria map to
  essentials continuity and fairness.
- The criteria MUST be implementable without creating a new profiling/surveillance regime (see privacy rails).

---

## B03.CH04.P04.S03 — Prohibited allocation forms: black-box scoring, profiles without appeal

Allocation decisions must remain contestable and reproducible. This excludes opaque or unappealable decision systems.

### Prohibited forms (non-exhaustive)
- **Black-box scoring**: any allocation where the decision rule cannot be inspected, explained, and reproduced.
- **Profile-based allocation without appeal**: decisions derived from risk scores or inferred attributes where the subject cannot
  see the basis and challenge it.
- **Vendor-owned decision engines** that prevent independent replication or restrict audit access.
- **Shadow criteria**: undocumented weights, hidden “business rules”, or manual overrides without logging.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any allocation mechanism MUST have an auditable rule description, version pinning, and decision logging.
- Individuals MUST have an objection/appeal route with access to the decision basis (within privacy constraints).
- If auditability or contestability fails, scarcity allocation MUST fail closed (suspend or revert to simpler rules).

## B03.CH04.P04.S04 — Price/fee discipline: cost recovery versus toll; take-rate caps where relevant

Fees may exist only as **cost recovery** within explicit rails, and must not become a mechanism of exclusion or extraction.

### Fee rails (minimum)
- **Cost recovery only**: fees must map to transparent cost categories and cannot include monopoly/toll margins.
- **No paid priority**: premium lanes, queue-jumping fees, bundling for access, or “membership fast tracks” are prohibited.
- **Affordability protection**: rules for waivers, caps, or subsidies consistent with R/B lower bounds.
- **Take-rate caps** (where intermediated): if a platform/operator mediates access, its take-rate must be capped and auditable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Fee schedules MUST be public, versioned, and linked to cost evidence.
- Any fee change MUST include a logged rationale and impact estimate on access and R/B.
- If fees correlate with exclusion outcomes in scarcity periods, the regime MUST be reviewed and corrected.

## B03.CH04.P04.S05 — Data and privacy: minimalism, logging, access to personal data

Allocation requires evidence, but evidence collection must be constrained to prevent authoritarian drift and mission creep.

### Privacy rails (minimum)
- **Data minimalism**: collect only what is necessary to apply published criteria.
- **Purpose limitation**: data collected for allocation cannot be repurposed for unrelated enforcement or profiling.
- **Logging without surveillance**: decisions must be logged, but personal data exposure should be minimised through aggregation,
  pseudonymisation, and strict access control.
- **Access rights**: subjects must be able to access records relevant to decisions affecting them, and correct errors.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The commons MUST publish a data inventory for allocation (fields, purpose, retention, access roles).
- Any prohibited linkage (profiling/surveillance expansion) MUST trigger hard-stop review and rollback.
- Logs MUST be retained long enough to enable appeals, audits, and incident reviews, with privacy-preserving controls.
