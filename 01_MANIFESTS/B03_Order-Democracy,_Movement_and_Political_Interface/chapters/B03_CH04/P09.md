# B03.CH04.P09 — Interoperability and scale: commons in networks and chains

This paragraph defines how commons-democracy scales beyond a single local service into **federations**, **multi-commons networks**,
and **cross-border interfaces** without reintroducing toll gates, black boxes, or fragmentation. Scaling is not primarily a growth
problem; it is an **interoperability** problem: access rules, identity, logging, and service levels must remain compatible enough
for users to move, for audits to replicate, and for allocation/maintenance decisions to be contestable across layers. Networked
commons also reintroduce classic extraction opportunities via **supply chains** (platform–supplier–financier), where tolls can be
embedded upstream, financed into long-term lock-ins, or disguised as “interoperability services”. The design target is therefore
a commons network that preserves (i) **local autonomy within rails**, (ii) **standardised interfaces for portability and audit**,
and (iii) **shock continuity** via surge capacity and mutual assistance.

---

## B03.CH04.P09.S01 — Federation: local commons → regional/national layer

Federation is the scaling form that preserves locality while preventing fragmentation.

### Federation model (minimum)
- **Local layer**: operational governance of access, prioritisation, maintenance execution, complaint handling.
- **Regional/national layer**: standards, interoperability enforcement, pooled surge capacity, cross-commons arbitration where needed.
- **Delegated powers only**: higher layers may set interface standards and minimum guarantees but may not redefine local boundary
  rules beyond the constitutive rails.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Federation MUST define which decisions are local vs federated, and MUST prevent role mixing (operator ≠ auditor ≠ standard-setter).
- Federated bodies MUST be subject to the same anti-capture and contestability rules as local commons (disclosure, rotation, audit).

---

## B03.CH04.P09.S02 — Interoperable standards: access, identity, logging, service levels

Interoperability is achieved through **stable, versioned standards** that allow portability and replication.

### Standard interface minimum
- **Access interface**: boundary-rule schema, scarcity protocol markers, eligibility categories (without profiling creep).
- **Identity / membership**: portable membership proofs and role claims (user, steward, operator) with data-minimalism.
- **Logging**: common log fields for access decisions, prioritisation outcomes, exceptions, sanctions, and appeals (version-pinned).
- **Service levels**: comparable metrics (uptime, coverage, queue time, reliability), plus maintenance backlog and incident taxonomy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Interoperability standards MUST be open, documented, versioned, and implementable by multiple parties (no vendor-owned standards).
- Any change to standards MUST include changelog + backward compatibility strategy or explicit deprecation with migration paths.

## B03.CH04.P09.S03 — Chain attribution: platform–supplier–financier in a commons context

Networked commons are exposed to extraction through chained actors who can embed tolls outside the commons boundary.

### Chain attribution targets
- **Platform layer**: distribution, ranking, admission control, interface gating.
- **Supplier layer**: provisioning, maintenance contractors, infrastructure providers.
- **Financier layer**: debt covenants, collateralisation attempts, revenue pledges, refinancing lock-ins.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons governance MUST maintain a chain map for critical services and MUST apply look-through: toll/extraction upstream counts as
  toll pressure on the commons even if not charged at the user interface.
- Contracts MUST prohibit side letters, exclusivity, and hidden pricing stacks that recreate tolls; audit access MUST extend to
  suppliers where they determine outcomes.

---

## B03.CH04.P09.S04 — Cross-border commons: minimum compatibility conditions

Cross-border operation requires a compatibility floor so that users retain access and decisions remain auditable.

### Minimum cross-border compatibility set
- **Definition pinning**: shared reference to the relevant definition set + version for access/logging/service metrics.
- **Portability**: membership recognition or bridging (explicit mapping rules), and clear requalification processes.
- **Audit cooperation**: mutual assistance for replication and incident investigation (within privacy/proportionality rails).
- **Dispute path**: defined arbitration/judicial interface for cross-jurisdiction conflicts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Cross-border bridges MUST be explicit variants/extensions if they introduce local parameter differences; they MUST NOT redefine
  constitutive terms while keeping the commons label.
- Where legal regimes conflict, the bridge MUST publish the deviation and classify compatibility status (fail-closed if unknown).

---

## B03.CH04.P09.S05 — Shock stress: surge planning and continuity under crisis

Scaling must include a shock regime that preserves essentials continuity without emergency drift into gating or toll reintroduction.

### Shock-stress design (minimum)
- **Surge capacity**: pooled resources, mutual aid protocols, reserve staffing, and pre-negotiated procurement with exitability.
- **Continuity minima**: service-level floors for essentials, prioritisation rules for scarcity, and transparent queue disciplines.
- **Emergency changes**: temporary parameter shifts only, with sunset/rollback and post-crisis restatements for metrics.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Crisis protocols MUST not enable paid priority, profiling expansions, or black-box allocation; any emergency deviation MUST be
  time-bounded and logged with rationale.
- After a shock, governance MUST publish an incident report with (i) decisions taken, (ii) exceptions granted, (iii) outcomes vs
  minima, and (iv) rollback actions, including any restatements needed for comparability.
