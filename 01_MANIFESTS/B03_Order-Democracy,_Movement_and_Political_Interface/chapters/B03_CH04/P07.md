# B03.CH04.P07 — Conflict resolution: internal disputes, external claims, and escalation

This paragraph defines the conflict-resolution architecture for commons-democracy as a **contestable**, **evidence-based**
process that protects openness, service minima, and maintenance discipline under stress. Commons governance inevitably produces
conflicts: between users and operators, between prioritisation choices under scarcity, and between commons rules and external
claimants (vendors, financiers, adjacent authorities). The resolution system must therefore be designed to prevent three failure
modes: **capture through procedure**, **black-box allocation**, and **de facto privatisation via gating**. Conflict resolution is
not a “community feel-good layer”; it is an order-critical mechanism that preserves the non-negotiables (open access within
boundary rules, auditability, and anti-toll substitution) while keeping decision-making operable.

---

## B03.CH04.P07.S01 — Internal conflicts: users vs managers; prioritisation; access disputes

Commons conflicts arise from structural tensions, not “bad behaviour”, and must be handled as governance cases.

### Common dispute classes (minimum)
- **Access disputes**: boundary-rule interpretation, alleged discrimination, gating attempts, paid priority claims.
- **Prioritisation disputes**: scarcity protocol activation, queueing criteria, continuity trade-offs.
- **Quality/maintenance disputes**: service minima breaches, backlog growth, neglect allegations.
- **Sanctions disputes**: penalties for misuse, suspension decisions, membership rights conflicts.
- **Representation disputes**: legitimacy of seats, rotation/recall conflicts, conflict-of-interest allegations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every dispute MUST map to an explicit rule surface: boundary rules, scarcity protocol, service minima, or governance mandate.
- Disputes MUST be triaged by order risk (e.g., potential gating/black-box/capture) and routed accordingly.

---

## B03.CH04.P07.S02 — Procedure: complaint → mediation → arbitration/tribunal → judicial review

The procedure must make decisions contestable without allowing sabotage-by-process.

### Minimum ladder
1. **Complaint intake**: structured submission, classification, and time-stamped registration.
2. **Mediation**: fast path for correctable misunderstandings and low-order-risk conflicts.
3. **Arbitration/tribunal**: binding decision by an independent body with defined competence and evidentiary standards.
4. **Judicial review**: legality/proportionality/evidence review, with protection against emergency “exception creep”.

### Procedural safeguards
- **Time limits** per stage (no indefinite limbo).
- **Non-suspensive defaults** where delaying tactics could create order regression (e.g., keep access open unless a narrow safety
  exception applies).
- **Standing rules**: who may file, who may represent, and how collective harms are handled.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A published procedure MUST exist with clear timelines, competence boundaries, and appeal conditions.
- No actor (operator, vendor, or governing body) MAY create ad-hoc procedure variants outside the published rails.

---

## B03.CH04.P07.S03 — Evidence: logs, measurement data, decision rationale, audit trail

Conflict resolution must be evidence-bearing; “trust us” is non-conform.

### Evidence minimum set
- **Decision logs**: who decided, on which rule basis, with which inputs, and which alternatives were rejected.
- **Measurement data**: service metrics, scarcity indicators, maintenance logs, allocation records.
- **Access records**: gate events, queue states, prioritisation triggers, sanction events (privacy-minimised but auditable).
- **Audit trail**: versioned rule set, definition set references, and change history (changelog).

### Evidence access
- Parties MUST have access to the evidence needed to contest decisions (with privacy protections via aggregation/redaction where
  necessary, but no blackouts).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If required logs or measurement data are missing, the decision MUST be treated as procedurally defective and escalated.
- Rule changes triggered by disputes MUST be traceable (version pinning + rationale).

## B03.CH04.P07.S04 — Escalation: hard stops for black-box allocation or gating innovations

Some conflict signatures indicate order breach and require immediate escalation.

### Hard-stop triggers (minimum)
- **Black-box allocation**: prioritisation driven by non-explainable scoring or undisclosed computation rules.
- **Gating innovation**: paid priority, bundling, membership gating, “premium lanes”, or vendor-controlled queues.
- **Selective enforcement patterns**: sanctions applied asymmetrically without evidence or appeal access.
- **Vendor lock-in drift**: refusal to provide logs, interfaces, or portability needed for contestability.

### Escalation actions (minimum)
- **Suspend** the disputed mechanism (allocation rule, gating feature, scoring system) pending independent review.
- **Enhanced audit mode**: forensic access, parallel replication, and cross-review.
- **Governance intervention**: temporary reconstitution/rotation when capture indicators appear.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Hard-stop triggers MUST be codified and MUST activate automatically or by a defined authority within fixed timelines.
- During escalation, access defaults MUST remain order-preserving (open access within boundary rules).

---

## B03.CH04.P07.S05 — Remedy: restoration, compensation, rule change, governance intervention

Remedies must neutralise harm and prevent recurrence, not merely “close the ticket”.

### Remedy classes (minimum)
- **Restoration**: reinstate access, reverse improper sanctions, undo wrongful gating/prioritisation outcomes where feasible.
- **Compensation**: when service denial or wrongful exclusion caused measurable harm (within defined compensation rails).
- **Rule correction**: clarify boundary rules, adjust scarcity protocol parameters (within rails), improve service minima.
- **Operational fixes**: maintenance surge, staffing changes, process redesign, queue management improvements.
- **Governance intervention**: rotation/recall, conflict-of-interest sanctions, procurement reset, vendor termination for non-compliance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every remedy MUST include a prevention component: which rule, control, or logging requirement changes to stop repeat failure.
- Remedies MUST be recorded in public-facing reporting (at least in aggregated form) and linked to versioned rule updates.
