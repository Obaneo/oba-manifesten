# B03.CH12.P03 — Governance metrics (minimum set): what is continuously measured

This paragraph defines the **minimum continuous metric suite** required by the democratic consistency test. These metrics do not measure “policy
success” in a sectoral sense; they measure **governance integrity**: whether OBA rails remain enforceable, auditable, contestable, and resistant to
capture. The suite is intentionally compact and structural: it targets the main drift channels—procurement/vendor dependence, revolving doors,
exceptions, enforcement softness, epistemic corruption (black-boxes/definition churn), representational bias, and legitimacy degradation.

A key design constraint is that metrics must be **machine-runnable**: each metric has a definition, data lineage, update cadence, threshold bands, and
linkage to actions (enhanced audit, rollback, hard stop, governance reset). Metrics are version-pinned to definition sets; changes require changelog and
(where needed) restatements.

## Core principles

* **Measure the drift channels, not the slogans**: the suite targets mechanisms that enable capture and regression.
* **Continuity over completeness**: stable measurement beats large dashboards that invite gaming and blackout risk.
* **Comparability is mandatory**: metrics are pinned to definition versions; semantic drift triggers restatements.
* **Metrics imply obligations**: each metric must have threshold-triggered procedures; otherwise measurement is decorative.

## B03.CH12.P03.S01 — Capture metrics: vendor concentration, revolving-door incidents, lobby-disclosure compliance

Capture metrics measure whether governance is becoming dependent on concentrated suppliers, conflicted personnel flows, or undisclosed influence.

### Minimum capture metric set

* **Vendor concentration**: share of critical tooling/contracts held by top suppliers; single-source rate; dependency on proprietary models.
* **Exitability score**: presence of portability, escrow, open interfaces, second implementation, and audit rights in critical contracts.
* **Revolving-door incidents**: role switches between regulator/operator/auditor/vendor/lobbyist within cooling-off windows; COI breaches.
* **Lobby disclosure compliance**: registration completeness, meeting disclosure rates, budget/objective disclosure, violation counts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Capture metrics **MUST** include both levels (concentration) and incidents (breaches/violations); one without the other is blind.
* Where vendor concentration rises or exitability falls, the response ladder must trigger procurement review and second-source requirements.
* Revolving-door data must be public in aggregate and case-linked where legally permissible; hidden conflicts void legitimacy.

## B03.CH12.P03.S02 — Enforcement metric: sanction rate, enforcement latency, recidivism rate, exception stacking

Enforcement metrics detect the common drift pattern: rules remain on paper but stop biting. These metrics differentiate a functioning order regime from a
symbolic one.

### Minimum enforcement metric set

* **Sanction rate**: proportion of verified breaches that result in sanctions; broken down by domain and severity.
* **Enforcement latency**: time from detection to action; long latency is a de facto exemption.
* **Recidivism rate**: repeat breaches by the same actors after enforcement; indicates softness or gaming.
* **Exception stacking**: frequency and concentration of stacked exceptions; renewal counts; selectivity patterns.
* **Selectivity indicators**: enforcement distribution across actor classes (size, sector, region) to detect captured softness.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* A declining sanction rate with stable breach detection is a primary drift signal and must trigger enhanced audit.
* Exception stacking must be tracked explicitly; “temporary” renewals without rollback are treated as structural erosion.
* If enforcement data cannot be produced with lineage and sampling clarity, the system is in measurement failure and must fail-closed on affected domains.

## B03.CH12.P03.S03 — Epistemic metric: reproducibility, audit coverage, model/rule churn, restatement frequency

Epistemic metrics measure whether governance remains contestable in knowledge terms. OBA requires that key computations and classifications can be
replicated and that audit coverage is sufficient to detect manipulation.

### Minimum epistemic metric set

* **Reproducibility status**: proportion of critical outputs reproducible by independent runs (data snapshots + version pinning + code/model access).
* **Audit coverage**: fraction of relevant population/transactions within audit scope; sampling rates; blind spots.
* **Model/rule churn**: frequency and magnitude of changes to rules/models/thresholds; high churn is a semantic drift surface.
* **Restatement frequency and lag**: how often restatements are required and how long they take; lag indicates laundering capacity.
* **Audit refusal patterns**: denied access incidents by agencies or vendors; repeat refusals are black-box drift.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If reproducibility declines, the system must escalate and may hard-stop on black-box outputs; governance-by-assertion is non-compatible.
* High model/rule churn without bridging and restatements is semantic drift by design and triggers definition freeze review.
* Audit coverage must be reported with uncertainty; hidden blind spots invalidate confidence claims.

## B03.CH12.P03.S04 — Representation metric: participation bias, inclusion, churn in commons representation

Representation metrics ensure that participation mechanisms do not become dominated by high-capacity groups or captured intermediaries. OBA treats
representation integrity as measurable because representational drift becomes legitimacy collapse.

### Minimum representation metric set

* **Participation bias**: demographic/sectoral skew of participants relative to affected population (time, resources, digital access).
* **Inclusion/access measures**: accessibility provisions, response rates, language/time support, compensation for participation costs.
* **Commons representation churn**: rotation compliance, tenure distribution, concentration of roles, reappointment patterns.
* **Intermediary dominance**: share of representation held by organised bodies; COI and funding linkages.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Participation bias above thresholds triggers redesign of channels (sampling, compensation, accessibility) rather than rhetorical defence.
* Commons governance must show rotation and anti-concentration; stable “permanent representatives” are capture risks.
* If representation metrics are unavailable, legitimacy claims must be bounded and procedural reviews triggered.

## B03.CH12.P03.S05 — Legitimacy metric: trust/acceptance (proxies), complaint volumes, appeal outcomes, procedural fairness

Legitimacy metrics capture whether the public experiences governance as fair, correctable, and non-arbitrary. Because “trust” is easily manipulated,
OBA uses **proxies** that link legitimacy to procedure and outcomes of contestation.

### Minimum legitimacy metric set

* **Complaint volumes**: rates and themes; spikes can indicate service failures, enforcement selectivity, or procedural breakdown.
* **Appeal outcomes**: uphold/reverse rates; reversal patterns indicate systemic error or biased enforcement.
* **Procedural fairness indicators**: timelines met, evidence access granted, decision explanation quality, accessibility compliance.
* **Acceptance proxies**: compliance rates, participation retention, service uptake, and contestation stability (not propaganda measures).
* **Incident credibility**: frequency of incident reports and post-mortems; low incidence can mean suppression, not success.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Legitimacy metrics must be interpreted with governance metrics: low complaints plus low contestability is a red flag (suppression).
* High reversal rates require system correction (rule clarification, training, tooling fixes), not punishment of appellants.
* If fairness indicators degrade during crisis, the system is in legitimacy drift and must trigger re-authorisation or governance reset pathways.
