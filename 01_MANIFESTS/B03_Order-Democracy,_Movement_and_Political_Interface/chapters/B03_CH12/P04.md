# B03.CH12.P04 — Early-warning indicators: drift signatures and thresholds

This paragraph defines the **early-warning indicator set** for the democratic consistency test: the minimum drift signatures that signal when OBA
governance is moving toward capture, regression, or authoritarian overreach *before* collapse becomes visible in macro outcomes. The purpose is to make
intervention **predictive rather than reactive**. Each indicator must be measurable, version-pinned, and bound to thresholds that trigger the response
ladder in P05.

Early-warning indicators are not “opinions.” They are **structural signatures**: changes in definitions, exceptions, reproducibility, procurement, audit,
and enforcement that historically precede loss of contestability. The test treats them as *governance health telemetry*.

## Core principles

* **Drift has signatures**: governance degradation is detectable in artefacts (changelogs, registers, audit access, sanction patterns).
* **Thresholds prevent discretion**: without thresholds, warnings become politically negotiable and therefore capturable.
* **Co-occurrence matters**: single anomalies may be noise; correlated indicators imply structural drift.
* **Restatements are integrity**: if semantics change, comparability must be repaired or decisions fail-closed.

## B03.CH12.P04.S01 — Semantic drift: definition shifting without changelog, metric-bridging gaps

Semantic drift is the corruption of meaning: governance appears stable while the definitions of “success,” “extraction,” “cap compliance,” or “commons
access” shift. This is the most silent and powerful drift channel because it changes evaluation itself.

### Semantic drift indicators (minimum)

* **Unlogged definition changes**: updates to definitions/tests without a changelog entry and version ID.
* **Goalpost movement**: thresholds altered without impact notes or governance re-authorisation.
* **Metric-bridging gaps**: definition changes occur but historical series are not bridged or restated; trend claims remain uncorrected.
* **Proxy substitution**: replacement of core metrics with easier proxies that change the meaning (e.g., “affordability” redefined to hide toll growth).
* **Terminology laundering**: “OBA” language applied while constitutive rails (caps/auditability) are weakened.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Any semantic drift indicator breach triggers **definition freeze review** and requires publication of changelog + bridging/restatement plan.
* If bridging/restatement is not feasible, decisions relying on trend comparability must fail-closed or be bounded by uncertainty.
* Repeated goalpost movement is itself a capture signal and triggers enhanced audit mode.

## B03.CH12.P04.S02 — Exception drift: exception ratio↑, renewals↑, selectivity↑, sunset failure

Exception drift is how rails are hollowed out while remaining “on paper.” It is the canonical mechanism of OBA-washing: the label stays, applicability
shrinks.

### Exception drift indicators (minimum)

* **Exception ratio↑**: share of cases/flows subject to exceptions increases beyond bands.
* **Renewals↑**: repeated renewal cycles become normal; “temporary” becomes structural.
* **Selectivity↑**: exceptions concentrate in high-power actors/sectors/regions; fairness declines.
* **Sunset failure**: expirations are not enforced; exceptions persist past deadlines.
* **Stacking**: multiple overlapping exceptions create a parallel regime (functional fork without naming).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Threshold breaches in exception ratio/renewals trigger escalation and may trigger hard stops if caps/auditability are materially eroded.
* Every exception must have logged impact and beneficiary scope; missing metadata invalidates compatibility claims.
* Persistent sunset failure forces re-authorisation or incompatibility classification (P07), not “extended transition.”

## B03.CH12.P04.S03 — Black-box drift: declining contestability, closed computation rules, audit refusal patterns

Black-box drift occurs when governance outputs become unchallengeable: rules, models, and decision logic become closed, proprietary, or operationally
opaque. This breaks the democratic form even if outcomes look good.

### Black-box drift indicators (minimum)

* **Contestability↓**: fewer decisions can be appealed effectively; evidence access declines.
* **Closed computation rules**: model cards absent; code/rule logic inaccessible; version pinning missing.
* **Reproducibility↓**: independent runs cannot replicate outputs; data snapshots unavailable.
* **Audit refusal patterns**: repeated denial of access by agencies or vendors; “confidentiality” becomes routine shield.
* **Tool monopoly**: single vendor/system becomes authoritative without second implementation or shadow computation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Material black-box drift triggers **hard stops** for affected domains: operate only on auditable subset until restored.
* Closed computation in coercive or allocative decisions is non-compatible unless contestability and replication are reinstated.
* Repeated audit refusal is treated as governance integrity breach requiring procurement reset and/or reconstitution.

## B03.CH12.P04.S04 — Capture drift: vendor lock-in signals, single-source procurement, audit interference

Capture drift is the shift of effective control from public governance to concentrated private or insider interests through procurement, staffing, and
influence infrastructure. It often co-occurs with exception drift and black-box drift.

### Capture drift indicators (minimum)

* **Vendor lock-in↑**: portability absent, escrow absent, interface closure, bundled suites, inability to exit.
* **Single-source procurement↑**: rising sole-source awards, emergency contracting without re-tender, concentration in critical components.
* **Audit interference**: scope narrowing, budget cuts targeted at audit, staff churn patterns, blocked access to procurement artifacts.
* **Revolving-door signals**: cooling-off breaches, staff moving to vendors/lobbying firms, consultancy stacking around policy drafting.
* **Influence opacity**: declining lobby disclosure compliance; untraceable funding streams.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Capture drift threshold breaches trigger enhanced audit mode and procurement integrity review; in critical domains, trigger governance reset actions.
* If audit interference is detected, independent oversight must be reconstituted with protected access rights.
* Vendor lock-in that prevents rollback is treated as a structural failure requiring immediate exitability remediation or suspension of dependence.

## B03.CH12.P04.S05 — Authoritarian drift: surveillance expansion, selective enforcement, emergency normalisation

Authoritarian drift occurs when conflict or crisis is used to expand coercive control, reduce contestation, and normalise emergency powers. In OBA this is
a formal incompatibility vector because it destroys the democratic form.

### Authoritarian drift indicators (minimum)

* **Surveillance expansion**: new profiling, cross-domain data linkage, risk scoring for enforcement without replicable criteria and appeals.
* **Selective enforcement**: enforcement patterns become group/region selective; scapegoating narratives correlate with sanctions.
* **Emergency normalisation**: emergency measures extend beyond sunset; renewals become routine; ex post audits absent.
* **Procedural compression**: consultation and appeal routes weakened; evidence access reduced; non-suspensive defaults abused to silence contestation.
* **Suppression signals**: low complaint volumes paired with low contestability and high coercive measures.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Authoritarian drift indicators trigger immediate safety-rail review (P02.S05 + CH11.P07) and may trigger hard stops and re-authorisation requirements.
* Emergency measures must be time-bounded with ex post audit and rollback capability; otherwise they count as constitutive deviation.
* If surveillance expansion substitutes for governance evidence, the regime fails contestability and cannot be labeled OBA-compatible.
