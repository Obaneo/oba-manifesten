# B03.CH12.P05 — Response ladder: what follows from signals (automatic and political)

This paragraph defines the **response ladder** of the democratic consistency test: the binding sequence of interventions that follows from early-warning
signals and threshold breaches. The ladder exists because detection without compelled response is a governance placebo. OBA therefore requires that a
subset of responses be **automatic** (triggered by thresholds) and that the remainder be **political but constrained** (requiring explicit
re-authorisation, publication, and compatibility labeling).

The ladder is designed to (i) restore auditability and contestability first, (ii) remove the benefits of drift through rollback, (iii) fail-closed under
black-box or measurement failure, (iv) reset institutions and tooling where capture is implicated, and (v) require new mandate when deviation is
constitutive. In short: **detect → verify → correct → stop if ungovernable → reset → re-authorise or fork.**

## Core principles

* **Signals imply obligations**: threshold breaches must produce actions, not debate.
* **Automatic where capturable**: the more a response could be politically suppressed, the more it must be automatic.
* **Restore contestability before expansion**: governance cannot “fix” problems with closed computation or discretion.
* **Rollback is integrity**: you cannot claim recovery while keeping captured outcomes.
* **Constitutive changes require mandate**: if rails change, democracy must explicitly re-authorise or relabel.

## B03.CH12.P05.S01 — Trigger → enhanced audit mode: scope expansion, forensic access, parallel review

Enhanced audit mode is the first response to warning signals: it increases verification capacity and reduces uncertainty. It is triggered by threshold
breaches in early-warning indicators (exceptions, sanctions, reproducibility, procurement concentration, audit interference).

### Enhanced audit mode actions (minimum)

* **Scope expansion**: increased sampling, broader domain coverage, inclusion of high-risk actor classes and exception-heavy segments.
* **Forensic access**: mandatory access to procurement artifacts, model/rule versions, decision logs, and data lineage (including vendor-held artifacts).
* **Parallel review**: audit-of-auditors; independent teams run shadow audits and reproduce computations.
* **Integrity freeze (limited)**: pause non-essential rule/model changes during the audit window to prevent moving targets.
* **Time-bounded reporting**: interim findings published with uncertainty bands and next steps.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Enhanced audit mode **MUST** have fixed deadlines; “open-ended investigation” is delay-as-veto.
* If forensic access is denied, escalation must proceed to hard stops and procurement reset pathways.
* Audit expansion must be logged with version IDs and case IDs; otherwise results cannot feed the consistency test.

## B03.CH12.P05.S02 — Correction → requalification/rollback: reverse decisions, contracts, tooling, and exceptions

When enhanced audit confirms drift, the next step is correction: not only identifying the problem but reversing it. Correction is where the test proves it
is not symbolic.

### Correction actions (minimum)

* **Requalification**: reclassify misclassified flows/positions (E vs productive), reapply caps, correct commons boundary rule application.
* **Rollback of exceptions**: revoke improper exemptions, halt renewals, restore baseline applicability, enforce sunset discipline.
* **Rollback of decisions**: re-run allocations and enforcement actions under corrected definitions and procedures.
* **Contract/tooling reversal**: terminate or re-tender captured procurement; reinstate open standards, replication rights, and exitability.
* **Restatement initiation**: if semantics/tooling changed, publish restatement plan and correct time series.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Confirmed drift **MUST** produce rollback review; keeping captured outcomes is non-compatible.
* Corrections must be published with linkage to incident reports, exception register updates, and changelog entries.
* If correction is blocked by technical lock-in, that blockage is itself a failure requiring governance reset (S04) and/or suspension (S03).

## B03.CH12.P05.S03 — Hard stops: suspend allocations/decisions under black-box governance or measurement uncertainty

Hard stops are fail-closed interventions that suspend high-stakes decisions when governability conditions are not met. They prevent unaccountable power
from continuing under cover of urgency.

### Hard-stop triggers (minimum)

* **Black-box governance**: key decisions cannot be replicated or contested due to closed computation or proprietary models.
* **Measurement failure**: missing logs, missing version pinning, dashboard blackout beyond allowed window, broken data lineage.
* **Audit obstruction**: denial of forensic access by agencies or vendors.
* **Cap erosion emergency**: exception explosion or sunset failure that materially breaks cap binding.
* **Authoritarian drift signals**: surveillance expansion and selective enforcement during crisis without strict bounds.

### Hard-stop actions (required)

* **Suspend** affected allocations/decisions (ΔM routing, major procurement awards, high-impact enforcement classifications).
* **Fail-closed to auditable subset**: continue only the parts that are verifiably governed under pinned definitions and open artefacts.
* **Publish stop notice**: scope, rationale, exit criteria, timelines, and interim continuity plan.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If the system cannot explain or reproduce its outputs, it **MUST NOT** continue making binding decisions in that domain.
* Hard stops must be rule-triggered; discretionary refusal to stop is a capture surface.
* “Emergency” does not waive hard stops; emergencies are exactly when hard stops are most necessary.

## B03.CH12.P05.S04 — Governance reset: rotation, reconstitution, second-source tooling, procurement reset

Governance reset is required when drift is not isolated but institutional: capture, interference, vendor dependence, or enforcement softness persists.
Reset changes structure and capability, not just policy text.

### Governance reset actions (minimum)

* **Rotation and reconstitution**: replace leadership where conflicts exist; rotate teams; implement cross-review and audit-of-auditors.
* **Second-source tooling**: deploy parallel implementations and shadow computation for critical metrics and classifications.
* **Procurement reset**: re-tender critical contracts with open standards, audit rights, portability, escrow, and exitability; reduce bundling.
* **Integrity firewalling**: strengthen separation of functions; protect audit budgets and access rights; enforce COI rules and cooling-off.
* **Capacity rebuild**: restore enforcement capacity and reduce latency; improve training and rule literacy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If vendor lock-in prevents auditability or rollback, procurement reset is mandatory, even if it causes short-term disruption.
* Reconstitution must be time-bounded with explicit exit criteria; otherwise reset becomes permanent emergency governance.
* Reset actions must publish measurable objectives (sanction rate recovery, latency reduction, reproducibility restoration) with deadlines.

## B03.CH12.P05.S05 — Re-authorisation: new mandate for a constitutive deviation (incl. fork classification)

When deviation is **constitutive**—it changes rails rather than parameters—democracy must explicitly re-authorise it. This is the boundary between normal
politics and regime change. Where re-authorisation fails or where constitutive deviations persist by stealth, fork classification and naming rules apply.

### Re-authorisation triggers (minimum)

* Structural cap erosion (exception regime becomes baseline).
* Permanent black-box allocation regimes or closed computation rules.
* Persistent emergency measures beyond sunset windows.
* Definition-set changes that break comparability without restatement and bridging.
* Prohibited linkage proposals normalised as governance infrastructure.

### Re-authorisation requirements (minimum)

* **Explicit delta statement**: what rail changes and why; impact analysis on E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM and democratic non-negotiables.
* **Special procedure**: heightened thresholds (supermajority/dual-key) appropriate to constitutive change.
* **Sunset + rollback plan**: even authorised deviations must have exitability and review.
* **Compatibility outcome**: if rails deviate, classify under B03.CH07/B03.CH12.P07 (compatible/partial/incompatible) with mandatory labeling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Constitutive deviation without re-authorisation is illegitimate and must be rolled back or formally labeled incompatible.
* If re-authorisation fails, continuation is prohibited; persistence implies fork and must trigger naming separation.
* No regime may retain “OBA” labeling while structurally breaking non-negotiables; that is washing by definition.
