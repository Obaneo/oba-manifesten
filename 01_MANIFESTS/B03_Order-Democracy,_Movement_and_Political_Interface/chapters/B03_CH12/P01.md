# B03.CH12.P01 — Purpose and scope: democratic consistency test as the capstone (pass/fail + escalation)

This paragraph defines the **democratic consistency test** as the capstone of Book 3: a **governance integrity test** that produces a **pass/fail**
status and binds the system to a pre-committed **escalation ladder** when drift signals appear. The test exists because order-democracy is not stable
by default: even under good intentions, institutions drift under incentives, emergencies, and capture pressure. OBA therefore requires a single,
integrated mechanism that continuously evaluates whether governance remains **contestable, auditable, definition-stable, and exception-bounded** while
preserving the constitutive rails (caps, commons discipline, ΔM auditability, and safety rails against overreach).

The test does not replace democratic choice on policy content; it constrains governance to remain *democratic in form* under OBA: measurable,
replicable, correctable, and resistant to laundering.

## Core principles

* **Consistency is measurable**: democratic integrity is treated as a measurable property with defined inputs and thresholds.
* **Pass/fail is necessary**: without a binary outcome, drift becomes negotiable and therefore capturable.
* **Escalation must be automatic where possible**: discretionary enforcement of drift signals is itself a capture surface.
* **Machine-readability is discipline**: fixed schemas, version IDs, and reproducibility requirements prevent semantic drift.

## B03.CH12.P01.S01 — Function: detection of drift, capture, and regression within OBA governance

The function of the test is to detect when OBA governance is no longer functioning as OBA, even if rhetoric remains. It treats drift as an endogenous
failure mode and focuses on governability indicators that precede visible collapse.

### What it detects (minimum)

* **Semantic drift**: definition shifting without changelog, missing bridging/restatements, proxy substitution that changes meaning.
* **Exception drift**: rising exception ratio, renewal normalisation, selective applicability, “temporary” holes becoming permanent.
* **Capture drift**: declining sanction rates, rising enforcement latency, audit obstruction, revolving-door patterns, procurement lock-in.
* **Black-box drift**: closed computation rules, unreplicable models, refusal of audit access, monopoly measurement.
* **Authoritarian drift**: surveillance expansion, prohibited linkages, emergency normalisation, selective enforcement justified as “order.”

### Functional outputs

* **Status label** (pass/fail or graded compatibility state defined in P07).
* **Triggered actions**: enhanced audit, rollback/requalification, hard stops, governance reset, re-authorisation/fork mapping.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* The test **MUST** be run continuously (scheduled cadence) and on-demand after incidents.
* Failure states **MUST** trigger the response ladder; publishing failure without action is non-compliance.
* If the test cannot be run due to missing data or reproducibility failure, the system is in **fail-closed** state by definition.

## B03.CH12.P01.S02 — Delimitation: the test assesses governance, not sector-by-sector policy content

The democratic consistency test evaluates the **integrity of governance**, not whether a specific sector policy is “good,” “left,” or “right.” It is a
meta-layer: it asks whether policy can be contested, audited, corrected, and rolled back under stable definitions.

### In scope

* Procedural integrity: separation of functions, contestability, transparency minimum, exception discipline, anti-overreach rails.
* Governance metrics: capture, enforcement, epistemic integrity, representation bias, legitimacy proxies.
* Thresholds and escalation: when intervention becomes mandatory.

### Out of scope

* Substantive policy preferences: exact cap numbers, specific tax designs, detailed sector programme choices (except where they break rails).
* Ideological adjudication or party recommendations.
* “Optimality” claims beyond auditable governance criteria.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Results **MUST** not be presented as an endorsement of a party or ideology; they are integrity findings.
* Sector policies may vary widely; the test only constrains them to remain within democratic, auditable form.
* Attempts to rewrite sector policy evaluation into the consistency test are semantic drift and must be rejected.

## B03.CH12.P01.S03 — Relations: inputs from Book 1/2/4 (measurability, transition, information order)

The test is a capstone because it integrates prerequisites from other books: it needs canonical definitions (Book 1), shock and transition logic (Book
2), and information-order constraints (Book 4) to evaluate governance integrity correctly.

### Interface with Book 1 (definitions and measurability)

* Uses canonical definitions of **extraction (E)**, caps, commons, ΔM, auditability and definition-set versioning.
* Ensures that metrics refer to pinned semantics, preventing “metric success by redefinition.”

### Interface with Book 2 (transition and shocks)

* Defines emergency deviation logic: what may be temporarily adjusted, under what sunsets, and with what rollback and re-authorisation triggers.
* Incorporates timing asymmetry and R/B stabilisation needs into escalation design (avoid backlash-driven rail breaks).

### Interface with Book 4 (information order and reproducibility)

* Requires reproducibility, model/rule transparency, replication tooling, and anti-black-box constraints.
* Prevents technocracy-by-assertion and model monopoly from replacing democratic contestability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Test runs **MUST** cite definition-set IDs and rule/model versions; otherwise results are uninterpretable.
* Emergency exceptions **MUST** be evaluated under Book 2 discipline (sunset/rollback/restatement); otherwise partial compatibility cannot be claimed.
* If information-order constraints are violated (closed computation), test outcome defaults toward fail/hard stop.

## B03.CH12.P01.S04 — Machine-readability: fixed inputs, outputs, version IDs, and thresholds

Machine-readability is not convenience; it is governance discipline. The test must be runnable by independent parties, comparable across time, and
resistant to semantic laundering.

### Machine-readable components (minimum)

* **Input schema**: fixed metric definitions, data sources, update cadence, aggregation rules, and uncertainty handling.
* **Version IDs**: definition-set version, computation/tooling version, and protocol version for thresholds and escalation mapping.
* **Threshold registry**: published thresholds with change-control (no silent adjustments), including rationale and impact notes.
* **Output schema**: status label, metric scorecard, breached thresholds, triggered actions, and required publication artefacts.
* **Audit hooks**: replication artefacts, sample IDs, and links to incident reports, exception registers, changelogs, and restatements.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* All runs **MUST** output machine-readable artefacts and human-readable summaries linked to the same IDs.
* Threshold changes **MUST** be versioned and restated where trend comparability is affected; “moving the goalposts” is a drift signal.
* If outputs are produced without reproducible inputs (missing logs/model versions), the run is invalid and triggers fail-closed operation until repaired.
