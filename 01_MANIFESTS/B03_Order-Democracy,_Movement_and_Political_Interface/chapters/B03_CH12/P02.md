# B03.CH12.P02 — Non-negotiables in democratic form: minimum procedural requirements

This paragraph defines the **non-negotiables** required for OBA governance to remain **democratic in form**. These are not ideological preferences;
they are **procedural invariants** without which OBA rails cannot be claimed to be governable, contestable, or legitimate. The democratic consistency
test (B03.CH12) is built on these invariants as pass/fail prerequisites: if any non-negotiable is structurally violated, the regime cannot be labeled
OBA-compatible regardless of sector outcomes or rhetorical alignment.

The non-negotiables are: **separation of functions**, **contestability**, **transparency minimum**, **exception discipline**, and **anti-overreach**
rails (privacy/control). Together they prevent the three canonical failure modes: (i) capture through role mixing and procurement, (ii) drift through
definitions/exceptions, and (iii) authoritarian overreach through surveillance and emergency normalisation.

## Core principles

* **Non-negotiables bind power**: they prevent decision authority from becoming self-justifying.
* **Democracy requires reversibility**: contestability + rollback are structural, not optional.
* **Transparency is semantic stability**: definition sets and changelogs prevent governance by relabeling.
* **Exceptions are the stealth channel**: without discipline, the rails are hollowed out while labels remain.
* **Overreach is a drift pathway**: surveillance and linkage creep are shortcuts that destroy contestability.

## B03.CH12.P02.S01 — Separation of functions: execution, audit, oversight, judicial review (no role mixing)

Democratic consistency requires **functional separation**: the actor who executes cannot be the actor who defines success, audits itself, or adjudicates
its own disputes. Role mixing is a capture surface and an epistemic corruption vector.

### Separation requirements (minimum)

* **Execution**: implements policy and operates commons/allocations under defined rules.
* **Audit**: verifies compliance and measurement integrity with independent authority and access rights.
* **Oversight**: sets parameters, approves exceptions under strict rules, and triggers escalation; cannot run operations.
* **Judicial review**: independent adjudication of legality, proportionality, and procedural correctness; must have access to evidence.

### Prohibited mixes (minimum)

* Operator auditing its own outputs as authoritative.
* Procurement/vendor management controlling audit scope or measurement rules.
* Oversight bodies controlling the data pipeline in ways that block replication.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Each function **MUST** have distinct authority, staffing, and budgets with conflict-of-interest regimes.
* Any detected role mixing in rail-adjacent domains triggers failure status until reconstitution is completed.
* “Internal audit” is acceptable only as a second-line control; it cannot substitute for independent audit.

## B03.CH12.P02.S02 — Contestability: objection/appeal, access to evidence, non-sabotage defaults

Democracy in OBA is not “voice” alone; it is **contestability**: the ability to challenge decisions on evidence and procedure, and to obtain effective
remedies. Contestability must be paired with non-sabotage defaults so that appeals do not become de facto vetoes.

### Contestability requirements (minimum)

* **Objection/appeal channels**: clear routing, timelines, and authority to order remedies (reclassification, rollback, sanctions).
* **Evidence access**: access to decision logs, definition sets, audit trails, and replication artefacts in privacy-safe form.
* **Remedies**: capability to reverse decisions, unwind exceptions, correct series (restatements), and re-run allocations.
* **Non-sabotage defaults**: appeals are non-suspensive by default; time-boxed stays only under defined harm thresholds.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* If affected parties cannot access evidence or cannot obtain remedies, the regime fails contestability.
* If appeals systematically halt enforcement (appeal-as-veto), the regime fails governability and triggers escalation redesign.
* Contestability must apply to **definitions and tooling** as well as to single decisions; otherwise semantic capture persists.

## B03.CH12.P02.S03 — Transparency minimum: definition sets, registers, dashboards, changelog discipline

Transparency in OBA is not general openness; it is **semantic and operational transparency**: the minimum artefacts required to make governance
measurable and replicable across time.

### Transparency minimum (required artefacts)

* **Definition sets**: canonical terms and tests with version IDs and applicability statements.
* **Registers**: exception registry, procurement/vendor registry, audit findings register, decision registry (where appropriate).
* **Dashboards**: core indicators (exceptions, sanctions, reproducibility status, allocation summaries, ΔG backlogs) with uncertainty bands.
* **Changelog discipline**: any change to definitions, thresholds, tooling, or protocols logged with rationale and impact notes.
* **Restatements/bridges**: where changes affect comparability, provide corrected series and mapping notes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Missing definition sets or missing changelogs is an automatic failure: the system cannot be evaluated.
* Dashboards without lineage (definition IDs, data sources, computation versions) are cosmetic and non-compliant.
* If transparency is reduced under crisis (“temporary secrecy”), measurement blackouts trigger hard-stop rules and failure status.

## B03.CH12.P02.S04 — Exception discipline: sunsets, rollback, non-stackability, impact registration

Exceptions are the canonical drift channel: they preserve labels while eroding rails. OBA therefore treats exception discipline as a constitutive
invariant.

### Exception discipline requirements (minimum)

* **Sunsets**: every exception has an expiration date; renewals require affirmative re-justification.
* **Non-stackability**: exceptions cannot be layered to create a parallel regime; stacking triggers review.
* **Impact registration**: each exception logs beneficiaries, scope, affected rails, and measured impacts on E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM.
* **Rollback readiness**: exceptions must be reversible; contracts/tooling must allow return to baseline.
* **Renewal limits**: repeated renewals trigger re-authorisation or classification as constitutive deviation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Exception ratio thresholds are binding: persistent breach implies incompatibility unless rolled back and repaired.
* Exceptions without logged impact and beneficiaries are prohibited.
* If exceptions expand during political conflict and sanctions decline, treat as capture and trigger enhanced audit mode.

## B03.CH12.P02.S05 — Anti-overreach: prohibited linkages and proportionality rails (privacy/control)

OBA governance cannot be democratic if it solves contestation with control expansion. Anti-overreach rails prevent surveillance creep, profiling, and
cross-system linkage that converts governability into coercive discretion.

### Anti-overreach requirements (minimum)

* **Prohibited linkages**: cross-domain data fusion for enforcement (welfare-policing-immigration-health-education-banking-platform identity) unless narrowly authorised.
* **Data minimalism**: collect only what is necessary for the specific auditable decision; no function creep.
* **Model contestability**: no coercive action based solely on unreplicable model outputs; explanations and appeals must be possible.
* **Proportionality**: the least intrusive mechanism compatible with order goals must be chosen; emergency powers must be time-bounded.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

* Surveillance expansion during conflict is a drift signal and triggers failure review unless strictly time-bounded and independently audited.
* Any enforcement regime relying on black-box profiling fails contestability and is non-compatible.
* Emergency measures must have sunset + ex post audit + rollback capability; otherwise “temporary” becomes structural overreach.
