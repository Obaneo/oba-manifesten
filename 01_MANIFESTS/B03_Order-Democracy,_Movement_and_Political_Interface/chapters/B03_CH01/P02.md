# B03.CH01.P02 — Constitutive invariants (non-negotiables) as democratic boundary conditions

This paragraph defines the **constitutive invariants** (“non-negotiables”) as **democratic boundary conditions**: constraints that
ordinary politics may not dilute without **formal fork/compatibility consequences**. Their purpose is not to replace democratic
choice, but to ensure that democratic choice operates **within auditable rails** that prevent capture, semantic drift, and the
reconstruction of a toll economy under new labels. These invariants are therefore treated as **binding constraints** for mandate
design, institutional architecture, and public status claims (“OBA-conform”, “OBA-compatible”, etc.).

---

## B03.CH01.P02.S01 — E-reduction as a political boundary condition (no toll economy)

The political baseline is that governance must produce a **measurable reduction of extraction** and prevent the re-emergence of
toll positions that monetize access, chokepoints, or claim control.

### Core principles
- **No toll economy**: democratic legitimacy is incompatible with structurally expanding toll positions under policy cover.
- **Function-over-label**: classification follows function and cashflow/control structure, not the stated label.
- **Anti-relabeling**: “fees”, “platform services”, “risk management”, “public-private partnerships”, etc. cannot be used to mask E.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any policy or institutional design claiming OBA status MUST demonstrate **measurable E-reduction** under the canonical tests.
- If E cannot be measured or audited for a domain (measurement blackout), governance MUST fail closed (hard-stop conditions apply).

---

## B03.CH01.P02.S02 — Caps as limits on claim and power positions (binding)

Caps are treated as **binding rails** that prevent durable domination through claim concentration, veto power, or stackable
exemptions.

### Core principles
- **Bindingness**: caps are not guidelines; they are enforceable constraints with defined remedies.
- **No exception stacking**: repeated “temporary” carve-outs are treated as erosion.
- **No threshold tricks**: structural avoidance via threshold shifts, netting, or reclassification is a compatibility risk.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Cap rules MUST be designed with **anti-avoidance** and **non-stackability** as first-class constraints.
- Persistent cap erosion (by carve-outs or enforcement collapse) MUST trigger escalation and may force reclassification as
non-compatible.

---

## B03.CH01.P02.S03 — Commons as a public substitute: access, maintenance, boundary rules

Commons are a **public substitute for toll positions**: they provide access and continuity through governance that is open,
auditable, and disciplined about upkeep.

### Core principles
- **Access by default**: open access within explicit boundary rules, not discretionary gating.
- **Maintenance discipline**: stewardship is not optional; neglect is a governance failure mode.
- **Boundary rules**: limits exist, but must be explicit, contestable, and non-discriminatory in function.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any commons claim MUST include (a) explicit boundary rules, (b) maintenance obligations and metrics, and (c) contestable
allocation/access procedures.
- “Commons” labeling without open access + maintenance + contestability MUST be treated as façade risk and escalated.

---

## B03.CH01.P02.S04 — R/B as the legitimacy axis: carrying capacity, lower bounds, distribution profiles

R/B defines legitimacy as a **bounded distributive regime**: essentials protection, carrying-capacity discipline, and enforceable
lower bounds that stabilise coalition support and prevent regression.

### Core principles
- **Lower bounds**: minimum protections are not optional policy taste; they are order-stability requirements.
- **Distribution profiles**: legitimacy must be evaluated across the distribution (not just averages).
- **Carrying capacity**: commitments must remain feasible under system constraints and maintenance obligations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Policies claiming OBA status MUST specify R/B-related **lower bounds** and show their distributional impact using the canonical
measurement frame.
- If distributional effects cannot be evidenced or audited, claims MUST be treated as non-conform until the evidence gap is closed.

---

## B03.CH01.P02.S05 — ΔM as the allocation interface: channel distribution, contestability, no black boxes

ΔM is the political allocation interface: it governs **how monetary/allocative space is routed** across channels, and must remain
auditable and contestable.

### Core principles
- **Channel visibility**: allocations must be traceable to explicit channels and rules.
- **Contestability**: affected parties must have defined paths to object, appeal, and trigger audit.
- **No black boxes**: opaque scoring/allocations undermine democratic legitimacy and enable capture.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- ΔM allocation MUST be implemented with **public channel definitions**, logging, and reproducible decision rules.
- Any black-box allocation mechanism that cannot be independently audited MUST trigger a hard stop or formal incompatibility
classification (depending on scope and persistence).
