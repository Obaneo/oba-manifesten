# B03.CH01.P04 — Democratic risks as the core problem of Book 3

This paragraph defines the **democratic risk model** that motivates Book 3. In OBA, the central political problem is not choosing
between “left” and “right”, but preventing **order-regression** under democratic pressure: capture, façade adoption, authoritarian
overreach, coalition breakdown, and legitimacy collapse. These risks are treated as **structural failure modes** that must be
designed against through mandates, institutions, transparency, contestability, and disciplined exception handling.

---

## B03.CH01.P04.S01 — Capture: lobbying, revolving doors, media and expert stacks

Capture is the systematic alignment of policy, enforcement, and allocation with **concentrated interests**, often without explicit
legal violation. It occurs via stacked influence channels that shape definitions, exceptions, audits, procurement, and narrative.

### Typical capture vectors (non-exhaustive)
- **Lobbying pressure**: agenda-setting, amendment insertion, technical “fixes” with distributive effects.
- **Revolving doors**: role switching across regulator/executor/auditor/vendor/lobby positions.
- **Media stacks**: controlled framing, selective amplification, definitional drift via repetition.
- **Expert stacks**: advisory ecosystems dependent on incumbents, vendors, or funders.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- OBA-governed systems MUST maintain anti-capture rails: disclosure, cooling-off rules, audit independence, procurement exitability,
  and public registries for exceptions and sanctions.
- If capture indicators breach defined thresholds, escalation MUST trigger: enhanced audits, rollback capability checks, and
  governance intervention.

---

## B03.CH01.P04.S02 — Populist façade: rhetorical adoption without enforcement

Façade adoption occurs when OBA language is used to claim legitimacy while the implementation keeps extraction channels intact and
avoids binding constraints.

### Signature pattern
- Strong rhetoric about caps/commons/fairness with **weak measurement**, **thin auditability**, and **exception-heavy** practice.
- Selective emphasis (e.g., redistribution claims) without **E-reduction** or without **binding caps**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No rhetorical claim counts without published **tests, evidence, and audit paths**.
- If enforcement mechanisms, audit trails, or contestability are absent, the policy MUST be treated as **non-conform** even if the
  narrative matches OBA vocabulary.

---

## B03.CH01.P04.S03 — Authoritarian drift: overreach, surveillance, selective caps

Authoritarian drift is the attempt to enforce “order” by expanding coercive control rather than by improving measurability,
contestability, and binding constraints. It often appears as “emergency governance” that becomes permanent.

### Signature pattern
- **Surveillance expansion** and profiling justified as anti-capture or anti-fraud.
- **Selective enforcement**: caps and scrutiny applied asymmetrically to opponents, minorities, or low-power groups.
- **Rule opacity**: closed models, secret thresholds, or black-box allocation justified as “necessary efficiency”.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- OBA governance MUST enforce prohibited linkage rules (privacy/control rails) and require proportionality, logging, and appeal.
- Any selective-cap regime or black-box governance MUST trigger a hard stop unless independently auditable and contestable.

---

## B03.CH01.P04.S04 — Coalition erosion: sequencing conflicts, swing groups, scapegoating

Coalition erosion is the political breakdown that results when costs are front-loaded, benefits are delayed, or burdens concentrate
on identifiable groups, producing backlash that opens the door to regression.

### Typical mechanisms
- **Sequencing conflict**: fast losses vs slow gains; implementation tempo misaligned with household/business buffers.
- **Swing group backlash**: middle percentiles and locally concentrated losers become veto players.
- **Scapegoating**: blame-shifting onto minorities or out-groups to preserve extraction channels.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Policies claiming OBA-conform status MUST disclose sequencing, burden distribution, and buffer design (R/B rails + essentials
  continuity) sufficient to avoid predictable backlash.
- If coalition stability metrics (complaints, compliance refusal, exception pressure) degrade beyond thresholds, escalation MUST be
  initiated (mandate review, pacing adjustments within rails, not invariant breakage).

---

## B03.CH01.P04.S05 — Legitimacy deficit: unclear mandates and non-contestable allocation

Legitimacy collapses when citizens and affected parties cannot see what is decided, by whom, on which basis, and cannot contest it.
In OBA terms, the key legitimacy failure is **non-contestable allocation**—especially through ΔM channels and commons governance.

### Signature pattern
- Ambiguous mandates (“technical necessity”) with weak public definition sets.
- Allocation decisions made through opaque criteria, vendor tools, or discretionary committees without appeal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Mandates MUST be translated into testable order criteria (E/caps/commons/ΔG/RB/ΔM) with public scorecards.
- Allocation MUST be contestable: decision logs, evidence access, appeal paths, and independent audits are non-negotiable.
