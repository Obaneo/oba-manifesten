# B03.CH01.P05 — Pre-commitment: democratic self-binding and institutional checks

This paragraph defines **pre-commitment** as the democratic mechanism by which OBA prevents predictable drift under pressure.
Pre-commitment is not “de-politicisation”; it is **self-binding**: the polity chooses, in advance, which rails it will not cross,
which signals trigger escalation, and which institutions must act when thresholds are breached. The function is to keep OBA
governance **fail-closed**, resistant to capture, and stable under shocks—without removing contestability.

---

## B03.CH01.P05.S01 — Who triggers what: escalation ladder and timelines (link to Book 2/1)

Pre-commitment requires a **trigger architecture**: defined events, responsible actors, and deadlines. Drift is managed by **rules of
activation**, not by ad-hoc discretion.

### Trigger objects (non-exhaustive)
- **Metric triggers**: exception ratio↑, sanction rate↓, reproducibility↓, ΔG degradation, E-stagnation/↑.
- **Process triggers**: missing decision logs, missing definition-set version pins, audit refusal patterns.
- **Shock triggers**: crisis windows that expand discretionary power (mandatory sunset + rollback readiness).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every critical governance domain (audit, ΔM allocation, commons access, exceptions) MUST have an explicit **escalation ladder**
  with named trigger owners, deadlines, and handover steps.
- Timelines MUST be fixed ex ante and MUST reference the relevant transition/enforcement interfaces in Book 2/1 (e.g., rollback
  readiness, sequencing constraints) rather than improvising during incidents.

---

## B03.CH01.P05.S02 — Transparency minimum: registers, scorecards, changelog discipline

Pre-commitment is only enforceable if the system publishes a **minimum transparency surface** that makes drift visible and
replicable.

### Transparency minimum (non-exhaustive)
- **Registers**: exceptions, sanctions, procurement/vendor concentration (where relevant), governance role assignments.
- **Scorecards**: per pillar (E, caps, commons/ΔG, R/B, ΔM) with thresholds and trends.
- **Changelog discipline**: definition-set versioning, parameter changes, restatements/bridging, and rationale.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- OBA-conform governance MUST publish and maintain the transparency minimum as a condition of legitimacy.
- Any unlogged change to definitions, thresholds, or allocation rules MUST be treated as a drift incident and MUST trigger
  escalation (fail-closed).

## B03.CH01.P05.S03 — Judicial review and objections: contestability without paralysis

OBA requires **contestability** as a constitutive property, but contestability can be weaponised to stall enforcement. Pre-commitment
therefore specifies objection paths that remain real while preventing procedural sabotage.

### Design constraints
- **Access to evidence**: logs, decision rationale, and applicable definition-set versions must be obtainable.
- **Procedural proportionality**: review scope matches impact; fast-track for urgent continuity risks.
- **Anti-sabotage defaults**: where regression risk is high, objections do not automatically suspend action.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Governance MUST provide objection/appeal paths with defined time limits, evidentiary standards, and escalation handling.
- Where non-suspensive defaults apply, exceptions MUST be explicit, justified, and logged (no silent stays).

---

## B03.CH01.P05.S04 — Prohibited linkages: limits on profiling and order-incompatible surveillance

Pre-commitment includes **negative commitments**: routes the polity agrees not to take, even under stress. Central among these are
prohibitions on profiling/surveillance linkages that break democratic order and convert “order enforcement” into coercive control.

### Prohibited linkage pattern (non-exhaustive)
- Profiling that determines access/priority without auditability and appeal.
- Cross-domain data coupling that expands surveillance under a governance pretext.
- Closed scoring systems that substitute discretion for contestability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Prohibited linkages MUST be enumerated, testable, and enforced as hard stops.
- Any attempt to introduce profiling/surveillance as an enforcement shortcut MUST trigger mandatory review, with sunset/rollback
  requirements and independent audit.

---

## B03.CH01.P05.S05 — Exit criteria: when policy is formally “no longer OBA”

Pre-commitment is meaningless without **exit criteria**: conditions under which the “OBA” label must be withdrawn to prevent
semantic drift and façade governance.

### Exit criteria classes (non-exhaustive)
- **Invariant breach**: cap erosion, structural carve-outs, or exception normalisation.
- **Auditability breach**: closed computation rules, missing logs, or reproducibility collapse.
- **Contestability breach**: blocked appeals, evidence denial, or black-box allocation.
- **Overreach breach**: prohibited linkage violations or emergency normalisation without sunsets.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Exit criteria MUST be defined ex ante and MUST map to a formal label outcome (compatible / partial / incompatible) with mandatory
  relabeling.
- When exit criteria are met, continuation under the OBA label MUST be prohibited until rollback/re-authorisation restores
  compatibility.
