# B03.CH01.P01 — Purpose and scope of the kernel (summary layer; no redefinition)

This paragraph defines the **kernel** of Book 3 as a **summary-and-interface layer** that connects the political/governance
material of Book 3 to the **Kernel Spec** as the canonical source of definitions and calculation rules. The kernel exists to keep
Book 3 decision-oriented and **anti-drift**: it must enable political reasoning and institutional design **without creating a second
canon**, and without rephrasing that slowly mutates the meaning of terms or invariants.

---

## B03.CH01.P01.S01 — Kernel as the interface between the political book and the Kernel Spec

The kernel is the **interface** that lets political actors, institutions, and the public work with OBA’s canonical definitions
without rewriting them.

### Core principles
- **Canonical source**: the Kernel Spec is the place where definitions, objects, and calculation rules live.
- **Interface role**: Book 3 uses those definitions to design **mandates, governance, checks, and escalation**.
- **No shadow canon**: Book 3 must not introduce alternative definitions, aliases, or “political” reinterpretations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any statement that relies on OBA terms (E, caps, commons/ΔG, R/B, ΔM, auditability, contestability, etc.) MUST reference the
  Kernel Spec as the canonical meaning source and MUST NOT redefine those terms locally.
- If Book 3 needs a concept that is definitional (not merely explanatory), it MUST be added upstream to the Kernel Spec (or the
  canonical standards layer), not invented inside Book 3.

---

## B03.CH01.P01.S02 — Scope: which core claims are politically relevant

The kernel includes only the **politically relevant core claims**: the minimal set of OBA constraints that democratic governance
must preserve and operationalise.

### In-scope objects (minimum set)
- **Constitutive invariants (non-negotiables)** that ordinary politics may not erode (e.g., binding caps; measurable E-reduction;
  commons access and maintenance discipline; auditability and contestability).
- **Political criteria and test questions** that classify whether a proposal can be treated as OBA-conform at the governance level.
- **Governance implications**: minimum transparency, registers/scorecards, escalation ladders, and exit criteria for “no longer OBA”.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The kernel MUST remain a **decision-facing map** of invariant rails and governance requirements, not an economic theory chapter
  and not a transition-implementation manual.
- Any evaluative statement (“OBA-conform”, “compatible”, “non-compatible”, etc.) MUST be framed as a **testable claim** with
  reference to the canonical definitions and the applicable governance criteria.

---

## B03.CH01.P01.S03 — Out of scope: economic elaboration (Book 1) and transition toolkit (Book 2)

The kernel explicitly excludes content that belongs to other books, to prevent duplication and drift.

### Out-of-scope domains
- **Economic elaboration**: detailed mechanisms, sector mappings, quantitative derivations, and economic rule elaboration belong in
  **Book 1** (and the Kernel Spec where definitional).
- **Transition toolkit**: sequencing instruments, rollout mechanics, operational programmes, and implementation playbooks belong in
  **Book 2**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Book 3 MUST NOT restate or re-derive Book 1 economic logic or Book 2 transition mechanics as if they were part of the kernel.
- Where political reasoning needs those details, Book 3 MUST **link out** to the relevant canonical passages (Book 1/2 and/or Kernel
  Spec), rather than embedding paraphrases.

---

## B03.CH01.P01.S04 — Reading rule: refer instead of rephrasing (anti-drift)

To prevent semantic drift, the kernel enforces a reading rule: **reference over rephrase**.

### Reading rule
- Use **direct references** to canonical definitions and rules (Kernel Spec; Books 1–2 where applicable).
- Use **minimal, non-definitional phrasing** only to explain why a political decision matters, not what a term “really means”.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a term is definitional, it MUST be referenced (Kernel Spec ID/path/version where applicable) and MUST NOT be “clarified” by
  introducing new synonyms or alternative wording that changes scope.
- If a reference is missing, ambiguous, or points to non-canonical text, the corresponding claim MUST be treated as **uncertain**
  and therefore **non-conform** until corrected (fail-closed against drift).
