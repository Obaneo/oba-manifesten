# B03.CH02.P06 — Procedural architecture: decision-making with built-in anti-drift

This paragraph specifies the **procedural architecture** required to keep order-democracy stable over time. The objective is
anti-drift: ensuring that political decisions remain **traceable**, **contestable**, and **compatible with invariants**, even
under pressure, crises, or institutional capture attempts. The architecture therefore embeds: (i) separation of functions,
(ii) explicit version control for definitions and parameters, (iii) disciplined exception governance, (iv) hard stops that
fail-closed under measurement or audit breakdown, and (v) public reporting that makes the system legible and enforceable.

The key rule is procedural: **no decision is valid inside OBA unless it can be logged, reproduced, audited, and appealed within
defined rails**.

---

## B03.CH02.P06.S01 — Separation of functions: execution, audit, oversight, judicial review

OBA requires structural separation to prevent role-mixing from becoming an invisible capture channel. Execution, audit, oversight,
and adjudication must be institutionally distinct, with defined interfaces and escalation paths.

### Function split (minimum)
- **Execution**: implements decisions; operates registries; enforces rules; runs allocations.
- **Audit**: verifies indicators, logs, and rule compliance; has forensic access; can trigger hard stops.
- **Oversight**: sets frames, approves exceptions, triggers escalation; cannot execute day-to-day operations.
- **Judicial review**: resolves objections/appeals; enforces evidence standards and proportionality.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Incompatible roles MUST be structurally separated; conflicts of interest MUST be declared, logged, and enforced (recusals).
- Audit MUST be able to access evidence without depending on the execution layer’s discretionary cooperation.

---

## B03.CH02.P06.S02 — Version control: changelog, impact analysis, backward compatibility

Anti-drift requires treating governance artifacts like a canonical system: definitions, parameters, registries, and reporting rules
must be versioned and changes must be explainable.

### Versioning discipline (minimum)
- **Changelog**: what changed, why, who authorised it, and when it takes effect.
- **Impact analysis**: expected effects on E/caps/commons–ΔG/RB/ΔM and on measurability/audit load.
- **Backward compatibility**: where possible, provide bridging so historical series remain interpretable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any change to definitions, thresholds, or decision procedures MUST reference a **version ID** and MUST be published with impact notes.
- If backward compatibility is not possible, the system MUST issue **restatements/bridges** and mark discontinuities explicitly.

---

## B03.CH02.P06.S03 — Exception governance: register, sunsets, non-stackability

Exceptions are a primary drift vector. OBA therefore treats exceptions as governed objects: always visible, always time-bounded,
and never stackable into a shadow rule system.

### Exception controls (minimum)
- **Exception register**: scope, rationale, beneficiaries/affected groups, impact estimate, authoriser, duration.
- **Sunsets**: automatic expiry unless reauthorised under higher threshold.
- **Non-stackability**: exceptions cannot compound to hollow out caps, commons access, or auditability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- An exception that is not registered and time-bounded MUST be treated as invalid (fail-closed).
- Renewal frequency and exception ratio MUST be monitored as drift indicators; threshold triggers MUST exist for escalation.

---

## B03.CH02.P06.S04 — Hard stops: measurement uncertainty, black-box allocation, cap erosion

Hard stops are the system’s “circuit breakers”: when core observability or invariant integrity is threatened, the default response
is to stop or narrow scope until auditable control is restored.

### Hard-stop triggers (minimum)
- **Measurement uncertainty**: indicators cannot be produced reliably or reproduced.
- **Black-box allocation**: decisions cannot be inspected, replicated, or appealed.
- **Cap erosion**: carve-outs, threshold tricks, or exception stacking threatens binding caps.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Under a hard-stop trigger, affected allocations/decisions MUST be suspended or reduced to an auditable subset.
- Any override of a hard stop MUST require explicit emergency mandate + sunset + enhanced audit mode.

---

## B03.CH02.P06.S05 — Public reporting: definitions, indicators, restatements

Public reporting is not PR; it is an enforcement surface. Reporting must allow third parties to understand what the system claims,
how it measures, and whether it is drifting.

### Reporting minimum
- Published **definition sets** and current versions in force.
- Core **indicators** across E/caps/commons–ΔG/RB/ΔM and governance metrics (sanction rate, exception ratio, audit coverage).
- **Restatements** and bridges when corrections or version shifts occur.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Reports MUST be produced on a predictable schedule and MUST include enough metadata to reproduce key results.
- When errors are discovered, the system MUST publish restatements and clearly label superseded outputs (no silent revisions).
