# B03.CH02.P07 — Limits of democratic choice: forbidden routes and “no-go zones”

This paragraph defines the **limits of democratic choice** under OBA order-democracy: specific routes are **procedurally and
substantively forbidden** because they predictably destroy measurability, contestability, or invariant integrity. These are not
“policy preferences” but **no-go zones**: if crossed, the system is no longer operating as OBA-governance, regardless of electoral
support or rhetorical framing.

The rationale is structural: some mechanisms (surveillance coupling, privatisation lock-ins, black-box allocation, cap hollowing,
and semantic redefinition) function as **capture multipliers** and **drift accelerators**. OBA therefore pre-commits to excluding
them as admissible democratic options inside the order rails.

---

## B03.CH02.P07.S01 — Prohibited linkages: profiling/surveillance that breaks the order

Certain linkages between governance and personal profiling create an enforcement apparatus that is not contestable, not
proportional, and easily weaponised. Once established, it becomes a durable route to selective enforcement and political control.

### Prohibited patterns (minimum)
- Allocation or access decisions driven by **opaque profiling**, risk scores, or behavioural surveillance.
- Cross-system linkage of identifiers enabling **function creep** (administrative data → enforcement control).
- Surveillance expansions justified as “order protection” without strict proportionality and appealability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any governance mechanism that requires profiling/surveillance beyond minimal operational necessity MUST be recognised as
  **order-incompatible** and MUST be blocked (or hard-stopped) until removed.
- Where identity is necessary, the design MUST use **data minimisation**, **purpose limitation**, and **logged access** with
  independent oversight and appeal rights.

---

## B03.CH02.P07.S02 — Prohibited privatisations: commons lock-in and toll gates

Privatisation becomes order-breaking when it converts commons functionality into toll positions that cannot be exited, audited, or
contested, thereby reintroducing extraction under a new institutional wrapper.

### Prohibited patterns (minimum)
- **Exclusive concessions** that create inescapable toll gates for essentials access.
- Contractual or technical **lock-in** (proprietary interfaces, data captivity, non-portable identities).
- “Public–private” hybrids where private operators control access/priority while public bodies bear downside risk.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any transfer of commons operations MUST preserve **open access within boundary rules**, **exitability**, and **auditability**.
- If a privatisation proposal introduces paid priority, bundling, lock-in fees, or non-portable access, it MUST be classified as
  an **order breach** and rejected as incompatible.

---

## B03.CH02.P07.S03 — Prohibited black boxes: allocation without auditability

Allocation that cannot be inspected and reproduced is not democratically contestable. “Technocratic” allocation becomes a shadow
sovereignty when citizens cannot trace why resources moved where they did.

### Prohibited patterns (minimum)
- Any allocation system whose decision logic is **non-transparent**, **non-reproducible**, or **non-auditable**.
- Vendor-controlled scoring systems without independent replication rights.
- Decision pipelines where key inputs or transformations are unavailable for appeal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any allocation affecting caps/commons access/ΔM distribution MUST be **auditable end-to-end** with decision logs and reproducible
  rules.
- If auditability cannot be achieved, a **hard stop** MUST apply (suspend, narrow scope, or revert to a simpler auditable method).

---

## B03.CH02.P07.S04 — Prohibited cap hollowing: permanent carve-outs and exception normalisation

Caps only work if they are binding. The main political attack is not open repeal but slow hollowing via exceptions, thresholds, and
category tricks that preserve the appearance of caps while defeating their effect.

### Prohibited patterns (minimum)
- Permanent carve-outs for insiders, sectors, or instruments.
- “Temporary” exceptions that renew indefinitely (exception normalisation).
- Threshold manipulation, netting tricks, or definitional hacks that remove exposures from cap scope.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Exceptions MUST be time-bounded, non-stackable, and impact-registered; repeated renewal MUST trigger escalation.
- Any structural change that reduces cap bindingness MUST be treated as **constitutive** (and may force fork classification).

---

## B03.CH02.P07.S05 — Prohibited semantic drift: redefinition outside the Kernel Spec

If the meaning of core terms can be politically rewritten ad hoc, the order rails collapse. OBA therefore fixes a source hierarchy:
definitions live in the Kernel Spec (and its governed versioning), not in opportunistic policy language.

### Prohibited patterns (minimum)
- Redefining canonical terms (E, caps, commons, ΔM, auditability/contestability) inside local policy without reference to the
  Kernel Spec.
- Inventing “local meanings” that change test outcomes while keeping labels.
- Quiet definitional shifts without changelog, bridging, and public disclosure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- All politically used OBA terms MUST **reference** the Kernel Spec definition IDs/versions; local text may elaborate but MUST NOT
  redefine.
- If a redefinition is proposed, it MUST be treated as a **constitutive change** and processed via version control + compatibility
  procedure (and may require fork labeling).
