# B03.CH02.P08 — Conflict and pluralism within order-democracy

This paragraph defines how **pluralism** and **political conflict** operate inside OBA order-democracy without dissolving its
constitutive rails. OBA assumes persistent disagreement about priorities, pacing, and sector sequencing; it does **not** assume
consensus. What it forbids is conflict that targets the **conditions of governability itself**: measurability, auditability,
contestability, binding caps, and commons integrity.

Order-democracy therefore distinguishes **legitimate conflict within the rails** from **illegitimate conflict aimed at breaking
the rails**, and provides a controlled pathway for escalation: from dispute → adjudication → (if necessary) explicit **fork
classification**, rather than semantic blending or covert drift.

---

## B03.CH02.P08.S01 — Legitimate conflicts: priorities within the rails (ΔM, pace, sector focus)

Legitimate conflict concerns choices that remain **parameterised** by the invariant framework. These disagreements are expected,
democratic, and resolvable through mandate procedures and contestable allocation.

### Typical legitimate conflict domains
- **ΔM priorities**: which channels receive budget first (maintenance vs expansion; buffer-building vs productive credit).
- **Pacing/sequencing**: fast versus gradual rollout; pilots versus full implementation.
- **Sector focus**: housing vs energy vs care vs infrastructure, within the same invariant constraints.
- **Distribution within rails**: how to hit R/B lower bounds with different mixes of instruments.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Legitimate conflict MUST be expressed as **parameter choices**, with explicit assumptions, evidence, and measurable outcomes.
- Disputes MUST be resolvable via documented procedures (decision logs, appeals, audits) without redefining terms or weakening
  auditability.

---

## B03.CH02.P08.S02 — Illegitimate conflicts: fighting to break invariants

Illegitimate conflict targets the system’s ability to remain non-capturable and contestable. The object is not priority-setting but
**rail-breaking**.

### Illegitimate conflict signatures (minimum)
- Proposals that hollow out **caps** through carve-outs, stacking, or threshold tricks.
- Attempts to replace contestable allocation with **black-box** governance.
- Moves to close commons access or introduce **toll gates** under new labels.
- Efforts to weaken evidence standards, disable logging, or block independent audits.
- Attempts to reframe surveillance/profiling as “order enforcement” (overreach drift).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Illegitimate rail-breaking attempts MUST trigger escalation (audit, hard stops, mandate review) and cannot be treated as normal
  political bargaining chips.
- Where the conflict object is an invariant, the dispute MUST be classified as **constitutive** and processed accordingly.

---

## B03.CH02.P08.S03 — Schism mechanics: when conflict leads to a fork

Some conflicts cannot be resolved inside a single compatible implementation. When parties insist on incompatible constitutive
choices, the correct outcome is not rhetorical compromise but formal separation.

### Fork triggers (minimum)
- Persistent attempts at cap hollowing, exception normalisation, or semantic drift.
- Repeated refusal of auditability/contestability requirements.
- Structural disagreement on non-negotiables (caps bindingness, commons openness, black-box prohibition).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- When incompatibility is substantive, the system MUST force an explicit decision: **variant** (within rails) vs **fork** (rail
  deviation), with public disclosure and compatibility labeling.
- “Semantic mixing” (keeping the OBA label while altering rails) MUST be treated as mislabeling and corrected.

---

## B03.CH02.P08.S04 — Re-integration: conditions for returning to compatible rails

Reintegration is possible after a split or severe conflict, but only under verifiable conditions. Otherwise reintegration becomes a
path for silent drift.

### Reintegration conditions (minimum)
- Re-acceptance of the **definition set and version** as canonical reference.
- Restoration of **auditability** (logs, replication, independent review).
- Removal or rollback of rail-breaking mechanisms (carve-outs, black boxes, lock-ins).
- Demonstrated compliance over a defined period, with public reporting.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Reintegration MUST be treated as a governed migration with **bridging**, changelog entries, and explicit compatibility status.
- Until conditions are met, the status MUST remain forked/non-compatible (fail-closed).

---

## B03.CH02.P08.S05 — Incident politics: crises as attempts to renegotiate invariants

Crises create pressure for shortcuts and “temporary” deviations that tend to become permanent. OBA treats crises as a predictable
attack surface for cap erosion, surveillance expansion, and exception stacking.

### Incident politics risk patterns (minimum)
- Emergency measures that bypass auditability and later resist rollback.
- Crisis narratives used to justify toll reintroduction or private lock-ins (“we need speed”).
- Selective enforcement and scapegoating under the banner of stability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Crisis measures MUST be time-bounded (sunset), reversible (rollback), and evidence-tracked (decision logs, post-mortems).
- Any crisis-driven deviation that persists beyond its sunset or cannot be audited MUST trigger escalation and potential fork
  classification.
