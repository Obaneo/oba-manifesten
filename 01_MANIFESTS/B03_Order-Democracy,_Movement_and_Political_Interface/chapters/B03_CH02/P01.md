# B03.CH02.P01 — Problem statement: democracy without order invariants is capture-prone

This paragraph states the core governance claim of **order-democracy**: if a democratic system does not embed **order invariants**
as binding rails, the democratic procedure itself becomes a mechanism for **capture**, **semantic drift**, and **exception stacking**.
In OBA terms, the highest-risk failure mode is not “bad policy preferences”, but **the erosion of the invariant constraints**
(caps, commons, measurability/auditability, and contestability) through formally legitimate political decisions.

Order-democracy therefore treats certain constraints as **constitutive**: they sit *above* ordinary political bargaining, not
because they are “true by expertise”, but because they are the minimal conditions under which democratic choice remains
**non-extractive**, **auditable**, and **reversible without drift**. Without these invariants, power concentrates endogenously:
actors with superior resources can convert political influence into durable **toll positions**, weaken enforcement, and reframe
definitions to preserve the label while hollowing out the substance.

---

## B03.CH02.P01.S01 — Democratic decision-making as an attack vector on caps and commons

In a capture-prone regime, democratic procedures can be used to legally dismantle the very constraints meant to protect the
public order. The attack surface is predictable:

- **Caps** can be weakened by “temporary” carve-outs, stacked exceptions, threshold tricks, and selective enforcement.
- **Commons** can be eroded by gating rules, access discrimination, maintenance underfunding, or “privatisation by interface”.
- **Auditability** can be neutralised by black-box allocation, proprietary metrics, or refusals of forensic access.
- **Contestability** can be disabled by procedural sabotage: delays, standing barriers, and appeal processes designed to exhaust challengers.

The key point is that these moves can be framed as legitimate democratic outputs while functioning as **order violations**.
Order-democracy treats such moves as violations of constitutive rails, even when they arise through ordinary majority rule.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any decision that **weakens caps, commons access/maintenance, measurability/auditability, or contestability** MUST be treated as
  an **order-impacting** change and MUST be routed through constitutive procedures (not ordinary politics).
- Exception mechanisms MUST be non-stackable, time-bounded (sunset), logged, and paired with rollback requirements; otherwise they
  MUST be treated as cap/commons erosion.

## B03.CH02.P01.S02 — Why OBA requires a “constitutive layer” (a non-votable core)

OBA requires a constitutive layer because the invariants are not “policy goals”; they are the **preconditions** for coherent
governance under uncertainty and conflict. A system without a non-votable core has no stable reference frame: definitions drift,
measurement becomes negotiable, and enforcement becomes optional.

The constitutive layer is therefore the democratic analogue of an integrity seal: it fixes **what must remain stable** so that
political disagreement can occur **within rails**. It also makes commitments **credible**: if caps, commons rules, auditability,
and contestability can be changed by ordinary bargaining, actors rationally invest in capturing those change points rather than
competing on outcomes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Constitutive rails MUST include: **caps bindingness**, **commons access + maintenance discipline**, **open definitions and
  reproducible calculation rules**, and **contestability guarantees** (objection/appeal + independent audit).
- Any proposal that changes a constitutive rail MUST be classified as a **constitutive decision** and MUST trigger heightened
  mandate requirements (see decision-space classification).

## B03.CH02.P01.S03 — Relation to OOA: why making everything “politically malleable” produces regression

OOA-style governance treats most constraints as negotiable and resolves conflict primarily via political bargaining, narrative
dominance, and discretionary exceptions. In that regime, “democratic flexibility” becomes a pathway to **regression**:

- Constraints are relaxed to accommodate concentrated interests.
- Exceptions accumulate and normalise, creating de facto carve-outs.
- Definitions are relabeled to preserve legitimacy while changing function (label-over-function).
- Audit and contestability degrade as decision systems become opaque, vendor-dependent, or discretionary.

From an OBA perspective, the regression mechanism is structural: when everything is politically malleable, the equilibrium
moves toward **extractive stability** (durable toll positions) rather than toward measurable E-reduction and commons substitution.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Policies MUST be evaluated by **function-over-label**: if outcomes or mechanisms replicate toll extraction, they MUST be treated
  as non-conform regardless of naming or narrative.
- “Flexibility” that operates via exception stacking, definition shifting, or closed computation MUST be treated as an OOA drift signal.

## B03.CH02.P01.S04 — Delimitation: order-democracy ≠ technocracy; contestability remains a requirement

Order-democracy does not outsource politics to experts. It separates **what is politically contestable** (parameters, priorities,
pacing, project choice within ΔM) from **what must remain invariant** (rails that prevent capture and semantic drift). Expertise
is used to implement and audit rules, not to replace the mandate.

Contestability is not optional: without structured objection/appeal, independent audit, and public traceability, a constitutive
layer can itself become a tool of domination. Order-democracy therefore couples non-negotiables with **anti-overreach rails**:
bounded powers, evidence standards, transparency minimums, and judicial review.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Constitutive rails MUST be paired with **contestability guarantees**: accessible objections, independent audit capacity, and
  judicial review with evidence access.
- Any allocation or enforcement mechanism that cannot be **explained, audited, and contested** MUST trigger a hard stop until
  it becomes contestable (fail-closed rule).
