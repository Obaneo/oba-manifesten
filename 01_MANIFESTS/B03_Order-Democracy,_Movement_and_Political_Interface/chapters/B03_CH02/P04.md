# B03.CH02.P04 — Why these invariants are democratically justified

This paragraph states why OBA’s order invariants are not “anti-democratic constraints” but **democratic preconditions**.
They exist to keep democratic choice meaningful over time by preventing the systematic failure modes that convert politics into
a capture contest: concentrated veto power, semantic drift, gated essentials, selective enforcement, and intergenerational
externalisation.

The justification is structural: democratic legitimacy requires (a) bounded dominance, (b) stable meanings, (c) continuity of
essentials, (d) protection against selective coercion, and (e) credible commitments to maintenance and future burdens. Ordinary
majority politics is not reliably self-binding on these dimensions—especially under crisis pressure—so OBA treats these rails as
constitutive.

---

## B03.CH02.P04.S01 — Protection against top-tail dominance and claim stacking

Democracy fails when a small set of actors can convert wealth/claims into durable **veto positions** and agenda control.
Caps and anti-extraction constraints are justified as anti-dominance infrastructure: they limit the ability to accumulate
blocking power and to purchase “exceptions” through influence.

### Democratic rationale
- Prevents the conversion of economic claims into political control.
- Preserves competitive pluralism by limiting durable choke points.

### Conformance implications
- Any policy that increases structural veto power (through carve-outs, lock-ins, or tolerated fee stacks) undermines democratic
  capacity and must be treated as an order breach.

---

## B03.CH02.P04.S02 — Protection against semantic drift and policy laundering

Democracy requires that citizens can understand what is being decided. If definitions can be quietly reworked, political choice
becomes a language game—policy laundering replaces accountability. Function-over-label, stable definition sets, and changelog
discipline are therefore democratically justified: they preserve **meaning integrity**.

### Democratic rationale
- Enables informed consent and contestation by stabilising terms.
- Blocks “OBA-washing” where the label remains but the rails dissolve.

### Conformance implications
- Redefinition outside the Kernel Spec (or without explicit fork procedures) is a democratic defect, not a technicality.

---

## B03.CH02.P04.S03 — Protection of essentials continuity and commons substitution

Democracy is fragile when essentials become discontinuous, precarious, or gated. Commons substitution and maintenance discipline
are justified because they keep **life-world continuity** intact and reduce the leverage of toll positions over basic needs.

### Democratic rationale
- Reduces coercive dependency: citizens are less hostage to private gates.
- Stabilises participation: people can engage politically when essentials are secured.

### Conformance implications
- Policies that create paid priority, lock-in fees, or maintenance neglect undermine democratic agency by reintroducing toll
  dependence.

---

## B03.CH02.P04.S04 — Protection of minorities against selective caps/enforcement

Majoritarian politics can drift into selective enforcement: rules applied harshly to out-groups and leniently to insiders.
Contestability, judicial review, and audit independence are justified as minority protections against arbitrary or politically
targeted application of caps and enforcement.

### Democratic rationale
- Protects equal citizenship under enforcement.
- Prevents order tools from becoming instruments of factional punishment.

### Conformance implications
- Selective caps or selective enforcement is an order breach even if the formal rules appear intact.

---

## B03.CH02.P04.S05 — Protection of future generations via maintenance and capex rails

Democracy has a known weakness: it discounts long-term maintenance and pushes costs forward (infrastructure decay, hidden
liabilities, underfunded commons). Maintenance-first and capex rails are justified as intergenerational fairness constraints:
they prevent the present from consuming the future’s option space.

### Democratic rationale
- Converts long-term obligations into visible, audited commitments.
- Prevents “debt-by-neglect” and irreversible degradation of commons capacity.

### Conformance implications
- Chronic maintenance underfunding, silent degradation, or capex substitution via private toll regimes counts as democratic
  regression and triggers escalation (hard stops where needed).
