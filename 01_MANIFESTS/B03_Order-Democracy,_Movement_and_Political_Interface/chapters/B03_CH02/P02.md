# B03.CH02.P02 — Decision space: classification of decisions by order impact

This paragraph defines a **decision-space taxonomy** for order-democracy. The purpose is to prevent semantic drift and capture by
making “what kind of decision is this?” a **mandatory first step** before substance is debated.

OBA distinguishes decisions by **order impact**: whether they can alter the invariant rails (constitutive), tune parameters within
rails (parametric), choose projects within authorised allocation space (discretionary), temporarily deviate under crisis with strict
reversion (emergency), or explicitly depart into a new compatible/incompatible line (fork).

This classification is not descriptive; it is **normative**. Each class has its own required mandate form, evidence burden,
transparency obligations, and rollback constraints. Misclassifying a decision (e.g., treating a constitutive change as a parametric
tweak) is a primary capture vector and must be treated as a governance defect.

---

## B03.CH02.P02.S01 — Constitutive: definitions, bindingness of caps, measurability, boundary rules

A **constitutive decision** changes the rails themselves: the meanings, constraints, and governance guarantees that make the system
OBA-class at all. Constitutive decisions are higher-order: they define what later decisions *mean* and what later decisions are
*allowed to do*.

### Typical constitutive objects
- **Definitions and classification rules** (e.g., what counts as extraction vs production; what counts as a commons).
- **Caps bindingness** (carve-out permissibility, threshold logic, enforcement commitments).
- **Measurability/auditability primitives** (open calculation rules, logging requirements, evidentiary standards).
- **Boundary rules** for commons access and maintenance obligations.
- **Contestability guarantees** (appeal rights, audit independence, evidence access).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any change to definitions, caps bindingness, measurability/auditability primitives, contestability, or commons boundary rules MUST
  be classified as **constitutive**.
- Constitutive decisions MUST require heightened mandate legitimacy (e.g., supermajority / special procedure) and MUST publish:
  (a) a versioned definition set, (b) a changelog + impact analysis, and (c) explicit backward-compatibility/bridging rules.

---

## B03.CH02.P02.S02 — Parametric: thresholds, rates, budget allocations within the rails

A **parametric decision** tunes numeric or procedural parameters **inside** constitutive rails without changing what the rails are.
It is the normal democratic domain: political priorities, distributive choices, and pacing choices expressed as parameters.

### Typical parametric objects
- **Thresholds** (within allowed bands) and enforcement triggers.
- **Rates** (fees, contributions, taxes) provided they do not recreate toll extraction and remain within cap/anti-avoidance rules.
- **Budget allocations** across programmes/channels, subject to ΔM rules and transparency requirements.
- **Pacing/sequencing** within an authorised transition corridor.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Parametric decisions MUST reference the current **definition-set version** they operate under and MUST demonstrate no constitutive
  rail is weakened.
- Parameter changes MUST be logged and publishable as a traceable diff (what changed, why, expected effects, and monitoring plan).

---

## B03.CH02.P02.S03 — Discretionary: project choices, prioritisation, pacing within ΔM

A **discretionary decision** selects concrete projects and priorities within already-authorised parametric budgets and within ΔM
allocation rules. Discretionary decisions are where implementation happens: which commons upgrades, which enforcement campaigns,
which capacity expansions.

### Typical discretionary objects
- **Project selection** (capex and service upgrades) inside an approved investment envelope.
- **Prioritisation** under scarcity protocols (essentials, vulnerability, continuity).
- **Operational pacing** and scheduling, provided it stays within authorised rails.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Discretionary decisions MUST be contestable: publish selection criteria, decision rationale, and audit trails sufficient to
  challenge outcomes.
- Discretionary decisions MUST NOT introduce new de facto rails (e.g., black-box scoring, proprietary gating, unlogged exceptions).

---

## B03.CH02.P02.S04 — Emergency decision: crisis powers with sunset/rollback

An **emergency decision** temporarily expands powers or permits deviations to maintain essentials and continuity under shock.
Emergency decisions are permitted only as **time-bounded exceptions** with strict anti-normalisation safeguards.

### Emergency constraints
- **Sunset by default**: automatic expiry unless reauthorised under higher threshold.
- **Rollback obligations**: technical/contractual reversibility and retention of logs/evidence.
- **Evidence discipline**: real-time transparency minimum and mandatory ex post audit.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any emergency deviation MUST specify: scope, duration, triggers, monitoring, sunset date, and rollback plan (including exitability).
- Emergency powers MUST NOT be used to establish permanent surveillance/profiling linkages or permanent carve-outs (hard prohibition).

---

## B03.CH02.P02.S05 — Fork decision: explicit variant choice with compatibility status

A **fork decision** is an explicit acknowledgement that a proposal cannot be represented as a mere parameter change inside the
existing rails. It initiates a formal choice between: (a) a compatible variant, (b) a partial/emergency deviation with strict sunsets,
or (c) an incompatible fork requiring relabeling and separate governance lineage.

Fork decisions exist to prevent “shadow forks”: incompatible divergence hidden inside rhetoric, exceptions, or definitional drift.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any proposal that changes a constitutive rail MUST trigger a fork-classification step: **variant / extension / fork** and assign
  a public **compatibility label**.
- Fork decisions MUST publish a registry entry: unique ID, delta on rails, definition-set versions, rationale, impact analysis, and
  the compliance/evidence plan for the new line.
