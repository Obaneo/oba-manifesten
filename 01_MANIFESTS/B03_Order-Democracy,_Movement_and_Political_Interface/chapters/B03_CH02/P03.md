# B03.CH02.P03 — Invariants: what cannot be hollowed out by ordinary politics

This paragraph defines the **order invariants** that must remain intact under ordinary democratic contestation. In OBA,
democracy governs within rails; it may not erode the rails themselves through incremental exceptions, definitional drift,
or procedural sabotage.

These invariants are the **minimum constitutional substrate** of order-democracy: they prevent capture by ensuring that the
core constraints—caps, E-reduction, commons substitution, measurability/auditability, and contestability—cannot be traded away
in exchange for short-term coalition gains, crisis rhetoric, or technical complexity.

The invariants are binding across the political interface: legislation, executive policy, procurement, commons governance,
audit regimes, and public communication. Any attempted weakening MUST be treated as a **constitutive deviation** and therefore
must trigger fork/compatibility handling (including relabeling where required).

---

## B03.CH02.P03.S01 — Caps: binding; no stackable exceptions; no threshold tricks

Caps are **binding constraints** on claim and power positions. They are not targets, aspirations, or “best-effort” guidelines.
Their purpose is to block structural dominance and the accumulation of veto power via claim stacking.

### Threat model
- **Exception stacking**: multiple “temporary” carve-outs that become permanent in aggregate.
- **Threshold tricks**: redefining thresholds, netting rules, entity boundaries, or categories to simulate compliance.
- **Selective enforcement**: caps applied to outsiders while insiders receive implicit exemptions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Caps MUST be enforceable without discretionary waivers; exceptions MUST be rare, explicitly registered, and non-stackable.
- Any cap exception MUST include: scope, quantified impact, sunset, rollback plan, and evidence/audit plan.
- Any rule change that materially weakens cap enforceability MUST be treated as constitutive (not parametric).

---

## B03.CH02.P03.S02 — E-reduction: function-over-label; anti-relabeling as a core rule

E-reduction (reducing toll/claim extraction) is an invariant: policy may not preserve extraction while renaming it.
The core safeguard is **function-over-label**: classification follows economic function and control structure, not legal or rhetorical
labels.

### Threat model
- **Relabeling** extraction as “innovation”, “efficiency”, “risk management”, or “service fees”.
- **Complexification**: multi-layer fee stacks and intermediaries to obscure extraction.
- **Policy laundering**: presenting OOA mechanisms under OBA language.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any policy claiming OBA status MUST show measurable E-reduction using canonical tests (look-through + function-over-label).
- Structures that increase opacity or create un-auditable fee layers MUST be presumed non-conform until disproven (fail-closed).

---

## B03.CH02.P03.S03 — Commons: open access within boundary rules + maintenance discipline

Commons substitution is an invariant: essential access must be secured via **open access** governed by explicit boundary rules and
sustained by a **maintenance-first** discipline. Commons cannot be converted into quasi-markets through gating, tolling, or
membership games.

### Threat model
- **Gating**: de facto restricted access through pricing, prioritisation for pay, or discriminatory eligibility.
- **Façade commons**: “commons” branding while control remains proprietary or exclusionary.
- **Maintenance decay**: underinvestment that forces re-privatisation or emergency toll regimes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Access MUST be the default; boundary rules MUST be explicit, contestable, and non-discriminatory.
- Maintenance obligations MUST be budgeted, logged, and audited; chronic neglect MUST trigger intervention mechanisms.

---

## B03.CH02.P03.S04 — Measurability/audit: replicable calculation rules, logging, traceability

OBA invariants are only real if they are **observable**. Measurability and auditability are therefore constitutive: without open,
replicable rules and traceable evidence, the system becomes black-box governance and invites capture.

### Threat model
- **Black-box allocation**: decisions not reproducible by third parties.
- **Measurement blackout**: missing logs, broken time series, unverifiable dashboards.
- **Model drift without lineage**: silent changes to methods, parameters, or definitions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Core claims MUST be backed by replicable calculation rules, versioned definition sets, and sufficient logging.
- Changes to measurement or definitions MUST publish changelogs and restatements/bridging where needed.
- If measurability collapses, the system MUST fail closed: suspend affected decisions/allocations until auditability is restored.

---

## B03.CH02.P03.S05 — Contestability: objection/appeal and independent audit as constitutive

Contestability is the democratic safety valve: affected parties must be able to challenge classifications, allocations, sanctions,
and boundary-rule applications with access to evidence. Without contestability, enforcement becomes discretionary and capture-prone.

### Threat model
- **Procedural sabotage**: appeals that are formally allowed but practically impossible (cost, delay, evidence denial).
- **Audit capture**: audits subordinated to operators or vendors; lack of forensic access.
- **Non-contestable allocation**: irreversible decisions with no review pathway.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Objection/appeal pathways MUST exist, must be timely, and must grant evidence access sufficient to argue the case.
- Audit must be structurally independent and empowered with forensic access and meta-audit capability.
- Defaults SHOULD be non-suspensive where procedural delay can be weaponised to cause regression; exceptions must be explicit and justified.
