# B03.CH07.P03 — Name usage: rules against semantic regression and washing

This paragraph establishes the **rules for name usage** within the OBA framework, detailing when the term "OBA" can be
legitimately used and how to avoid **semantic drift** and **washing**. The goal is to ensure that only compliant implementations
are allowed to label themselves with the OBA status and that any deviation from core principles is properly flagged with the
appropriate terminology.

### Core principles
- **OBA** as a **reserved label**: the term "OBA" is strictly controlled, and its usage is restricted to implementations that
  fully comply with the **Kernel Spec** and **non-negotiables**.
- **Prohibited misrepresentation**: applying "OBA" to implementations that diverge significantly from the OBA framework is
  prohibited, as it misleads stakeholders and undermines the integrity of OBA.
- **Status labels**: clear and consistent terminology is critical to differentiate between fully compliant, partially compliant,
  and non-compliant implementations.

---

## B03.CH07.P03.S01 — “OBA” as a reserved label: conditions for use

The term **“OBA”** is a reserved label that can only be used by systems or policies that fully conform to the OBA framework
defined in the **Kernel Spec**. This includes adhering to **core invariants** such as caps, commons, E-reduction, and ΔM.

### Core principles
- **Full compliance**: Only systems that fully comply with all **core invariants** can use the “OBA” label.
- **No dilution**: The “OBA” label cannot be used for systems that have deviated from the **non-negotiables** defined in the
  **Kernel Spec**.
- **Verification**: Use of the "OBA" label must be supported by **evidence**, including **compatibility tests** and **audit trails**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any implementation claiming the “OBA” label MUST pass a **compatibility test** and be listed in the **OBA registry**.
- The **compatibility test** must ensure full adherence to the **core principles** and **non-negotiables** of OBA.

---

## B03.CH07.P03.S02 — Prohibited misrepresentation: OBA language applied to OOA practice (washing)

It is prohibited to apply OBA terminology to practices or systems that fall under the **OOA** framework or diverge significantly
from the established **OBA** principles. This constitutes **OBA-washing**, where a system falsely claims to be OBA-compliant
without meeting the core requirements.

### Core principles
- **OBA-washing**: referring to systems or policies as “OBA” when they are based on **OOA** practices or significantly deviate
  from the OBA framework.
- **False claims**: Any system making **false claims** about OBA compliance undermines the integrity of the framework.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Enforcement**: Any instance of **OBA-washing** must be immediately flagged and corrected. The misrepresented system should
  not be allowed to carry OBA status until it complies with the **core principles**.
- **Public disclosure**: The OBA community must be made aware of any **misrepresentation** of OBA principles, including a clear
  explanation of the deviations.

---

## B03.CH07.P03.S03 — “OBA-compatible” versus “OBA-inspired”: status labels

When referring to implementations that are not fully compliant with OBA but still align with many of its core principles, the
labels “**OBA-compatible**” and “**OBA-inspired**” should be used. These labels distinguish between systems that are
**partially** compliant with OBA and those that merely draw inspiration from OBA without meeting the key requirements.

### Core principles
- **OBA-compatible**: Systems that **partially** meet the core OBA principles but still respect the non-negotiables and can
  operate within the OBA framework.
- **OBA-inspired**: Systems that are **inspired by** OBA but do not meet the core requirements and cannot operate fully
  within the OBA system.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **OBA-compatible** systems must be subjected to a **compatibility test**, but with fewer constraints than OBA-conform
  systems.
- **OBA-inspired** systems should clearly communicate their status, ensuring stakeholders understand that they are not fully
  OBA-compliant.

---

## B03.CH07.P03.S04 — Public disclosure: which rails deviate and why

Public disclosure of **deviations** from OBA principles is essential for transparency and accountability. When systems claim
to be **OBA-compatible** or **OBA-inspired**, the public must be informed about which **rails** deviate and the reasons for those
deviations.

### Core principles
- **Transparency**: Deviations from OBA should be fully disclosed to ensure clarity about the extent of compliance.
- **Accountability**: Stakeholders must be able to evaluate the reasons for deviations and their potential impact on the system’s
  overall compatibility with OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any system claiming **OBA-compatible** or **OBA-inspired** status MUST publicly disclose which elements deviate from the OBA
  core and why.
- Systems should provide a **rationale** for why deviations exist and how they intend to maintain compatibility with OBA in the
  future.

---

## B03.CH07.P03.S05 — Enforcement: sanctions for mislabeling in policy / commons / governance

Enforcement mechanisms must be in place to **sanction** mislabeling and ensure that the OBA framework maintains its integrity.
Mislabeling, such as falsely claiming OBA status or misrepresenting compatibility, undermines the transparency and trust in the
system.

### Core principles
- **Mislabeling**: Any **misleading** or **false** claim about OBA status must be corrected and sanctioned.
- **Sanctions**: Systems that mislabel their compliance with OBA must face **sanctions**, which could include removal of the
  OBA label, public retraction, and potential penalties.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Sanctions** should be applied in the event of **OBA-washing**, **misrepresentation**, or failure to follow the appropriate
  **naming conventions**.
- Any **mislabeling** must be publicly corrected, with a **traceable changelog** explaining the actions taken and the
  compliance status of the system.
