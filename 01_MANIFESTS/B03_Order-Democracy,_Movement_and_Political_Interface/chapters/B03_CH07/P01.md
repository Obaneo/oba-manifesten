# B03.CH07.P01 — Purpose and delimitation: forks as necessary plurality without losing the core

This paragraph defines **forks**, **variants**, and **name usage rules** as the governance infrastructure that allows OBA to
exist across jurisdictions, cultures, and shock regimes without dissolving into semantic drift. Forks are treated as a
**normal and expected** phenomenon: OBA is designed to be implemented under heterogeneous conditions, which makes variation
inevitable. However, variation must occur under a **formal compatibility discipline**: any deviation is classified,
published, versioned, and testable, so that pluralism does not become **washing**, **drift**, or **incompatible shadow
canons**.

This chapter is therefore **normative** for any party that claims OBA status labels (e.g., *OBA-conform*, *OBA-compatible*,
*OBA-inspired*) in policy, institutional design, commons governance, or public communication.

---

## B03.CH07.P01.S01 — Why variants are inevitable: context, culture, shocks, and institutional baselines

OBA will be deployed under differing institutional baselines (constitutional structures, administrative capacity, legal
traditions), cultural norms (representation models, legitimacy intuitions), and shock profiles (war, energy scarcity,
migration waves, financial crises). These differences produce **legitimate parametric variation**—pace, prioritisation,
threshold selection, sequencing—without altering the constitutive rails.

### Core principles
- **Plurality is structural**: the same OBA rails can be instantiated via different procedures and parameter values.
- **Local fit is not a licence to redefine**: adaptation must remain within the rails (variant) or be declared as a fork.
- **Shocks intensify drift pressure**: emergency politics increases the risk of exception stacking and semantic laundering.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any jurisdictional implementation MUST state which elements are **parametric** (variant space) and which are **constitutive**
  (fork-sensitive).
- Under crisis regimes, any deviation from normal procedure MUST carry explicit **sunsets**, **rollback paths**, and **publication**
  duties consistent with the compatibility framework.

---

## B03.CH07.P01.S02 — Why name usage is not optional: preventing semantic drift and OBA-washing

The label “OBA” is an informational object: it signals a **class membership claim**. If name usage is not governed, actors can
retain the label while hollowing out the rails through selective enforcement, definitional shifts, black-box allocation, or
permanent carve-outs. Name discipline is therefore the primary defence against **semantic drift** and **washing**.

### Threat model (non-exhaustive)
- **Semantic drift**: gradual redefinition of key objects so that rails become optional in practice.
- **Status laundering**: “OBA language” applied to OOA-compatible institutions (caps in rhetoric, carve-outs in reality).
- **Shadow canon**: local “definitions” that conflict with the Kernel Spec while still claiming OBA status.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any use of the label **OBA** (or OBA status labels) MUST be treated as a **testable claim** and MUST be linked to a published
  **compatibility status** and definition-set version.
- If compatibility status is missing, outdated, or non-reproducible, the label MUST be treated as **non-conform** (fail-closed).

## B03.CH07.P01.S03 — Delimitation: economic definitions remain canonical in the Kernel Spec and Book 1

This chapter governs the **political and governance layer** of forks and compatibility. It does not redefine the economic core.
Canonical definitions of extraction (E), caps, commons/ΔG, R/B, and ΔM—and any calculation rules—remain anchored in the
**Kernel Spec** and the economic elaboration of **Book 1**. Book 3 references these sources rather than rewriting them, to avoid
drift through paraphrase.

### Scope boundaries
- **In scope**: classification of deviations (fork/variant/extension), name usage rules, compatibility test requirements,
  publication and registry duties, and correction mechanisms.
- **Out of scope**: changing economic primitives, redefining canonical objects, or introducing new calculation rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any attempt to alter canonical economic definitions MUST be treated as a **constitutive deviation** and therefore as a **fork**
  subject to the fork protocol and compatibility re-evaluation.
- Book 3 artifacts MUST **reference** canonical definitions and MUST NOT create alternative definitions via paraphrase.

## B03.CH07.P01.S04 — Output: formal fork protocol, compatibility test, and registries as public infrastructure

Pluralism is made safe by converting disagreement into **structured metadata** rather than informal rhetoric. The output of this
chapter is a set of formal instruments that make deviations visible, comparable, and contestable: a fork protocol, a compatibility
test, and registries.

### Output components
- **Fork protocol**: a required process for proposing, classifying, reviewing, deciding, and publishing deviations.
- **Compatibility test**: a minimum pass/fail gate for claiming OBA status labels.
- **Registries**: public records for forks/variants, definition-set versions, bridging mappings, and deprecations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any deviation proposal MUST be published with: (i) classification (variant/extension/fork), (ii) impacted rails, (iii)
  measurement plan + audit plan, (iv) compatibility scorecard, and (v) versioned definition-set references.
- Any approved fork/variant MUST be assigned a unique registry ID and MUST maintain a changelog and bridging rules for
  comparison/migration across versions and jurisdictions.
