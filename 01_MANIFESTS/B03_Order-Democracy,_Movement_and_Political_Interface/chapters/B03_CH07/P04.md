# B03.CH07.P04 — Compatibility test: minimum criteria (pass/fail)

This paragraph outlines the **minimum criteria** that any system must meet to be considered compatible with the OBA framework.
These criteria ensure that implementations maintain the core principles of OBA, focusing on **E-reduction**, **caps**, **commons**,
**R/B**, **ΔM**, and **auditability**. Systems must pass these criteria to be labeled as **OBA-compliant**.

## Core principles
- **Pass/fail criteria**: The compatibility test is a binary assessment: a system either meets the criteria (pass) or does not (fail).
- **Testable requirements**: The criteria are **measurable**, **audit-able**, and must be demonstrably supported by evidence.
- **Preservation of invariants**: The criteria focus on preserving the core principles of OBA, including caps, commons access,
  transparency, and auditability.

---

## B03.CH07.P04.S01 — E: measurable reduction of toll/claim extraction (function-over-label)

The first criterion requires that any system claiming OBA status demonstrates a **measurable reduction** in toll/claim extraction.
This ensures that **economic rents** and **extraction** are minimized within the system, in line with the core principles of **E-reduction**.

### Core principles
- **Function-over-label**: The system must show a **functional reduction** in claim extraction, not just claim it.
- **Measurability**: The reduction in toll/claim extraction must be measurable and supported by **data and metrics**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems must provide **evidence** that shows a reduction in extraction in **quantifiable terms** (e.g., reduction in fees, tolls, or rents).
- Systems that fail to show this reduction **fail the test** and must not be labeled as OBA-compliant.

---

## B03.CH07.P04.S02 — Caps: binding, no structural carve-outs, no threshold tricks

The second criterion ensures that **caps** (limits on claims and power) are enforced and **binding**, with no exceptions or loopholes
that could undermine their effectiveness. Caps must be **non-negotiable**, ensuring that no system can bypass the established limits.

### Core principles
- **Binding caps**: Caps must be **mandatory** and enforced without any exceptions or carve-outs.
- **No threshold tricks**: Systems cannot create **hidden exceptions** through tricks like **threshold manipulation** or **threshold breaches**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems must show that caps are **enforced** at all times and that no **exceptions** or **loopholes** are allowed.
- Any attempt to circumvent caps will result in the system failing the compatibility test.

---

## B03.CH07.P04.S03 — Commons/ΔG: open access within boundary rules + maintenance discipline

This criterion ensures that access to commons resources remains **open**, with **transparent boundary rules** and **maintenance
discipline**. The management of commons and **ΔG** (public goods) must adhere to clear rules and be **maintained** to prevent
decay or enclosure.

### Core principles
- **Open access**: Commons must remain **accessible** to all eligible participants.
- **Boundary rules**: Access to commons must be governed by clear, publicly available **rules**.
- **Maintenance discipline**: **Ongoing maintenance** and **upkeep** of commons must be prioritized to avoid **decay** or **deterioration**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems must demonstrate **open access** to commons and show evidence of **maintenance** practices, ensuring the continued
  **availability** of commons resources.
- Failure to provide adequate **maintenance** or to adhere to **boundary rules** will result in failure to meet the compatibility
  criteria.

---

## B03.CH07.P04.S04 — R/B: lower bounds and distributive improvement (p10/p25/median)

The **R/B (Redistribution/Benefit)** criterion ensures that policies within the system adhere to **lower bounds** for **distributive
justice**. This includes **protecting essential goods** and ensuring that **benefits** are **distributed fairly** across all
segments of the population, with specific attention to the **lower percentiles** (p10, p25).

### Core principles
- **Lower bounds**: Systems must **protect essential goods** and services, ensuring that no one falls below certain **minimum
  thresholds**.
- **Distributive improvement**: The system must show **improvement** in the equitable distribution of benefits, especially for
  marginalized groups.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems must ensure **distributive fairness**, with **clear evidence** that benefits are improving and that **minimum standards**
  are maintained across all population segments.
- Failure to meet these requirements results in the system **failing the test**.

---

## B03.CH07.P04.S05 — ΔM: channel allocation testable, allocation contestable, no black boxes

This criterion focuses on the transparency and accountability of **ΔM (monetary space)** allocations. All channels must be
**testable**, **contestable**, and free from **black-box** mechanisms that obscure how resources are allocated.

### Core principles
- **Testable allocation**: All allocation mechanisms must be **auditable** and **testable** to ensure they are working as intended.
- **Contestability**: Stakeholders must be able to **contest allocations**, ensuring that they are subject to review and potential
  revision.
- **No black boxes**: Systems that rely on **opaque**, **non-transparent** mechanisms for allocation will fail the test.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems must ensure that **all allocation processes** are transparent, with **auditable channels** and mechanisms for **public
  review**.
- Failure to provide transparency or to allow **contestability** will result in **non-compliance**.

---

## B03.CH07.P04.S06 — Auditability: open definitions, logging, replicability, restatements

This final criterion ensures that all systems claiming OBA status are **auditable**. This includes **open definitions**, the
availability of **logs**, the ability to **replicate** processes, and a **commitment to restatements** in case of changes.

### Core principles
- **Open definitions**: All **key terms** and concepts must be **clearly defined** and publicly available.
- **Logging**: Systems must maintain **logs** that are transparent and allow for **reproducibility** of decisions.
- **Restatements**: Any changes to definitions or policies must be publicly **restated**, with a **traceable changelog**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems must ensure that all decisions and **calculations** are logged, and that they can be **replicated** by external
  auditors.
- Failure to provide open definitions, maintain logs, or allow for **restatements** will result in a failure of the compatibility
  test.
