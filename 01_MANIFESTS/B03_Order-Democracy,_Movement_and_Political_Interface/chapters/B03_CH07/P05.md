# B03.CH07.P05 — Process: how a fork/variant is formally established

This paragraph describes the formal **process** by which a **fork** or **variant** is established within the OBA framework. It
ensures that any deviation from the core rules is **transparent**, **well-documented**, and **open to public review**. The process
involves clear steps for proposal, evidence collection, independent review, and final decision-making.

## Core principles
- **Formal procedure**: The establishment of a fork or variant follows a **structured and traceable process**.
- **Public engagement**: The process includes opportunities for **public consultation** and **independent review**.
- **Impact analysis**: Any proposed changes must include an **impact analysis** to assess their consequences.

---

## B03.CH07.P05.S01 — Initiation: proposal with delta on rails, rationale, and impact analysis

The first step in the process is the **initiation** of a proposal. This proposal must clearly state the **delta** (difference)
from the existing **rails** (core rules) and include a **rationale** for the change, as well as a **detailed impact analysis**.

### Core principles
- **Delta on rails**: The proposal must identify the **specific change** from the existing core rules (constitutive rails).
- **Rationale**: A **clear justification** for the change must be provided, showing why the new proposal is necessary or beneficial.
- **Impact analysis**: The proposal must include an **analysis of the potential impacts** of the change on the system, stakeholders, and
  OBA principles.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A proposal must **document** the delta, rationale, and impact analysis, making the **changes transparent** and **justifiable**.

---

## B03.CH07.P05.S02 — Evidence: measurement plan, audit plan, uncertainty bands, “unknown” discipline

The proposal must be supported by **evidence** that demonstrates the change is both **measurable** and **auditable**. This
includes a **measurement plan**, an **audit plan**, and a strategy for addressing any **uncertainty** in the proposal's effects.

### Core principles
- **Measurement plan**: The proposal must define **how success** will be measured and what **indicators** will be tracked.
- **Audit plan**: The proposal must include a **clear plan** for **auditing** the implementation and results of the change.
- **Uncertainty management**: The proposal must **acknowledge uncertainties** and outline plans to address them, including methods
  for dealing with **unknown variables**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A proposal must be supported by a **measurable plan** and **audit framework** to ensure **accountability** and **reproducibility**.

---

## B03.CH07.P05.S03 — Review: independent audit + public consultation (where appropriate)

Before any decision is made, the proposal must undergo an **independent audit** and, where appropriate, a **public consultation**
to gather feedback from stakeholders and experts.

### Core principles
- **Independent audit**: The proposal must be reviewed by an **independent** body to ensure it aligns with OBA principles.
- **Public consultation**: For major changes, there should be a **public consultation** period where stakeholders can provide input.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Proposals must be subject to **external review** and, where needed, **public consultation** to ensure **broad-based accountability**.

---

## B03.CH07.P05.S04 — Decision: classification (variant/extension/fork) + name label

After the review process, a decision must be made about whether the proposed change qualifies as a **variant**, **extension**, or
**fork**. The proposal must then be **labeled** accordingly, using the appropriate **name label** to indicate its status.

### Core principles
- **Fork/variant classification**: The proposal must be classified as either a **variant** (parametric change), an **extension** (an
  add-on without changing the core), or a **fork** (a deviation from the constitutive rails).
- **Name label**: The proposal must be assigned a **name label** to clearly indicate its status and its level of compatibility
  with the OBA framework.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every proposal must be classified and **labeled appropriately**, ensuring clarity about its impact on OBA.

---

## B03.CH07.P05.S05 — Publication: changelog, definition set, compatibility scorecard

Finally, the proposal and its decision must be made public, including the **changelog** (tracking the history of changes), the
**definition set** (ensuring clarity on terms), and the **compatibility scorecard** (demonstrating how the proposal aligns with
OBA principles).

### Core principles
- **Changelog**: A **detailed record** of changes must be published, showing how the proposal has evolved over time.
- **Definition set**: The **set of definitions** used in the proposal must be clearly articulated and publicly available.
- **Compatibility scorecard**: A **compatibility scorecard** must be included, showing how the proposal meets OBA criteria.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Proposals must be **fully transparent** and **accountable** through the publication of these documents, ensuring that the changes
  are **traceable** and **auditable**.
