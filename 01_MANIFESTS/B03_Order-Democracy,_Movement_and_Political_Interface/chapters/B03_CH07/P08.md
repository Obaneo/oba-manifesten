# B03.CH07.P08 — Abuse patterns: strategic forks and façade variants

This paragraph defines the dominant **abuse patterns** that exploit plurality (forks/variants) to produce **semantic regression**
while preserving OBA branding. The objective is to make these patterns **detectable**, **auditable**, and **actionable** through
a shared set of red flags and governance responses.

Abuse patterns are treated as **adversarial behaviours**: actors will attempt to (i) keep the name, (ii) weaken rails, and (iii)
raise the cost of detection by obscuring measurability, shifting definitions, or burying exceptions.

---

## B03.CH07.P08.S01 — Token forks: rhetoric only, no measurable changes in E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM

A token fork is a **branding move**: it claims a fork/variant status, but the operational regime produces **no measurable change**
on the test axes, or actively refuses measurement. Token forks are often used to defuse pressure while preserving extraction and
claim positions.

### Typical tactics
- **Narrative substitution**: reframe goals and values while avoiding quantified targets.
- **Metric evasion**: redefine indicators, remove baselines, or declare outcomes “too complex to measure”.
- **Pilot theatre**: endless small pilots with no scale commitments, no hard stops, no enforcement layer.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any fork/variant claim MUST publish a **baseline**, **targets**, and **measurement methods** for E/RB/caps/ΔG/ΔM where relevant.
- If measurable deltas are absent or method trails are missing, the status MUST default to **non-compatible** (or **unknown → fail**)
  under the name usage regime.

---

## B03.CH07.P08.S02 — Capture forks: exceptions built in for insiders

A capture fork embeds **structural carve-outs** that preserve incumbent claim and power positions while appearing reformist. It is
characterised by exception regimes that are **selective**, **renewable**, and **hard to contest**, and by enforcement patterns that
avoid powerful actors.

### Typical tactics
- **Exception stacking**: multiple “temporary” carve-outs that collectively become permanent.
- **Threshold gaming**: eligibility rules that exclude top-tail exposures from cap application.
- **Regulatory outsourcing**: enforcement moved into industry bodies or vendor-controlled systems.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Fork/variant designs MUST prohibit **stackable exceptions** and MUST require **sunsets + reauthorisation** with higher thresholds.
- Any exception regime MUST be published in an **exceptions register** with scope, duration, beneficiaries (as permitted under
  privacy rails), rationale, and measurable impact.
- Selective enforcement patterns MUST trigger escalation (audit + oversight) and may trigger relabeling as **non-compatible**.

---

## B03.CH07.P08.S03 — Authoritarian forks: overreach, surveillance, selective enforcement

An authoritarian fork claims order and enforcement but breaks democratic rails through **profiling**, **surveillance expansion**,
and **selective application** of caps and sanctions. It often preserves elite extraction by focusing coercion downward while keeping
top-tail regimes porous.

### Typical tactics
- **Order-by-control**: replace contestability with administrative discretion and secrecy.
- **Profiling linkage**: couple allocation/enforcement to surveillance systems that cannot be appealed or audited independently.
- **Selective caps**: caps applied to politically weak groups; exemptions for strategic sectors or connected actors.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any linkage that breaks **contestability**, **privacy rails**, or **independent audit** MUST be treated as **incompatibility**.
- Enforcement MUST be bound to transparent rules, logged decisions, and appeal routes; selective enforcement MUST trigger hard stops
  where regression risk exists.

---

## B03.CH07.P08.S04 — Vendor forks: black-box allocation and proprietary measurement regimes

A vendor fork shifts the effective canon from public rules to proprietary tooling: allocation and measurement become **black boxes**,
model outputs are non-reproducible, and the system becomes non-exitable. The fork is then “owned” by the vendor rather than governed
by the polity.

### Typical tactics
- **Proprietary metrics**: indicators cannot be replicated without vendor access.
- **Model opacity**: “trade secret” claims block audit and appeal.
- **Lock-in governance**: procurement terms prevent second implementations, shadow computation, or data portability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Allocation and measurement systems MUST be **auditable, reproducible, and exit-able** (open calculation rules; version pinning;
  logging; portability; second implementation or shadow computation where required).
- If tooling prevents independent verification, the regime MUST be labeled **non-compatible** and MUST not use OBA-compatible labels.

---

## B03.CH07.P08.S05 — Detection: exception ratio↑, sanction rate↓, reproducibility↓, E↑

Abuse detection is operationalised via a small number of **high-signal governance indicators** that tend to move in predictable ways
under capture, façade behaviour, or authoritarian/vendor drift.

### Primary detection signals (non-exhaustive)
- **Exception ratio↑**: rising count, widening scope, repeated renewals, or concentration among insiders.
- **Sanction rate↓**: enforcement declines despite stable or rising anomaly indicators (or enforcement concentrates on weak actors).
- **Reproducibility↓**: calculations cannot be replicated; methods change without changelog; missing baselines/restatements.
- **E↑ (or E not falling)**: toll/claim extraction rises, or claimed reductions are not borne out in measured outcomes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Governance MUST define **thresholds and triggers** for these signals that automatically escalate to **trigger-audits**, public
  review, and—where criteria are met—**hard stops**.
- When detection signals breach thresholds, the default response MUST be **fail-closed on labeling** (downgrade to partial/uncertain
  or non-compatible) until corrective evidence is produced.
- Detection outcomes MUST be logged in public dashboards/registries, including rationale, methods, and subsequent corrective actions.
