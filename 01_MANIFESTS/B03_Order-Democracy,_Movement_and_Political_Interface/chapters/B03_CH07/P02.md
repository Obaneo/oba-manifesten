# B03.CH07.P02 — Conceptual framework: fork, variant, extension, and incompatibility

This paragraph defines the **conceptual framework** for classifying deviations in the OBA system, introducing key terms and
distinctions to properly handle variation while maintaining the integrity of the core. Understanding the difference between
**forks**, **variants**, **extensions**, and **incompatibilities** is essential to safeguarding the system from semantic drift,
while still allowing for pluralism, flexibility, and adaptation in different jurisdictions or under different contexts.

The goal is to establish clear and testable guidelines to maintain **compatibility** across different implementations of OBA
while enabling **necessary adaptations** under different circumstances.

---

## B03.CH07.P02.S01 — Fork: deviation on constitutive rails (non-negotiables or definitions)

A **fork** occurs when a deviation from the **constitutive rails** (non-negotiables or canonical definitions) is proposed,
altering the foundational structure of OBA. A fork is a **fundamental break** with the established system and requires formal
recognition, reclassification, and testing. Forks must be clearly identified, published, and subjected to the full **compatibility
protocol**.

### Core principles
- **Constitutive rails**: these are the foundational, non-negotiable elements of OBA—such as caps, commons, E-reduction,
  and ΔM—that cannot be altered without fundamentally changing the nature of OBA.
- **Formal reclassification**: any fork proposal must undergo a formal review process and be classified based on its impact on
  the OBA system.
- **Compatibility re-evaluation**: forks will trigger a full re-evaluation of compatibility status.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any fork proposal MUST undergo a **compatibility test**, be given a unique **ID**, and be listed in the public **fork registry**.
- The **changelog** for forks must be detailed, with clear explanations of why the deviation occurred, its impact on the system,
  and how it will be governed.

---

## B03.CH07.P02.S02 — Variant: parametric deviation within rails (thresholds, pace, priorities)

A **variant** is a **parametric** deviation from the rails, where certain **adjustable parameters** (such as thresholds, pace,
and priorities) are modified within the allowable range of the OBA system. Unlike forks, variants do not break the **core
structure** of OBA but allow for contextual or jurisdictional adjustments, provided they remain within the bounds of the
**constitutive rails**.

### Core principles
- **Adjustable parameters**: thresholds, rates, priorities, sequencing, and pacing that can be modified without altering
  the non-negotiables of the system.
- **Flexibility within rails**: variants maintain adherence to the essential elements of OBA while enabling **localised**
  adjustments based on needs, context, or urgency.
- **Testable limits**: variants must be measured against pre-defined boundaries to ensure compliance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any implementation proposing a variant MUST specify which **parameter(s)** are modified, how they are applied, and provide
  a **compatibility scorecard**.
- The **compatibility test** for variants is less rigorous than for forks but still requires an **audit trail** of changes.

---

## B03.CH07.P02.S03 — Extension: additional modules without changing the core (add-on, optional)

An **extension** refers to an **add-on module** or **optional component** that can be integrated with the OBA system without
altering its **core** structure or **non-negotiable** elements. Extensions may provide supplementary functionality, such as
additional reporting tools, specialized features, or enhancements to meet specific needs, but they must **not** compromise the
core principles of OBA.

### Core principles
- **Non-invasive**: extensions must not interfere with or change the **core** definitions and calculations of OBA.
- **Additive**: extensions add value without modifying the system’s foundational structures.
- **Modular**: these components are optional and can be adopted without mandatory implementation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Extensions must be **documented** and **transparent**, specifying which parts of OBA they complement or augment.
- **Compatibility** testing for extensions is minimal since they do not affect the core functionality.

---

## B03.CH07.P02.S04 — Incompatibility: break in measurability/auditability/contestability

**Incompatibility** occurs when a deviation fundamentally breaks key OBA attributes, such as **measurability**, **auditability**, or
**contestability**. If an implementation results in **non-transparent** operations or **undetectable** allocation mechanisms,
it is deemed **incompatible** with the OBA framework. Such implementations cannot claim OBA status under any circumstances.

### Core principles
- **Measurability**: all OBA components must be quantifiable and capable of being tested for compliance.
- **Auditability**: operations must allow for verification, replication, and transparency in decision-making processes.
- **Contestability**: all major decisions must be subject to objection or appeal, and independent review must be available.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any deviation that results in **incompatibility** with the core attributes of OBA MUST be treated as a **failed** implementation,
  requiring **reversion** or **rollback**.
- **Incompatible** components MUST NOT be labeled as OBA-conform or OBA-compatible, and such labels must be corrected and
  publicly rectified.

---

## B03.CH07.P02.S05 — Naming conventions: labels for compatible, partial, non-compatible

Clear **naming conventions** are essential for distinguishing between various levels of compliance and ensuring proper **labeling**
of forks, variants, extensions, and incompatible implementations. The correct usage of **OBA** labels prevents confusion, protects
against **semantic drift**, and maintains the integrity of the OBA system.

### Core principles
- **OBA-conform**: Full adherence to the Kernel Spec and OBA rails, no deviations from non-negotiables.
- **OBA-compatible**: Deviations within acceptable parametric limits (variants), still adhering to all core principles.
- **OBA-inspired**: Implementations that draw inspiration from OBA but do not meet core requirements or have significant deviations.
- **OBA-incompatible**: Major deviations that break the core principles of OBA (e.g., loss of measurability or contestability).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- All OBA status labels MUST be assigned based on the implementation's compliance with the core principles.
- **Forks** and **variants** MUST carry clear **labels** and be published in the **fork registry** with complete **changelog**.
