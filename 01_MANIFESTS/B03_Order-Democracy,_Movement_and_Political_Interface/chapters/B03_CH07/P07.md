# B03.CH07.P07 — Governance implications: pluralism without fragmentation

This paragraph defines the **governance implications** of allowing forks and variants under OBA: pluralism is permitted and often
necessary, but must not degrade into **semantic drift**, **label chaos**, or **strategic fragmentation**. Governance must therefore
combine **local autonomy** (within rails) with **central invariants**, and provide **clear arbitration, enforcement, reintegration,
and separation protocols**.

---

## B03.CH07.P07.S01 — Federated governance: local variants with central invariants

OBA governance is **federated**: local jurisdictions and institutions may adopt **variants** (parametric choices) while preserving
a shared set of **constitutive invariants**. Federation prevents both extremes: (i) a single uniform blueprint that ignores context,
and (ii) uncontrolled divergence that makes “OBA” meaningless.

### Core principles
- **Central invariants, local parameters**: invariants define the rails; parameters encode local choice.
- **Shared tests**: compatibility is determined by common test axes and evidence standards, not by narrative alignment.
- **Traceability**: every local choice must be linkable to versioned definition sets and registries.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Federated governance MUST define and publish which elements are **constitutive** (non-negotiable) and which are **parametric**
  (variant space).
- Local variants MUST publish **version-pinned definition sets**, parameter values, and compatibility status.
- Any local deviation on constitutive rails MUST be classified as a **fork** and MUST be labeled accordingly.

---

## B03.CH07.P07.S02 — Conflict arbitration: who decides in a compatibility dispute

Compatibility disputes are inevitable. Governance must specify **who arbitrates**, under what **procedure**, using what **evidence
standards**, and with what **appeal routes**, so that disputes do not collapse into political power contests or branding wars.

### Core principles
- **Evidence-led arbitration**: disputes are resolved by test outcomes and published methods, not authority claims.
- **Institutional separation**: the body that implements a variant must not be the final judge of its own compatibility.
- **Contestability without paralysis**: decisions must be appealable, but timelines and defaults must prevent indefinite stalling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A compatibility dispute MUST be decided by an **independent arbiter** (or panel) that is procedurally separate from the
  implementing entity.
- Arbitration MUST require: (i) the claimed definition set + version, (ii) the claimed parameters, (iii) the published tests and
  evidence, (iv) a reproducible method trail.
- Governance MUST define an **appeal route** and deadlines; until resolution, the public label MUST default to **uncertain** or
  **non-compatible** (fail-closed on name usage).

---

## B03.CH07.P07.S03 — Prevention of “fork shopping”: incentives and enforcement

“Fork shopping” occurs when actors strategically choose the most permissive fork/variant label to evade constraints (caps, audit,
commons rules, E-reduction) while retaining the prestige of “OBA”. Preventing this is a governance requirement, not a cultural hope.

### Core principles
- **No competitive degradation**: variants must not compete by weakening rails.
- **Cost of mislabeling**: if misrepresentation is cheap, it will dominate in adversarial environments.
- **Registry-first enforcement**: claims must be checkable against registries and scorecards, not inferred from messaging.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Public use of “OBA” labels MUST be conditional on an entry in the **fork/variant registry** with current status and scope.
- Governance MUST enforce **sanctions** for mislabeling and for “laundering” OOA practice under OBA language (see name usage rules).
- Compatibility scorecards MUST be published in a comparable format across jurisdictions to prevent selective reporting.

---

## B03.CH07.P07.S04 — Reintegration: conditions for return to compatible rails

Forks and incompatible implementations may later converge back toward compatibility. Governance must define **reintegration** as a
formal process: what must be changed, what evidence is required, and how the label is updated.

### Core principles
- **Reintegration is earned**: compatibility is restored by passing tests, not by declaring intent.
- **Bridging and migration**: reintegration requires explicit mapping from old definitions/parameters to the compatible set.
- **Institutional correction**: reintegration often requires governance changes (auditability, contestability, sanctions), not only
  parameter changes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Reintegration MUST require: (i) a published **delta** from the incompatible state to the compatible rails, (ii) an evidence and
  audit plan, (iii) a defined migration/bridging document, (iv) retesting against the compatibility criteria.
- Until reintegration is confirmed, the implementation MUST retain its non-compatible (or partial) label; “silent reintegration” is
  prohibited.
- Reintegration decisions MUST be recorded in the registry with date, rationale, and versioned references.

---

## B03.CH07.P07.S05 — Break management: when separation is better than sham unity

Sometimes a real conflict over constitutive rails cannot be resolved without coercion or deception. Governance must allow **clean
separation**—a declared fork with correct labels—rather than “sham unity” where incompatible rails are smuggled in while retaining
the OBA name.

### Core principles
- **Honesty over branding**: clear forks preserve clarity; hidden divergence destroys the meaning of “OBA”.
- **Non-hostile coexistence**: separation can be legitimate if it is transparent, registry-bound, and non-misleading.
- **Containment**: break management protects interoperability where possible and prevents contagion of mislabeling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If constitutive disagreement persists, governance MUST prefer an **explicit fork** with proper labeling over continued
  “compatibility theater”.
- Break management MUST specify: (i) the fork’s scope, (ii) the deviating rails, (iii) the compatibility status, (iv) any bridging
  metadata for comparison, and (v) rules preventing cross-label contamination.
- Entities MUST NOT claim OBA-compatible status while operating under forked constitutive rails; name usage MUST match registry
  status at all times.
