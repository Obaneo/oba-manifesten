# B03.CH07.P10 — Integration with tests: compatibility as an input for democratic consistency

This paragraph highlights the importance of **compatibility status** in evaluating the **democratic consistency** of OBA systems.
Compatibility is not just a technical requirement but also a crucial element in maintaining **democratic integrity** over time. It
links directly to the governance metrics, providing a basis for assessing whether an entity or practice is still aligned with the
core principles of OBA.

It is necessary to evaluate the **compatibility status** when considering any OBA-related policy, system, or entity. This ensures
that no system that is incompatible with OBA is allowed to claim the status or further erode democratic principles.

---

## B03.CH07.P10.S01 — Compatibility status as a parameter in policy mandates

**Compatibility status** is a **core criterion** that must be considered when formulating or evaluating any OBA-related policy,
program, or entity. It serves as an indicator of whether the policy or system adheres to the foundational principles of OBA.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Policy mandates** must incorporate **compatibility** as a fundamental **input**. If a policy or system is **incompatible**, it
  should not be approved or should be rectified to meet **compatibility requirements**.
- Mandates must **define clear thresholds** for **compatibility status**, ensuring that policies can only proceed if they
  conform to OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any OBA-related **policy mandate** must **explicitly reference compatibility status** and provide evidence that the policy
  complies with OBA standards.
- Policies failing the compatibility test must **not proceed** until re-evaluated and modified to align with the OBA framework.

---

## B03.CH07.P10.S02 — Public scorecards per variant/fork

For transparency and accountability, **public scorecards** must be used to track and report the **compatibility status** of
different **variants** and **forks** of OBA systems. These scorecards offer a clear, auditable measure of how well different
entities conform to the OBA framework.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Public scorecards** should be **regularly updated** to reflect the most recent status of compatibility for each variant/fork.
- Each scorecard must include **compatibility status**, **issues identified**, and **corrective actions** taken (if any).
  
### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a variant/fork is **found incompatible** with OBA, its **scorecard** must clearly indicate this, along with the **reasons**
  and **necessary actions**.
- Variants/forks **not compliant** with OBA should be **flagged publicly** with specific details on their divergence from OBA
  principles.

---

## B03.CH07.P10.S03 — Audit requirements per status (compatible vs partial vs non-compatible)

Audit requirements must vary according to the **compatibility status** of the entity or system in question. Systems that claim
**OBA status** but fail to meet the full compatibility criteria must undergo a more **rigorous audit** to ensure compliance
with OBA standards.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Full audit**: Systems that claim **full OBA compliance** but fail to meet any compatibility criteria must undergo a
  **complete audit**.
- **Partial audit**: Systems with **partial compliance** will undergo a **streamlined audit**, focusing on the areas of non-
  compliance and the **impact** on the broader system.
- **Non-compliant systems** must undergo a **full-scale investigation** to evaluate the extent of incompatibility and potential
  violations of OBA standards.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Systems failing to meet the **compatibility status criteria** should be **audited** based on their specific level of compliance
  (full, partial, or non-compliant).
- Any entity found **non-compliant** must immediately **stop using the OBA label** until it corrects its alignment.

---

## B03.CH07.P10.S04 — Scenario and shock stress: forks under crisis pressure

Forks and variants of OBA systems must be evaluated under **scenario and shock stress** to test their resilience under extreme
conditions. This ensures that the integrity of the OBA system is maintained even when subjected to crisis situations, such as
economic collapse, environmental disasters, or geopolitical tensions.

### Actions for enforcement
- Conduct **stress testing** of **forks and variants** to assess whether they maintain OBA compliance during times of crisis.
- Test the **adaptability** of forks to **emergency mandates** and other **crisis regimes** while preserving **fundamental principles**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any fork or variant that cannot withstand **stress scenarios** while maintaining the **core OBA principles** (e.g., caps, commons)
  must be **reclassified** and **re-evaluated** for OBA compliance.

---

## B03.CH07.P10.S05 — Version control: lessons learned without core break (traceable changelog)

As OBA systems evolve, **version control** must be used to track all changes to the system, especially when modifying the
compatibility status or introducing new forks. This ensures that **lessons learned** are captured without introducing core
breaks into the system, preserving OBA integrity over time.

### Actions for enforcement
- Implement a **traceable changelog** that records every modification to the OBA system, noting changes in **compatibility status**
  and the **rationale** behind each modification.
- Any change that alters the **core rails** (non-negotiables) must be properly **documented** and evaluated for compatibility.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Changelog entries** must be **clear, complete**, and **transparent**, showing the evolution of a system's compatibility
  status and **impact analysis** for each modification.
- Any **core breaks** introduced by forks or updates must trigger a **reassessment** of the system's **OBA status**.
