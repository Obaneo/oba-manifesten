# B03.CH07.P09 — Correction mechanisms: what to do under incompatibility or mislabeling

This paragraph outlines the necessary **correction mechanisms** that must be applied when incompatibility or mislabeling of
**OBA status** occurs. It addresses corrective actions for semantic issues (misleading labels), institutional failures (regulatory
capture), legal misrepresentations, and economic misallocations.

Correction mechanisms are critical for **maintaining the integrity** of OBA standards and ensuring that deviations from the core
criteria (the "rails") are detected, rectified, and prevented from undermining the broader governance framework.

---

## B03.CH07.P09.S01 — Semantic correction: enforce relabeling and public rectification

When **semantic drift** or **mislabeling** occurs, the **core rules** must be enforced by requiring **relabeling** and **public
rectification**. Any misuse of OBA status, including "OBA-inspired" or "OBA-compatible" when criteria are not met, must be
corrected to prevent confusion and potential policy distortion.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Relabeling**: Public statement that corrects the OBA status, including rationales for why the previous label was
  incorrect.
- **Rectification**: Ensure the corrected label is reflected in all relevant materials, including public documents, audits,
  and registry listings.
  
### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If the OBA status is **incorrectly assigned**, immediate action is required for **relabeling** and public clarification,
  including publishing a **corrective statement** explaining the discrepancy.
- Any **mislabeling** must be **corrected** in the **official registries** and associated public communication.

---

## B03.CH07.P09.S02 — Institutional correction: audit escalation, hard stops, governance reset

If incompatibility is detected, **institutional correction** becomes necessary. This involves escalation through the
**audit process**, **hard stops** to prevent further damage, and **governance reset** to ensure the integrity of the system.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Audit escalation**: Trigger independent audits to investigate the extent of incompatibility or misrepresentation.
- **Hard stops**: Implement measures to halt further misrepresentation or non-compliance, especially in cases where core
  definitions or decision-making procedures are at risk.
- **Governance reset**: In case of systemic failure, institutions responsible for governance (e.g., oversight bodies) should
  undergo **reconstitution** to restore their ability to uphold OBA standards.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any **institutional failure** to address incompatibility or mislabeling triggers **audit escalation** and **hard stops**.
- If institutional behavior is found to be systematically misaligned with OBA standards, a **governance reset** must be
  initiated to restore proper functioning.

---

## B03.CH07.P09.S03 — Legal correction: sanctions, withdrawal of label, remedial measures

**Legal correction** is required in cases of **fraudulent misrepresentation**, **regulatory capture**, or any intentional efforts
to deceive stakeholders about the true OBA status. This includes **sanctions**, **withdrawal of the OBA label**, and the
imposition of **remedial measures**.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Sanctions**: Financial or legal penalties on entities found to be violating OBA standards or falsely claiming OBA status.
- **Withdrawal of label**: In cases of deliberate mislabeling, the OBA label must be **withdrawn** immediately from the entity
  or practice.
- **Remedial measures**: Require entities found in violation to **correct the issues**, including paying restitution or
  addressing any underlying legal flaws in the system.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a party is found **misrepresenting** OBA status, **sanctions** (including penalties, fines, or legal action) will apply.
- The **OBA label** must be **withdrawn** for any **false claims** or non-compliance, and **corrective legal actions** must
  be enforced as necessary.

---

## B03.CH07.P09.S04 — Economic correction: neutralisation of extraction and contract rollback

In the case of **economic misallocation**, such as when extraction exceeds acceptable limits or contracts are unfairly structured,
**economic corrections** must be implemented. This involves **neutralising unfair extraction** and potentially **rolling back
contracts** that violate OBA principles.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Neutralising extraction**: Taking steps to reverse or reclaim wealth or benefits that were extracted beyond the
  **acceptable limits** defined by OBA (e.g., redistributing excessive profits).
- **Contract rollback**: In cases of **unfair contracts** that violate OBA principles, contracts may need to be **terminated**
  or **rewritten** in accordance with OBA rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any **excessive extraction** beyond the set thresholds of OBA principles must be **neutralised**, including redistribution
  of improperly extracted value.
- Contracts violating OBA principles MUST be subject to **rollback** in order to restore compliance.

---

## B03.CH07.P09.S05 — Exit criteria: when formally “no longer OBA” (link to B03.CH12)

When a fork/variant reaches the point where it **no longer adheres** to the core principles of OBA, it is formally **deemed
incompatible** and must be categorized as **non-OBA**. This includes meeting **exit criteria**, which are formal conditions
under which a system, policy, or entity can no longer be considered part of the OBA framework.

### Actions for enforcement
- **Exit conditions**: Define specific points at which a system or practice must exit the OBA framework, including when it
  cannot comply with the foundational principles (e.g., caps, commons, auditability, contestability).
- **Formal declaration**: A public declaration must be made to **inform stakeholders** that the entity is no longer **OBA-compliant**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If the **exit criteria** are met, the entity, policy, or system must be **officially labeled as incompatible** with OBA.
- The system must **cease using OBA status** and **return to compliance procedures** for reclassification if possible.
