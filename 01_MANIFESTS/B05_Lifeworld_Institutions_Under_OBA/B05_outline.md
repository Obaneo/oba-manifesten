# B05 — Outline (English)

## B05.TITLE — Lifeworld & Institutions under OBA (Work, time, security, and “day-in-the-life” sector cases)

## B05.CH01 — Kernel: the lifeworld layer as a test (from macro-invariants to experienced GROSS/NET/BURDENS)

* B05.CH01.P01 — Purpose and status of the lifeworld layer

  * B05.CH01.P01.S01 — Lifeworld layer as a test layer: not narrative, but a validation interface
  * B05.CH01.P01.S02 — Relation to Kernel Spec and Codex: derived claims, no new definitions
  * B05.CH01.P01.S03 — Why this is not optional: legitimacy, detection of washing, resistance to framing
  * B05.CH01.P01.S04 — Fail-closed principle: if the lifeworld test cannot be executed, the implementation is incomplete

* B05.CH01.P02 — Translation function: from macro-invariants to experienced quantities

  * B05.CH01.P02.S01 — Map: P/E/C → households, contracts, prices, access, maintenance
  * B05.CH01.P02.S02 — Map: B,T,N,L,R → “agenda reality” (what can I pay/do without being clamped)
  * B05.CH01.P02.S03 — Map: caps (W/I) → status incentives, career paths, everyday circumvention routes
  * B05.CH01.P02.S04 — Map: ΔM → visible channel effects (essentials, commons, prohibited asset/claim support)

* B05.CH01.P03 — The minimal “experience set” per household (testable observables)

  * B05.CH01.P03.S01 — Minimum: GROSS (B) and composition (labour/capital/transfers/in-kind)
  * B05.CH01.P03.S02 — Minimum: NET (N) and levies (T), including timing and predictability
  * B05.CH01.P03.S03 — Minimum: BURDENS (L) essentials basket with lock-in/exit-friction markers
  * B05.CH01.P03.S04 — Minimum: RESIDUAL SPACE (R) and R/B profile (incl. volatility and tail-risk)

* B05.CH01.P04 — Lifeworld test as a “contract clamp test”

  * B05.CH01.P04.S01 — Lock-in detection: exit fees, exclusivity, portability, switching-cost proxies
  * B05.CH01.P04.S02 — Toll-layer detection: fee stacks in essentials (housing/energy/care/digital)
  * B05.CH01.P04.S03 — Block-power detection: choke points (platform/rail/identity/logistics) in daily routines
  * B05.CH01.P04.S04 — Remedy implication: unbundling, public option, procurement rails, reclassification

* B05.CH01.P05 — Signal → analysis → norming → intervention as a lifeworld implementation pattern

  * B05.CH01.P05.S01 — Signal discipline: which lived signals count (without data maximalism)
  * B05.CH01.P05.S02 — Analysis: make the causal chain explicit (ΔM→commons→L→R/B) with uncertainty
  * B05.CH01.P05.S03 — Norming: thresholds (α, baseline access, lock-in caps) and conflict rules
  * B05.CH01.P05.S04 — Intervention templates: repeatable interventions (no ad-hoc deals), with measurement and rollback path

* B05.CH01.P06 — CSA as a public correction loop (Collective Situation Audit)

  * B05.CH01.P06.S01 — CSA output: periodic public dashboards from public tables (no “dashboard-only”)
  * B05.CH01.P06.S02 — CSA cadence: periodic + event-driven (shocks, price breaks, governance incidents)
  * B05.CH01.P06.S03 — CSA contestability: citizens/organisations can challenge claims via dispute flow
  * B05.CH01.P06.S04 — CSA anti-capture: audit the guardian, rotation, transparency on exceptions

* B05.CH01.P07 — Non-reductive measurement rails: autonomy, privacy, and contestability

  * B05.CH01.P07.S01 — Ban on reduction: wellbeing/agency is not a single KPI; metrics are bounded signals
  * B05.CH01.P07.S02 — Autonomy rails: measurement duty must not become behavioural coercion or surveillance rent
  * B05.CH01.P07.S03 — Experience evidence: qualitative signals as formal input (with a testable procedure, not “anecdote”)
  * B05.CH01.P07.S04 — Risk: cognitive capture via “well-intended” optimisation; mitigation via plurality and contestability

* B05.CH01.P08 — Evolutionary infrastructure: institutions that can correct without drift

  * B05.CH01.P08.S01 — Modular change: patches, pilots, rollback, deprecation (no semantic break)
  * B05.CH01.P08.S02 — Maintenance-first in institutions: execution capacity as a commons (ΔG of governance)
  * B05.CH01.P08.S03 — Learning systems: feedback into Codex/benchmarks with version control and restatements
  * B05.CH01.P08.S04 — Stability versus adaptation: what is invariant (kernel) and what evolves parametrically (practice)

* B05.CH01.P09 — Lifeworld compatibility: minimum criteria for “OBA works for people”

  * B05.CH01.P09.S01 — Criterion 1: L declines or becomes contestable (exit without clamp) in essentials
  * B05.CH01.P09.S02 — Criterion 2: share_below_alpha declines and tails stabilise (not just median gain)
  * B05.CH01.P09.S03 — Criterion 3: commons access and maintenance visibly improve (ΔG positive and auditable)
  * B05.CH01.P09.S04 — Criterion 4: circumvention is functionally recognised and sanctioned (no sham compliance)

* B05.CH01.P10 — Measurement and publication requirements specific to the lifeworld layer

  * B05.CH01.P10.S01 — Public tables: cohort profiles, essentials indices, lock-in proxies, incidents (aggregated)
  * B05.CH01.P10.S02 — Privacy minimalism: no person tracking; join safety; suppression for small-n
  * B05.CH01.P10.S03 — Reproducibility: method_id, definition-set ID, uncertainty bands, revision policy
  * B05.CH01.P10.S04 — Hard stop: unobservable claims (“trust me”) → no lifeworld-compatibility label

* B05.CH01.P11 — Interface with sector cases and day-in-the-life chapters

  * B05.CH01.P11.S01 — Templates: applying the same test logic to housing/energy/care/digital
  * B05.CH01.P11.S02 — Case structure: before/after, clamps, exit paths, institutional interventions, measurement outputs
  * B05.CH01.P11.S03 — Link to sector canvas: bottlenecks, E sources, commons option, cap compatibility, R/B effect
  * B05.CH01.P11.S04 — Link to Codex: which schemas, validators, and audit packs are mandatory per case

* B05.CH01.P12 — Failure modes of the lifeworld layer (and why they are systemic)

  * B05.CH01.P12.S01 — Technocratic drift: KPIs defeat agency; mitigation via CSA + contestability
  * B05.CH01.P12.S02 — Bureaucratic over-measurement: data maximalism → surveillance and execution failure (non-conforming)
  * B05.CH01.P12.S03 — Narrative capture: cosmetic “day-in-the-life” without measurement linkage → washing
  * B05.CH01.P12.S04 — Selective execution: differences by region/class without transparency → legitimacy breach and sabotage risk

  - B05.CH02.P02.S02 — Commons maintenance as a work category: maintenance is first-class (ΔG), not “overhead”
  - B05.CH02.P02.S03 — Limits on status competition: caps shift incentives from money/status to skill/contribution

- B05.CH02.P03 — Allocation of labour: where people move as E contracts
  - B05.CH02.P03.S01 — E contraction: which functions disappear (tolls, lock-in, rent-seeking, fee-stacks, bullshit work)

  - B05.CH02.P03.S03 — Transition frictions: matching, regional mismatches, skill gaps, timing
  - B05.CH02.P03.S04 — ΔM channel role: public credit channels for reskilling, mobility, and commons capex

- B05.CH02.P04 — Bullshit jobs: definitions, detection, and phase-out logic

- B05.CH02.P05 — Labour power and contract clamp (control/block in work relations)

  - B05.CH02.P05.S04 — Dispute/contestability: fast objection procedures and audits of large employers/sectors

- B05.CH02.P06 — Wage formation and income structure under I-MAX

- B05.CH02.P07 — Security tied to work (and decoupling where necessary)

  - B05.CH02.P07.S02 — Unemployment/transition: rights as stabilisers of R/B, not as discipline devices
  - B05.CH02.P07.S03 — Flex vs precarity: criteria for permissible flexibility (no hidden L)
  - B05.CH02.P07.S04 — Shock resilience: crisis protocols that preserve work continuity without reintroducing E

- B05.CH02.P08 — Reskilling and transition pathways (personal migration through the order)

## B05.CH04 — Security and buffers: income floor, risks, shocks, insurance, and fallback commons

- B05.CH04.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of security and buffers
  - B05.CH04.P01.S01 — Purpose: stabilise R/B tails without opening new extraction channels
  - B05.CH04.P01.S02 — Scope: income floor, buffers, insurance, shock regimes, fallback commons, emergency procedures
  - B05.CH04.P01.S03 — Binding: Definition Set II (B,T,N,L,R), ΔM channel rails, commons standard, evidence standards
  - B05.CH04.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: security that becomes dependent on lock-in, surveillance, or discretionary deals is non-conform

- B05.CH04.P02 — Risk taxonomy (what must be covered)
  - B05.CH04.P02.S01 — Life risks: illness, disability, old age, care dependency, death
  - B05.CH04.P02.S02 — Labour risks: job loss, income volatility, sector transition, skills obsolescence
  - B05.CH04.P02.S03 — Essentials risks: housing-cost shock, energy prices, mobility constraints, digital exclusion
  - B05.CH04.P02.S04 — System shocks: inflation, supply disruptions, financial stress, geopolitical blockades

- B05.CH04.P03 — Income floor as a lifeworld invariant (minimum R)
  - B05.CH04.P03.S01 — Target: structural decline in share_below_alpha; tail-risk as the primary metric
  - B05.CH04.P03.S02 — Instrument mix: cash, in-kind commons, essentials price discipline (L reduction)
  - B05.CH04.P03.S03 — Conditionality: limited, transparent, non-disciplining; no bureaucratic toll
  - B05.CH04.P03.S04 — Relation to work: the floor stabilises transition and strengthens exit power (link CH02)

- B05.CH04.P04 — Household-level buffers (without moral hazard and without clamp dynamics)
  - B05.CH04.P04.S01 — Liquidity buffer: minimum reserve/buffer rules, automatic build-up where possible
  - B05.CH04.P04.S02 — Debt and arrears buffer: early intervention without penalty interest/fee-stacks
  - B05.CH04.P04.S03 — Time buffer: leave/scheduling rights as shock absorbers (link CH03)
  - B05.CH04.P04.S04 — Privacy/agency: no mandatory micromanagement budgeting as a condition

- B05.CH04.P05 — Insurance: public/commons rails versus private toll
  - B05.CH04.P05.S01 — Functional definition: insurance as risk pooling (P) vs premium/claims manipulation (E)
  - B05.CH04.P05.S02 — Baseline coverage: minimum set (health, disability, unemployment, old age)
  - B05.CH04.P05.S03 — Private add-ons: allowed within rails; prohibited lock-in and hidden L via clamp contracts
  - B05.CH04.P05.S04 — Contestability: transparent policy terms, fast objection flow, audit of claim denials

- B05.CH04.P06 — Shock regimes and emergency protocols (temporary, controlled)
  - B05.CH04.P06.S01 — Trigger criteria: measurable (price breaks, shortage indicators, system stress) with public notice
  - B05.CH04.P06.S02 — Temporariness: sunsets, size caps, mandatory unwind plan (no permanent emergency)
  - B05.CH04.P06.S03 — Instruments: essentials price stabilisation, temporary income support, rationing with boundary rules
  - B05.CH04.P06.S04 — Hard stop: emergency regime must not become an asset/claim-support channel (ΔM rails)

- B05.CH04.P07 — Fallback commons: “last resort” infrastructure
  - B05.CH04.P07.S01 — Domains: shelter/housing, energy baseline, food support, care continuity, legal assistance
  - B05.CH04.P07.S02 — Access: fast eligibility with minimal proof burden; non-discrimination
  - B05.CH04.P07.S03 — Continuity: maintenance-first, stock/logistics, crisis staffing
  - B05.CH04.P07.S04 — Anti-stigma: design that normalises access rather than disciplines

- B05.CH04.P08 — Prevention: early-warning in the lifeworld
  - B05.CH04.P08.S01 — Indicators: arrears, waiting-time spikes, essentials-index breaches, incident rates (aggregated)
  - B05.CH04.P08.S02 — Prevention instruments: mediation, restructuring, temporary subsidies with traceability
  - B05.CH04.P08.S03 — Anti-avoidance: prevent prevention from routing through private fee-stacks (non-conform)
  - B05.CH04.P08.S04 — Public correction loop: a CSA-like feedback loop on failing buffers (link CH01)

- B05.CH04.P09 — Administrative friction as hidden L (and how to reduce it)
  - B05.CH04.P09.S01 — Friction types: form burden, waiting time, proof pressure, re-assessments, digital exclusion
  - B05.CH04.P09.S02 — Standardisation: one-stop interface, uniform criteria, machine-readable decision rules
  - B05.CH04.P09.S03 — Anti-bureaucratic toll: procedures that generate friction are redesigned/requalified
  - B05.CH04.P09.S04 — Measurability: friction proxies (lead time, drop-off, appeal rates) without personal profiling

- B05.CH04.P10 — Financing architecture (link to T and ΔM)
  - B05.CH04.P10.S01 — T architecture: contributions as legitimacy, not as toll; transparent destination
  - B05.CH04.P10.S02 — ΔM channel role: shock absorption via allowed channels; leakage tests mandatory
  - B05.CH04.P10.S03 — Ring-fencing: buffers and maintenance budgets are protected from reallocation toward E
  - B05.CH04.P10.S04 — No-arbitrage: security must not be used to evade caps or subsidise E

- B05.CH04.P11 — Public versus local execution (institutions and governance)
  - B05.CH04.P11.S01 — Central minima, local parameters: uniform rights with local implementation within rails
  - B05.CH04.P11.S02 — Contestability: objection/appeal, audit of the guardian, public performance registers
  - B05.CH04.P11.S03 — Anti-capture: no discretionary exception deals; everything via templates and audit packs
  - B05.CH04.P11.S04 — Continuity: execution capacity as infrastructure (ΔG of governance)

- B05.CH04.P12 — Measurement and publication requirements (security as lifeworld test)
  - B05.CH04.P12.S01 — Core statistics: share_below_alpha, tail volatility, arrears rates, uptake, drop-off

## B05.CH18 — Failure modes in the lifeworld: resentment, sabotage, evasion, bureaucratic drift, capture

- B05.CH18.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of lifeworld failure modes
  - B05.CH18.P01.S01 — Purpose: systematic early-warning and stop rules for implementations that “formally comply” but break the lifeworld
  - B05.CH18.P01.S02 — Scope: resentment, sabotage, evasion, bureaucratic drift, capture (local/national/sector)
  - B05.CH18.P01.S03 — Binding: lifeworld test (B,T,N,L,R + tails), contestability, auditability, anti-evasion, and crisis rails
  - B05.CH18.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: when breach indicators repeat, scaling is blocked and remediation is mandatory

- B05.CH18.P02 — Failure-mode taxonomy: five classes and their causal logic
  - B05.CH18.P02.S01 — Resentment: experienced injustice/friction despite macro claims (perception ≠ propaganda, but a signal)
  - B05.CH18.P02.S02 — Sabotage: active interference via non-compliance, strikes, understaffing, slow-roll, vandalism
  - B05.CH18.P02.S03 — Evasion: extraction shifts into new forms/channels (legal/technical/financial/international)
  - B05.CH18.P02.S04 — Drift/capture: institutions shift toward self-preservation, data maximalism, vendor lock-in, exception culture

- B05.CH18.P03 — Resentment: mechanism (why it emerges)
  - B05.CH18.P03.S01 — L stays high or unpredictable: essentials costs do not visibly fall, or volatility/tails rise
  - B05.CH18.P03.S02 — Administrative friction: “rights” exist, but access requires time, shame, or digital hurdles
  - B05.CH18.P03.S03 — Symbolism > materiality: narratives/rituals without measurable improvement (ΔG/L/RB)
  - B05.CH18.P03.S04 — Status shock: old status pathways vanish without new legitimate arenas (link to CH13)

- B05.CH18.P04 — Resentment: detection and indicators (early warning)
  - B05.CH18.P04.S01 — Cohort fractures: certain regions/occupations see L rise or tails worsen relative to median gains
  - B05.CH18.P04.S02 — Friction indicators: cycle times, drop-off, appeal rates, wait-time spikes, erroneous decisions
  - B05.CH18.P04.S03 — “Non-belief” as a signal: mismatch between dashboards and experience reporting (procedurally collected)
  - B05.CH18.P04.S04 — Media/platform signals: rising misinformation susceptibility at concrete friction points (no moralizing)

- B05.CH18.P05 — Resentment: mitigation (what works structurally)
  - B05.CH18.P05.S01 — L-first correction: accelerate essentials price rails/commons exits before additional redistribution narratives
  - B05.CH18.P05.S02 — Friction reduction: one-stop shop, standard procedures, minimal evidentiary burden, fast interim relief
  - B05.CH18.P05.S03 — Transparency with repair: public incident logs + restatements + visible repair path (not “PR”)
  - B05.CH18.P05.S04 — Plurality and contestability: strengthen dissent/challenge infrastructure rather than communications management

- B05.CH18.P06 — Sabotage: mechanism and forms
  - B05.CH18.P06.S01 — Operational sabotage: paper compliance, deliberate degradation of data quality, execution delays
  - B05.CH18.P06.S02 — Labor conflict: failures in key capacities (healthcare, grid ops, administration) in response to overload or status loss
  - B05.CH18.P06.S03 — Political sabotage: carve-outs, exceptions, maintenance budget hollowing, vetoes on interop/standards
  - B05.CH18.P06.S04 — Market sabotage: price manipulation, supply withholding, intensified lock-in by dominant providers

- B05.CH18.P07 — Sabotage: detection and escalation
  - B05.CH18.P07.S01 — Integrity indicators: logging anomalies, missing lineage, signature mismatches, “silent edits”
  - B05.CH18.P07.S02 — Performance fractures: sudden increases in cycle times/backlogs without an external shock
  - B05.CH18.P07.S03 — Concentration/market signals: collusion indicators, margin spikes, deeper fee stacks
  - B05.CH18.P07.S04 — Escalation path: anomaly → forensic audit → governance reset/remedies → (if needed) temporary intervention

- B05.CH18.P08 — Sabotage: mitigation and hard stops
  - B05.CH18.P08.S01 — Capacity-first: ringfence execution capacity and maintenance budgets (ΔG of governance)
  - B05.CH18.P08.S02 — Separation of powers: strictly separate decision/execution/oversight/appeal; rotate key roles
  - B05.CH18.P08.S03 — Procurement rails: standardize vendor lock-in triggers (escrow/open standards) and exit assistance
  - B05.CH18.P08.S04 — Hard stop: proven selective enforcement or integrity breach → immediate suspension of conformance claims

- B05.CH18.P09 — Evasion: core logic (why it is systemic)
  - B05.CH18.P09.S01 — Incentives shift: caps/rails change payoffs; actors search for new claim routes
  - B05.CH18.P09.S02 — Semantic arbitrage: new labels and contract forms attempt to bypass definitions
  - B05.CH18.P09.S03 — Channel innovation: new rails (fintech/crypto), new intermediation, new bundles
  - B05.CH18.P09.S04 — International arbitrage: treaty mismatch, re-domiciliation, transfer pricing, IP structures

- B05.CH18.P10 — Evasion: detection (shift taxonomy in a lifeworld context)
  - B05.CH18.P10.S01 — Legal shifts: reclassification into services/licenses/consultancy; franchise and royalty stacks
  - B05.CH18.P10.S02 — Technical shifts: bundling, dark patterns, API gating, pay-to-unlock, identity as a clamp
  - B05.CH18.P10.S03 — Financial shifts: securitization, fee stacks, synthetics, leverage as hidden exposure
  - B05.CH18.P10.S04 — International shifts: treaty shopping, IP-box, nominee ownership, offshore conduits (UBC graphs)

- B05.CH18.P11 — Evasion: remedies (reclassification and channel control)
  - B05.CH18.P11.S01 — Reclassification: substance-over-form, look-through, presumptions under opacity (fail-closed)
  - B05.CH18.P11.S02 — Contract remedies: prohibited clauses, standard templates, unbundling, take-rate caps, portability mandates
  - B05.CH18.P11.S03 — Channel remedies: tagging, leakage tests, forbidden channels, rail governance with due process
  - B05.CH18.P11.S04 — Versioning: rapid updates via Codex + transparent precedent registry (without core break)

- B05.CH18.P12 — Bureaucratic drift: mechanism (how “good intentions” fail)
  - B05.CH18.P12.S01 — Metric drift: measurement becomes the goal; Goodhart shifts behavior toward scores instead of lifeworld gains
  - B05.CH18.P12.S02 — Over-measurement and data maximalism: privacy breach + execution load (administrative L) rises
  - B05.CH18.P12.S03 — Procedure inflation: exceptions, counters, evidentiary burden grow; access becomes a gatekeeping ecosystem
  - B05.CH18.P12.S04 — Tooling capture: systems become complex and vendor-dependent; contestability disappears

- B05.CH18.P13 — Bureaucratic drift: detection and correction
  - B05.CH18.P13.S01 — L via administration: rising cycle time, paperwork burden, drop-off, appeal backlog
  - B05.CH18.P13.S02 — “Dashboard without remedy”: lots of reporting, little redesign, few restatements
  - B05.CH18.P13.S03 — Privacy incidents: scope creep, secondary use, identity-linkage without necessity
  - B05.CH18.P13.S04 — Correction: simplification sprints, data-minimalism audits, deprecate procedures, tooling unbundling

- B05.CH18.P14 — Capture: forms (economic, political, epistemic)
  - B05.CH18.P14.S01 — Economic capture: vendors/sectors steer procurement, standards, exceptions (carve-outs)
  - B05.CH18.P14.S02 — Political capture: emergency regimes without sunsets, selective enforcement, maintenance budget hollowing
  - B05.CH18.P14.S03 — Epistemic capture: “experts” monopolize definitions/benchmarks; dissent becomes impossible
  - B05.CH18.P14.S04 — Cultural capture: status networks replace money caps via gatekeeping (link to CH13)

- B05.CH18.P15 — Capture: detection, evidence, and hard stops
  - B05.CH18.P15.S01 — Red flags: repeated exceptions for the same positions, revolving-door patterns, vendor dominance graphs
  - B05.CH18.P15.S02 — Audit signals: skewed audit coverage, suppressed findings, missing incident disclosure
  - B05.CH18.P15.S03 — Public accountability: conformance downgrade + forensic audit + governance reset protocol
  - B05.CH18.P15.S04 — Hard stop: proven capture → immediate suspension of discretionary powers and template reset

- B05.CH18.P16 — Human agency: why failure modes cannot be “measured away”
  - B05.CH18.P16.S01 — Agency as a constraint: people react to clamps, shame, uncertainty; that is causality, not noise
  - B05.CH18.P16.S02 — Limits on nudging/scoring: behavioral steering via monitoring creates new E axes and legitimacy fracture
  - B05.CH18.P16.S03 — Experience signals: procedurally embedded qualitative input is required alongside quantitative metrics
  - B05.CH18.P16.S04 — Implication: design must expand choice/exit; do not attempt to “optimize” behavior via totalization

- B05.CH18.P17 — Evolutionary infrastructure: failure modes as fuel for controlled iteration
  - B05.CH18.P17.S01 — Patch cycle: detect → hypothesis → intervention → audit → public restatement → deprecation
  - B05.CH18.P17.S02 — Backward compatibility: changes improve the lifeworld without a semantic core break
  - B05.CH18.P17.S03 — Rollback discipline: harm to L/tails/privacy/integrity triggers immediate rollback
  - B05.CH18.P17.S04 — Speed vs stability: fast Codex updates, slow core updates (Kernspec remains canonical)

- B05.CH18.P18 — Integration: how CH18 makes the rest of Book 5 enforceable
  - B05.CH18.P18.S01 — Link to sector cases (CH17): every sector case includes explicit failure-mode checks and stop rules
  - B05.CH18.P18.S02 — Link to day-in-the-life (CH14–CH16): cases act as policy regression tests
  - B05.CH18.P18.S03 — Link to local institutions (CH12): contestability/auditability are primary, not optional
  - B05.CH18.P18.S04 — Link to transition conflict (Book 2): sabotage/retaliation scenarios and mitigations are predefined

- B05.CH18.P19 — Measurement and publication requirements (early-warning dashboard)
  - B05.CH18.P19.S01 — Resentment proxies: friction indices, appeal volumes, drop-off, wait times, cohort fractures
  - B05.CH18.P19.S02 — Sabotage proxies: integrity alerts, execution anomalies, incident rates, capacity collapses
  - B05.CH18.P19.S03 — Evasion proxies: shift indicators, fee-stack depth, offshore/synthetic exposure (aggregated)
  - B05.CH18.P19.S04 — Capture proxies: exceptions register, vendor dominance, audit coverage anomalies, revolving-door stats

- B05.CH18.P20 — Close: stop rules and minimal repair paths
  - B05.CH18.P20.S01 — Stop rule A: rising essentials-L or worsening tails without an external shock → halt scaling
  - B05.CH18.P20.S02 — Stop rule B: privacy/integrity breach (silent edits, unsigned changes, scope creep) → immediate rollback
  - B05.CH18.P20.S03 — Stop rule C: proven selective enforcement or capture → governance reset + conformance suspension
  - B05.CH18.P20.S04 — Repair path: forensic audit → restatement → template redesign → recertification (no “muddling through”)
