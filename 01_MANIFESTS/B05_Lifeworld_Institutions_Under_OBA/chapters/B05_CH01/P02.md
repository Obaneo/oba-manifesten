# B05.CH01.P02 — Translation function: from macro-invariants to experienced quantities

This paragraph specifies the **translation function** that makes the lifeworld layer executable: a deterministic mapping from
macro-level invariants and classifications to **experienced quantities** at the household/contract/access interface. The purpose is
not to “humanise” the Kernel, but to ensure that macro compliance produces **observable** and **contestable** outcomes in everyday
life: prices paid, access obtained, maintenance delivered, exit friction faced, and clamp pressure on action space.

The translation function is a **bridge rule**: it does not introduce new definitions. It binds canonical objects (P/E/C, B/T/N/L/R,
caps W/I, ΔM channels) to a fixed set of lifeworld observables and required publication surfaces. If the mapping cannot be executed
with the specified inputs, the lifeworld layer fails closed and the implementation is incomplete (see B05.CH01.P01.S04).

---

## B05.CH01.P02.S01 — Map: P/E/C → households, contracts, prices, access, maintenance

P/E/C are macro classifications, but they are experienced through concrete interfaces: contracts, tariffs, queues, eligibility rules,
quality minima, and maintenance backlogs. The translation function therefore requires a mapping from each P/E/C object to the
household-facing mechanisms that generate costs, constraints, and exposure.

### Mapping targets (household interface)
- **Contracts**: wage/fee arrangements, tenancy/lease, service subscriptions, credit/insurance, platform terms, procurement-driven
  access agreements.
- **Prices and tariffs**: what is paid per unit, the structure of fees (fixed/variable), and the presence of stacked toll layers.
- **Access and eligibility**: queues, rationing rules, boundary criteria, exceptions, and appeal rails.
- **Maintenance and reliability (ΔG at the interface)**: uptime, repair times, incident rates, backlog visibility, and service minima.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- For each essential domain covered by an implementation, the mapping MUST identify the household-facing **contract forms** and
  **access rules** through which P/E/C classifications are realised (no “macro-only” compliance).
- The mapping MUST include a maintenance/reliability observable set (service minima + incident/backlog proxies) sufficient to detect
  deterioration being used as an informal clamp.

## B05.CH01.P02.S02 — Map: B,T,N,L,R → “agenda reality” (what can I pay/do without being clamped)

B/T/N/L/R become politically relevant only when they correspond to an individual’s or household’s **agenda reality**: the actionable
space after levies and essential burdens, under volatility and tail-risk. The translation function defines how the macro objects
appear as lived constraints.

### Agenda reality as a derived interface
- **GROSS (B)**: income flows and composition (labour/capital/transfers/in-kind) as the starting resource envelope.
- **LEVIES (T)**: timing and predictability of deductions, contributions, and mandatory charges (including quasi-fiscal fees).
- **NET (N)**: disposable amount after levies, with timing and volatility explicitly represented.
- **BURDENS (L)**: essentials basket costs plus lock-in/exit friction markers (fees, deposits, switching barriers).
- **RESIDUAL SPACE (R)**: remaining action space after essentials, including volatility and tail-risk (shock sensitivity).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Implementations MUST publish cohort-level (aggregated) B/T/N/L/R profiles sufficient to evaluate whether **R increases** and clamp
  pressure decreases, not merely whether medians move.
- The mapping MUST treat timing and predictability as first-class: a “net gain” that arrives late or unpredictably is a clamp risk
  and must be visible in the lifeworld outputs.

## B05.CH01.P02.S03 — Map: caps (W/I) → status incentives, career paths, everyday circumvention routes

Caps W/I are macro rails, but they generate lived incentives: which status goods are pursued, which career paths dominate, and which
circumvention behaviours emerge when caps are binding or arbitraged. The lifeworld layer must therefore translate caps into observable
social and behavioural surfaces without collapsing into surveillance or moral judgement.

### Incentive surfaces (non-behaviourist, observable)
- **Status incentive map**: which scarce positions and rewards become dominant targets under the cap regime (signals of “winning”).
- **Career path pressure**: credential races, gatekeeping professions, monopolised ladders, and paid access to opportunity.
- **Circumvention routes**: off-balance perks, fee-stack evasions, reclassification games, bundled benefits, and shadow markets.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The mapping MUST identify the primary **cap-pressure surfaces** where circumvention is likely (contract forms, perks, bundled
  access, reclassification points) and specify observable proxies that can be audited at aggregate level.
- Any detected systematic circumvention route MUST be treated as a lifeworld failure signal and MUST trigger correction mechanisms
  (reclassification, unbundling, disclosure rails, sanctions), not “acceptance as culture.”

## B05.CH01.P02.S04 — Map: ΔM → visible channel effects (essentials, commons, prohibited asset/claim support)

ΔM channel rules are experienced as concrete routing effects: who pays, who receives, what is subsidised, what becomes cheaper or more
available, and whether hidden asset/claim support re-enters via intermediaries. The translation function requires channel visibility
at the lifeworld surface.

### Channel effects at the interface
- **Essentials effects**: price changes, fee-stack removal, access expansion, reliability/maintenance improvements, queue dynamics.
- **Commons effects**: open-access availability, boundary rules, service minima, contestability, and maintenance discipline.
- **Prohibited support detection**: asset/claim support and opacity reappearing through complex intermediaries, bundled contracts, or
  “temporary” exceptions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- For any ΔM-relevant instrument or routing decision, the mapping MUST specify the expected household-facing effect on essentials and
  commons access/reliability, and the observable outputs used to confirm it.
- The mapping MUST include prohibited-support detection hooks at the lifeworld surface (fee stacks, bundling, intermediary opacity,
  lock-in reintroduction); if these cannot be observed, the implementation fails closed for lifeworld compatibility claims.
