# B05.CH01.P05 — Signal → analysis → norming → intervention as a lifeworld implementation pattern

This paragraph specifies the **lifeworld implementation pattern** as a disciplined pipeline: **signal → analysis → norming → intervention**.
Its purpose is to prevent two symmetric failures: (1) narrative politics (“stories” without testable linkage to order objects), and
(2) technocratic overreach (data maximalism and behavioural coercion disguised as measurement). The lifeworld layer is therefore an
**execution interface**: it turns lived experience into **bounded, testable signals**, maps them to causal hypotheses over canonical
objects (ΔM, commons, burdens L, residual space R/B), and then produces **normed thresholds** and **repeatable interventions** with
measurable rollback paths.

The pattern is explicitly **anti-ad-hoc**. If a lifeworld claim cannot be expressed as: (a) a signal definition, (b) a causal chain
with uncertainty, (c) a normed threshold and conflict rule, and (d) an intervention template with measurement and rollback, then it
is not an implementation pattern but a political gesture.

---

## B05.CH01.P05.S01 — Signal discipline: which lived signals count (without data maximalism)

Signals are lived-world observables that are **meaningful, bounded, and testable**. They are not “everything that can be tracked,”
and they are not free-form anecdotes. Signal discipline defines **what counts**, **how it is captured**, and **what it is allowed
to imply**.

### Signal classes (illustrative; bounded)
- **Access signals**: inability to obtain baseline access (housing, energy, care, digital), queue times, denial rates, rationing.
- **Burdens signals (L)**: sudden essential cost spikes, fee-stack growth, arrears incidence, forced downgrade patterns.
- **Lock-in signals**: exit-fee incidence, portability failures, switching delays, contract term traps.
- **Block-power signals**: black-box denials, deplatforming without appeal, forced bundling/terms acceptance for essentials.
- **Commons service signals (ΔG)**: incident rates, maintenance backlogs, degradation of minima, repair time distributions.

### Data-minimal capture rules
- Prefer **aggregated, cohort-level** capture over person-level tracking.
- Prefer **event-based** capture (incidents, denials, fee changes) over continuous surveillance.
- Require **procedural testability** for qualitative inputs (structured collection + coding rules + appeal/verification path).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every lifeworld signal MUST have a `signal_id`, a definition, an observation method, and an explicit scope boundary (what it does
  *not* claim).
- Signals that require individual behavioural tracking, identity linkage across domains, or “always-on monitoring” are non-conform
  unless explicitly justified under privacy minimalism rails (and then still default-disallowed in essentials contexts).

## B05.CH01.P05.S02 — Analysis: make the causal chain explicit (ΔM→commons→L→R/B) with uncertainty

Signals become governance-relevant only through analysis that makes the **causal chain explicit**, including uncertainty. The goal is
not to “prove” a story, but to produce **decision-grade hypotheses** that can be tested, contested, and corrected.

### Causal chain discipline (canonical objects by reference)
- **ΔM channel hypothesis**: which instrument/channel structures plausibly create the observed clamp/toll/denial pattern.
- **Commons linkage**: whether absence/weakness of a commons option (or ΔG decay) explains persistence of burdens or lock-in.
- **Burdens L effect**: how the mechanism raises essential burdens (levels, volatility, tails) and who is exposed.
- **Residual space R/B effect**: how the burden changes the feasible life agenda (what can be done without clamp), including tail risk.

### Uncertainty requirements
- State confounders and alternative explanations explicitly (market shock, supply constraints, enforcement change, seasonality).
- Use bands/intervals where possible (not point certainty), and label what is assumed vs observed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any analysis output MUST include: (a) signal set used, (b) hypothesised mechanism chain, (c) uncertainty/confounders, and (d)
  testable predictions that would falsify or downgrade the hypothesis.
- “Dashboard-only” correlation without a mechanism statement is non-conform for lifeworld governance purposes.

## B05.CH01.P05.S03 — Norming: thresholds (α, baseline access, lock-in caps) and conflict rules

Norming turns analysis into governance rails by specifying **thresholds** and **conflict rules**. It is where politics legitimately
chooses parameters—*within canonical constraints*—without redefining the canon.

### Norm objects (illustrative)
- **α-related thresholds**: tolerable share-below-α levels, tail-risk triggers, cohort deterioration alarms.
- **Baseline access minima**: definitional minima for essentials access (service levels, waiting times, denial-rate caps).
- **Lock-in caps**: maximum permissible exit fees, maximum switching times, portability obligations, bundling prohibitions.
- **Incident thresholds**: triggers for escalation, audits, temporary controls, or emergency procedures (with sunset discipline).

### Conflict rules (mandatory)
- **Scarcity allocation**: how to prioritise access under scarcity without hidden gatekeeping.
- **Trade-off governance**: how to resolve conflicts between affordability, quality, and investment without ad-hoc carve-outs.
- **Appeal rails**: who can challenge thresholds, based on what evidence, and how revisions are logged (restatement discipline).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every threshold MUST have: `threshold_id`, unit definition, measurement method, review cadence, and a revision policy.
- Any norming that relies on discretionary exceptions without sunset/rollback is treated as drift-prone and must be escalated as a
  governance risk.

## B05.CH01.P05.S04 — Intervention templates: repeatable interventions (no ad-hoc deals), with measurement and rollback path

Interventions must be **templated**: repeatable packages that specify action, measurement, governance, and rollback. The purpose is
to prevent “case-by-case” bargaining from becoming a hidden extraction channel or a political patronage surface.

### Template structure (minimum)
- **Trigger**: which signals/threshold breaches activate the template.
- **Mechanism target**: which clamp/toll/block-power mechanism is being neutralised.
- **Action set**: allowed actions (e.g., unbundling mandate, exit-fee cap, portability enforcement, procurement rail change,
  commons/public-option activation, reclassification procedure).
- **Measurement pack**: which signals must move, over what horizon, and what counts as improvement vs displacement.
- **Governance rails**: decision log requirements, auditability, appeal path, and exception handling.
- **Rollback/deprecation**: conditions to revert, scale down, or replace the intervention; sunset by default unless re-authorised.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No intervention may be executed as a one-off “deal” without a template; deals without templates are treated as non-conform because
  they cannot be audited or replicated.
- Every intervention MUST define rollback triggers and must publish post-intervention restatements (what changed, what was learned,
  and what parameters were revised).
