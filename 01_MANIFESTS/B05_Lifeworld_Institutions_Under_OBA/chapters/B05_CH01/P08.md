# B05.CH01.P08 — Evolutionary infrastructure: institutions that can correct without drift

This paragraph specifies the **evolutionary infrastructure** required for the lifeworld layer to correct reality-mismatches without
sliding into semantic drift, discretionary patchwork, or “dashboard governance.” The problem is structural: once the lifeworld test
produces friction, institutions will be pressured to (a) redefine terms, (b) cherry-pick signals, or (c) ship ad-hoc deals that
silence complaints without repairing clamp dynamics. The lifeworld layer therefore requires a correction architecture that is
**modular, reversible, versioned, and maintenance-first**.

Evolution here is not “continuous reinvention.” It is a controlled change system: **patches and pilots** are permitted, but they must
be traceable, rollbackable, and non-breaking at the semantic level. Learning must feed into the Codex/benchmarks with explicit
restatements, while kernel invariants remain stable and only **parametric practice** evolves.

---

## B05.CH01.P08.S01 — Modular change: patches, pilots, rollback, deprecation (no semantic break)

Corrections MUST be implemented as modular change units that preserve meaning by ID and allow reversible experimentation. The system
assumes that some interventions will fail or have second-order effects; irreversibility is therefore treated as a governance risk.

### Change primitives (minimum set)
- **Patch**: bounded modification to thresholds, procedures, or mappings within existing semantics.
- **Pilot**: time- and scope-limited deployment with explicit success/failure criteria and exit plan.
- **Rollback**: pre-defined reversal procedure with triggers, authority, and restoration of prior state.
- **Deprecation**: retiring a practice or method while preserving interpretability of historical records.

### No semantic break rule
- Definitions and invariants MUST NOT be silently changed via “implementation updates.”
- If meaning changes, it MUST be expressed as a new version with explicit restatement and compatibility labeling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every change MUST have: change_id, scope, start date, owner role, hypothesis, success metrics, failure triggers, and rollback plan.
- Any change without an executable rollback path MUST be treated as high-risk and requires escalation-grade authorisation (and may be
  prohibited for constitutive rails).

## B05.CH01.P08.S02 — Maintenance-first in institutions: execution capacity as a commons (ΔG of governance)

The lifeworld layer fails most often not because signals are missing, but because **execution capacity** decays: backlogs accumulate,
audits become symbolic, disputes stall, and exceptions replace maintenance. Execution capacity is therefore treated as a **commons of
governance**: it must have explicit maintenance obligations, resourcing rules, and degradation indicators (ΔG of governance).

### Governance maintenance doctrine
- **Backlog visibility**: publish queues for disputes, audits, restatements, and remediation tasks.
- **Ring-fenced capacity**: minimum staffing/budget floors for the correction loop; no discretionary starvation.
- **Incident discipline**: shocks and clamp signals create mandatory workorders, not optional “reviews.”
- **Anti-burnout rails**: prevent participation and audit systems from collapsing under overload.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If dispute/audit/remediation backlogs exceed published limits (or if publication ceases), the implementation MUST be treated as
  failing the lifeworld layer (fail-closed until capacity is restored).
- Exception use that substitutes for routine maintenance (repeat carve-outs, repeated “temporary” waivers) MUST trigger escalation
  and an institutional ΔG repair plan.

## B05.CH01.P08.S03 — Learning systems: feedback into Codex/benchmarks with version control and restatements

Lifeworld corrections generate knowledge: where clamps form, how washing manifests, which proxies fail, and which interventions
actually shift L, R/B tails, or commons maintenance. That learning must be absorbed without rewriting history or creating local
dialects.

### Learning pipeline (minimum)
- **Evidence capture**: store cases as auditable records (signals → analysis → decision → intervention → measured outcomes).
- **Codex feedback**: propose updates to schemas, validators, benchmarks, and templates using version control.
- **Restatements**: when methods or mappings change, publish restatements so historical series remain interpretable.
- **Cross-case comparability**: maintain stable cohort definitions and mapping IDs unless explicitly versioned.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any update to benchmarks/methods MUST include: prior version reference, change rationale, expected effect, and restatement policy.
- “Quiet fixes” (method changes without restatement) are treated as washing surfaces and trigger audit escalation.

## B05.CH01.P08.S04 — Stability versus adaptation: what is invariant (kernel) and what evolves parametrically (practice)

Evolution must respect a strict boundary: **kernel invariants** remain stable; **practice** evolves parametrically within those
rails. The lifeworld layer enforces this boundary by treating some objects as constitutive (non-negotiable) and others as
parameterisable (tunable).

### Invariant set (examples of what cannot drift here)
- Canonical definitions and classification tests (Kernel Spec / Book 1 by reference).
- The fail-closed principle for untestable claims.
- Contestability, auditability, and reproducibility requirements for lifeworld compatibility labeling.

### Parametric practice (examples of what may evolve)
- Threshold levels and bands (α, lock-in caps) within published decision rails.
- Proxy sets and sampling strategies, provided they remain bounded, versioned, and contestable.
- Intervention templates and sequencing choices, provided rollbackability and measurement linkage are maintained.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any attempt to “solve” lifeworld failure by redefining invariants is non-conform; adaptation must operate via parameters or
  interventions, not via semantic escape.
- Every adaptive change MUST declare whether it is (a) parametric within invariants, or (b) a versioned update requiring explicit
  restatement and compatibility labeling.
