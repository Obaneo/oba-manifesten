# B05.CH01.P12 — Failure modes of the lifeworld layer (and why they are systemic)

This paragraph specifies the primary **failure modes** of the lifeworld layer and treats them as **systemic** risks rather than
accidents. The lifeworld layer exists to bind macro claims to experienced reality under evidence and contestability rails. When it
fails, the overall implementation becomes vulnerable to **washing**, **capture**, and **legitimacy collapse**—even if the macro
framework remains formally intact.

Failure modes are systemic because they are produced by predictable incentives: optimisation pressure, bureaucratic self-expansion,
political shielding, vendor lock-in, and narrative competition. The lifeworld layer therefore requires explicit mitigations (CSA,
contestability, privacy minimalism, publication discipline, anti-selective enforcement) and must fail-closed when those mitigations
cannot be executed.

---

## B05.CH01.P12.S01 — Technocratic drift: KPIs defeat agency; mitigation via CSA + contestability

Technocratic drift occurs when the lifeworld layer is reduced to a dashboard regime: a small set of KPIs becomes the surrogate for
“people’s lived reality,” and optimisation against those KPIs begins to **reshape behaviour** and **override agency**. The system
then optimises *for the metric* rather than *for the order constraint*.

### Drift mechanism
- **Single-axis dominance**: one index becomes decisive, sidelining plural signals and contextual exceptions.
- **Optimisation pressure**: agencies and vendors learn to hit the metric while preserving clamp mechanics elsewhere.
- **Agency defeat**: people are treated as inputs to be managed rather than participants with contestable claims.

### Mitigation (binding)
- CSA MUST operate as the correction loop: publish plural tables, accept challenges, force restatements.
- Contestability MUST be real: disputes can trigger audits, schema reviews, and remedy pipelines (not just “feedback”).
- Any KPI used for governance MUST be paired with anti-gaming checks and a published uncertainty discipline.

---

## B05.CH01.P12.S02 — Bureaucratic over-measurement: data maximalism → surveillance and execution failure (non-conforming)

Bureaucratic over-measurement happens when the lifeworld layer expands into a comprehensive data appetite: “if we measure everything,
we can govern better.” This produces two coupled failures: (1) **surveillance rent** risk and autonomy erosion, and (2) **execution
collapse** due to measurement overload, low signal-to-noise, and inability to maintain pipelines.

### Failure mechanism
- **Data maximalism**: collection expands beyond minimal observables; joins become unsafe; proxies multiply.
- **Operational overload**: measurement obligations exceed institutional capacity; updates lag; outputs become stale or unverifiable.
- **Privacy breach risk**: increased identifiability, coercive measurement duties, and vendor-controlled data infrastructure.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- The lifeworld layer MUST be data-minimal by design (no person tracking; join safety; suppression for small-n).
- If minimal privacy and execution constraints cannot be met, the lifeworld layer MUST be treated as **non-conforming** and MUST
  fail-closed (no compatibility label).

---

## B05.CH01.P12.S03 — Narrative capture: cosmetic “day-in-the-life” without measurement linkage → washing

Narrative capture occurs when “day-in-the-life” content becomes a legitimacy theatre: compelling stories are used to imply success,
while the measurement linkage (B/T/N/L/R mappings, lock-in proxies, publication tables, reproducibility) is absent or optional.
The result is washing: an implementation claims people-centeredness while keeping clamps intact.

### Capture mechanism
- **Anecdote substitution**: stories replace observables and causal chains.
- **Selective showcase**: cherry-picked cohorts/regions are highlighted; adverse signals are excluded.
- **Non-reproducibility**: no method_id, no definition-set reference, no restatement policy—claims cannot be checked.

### Hard rule (binding)
- Narrative without measurement linkage is **non-conform**: day-in-the-life outputs MUST reference the case template outputs and
  published tables (B05.CH01.P10), including uncertainty and revision policy.
- If a claim cannot be re-executed as a test instance, it MUST not be used as evidence of compatibility.

---

## B05.CH01.P12.S04 — Selective execution: differences by region/class without transparency → legitimacy breach and sabotage risk

Selective execution is the most politically destabilising failure mode: the lifeworld layer is applied unevenly across regions,
classes, or identity-linked groups, while public reporting masks or normalises these divergences. This produces legitimacy breach,
strategic resistance, and sabotage dynamics (people conclude the system is a façade or a tool for targeted discipline).

### Failure mechanism
- **Uneven enforcement**: clamp neutralisation, access improvements, or remedies occur only where politically convenient.
- **Hidden divergence**: aggregation hides distributional splits; suppression is misused as opacity.
- **Cynicism loop**: affected groups disengage, circumvent, or actively undermine governance mechanisms.

### Mitigation (binding)
- Publication MUST include distributional visibility: cohort and region breakdowns within join-safety constraints.
- Exceptions MUST be transparent (exception registers + sunset/rollback discipline).
- If selective execution cannot be detected and corrected, the implementation MUST be treated as incompatible pending remediation.
