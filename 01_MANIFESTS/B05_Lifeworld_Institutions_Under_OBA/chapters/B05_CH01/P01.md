# B05.CH01.P01 — Purpose and status of the lifeworld layer

This paragraph defines the **purpose** and **normative status** of the lifeworld layer in Book 5: it is the **validation interface**
that translates macro-invariants into experienced, testable quantities, and thereby determines whether an OBA-aligned order is
actually **liveable, contestable, and non-clamping** in everyday essentials.

The lifeworld layer is treated as **order-critical** because macro compliance can coexist with lived extraction via fee stacks,
lock-in, and block power embedded in contracts and access channels. Therefore, the lifeworld layer exists to prevent “compatibility”
from collapsing into model-claims, dashboards, or rhetorical frames. It forces implementations to show—using reproducible methods and
publicly auditable outputs—what happens to **GROSS (B), NET (N), BURDENS (L), and RESIDUAL SPACE (R)** at the household interface.

Delimitation is strict: canonical definitions, calculation rules, and economic classification logic remain in the **Kernel Spec** and
the **Codex**. This chapter specifies the **test obligation**: which lifeworld observables must exist, under what publication rails,
and how claims become contestable without turning measurement into surveillance.

---

## B05.CH01.P01.S01 — Lifeworld layer as a test layer: not narrative, but a validation interface

The lifeworld layer is not “communication” and not “case storytelling.” It is a **formal test layer** whose function is to validate
that macro invariants manifest as **experienced reductions in clamp and extraction** across essentials and common routines.

### Functional position
- **Not narrative**: stories may illustrate, but do not count as evidence unless mapped to testable observables and procedures.
- **Validation interface**: it connects the invariant layer to lived constraints: what can households pay, access, exit, and contest.
- **Clamp detection**: it surfaces hidden toll layers (fee stacks), lock-in (exit friction), and block power (choke points) that
  can persist even when macro aggregates look “improved.”

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any “lifeworld” claim MUST be expressed as a **testable statement** (observable + method + scope + uncertainty), not as a vignette.
- Implementations MUST maintain a defined set of lifeworld observables (B/N/L/R and essential access/exit proxies) sufficient to
  execute clamp and toll-layer detection.

---

## B05.CH01.P01.S02 — Relation to Kernel Spec and Codex: derived claims, no new definitions

This chapter does not define new primitives. It derives **lifeworld claims** from canonical definitions and calculation rules, and
binds them to publication and contestability rails.

### Scope boundary
- **Kernel Spec is canonical**: definitions, invariants, and computation rules are imported by reference.
- **Codex is operational canon**: schemas, validators, audit packs, method identifiers, restatement rules, and reproducibility rails
  constrain how lifeworld outputs are produced and revised.
- **Derived claims only**: lifeworld statements must be derivable from canonical definitions and from the specified mapping function
  (macro → household/contract/access observables).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any lifeworld artifact MUST reference the relevant **definition-set ID + version** and **method_id** (Codex) rather than restating
  meaning locally.
- If a lifeworld claim cannot be traced to canonical definitions and a reproducible method, it MUST be treated as **non-conform**
  (semantic drift risk).

## B05.CH01.P01.S03 — Why this is not optional: legitimacy, detection of washing, resistance to framing

Without a lifeworld test, compatibility claims become vulnerable to **washing**, selective dashboards, and framing battles. The
lifeworld layer is therefore a legitimacy and anti-drift requirement: it makes the order **answerable** to people’s experienced
constraints in essentials and daily routines.

### Why it matters (order logic)
- **Legitimacy**: governance is only stable if people can see and contest how the order affects burdens, access, and exit capacity.
- **Washing detection**: macro improvements can hide household-level regressions via contract clamps, toll layers, and scarcity gating.
- **Framing resistance**: the test layer anchors debate in observable changes (with uncertainty), reducing capture by slogans,
  selective anecdotes, or partisan re-labeling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any implementation claiming compatibility MUST publish lifeworld outputs sufficient to detect: (i) lock-in/exit friction, (ii) fee
  stacks/toll layers in essentials, and (iii) block-power choke points affecting routine access.
- Lifeworld outputs MUST be contestable via a defined dispute flow (challenge → evidence → review → restatement) rather than
  “trust-based” acceptance.

---

## B05.CH01.P01.S04 — Fail-closed principle: if the lifeworld test cannot be executed, the implementation is incomplete

The lifeworld layer operates under **fail-closed** rules. If the test cannot be executed, claims about “works for people” are not
merely uncertain; they are invalid as compatibility assertions.

### Fail-closed triggers (non-exhaustive)
- Missing minimal observables (B/N/L/R and essentials access/exit proxies).
- No method discipline (no method_id, no definition-set version pinning, no reproducibility path).
- No publication surface (outputs exist only as internal dashboards or narrative reports).
- No contestability rails (no dispute flow, no restatement policy, no audit packs).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If fail-closed triggers apply, the implementation MUST be classified as **incomplete** and MUST NOT claim lifeworld compatibility
  until remediation is delivered.
- Scaling or mandate expansion MUST be blocked until the lifeworld test is executable and produces auditable, contestable outputs.
