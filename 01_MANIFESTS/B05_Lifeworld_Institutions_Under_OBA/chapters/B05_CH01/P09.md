# B05.CH01.P09 — Lifeworld compatibility: minimum criteria for “OBA works for people”

This paragraph defines **lifeworld compatibility** as a **minimum, testable acceptance condition** for any implementation that claims
OBA conformance. It is not a popularity metric, not a narrative of wellbeing, and not an invitation to redefine economic canon. It is
a **hard interface test**: do households, in lived practice, experience a measurable reduction of clamp dynamics and an increase of
contestability, such that the order is not merely “correct on paper” but operationally **non-extractive and governable**.

Lifeworld compatibility is therefore expressed as **criteria**, not slogans. Criteria are evaluated against published methods and
public tables (with bounded privacy rails), and they are fail-closed: if the data and procedures needed to evaluate them are missing,
the compatibility label cannot be granted.

---

## B05.CH01.P09.S01 — Criterion 1: L declines or becomes contestable (exit without clamp) in essentials

The first compatibility condition is that **BURDENS (L)** in essentials (housing, energy, care, digital access, basic mobility where
applicable) either **declines** or becomes **functionally contestable**, meaning households can exit or switch without punitive clamp.

### What counts (binding)
- **Decline**: essentials burden ratios improve (levels or distribution), or service minima increase without hidden toll layers.
- **Contestability**: households can switch providers/arrangements with bounded friction; lock-in proxies fall (exit fees, exclusivity,
  portability barriers, switching costs).

### What does not count
- Cosmetic subsidies that leave toll positions intact and reappear as higher fees/rents.
- “Choice” without portability or with punitive exit conditions (choice-as-clamp).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Essentials domains MUST publish lock-in/exit-friction proxies and burden indices sufficient to test “exit without clamp.”
- If L does not decline and contestability does not improve, the implementation MUST be treated as lifeworld-incompatible until
  a remedy path is executed.

---

## B05.CH01.P09.S02 — Criterion 2: share_below_alpha declines and tails stabilise (not just median gain)

Compatibility is distributional. It is not satisfied by median improvements if the bottom share remains clamped or if tail-risk grows.
The second condition is that **share_below_alpha declines** and **tails stabilise**, i.e., fewer households fall below the defined
minimum viability threshold and extreme downside events become rarer or less severe.

### What counts (binding)
- Reduction in the fraction of households below α (by cohort), not just aggregate uplift.
- Tail stabilisation: reduced volatility and fewer extreme negative shocks in R/B profiles (and/or faster recovery with bounded harm).

### What does not count
- “Average” gains that coincide with increased tail losses, regional divergence, or cohort-specific clamp persistence.
- Reclassification tricks that move people across labels without changing lived constraints.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Published cohort tables MUST support testing share_below_alpha and tail-risk indicators (with uncertainty bands where relevant).
- If median improves but share_below_alpha does not fall (or tail-risk increases), the implementation MUST be flagged as failing the
  lifeworld layer, triggering escalation and redesign.

---

## B05.CH01.P09.S03 — Criterion 3: commons access and maintenance visibly improve (ΔG positive and auditable)

Because commons are the primary non-extractive substitute for toll positions, lifeworld compatibility requires visible improvement in
**commons access** and **commons maintenance**. This is not “more programs.” It is a measurable shift toward **rule-based access with
service minima** and **maintenance discipline** (ΔG positive and auditable).

### What counts (binding)
- Increased access within explicit boundary rules (fewer arbitrary denials, more predictable service).
- Maintenance backlogs decline or are actively governed with published lifecycle discipline and incident thresholds.
- Auditability: decisions, exceptions, and performance are evidenced through public registers/tables.

### What does not count
- “Commons” claims that rely on vendor lock-in, black-box allocation, or maintenance decay masked by dashboards.
- Access expansions that degrade service minima without transparent rationing rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons domains MUST publish ΔG-relevant maintenance indicators (backlog, uptime/quality minima, incident counts, response times)
  and the governance evidence needed to audit them.
- If ΔG is negative or un-auditable, the commons contribution to lifeworld compatibility fails by definition.

---

## B05.CH01.P09.S04 — Criterion 4: circumvention is functionally recognised and sanctioned (no sham compliance)

The lifeworld layer treats circumvention as a primary washing channel: actors comply “on paper” while preserving extraction through
side contracts, fee stacks, bundling, intermediated claims, or selective enforcement. The fourth condition is that **circumvention is
functionally recognised** (detected as the same economic function) and **sanctioned or neutralised** through repeatable remedies.

### What counts (binding)
- Detection regimes that identify functional equivalents (not just label matches): fee-stack reconstruction, reclassification triggers,
  prohibited channel flags, and evidence-based enforcement.
- Remedies that neutralise the extraction function (unbundling, portability mandates, procurement rails, contract invalidation where
  applicable), not symbolic fines that are priced in.
- Public contestability: citizens/organisations can challenge sham compliance via dispute flows.

### What does not count
- Enforcement focused on “bad actors” narratives while leaving the circumvention template intact.
- Exception regimes that become permanent carve-outs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Implementations MUST publish circumvention indicators and enforcement outcomes (aggregated) sufficient to demonstrate that sham
  compliance is not the dominant equilibrium.
- If circumvention persists without functional recognition and remedy, the implementation MUST be labeled lifeworld-incompatible,
  regardless of claimed headline outcomes.
