# B05.CH01.P04 — Lifeworld test as a “contract clamp test”

This paragraph specifies the lifeworld test operationally as a **contract clamp test**: a systematic procedure to detect whether
households are *functionally clamped* by contract structures, fee architectures, and choke points—regardless of how benign the
macro story sounds. The lifeworld layer does not treat “choice” as a given; it treats **exitability** as the primitive, and it
treats the erosion of exitability as a measurable drift signal.

A clamp is present when a household’s feasible action set is reduced by **lock-in**, **toll layers**, or **block-power choke points**
such that the household must pay (or comply) under conditions that are not contestable, substitutable, or escapable without
disproportionate loss. The contract clamp test is therefore the frontline detector for washing: macro compliance claims that ignore
micro-level coercion via contracts.

---

## B05.CH01.P04.S01 — Lock-in detection: exit fees, exclusivity, portability, switching-cost proxies

Lock-in is the primary mechanism by which nominal competition becomes irrelevant in lived life. The clamp test detects lock-in by
measuring **exit cost**, **exit delay**, and **exit feasibility** (not merely the existence of “alternatives”).

### Lock-in markers (non-exhaustive; domain-adapted)
- **Exit fees and penalties**: termination fees, early repayment charges, “administration” penalties, forfeited deposits.
- **Exclusivity constraints**: required bundling, exclusive supply clauses, non-competes (direct or functional), forced single-homing.
- **Portability constraints**: number portability, data portability, identity portability, contract transfer restrictions.
- **Switching-cost proxies**:
  - time-cost (paperwork hours, waiting times),
  - coordination cost (multi-party approvals, landlord/insurer/utility dependencies),
  - uncertainty cost (opaque final bills, unpredictable reconciliations),
  - risk cost (service interruption, credit damage, loss of entitlements).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any sector or regime claiming lifeworld compatibility MUST publish lock-in markers for each essentials domain where contracts govern
  access; absence of lock-in data is treated as non-observability and therefore non-conform.
- Where portability is technically feasible but contractually blocked, the blockage MUST be treated as a clamp signal (not as a
  “business model choice”).

## B05.CH01.P04.S02 — Toll-layer detection: fee stacks in essentials (housing/energy/care/digital)

Toll layers are extraction surfaces that hide behind “service provision” while behaving like controlled access gates. The clamp test
detects toll layers by decomposing total burdens into **base price + mandatory add-ons + conditional penalties + platform takes**.

### Toll-layer markers (non-exhaustive)
- **Fee stacks**: fixed fees, metering fees, “service” charges, account fees, mandatory memberships, handling fees.
- **Conditional penalties**: late fees, reconnection fees, administrative penalties, price discrimination via default traps.
- **Bundling and paid priority**: required bundles for baseline access; priority access sold as “premium” for essentials.
- **Intermediation takes**: platform take-rates, broker commissions embedded in access, procurement spreads that behave as tolls.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Essentials reporting MUST separate base price from fee stacks; “all-in” numbers without decomposition are non-conform for clamp
  detection because they obscure the extraction mechanism.
- Any recurring fee layer without a contestable cost basis (or without an exit path) MUST be flagged as a toll candidate and routed
  to remedy analysis.

## B05.CH01.P04.S03 — Block-power detection: choke points (platform/rail/identity/logistics) in daily routines

Block-power exists when an actor controls a choke point such that it can **deny, delay, degrade, or condition access** in a way that
forces compliance or payment. In the lifeworld layer, block-power is detected by mapping daily routines to the infrastructures that
cannot be practically bypassed.

### Choke point classes (illustrative)
- **Platform choke points**: app stores, payment rails, marketplaces, dominant intermediaries for jobs/housing/services.
- **Rail choke points**: energy networks, transport corridors, telecom last-mile, payment and identity rails.
- **Identity choke points**: digital ID, KYC regimes, credit scoring gatekeepers, credentialing systems tied to essentials access.
- **Logistics choke points**: last-mile delivery monopolies, warehouse networks, routing/dispatch layers for services.

### Detection markers (minimum)
- **Denial power**: rate of denied access, unexplained refusals, deplatforming/blacklisting patterns.
- **Conditioning power**: forced acceptance of terms unrelated to the service (data extraction, bundling, arbitration-only).
- **Degradation power**: throttling, queue discrimination, shadow bans, service downgrades as leverage.
- **Contestability deficit**: absence of appeal rails, lack of logs, non-reproducible decisions (“black-box denial”).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Where a choke point is essential to daily routines, the implementation MUST specify contestability rails (logs, appeal, timing,
  remedy paths). Black-box denial in essentials contexts is treated as a hard clamp signal.
- A system that shifts block-power from prices to identity/terms enforcement is non-conform even if headline prices fall.

## B05.CH01.P04.S04 — Remedy implication: unbundling, public option, procurement rails, reclassification

The clamp test is not only diagnostic; it is **remedy-directing**. Each clamp class maps to a constrained remedy set, prioritising
structural exitability over moral condemnation or “consumer education.”

### Remedy mapping (typical)
- **Lock-in → exitability restoration**
  - ban/cap exit fees,
  - enforce portability,
  - shorten minimum terms,
  - standardise contract interfaces,
  - require default unbundled offers.
- **Toll layers → de-layering and cost-basis discipline**
  - unbundle mandatory add-ons,
  - cap take-rates in essentials,
  - mandate fee transparency + cost basis,
  - prohibit paid priority for baseline access.
- **Block-power → governance and contestability**
  - enforce logs and appeal rails,
  - impose non-discrimination and due-process constraints,
  - require interoperability/open access protocols,
  - create bypass rails (public option / multi-rail redundancy).
- **Structural mislabeling → reclassification**
  - requalify “service fees” as extraction where functionally toll-like,
  - reclassify contracts (employment vs fee-stack sham; public utility vs platform gate),
  - treat disguised claim/asset support or prohibited channels as non-conform.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- For each detected clamp, the implementation MUST specify a remedy path with measurable rollback criteria; “awareness” or voluntary
  codes do not satisfy remedy requirements.
- If clamp remedies require discretionary exceptions or opaque enforcement, the remedy regime itself MUST be treated as a new clamp
  surface (escalate to governance/audit rails).
