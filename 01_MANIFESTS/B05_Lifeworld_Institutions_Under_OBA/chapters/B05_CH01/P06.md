# B05.CH01.P06 — CSA as a public correction loop (Collective Situation Audit)

This paragraph specifies **CSA (Collective Situation Audit)** as the lifeworld layer’s **public correction loop**: a repeatable,
institutionalised process that turns lifeworld signals, analysis, and interventions into **contestable, versioned public claims**.
CSA exists to prevent three failure modes that otherwise dominate governance: (1) **private dashboards** without reproducible inputs,
(2) **closed discretion** (“trust me” claims) under political or vendor pressure, and (3) **narrative capture** where symbolic
stories replace auditable changes in burdens, access, and clamp dynamics.

CSA is not “communications.” It is an **audit-like publication regime**: outputs are derived from **public tables** (with privacy
minimalism), tied to definition-set IDs, and structured so external parties can reproduce, critique, and dispute claims. CSA is also
explicitly **anti-capture**: it treats the audit function itself as a capture surface and therefore requires rotation, transparency
on exceptions, and audit-of-auditors (“audit the guardian”).

---

## B05.CH01.P06.S01 — CSA output: periodic public dashboards from public tables (no “dashboard-only”)

CSA outputs are **dashboards as views**, not as authority. The authority is the **public tables** (and their definition sets,
methods, and revision policy). Dashboards are permitted only insofar as they are a navigational layer over reproducible sources.

### Required CSA output bundle (minimum)
- **Public tables**: aggregated cohort profiles and indices (B, T, N, L, R/B), essentials baskets, lock-in proxies, incident counts,
  commons service/maintenance proxies (ΔG where applicable), and channel indicators linked to ΔM categories.
- **Dashboard views**: summaries, distributions, tail indicators, and threshold breach reports that can be traced to table rows.
- **Method pack**: `method_id`, `definition_set_id`, sampling/aggregation rules, uncertainty bands, suppression rules (small-n), and
  comparability notes.
- **Restatements**: revision log for data/method changes; “what changed and why” is mandatory.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any CSA dashboard element MUST link to the underlying public table and method metadata. “Dashboard-only” outputs are non-conform.
- Any published claim MUST be reproducible from the public tables under the referenced method/definition set; otherwise it triggers
  a hard-stop classification (“unobservable claim”).

## B05.CH01.P06.S02 — CSA cadence: periodic + event-driven (shocks, price breaks, governance incidents)

CSA runs on a dual cadence: **periodic** (to create stable comparability) and **event-driven** (to prevent delayed recognition of
clamp dynamics and governance failures). The cadence is a governance parameter, but it must be explicit and logged.

### Periodic cadence (baseline)
- Regular publication cycle (e.g., monthly/quarterly) for core tables and summaries.
- Scheduled review windows for threshold updates and intervention template performance.

### Event-driven triggers (minimum set)
- **Shock triggers**: sudden price breaks in essentials, supply disruptions, or rapid volatility spikes that affect L or R/B tails.
- **Governance incidents**: exception explosion, audit failure, vendor outage/lock-in incident, black-box decision controversy.
- **Clamp signals**: abrupt rise in lock-in proxies, denial rates, fee-stack growth, or block-power incidents in daily routines.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- CSA MUST define trigger thresholds and response timelines (publish within X days of trigger; provisional output allowed but must be
  restated).
- Failure to publish under a trigger is treated as an institutional failure mode and must be recorded as such (no silent omission).

## B05.CH01.P06.S03 — CSA contestability: citizens/organisations can challenge claims via dispute flow

CSA is constitutively **contestable**. The point is not “public consultation” but a formal **dispute flow** that allows external
parties to challenge: data quality, method choices, classification, threshold setting, and claimed causal chains. Contestability is
the anti-drift rail that prevents a closed measurement priesthood.

### Dispute flow (minimum)
- **Standing**: citizens, organisations, and affected role-groups may file disputes under a defined procedure.
- **Grounds**: (a) reproducibility failure, (b) method mismatch, (c) definition-set misuse, (d) threshold misapplication, (e) omitted
  confounders, (f) selective reporting, (g) exception abuse.
- **Process**: intake → triage → public docket → response requirement → escalation path → resolution and restatement.
- **Remedies**: correction, re-publication, method revision, threshold revision, audit trigger, or intervention rollback.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- CSA MUST publish a dispute register (with privacy-safe handling) and MUST log resolutions and restatements.
- If disputes cannot be processed (capacity loss, legal blockage, tooling failure), CSA must **fail-closed**: affected claims cannot
  be used for compatibility labeling or mandate justification until contestability is restored.

## B05.CH01.P06.S04 — CSA anti-capture: audit the guardian, rotation, transparency on exceptions

CSA treats the auditing/publishing function as a **capture surface**. Therefore CSA includes explicit anti-capture rails: audit the
auditor, rotate authority positions, and expose exception usage. The purpose is to prevent “measurement governance” from becoming a
new veto layer.

### Anti-capture rails (minimum)
- **Audit the guardian**: independent review of CSA methods, sampling, classification, and dispute handling; publish findings and
  required corrections.
- **Rotation**: time-bounded appointments for key CSA roles; cooling-off and conflict-of-interest rules; diversity of oversight.
- **Exception transparency**: every exception to publication, method, or threshold application must be logged, sunset-bound, and
  publicly visible in aggregated form (no permanent carve-outs).
- **Vendor/lock-in safeguards**: reproducibility must not depend on a single vendor toolchain; exportability and open method specs
  are mandatory.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A CSA without audit-of-auditors, rotation, and exception transparency MUST be treated as drift-prone and therefore non-conform for
  lifeworld compatibility claims.
- Exception growth, dispute suppression, or repeated reproducibility failures are escalation triggers that must activate governance
  intervention templates (including potential reclassification of the implementation status).
