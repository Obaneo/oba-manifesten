# B05.CH01.P10 — Measurement and publication requirements specific to the lifeworld layer

This paragraph specifies the **measurement and publication surface** required to execute the lifeworld layer as a **testable**
validation interface. The lifeworld layer is not satisfied by internal dashboards, consultant reports, or narrative claims. It
requires **publicly auditable outputs** that allow third parties to reproduce the evaluation of lifeworld compatibility criteria
(B05.CH01.P09) within strict privacy rails.

The lifeworld layer therefore imposes **publication duties** (what must exist) and **method duties** (how it must be produced). These
duties are intentionally minimal in data scope yet maximal in auditability: the goal is to make claims contestable without turning
the population into a tracking substrate.

---

## B05.CH01.P10.S01 — Public tables: cohort profiles, essentials indices, lock-in proxies, incidents (aggregated)

The lifeworld layer MUST publish a bounded set of **public tables** sufficient to test burdens, contestability, tails, and commons
maintenance outcomes. Outputs MUST be cohort-based and aggregated; they exist to support reproduction, comparison, and dispute, not
individual scoring.

### Required public tables (minimum set; binding)

**1) Cohort profiles**
- Cohorts defined by stable, privacy-safe dimensions (e.g., region bands, household type bands, income/wealth bands, tenure bands),
  with explicit cohort_id grammar.
- For each cohort: B/T/N/L/R summary statistics, distributions (quantiles), volatility/tail markers, and uncertainty bands.

**2) Essentials indices**
- Essentials basket indices for housing/energy/care/digital (and other locally-defined essentials where applicable), including:
  - level indices, burden ratios, and scarcity/service minima markers,
  - decomposition where possible (price vs fees vs taxes vs non-cash requirements),
  - time series sufficient to observe shocks and recoveries.

**3) Lock-in and exit-friction proxies**
- For each essentials domain, publish lock-in proxies such as:
  - exit fees, minimum terms, exclusivity prevalence, portability availability, switching cost markers,
  - complaint-to-switch ratios, cancellation friction indicators, and other bounded, testable proxies.

**4) Incident and governance outputs (aggregated)**
- Incident counts and severity bands (service failures, price breaks, governance incidents),
- exception counts and reasons (bounded taxonomy),
- enforcement outcomes and remedy types (aggregated), including time-to-remedy distributions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Public tables MUST be sufficient to test the B05.CH01.P09 criteria per cohort (including tails, not just averages).
- “Dashboard-only” outputs (screens without tables / exportable data) MUST be treated as non-conform.

## B05.CH01.P10.S02 — Privacy minimalism: no person tracking; join safety; suppression for small-n

The lifeworld layer is executed under strict privacy rails. Publication MUST be compatible with **data minimalism** and MUST not
create a surveillance rent surface. The goal is contestability, not behavioural governance.

### Privacy rails (binding)

**No person tracking**
- No individual-level longitudinal identifiers in published outputs.
- No publication of raw event logs that allow reconstruction of individual trajectories.

**Join safety**
- Publication MUST avoid enabling linkage across tables that re-identifies persons or micro-groups.
- Where joins are necessary for reproducibility, they MUST be performed on cohort_id and time windows only, with documented join rules.

**Small-n suppression**
- Any cell below a published minimum threshold MUST be suppressed or coarsened.
- Rare-event reporting MUST use severity banding and time aggregation to avoid singling out.

**Data minimism by design**
- Only collect and publish what is required to execute the test criteria; additional “nice to have” metrics MUST be treated as suspect
  unless explicitly justified against surveillance and capture risk.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If privacy rails cannot be maintained at the required publication surface, the lifeworld layer MUST fail-closed (see S04): claims
  cannot be labeled compatible.

## B05.CH01.P10.S03 — Reproducibility: method_id, definition-set ID, uncertainty bands, revision policy

All published lifeworld outputs MUST be reproducible. Reproducibility here means: an independent evaluator can recompute the published
tables from the declared method and inputs (within the allowed access regime), and can detect when methods or definitions change.

### Required reproducibility metadata (binding)
- **method_id**: stable identifier of computation method/version for each table (including dependencies).
- **definition-set ID + version**: canonical references for all terms used (Kernel Spec / Book 1); no local redefinitions.
- **Uncertainty bands**: confidence/uncertainty reporting appropriate to measurement method (sampling error, model uncertainty,
  imputation notes, known blind spots).
- **Revision policy**: explicit restatement rules (what triggers a revision, how revisions are logged, how prior versions remain
  accessible and comparable).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any table without method_id and definition-set references MUST be treated as non-auditable.
- Revisions MUST be logged as restatements, not silent overwrites; silent changes are treated as drift/capture indicators.

---

## B05.CH01.P10.S04 — Hard stop: unobservable claims (“trust me”) → no lifeworld-compatibility label

The lifeworld layer is fail-closed. If the minimal publication surface or reproducibility rails are missing, then lifeworld claims are
**unobservable**, and unobservable claims are non-conform by definition.

### Hard-stop rule (binding)
- If an implementation cannot publish the required public tables (S01) under the privacy rails (S02), with reproducibility metadata
  (S03), then it MUST NOT receive any **lifeworld-compatibility label** and MUST be treated as incomplete.

### Implication
- “Trust me” governance (closed computation, proprietary dashboards, withheld tables, non-reproducible indices) is treated as a
  structural failure mode, not a temporary inconvenience.
- The only acceptable response is remediation: reduce scope, simplify methods, adjust cohorting, or redesign measurement so the test
  becomes executable under minimal data and maximal auditability.
