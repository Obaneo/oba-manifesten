# B05.CH01.P03 — The minimal “experience set” per household (testable observables)

This paragraph defines the **minimal experience set**: the smallest set of household-facing observables required to execute the
lifeworld test without slipping into narrative or data maximalism. The lifeworld layer is only as strong as its inputs; therefore
the experience set is defined as **testable**, **auditable**, and **publishable in aggregate**. It is deliberately minimal so that it
can be maintained as a public correction loop (CSA) without turning measurement into surveillance rent.

The experience set is a **measurement contract**: it specifies what must be observable for any implementation to claim that macro
order invariants are real in lived life. If any element is missing, unmeasurable, or non-reproducible, lifeworld compatibility
claims MUST fail closed (no “trust me” substitution).

---

## B05.CH01.P03.S01 — Minimum: GROSS (B) and composition (labour/capital/transfers/in-kind)

Households experience the system first as **gross inflow** (B), but B is only meaningful if composition is visible: labour, capital,
transfers, and in-kind access are structurally different exposures and produce different clamp risks.

### Required observables (minimum)
- **B_total**: total gross inflow over a defined window (with period definition).
- **B_composition**: shares by source class:
  - labour income,
  - capital income,
  - public transfers,
  - in-kind entitlements/access (valued via a declared method_id, not ad hoc).
- **Volatility marker for B**: variance/dispersion indicator over the period (cohort-level), sufficient to detect unstable inflows.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any published B measure MUST include composition; a single gross number without source structure is non-conform for lifeworld tests.
- In-kind valuation MUST reference a declared method_id and definition-set version; otherwise the input is treated as non-reproducible.

---

## B05.CH01.P03.S02 — Minimum: NET (N) and levies (T), including timing and predictability

The lifeworld clamp is rarely about annual totals; it is about **timing**, **predictability**, and the ability to plan. Therefore N
and T must be observable as flows with schedule properties, not merely as end-of-year aggregates.

### Required observables (minimum)
- **T_total**: total levies and mandatory charges (taxes, contributions, quasi-fiscal fees) over the same period as B.
- **T_timing profile**: when levies hit (with cadence markers: monthly/quarterly/event-driven) and presence of cliff effects.
- **T_predictability marker**: indicator of variance between expected and realised levies (rule volatility / surprise charges).
- **N_total**: net disposable amount over the period (B − T), consistent with canonical definitions (referenced, not restated).
- **N_timing profile**: when N is actually available (payment timing, withholding, arrears).
- **N_predictability marker**: stability indicator (variance + tail events), sufficient to detect “net on paper, clamp in practice.”

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- N and T MUST be published with timing/predictability markers; annual-only publication is insufficient for lifeworld compatibility.
- Any regime relying on discretionary or opaque levy adjustments MUST disclose the adjustment class and frequency; otherwise the test
  input is treated as non-conform.

---

## B05.CH01.P03.S03 — Minimum: BURDENS (L) essentials basket with lock-in/exit-friction markers

L is the experienced pressure point: the essentials basket plus the **frictions** that prevent substitution and exit. The experience
set must therefore include both price/burden levels and lock-in markers that detect clamp via contracts.

### Required observables (minimum)
- **L_essentials basket**: aggregated burden for the essentials set (as defined canonically elsewhere), with domain breakdown:
  housing, energy, care, digital connectivity, mobility/logistics (where relevant to the implementation scope).
- **Fee-stack marker**: presence/extent of layered fees (fixed fees, service fees, platform takes, mandatory bundles).
- **Lock-in / exit-friction markers** (domain-appropriate, cohort-level):
  - exit fees / penalties,
  - deposit size and recovery time,
  - exclusivity/portability constraints,
  - switching costs (administrative + monetary proxies),
  - minimum term / renewal traps.
- **Scarcity/queue marker**: access constraints that behave like a price (waiting time, rationing frequency, denial rates).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any essentials measure MUST include at least one lock-in/exit-friction marker per domain where contracts govern access; otherwise L
  becomes cosmetically “cheap” while clamp is hidden in exit barriers.
- If the essentials basket definition is changed locally (instead of referenced canonically), the implementation is non-conform.

## B05.CH01.P03.S04 — Minimum: RESIDUAL SPACE (R) and R/B profile (incl. volatility and tail-risk)

R is the lifeworld action space: what remains after essentials and levies, under uncertainty. The lifeworld layer treats R not as a
comfort metric but as a **governability metric**: insufficient or unstable R produces susceptibility to coercion, capture narratives,
and forced compliance via clamp.

### Required observables (minimum)
- **R_total**: residual space over the period, derived consistently from published B/T/L (no hidden adjustments).
- **R/B ratio**: residual relative to gross, as the primary clamp indicator (cohort-level distribution, not just mean).
- **R_volatility marker**: variance/dispersion of residual space over time (instability = clamp risk).
- **Tail-risk marker**: frequency/severity proxy for “R collapse” events (e.g., residual below a defined floor for a defined window),
  with the floor referenced to a canonical threshold (α or equivalent) where applicable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- R MUST be published as a distribution with tail markers; a single average R is non-conform because it hides clamp dynamics.
- If R cannot be derived reproducibly from the published inputs (B, T, L), lifeworld compatibility claims MUST fail closed.
