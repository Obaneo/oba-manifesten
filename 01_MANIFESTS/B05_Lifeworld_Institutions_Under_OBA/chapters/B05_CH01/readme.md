# B05.CH01 — Kernel: the lifeworld layer as a test (from macro-invariants to experienced GROSS/NET/BURDENS)

This chapter defines the **lifeworld layer** as the **human-facing validation interface** of OBA: it turns macro-invariants into
experienced, testable quantities and makes “OBA works” **auditable at the level of households, routines, and essentials**.
It is not a storytelling layer and it is not a new definition layer. It is a **compatibility test layer** that detects washing,
drift, and clamp dynamics that remain invisible in macro aggregates.

The lifeworld layer binds the order to what people can actually do: **GROSS (B)**, **NET (N)**, **BURDENS (L)**, and **RESIDUAL SPACE (R)**,
and how these behave under shocks (tails). It forces implementations to surface lock-in, fee stacks, and block power in everyday
domains (housing, energy, care, digital), and it requires **fail-closed** behavior: if the lifeworld test cannot be executed with
reproducible methods and public tables, the implementation is incomplete and may not claim compatibility.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **claiming OBA compatibility**: treat CH01 as the minimum proof obligation that the order is felt as lower clamp and
  higher exit capacity in essentials, not only as a macro narrative.
- If you are **designing institutions and measurement**: use CH01 to derive the minimal lifeworld observables, publication rails,
  privacy minimalism rules, and contestability procedures (dispute flow, audit packs, restatements).
- If you are **building sector cases or day-in-the-life chapters**: use CH01 as the template discipline: every case must map back
  to B/T/N/L/R, clamp indicators, and ΔM/commons effects, with before/after outputs and uncertainty.
- If you are **auditing implementations**: use CH01 as the early-warning surface for drift: rising administrative friction, hidden
  fee-stack regrowth, selective execution across cohorts/regions, and “dashboard-only” claims.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for Book 5 because it specifies the **compatibility test** that prevents OBA from degenerating into
macro-claims, PR, or technocratic dashboards. CH01 does not redefine the kernel; it **binds it** to lived observables and to
procedures that remain contestable under disagreement.

Any implementation that:
- cannot publish the minimal lifeworld tables (with method_id, definition-set IDs, uncertainty bands),
- cannot diagnose lock-in/fee stacks/block power in essentials,
- or cannot support dispute flow and restatement,
must be treated as **fail-closed**: it may not claim lifeworld compatibility and may not scale without redesign.
