# B05.CH14.P05 — Shock moments (pre-OBA): what breaks the R/B tail

This paragraph specifies the **shock moments** used in the pre-OBA baseline case to expose **tail fragility**: how small disturbances propagate through lock-ins, fee stacks, and administrative friction to push **R/B** below viability thresholds. Shocks are not “story beats”; they are **standardized stressors** used to (i) reveal where block power bites, (ii) quantify cascade multipliers, and (iii) establish measurable *before/after* improvements under OBA rails.

---

<a id="b05ch14p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH14.P05.S01 — Small shock: broken washing machine/phone → emergency credit → fee cascade

A small durable-goods failure is a common trigger for cascading extraction in low R/B households because replacement is time-sensitive (work/care) and savings buffers are thin.

### Standard shock definition
- `shock_type`: `small_durable_failure`
- `trigger_event`: {washing_machine_break, phone_failure, essential_appliance_failure}
- `shock_cost_gross`: replacement/repair price band
- `urgency_level`: high (service disruption is immediate)
- `buffer_available_pre`: emergency liquidity available at baseline (band)

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Emergency credit access path:
  - `credit_channel` ∈ {overdraft, payday_short_term, BNPL, informal_loan, credit_card, pawn, employer_advance}
  - `access_gate`: eligibility checks, speed-to-cash, platform/identity dependence
- Fee cascade surface:
  - origination fees, late fees, rollover fees, collections fees
  - penalty triggers tied to timing mismatch (T) and admin friction

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** model the shock as a **cashflow timeline** (T-sensitive), not a single cost number.
- The case **MUST** itemize the **cascade multipliers** (fees/penalties/interest) separately from principal.
- If emergency credit is used, the case **MUST** report whether repayment creates secondary arrears (rent/energy/digital), i.e., spillover into essentials lock-in.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-SHK-01 (Timeline):** shock includes a pay-timing timeline with at least two due dates (repair/replace vs bills).
- **DL-SHK-02 (Cascade itemization):** fees/penalties are broken out from principal.
- **DL-SHK-03 (Spillover):** presence/absence of arrears spillover is explicitly declared.

## B05.CH14.P05.S02 — Time shock: sick child/schedule change → income dip + extra childcare costs

Time shocks reveal schedule capture and the fragility of income streams under variable hours and caregiving constraints.

### Standard shock definition
- `shock_type`: `time_shock`
- `trigger_event`: {child_sickness, daycare_closure, mandatory_appointment, schedule_change}
- `income_impact`: hours lost band + probability of shift replacement
- `expense_impact`: extra childcare/transport band
- `admin_load_delta`: additional calls/forms required (time band)

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Income volatility channel:
  - employer/agency discretion, cancellation policy, platform deactivation risk
- Care-cost channel:
  - availability of affordable childcare alternatives, cancellation fees, last-minute pricing
- Contract penalty channel:
  - missed payments due to income timing → late fees → arrears → threat escalation

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** represent income loss as both **B reduction** and **T shift** (delayed/partial pay), where applicable.
- The case **MUST** record whether the household has any contestable accommodation rights (schedule predictability, sick leave) and whether they are exercisable in practice.
- The case **MUST** capture the administrative time burden produced by the shock (because time scarcity is a primary lock-in amplifier).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-TIME-01 (B+T effect):** income dip is expressed as B change and/or timing change (T).
- **DL-TIME-02 (Care-cost visibility):** childcare/transport delta is stated.
- **DL-TIME-03 (Time burden):** admin load delta is expressed as a measurable proxy (steps/time band).

## B05.CH14.P05.S03 — Price shock: energy/food rises → arrears → disconnection threat

Price shocks reveal how essentials inflation or tariff jumps translate into arrears and threat-based discipline, especially under opaque surcharges and penalty regimes.

### Standard shock definition
- `shock_type`: `price_shock_essentials`
- `trigger_event`: {energy_tariff_jump, food_price_jump, surcharge_introduction}
- `price_delta`: % band or absolute band (domain-specific)
- `bill_due_mismatch`: whether timing aligns with pay cycles (T interaction)
- `arrears_threshold`: the minimum missed amount that triggers enforcement actions (if known)

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Energy disconnection regime:
  - notice period, grace window, reconnection fee, prepayment trap
- Food substitution regime:
  - ability to substitute without hidden costs (travel/time/health constraints are noted but not used as success KPIs)
- Penalty surface:
  - late fees, admin fees, “service charges”, escalation steps

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** report the enforcement threshold and timeline: from price jump → arrears → threat notice → disconnection risk.
- The case **MUST** separate base price change from added surcharges/fees (opacity is flagged as a lock-in marker).
- The case **MUST** record whether dispute paths exist (billing error challenge, tariff switch) and whether they are practical under time/identity constraints.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-PRC-01 (Timeline to threat):** arrears and disconnection timeline is stated (bands acceptable).
- **DL-PRC-02 (Base vs fees):** base price delta is separated from surcharges/penalties.
- **DL-PRC-03 (Contestability):** availability and viability of switching/dispute are declared.

## B05.CH14.P05.S04 — Administrative shock: clawback, wrong decision, long appeal cycle

Administrative shocks expose the asymmetry between fast enforcement and slow correction: a core tail driver in low R/B households where errors become quasi-permanent due to friction and threat.

### Standard shock definition
- `shock_type`: `administrative_shock`
- `trigger_event`: {clawback_notice, benefit_suspension, wrong_decision, verification_loop}
- `amount_at_risk`: band
- `enforcement_speed`: time-to-withholding/collection band
- `correction_speed`: time-to-appeal-decision band
- `proof_burden`: document steps/requirements band

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Cashflow seizure:
  - withholding, offsetting, automatic debits, account freezes (if applicable)
- Appeal friction:
  - deadlines, documentation, portal access/identity lockout, call waits
- Secondary cascades:
  - arrears triggered by lost income → penalties → collections → compounding lock-in

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** express administrative shock as an asymmetry: `enforcement_speed` versus `correction_speed`.
- The case **MUST** include a measurable proxy for proof burden (steps/time/deadlines) and identify any identity access dependency.
- The case **MUST** identify whether provisional relief exists (suspension of enforcement during dispute); if absent, the absence is itself a tail amplifier.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-ADM-01 (Speed asymmetry):** enforcement and correction speeds are both present.
- **DL-ADM-02 (Proof burden):** proof burden proxy is present.
- **DL-ADM-03 (Provisional relief):** presence/absence of provisional relief is explicitly declared.

---

### Output format (shock summary)

At minimum, the baseline case **MUST** emit a shock summary table (or structured list) with:

- `shock_type` | `domain` | `trigger_event` | `B/T impact` | `L impact` | `cascade_multiplier` | `threat_vector` | `recovery_time_band`

If shocks cannot be linked to the lock-in map and fee inventory (P03/P04), the case is non-conform under CH14 fail-closed rules.
