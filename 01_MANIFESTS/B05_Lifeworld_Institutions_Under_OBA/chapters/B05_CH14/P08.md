# B05.CH14.P08 — New day structure: time, work, and care after friction reduction

This paragraph specifies the **post-OBA day structure** as an observable consequence of reduced lock-in, collapsed fee stacks, and improved contestability. The intent is not lifestyle narration but **structural time accounting**: how schedule predictability, administrative burden, and real exit options change the household’s usable time and decision space. “Health and stress” are treated as **experience signals** with procedural input—not as success KPIs.

---

<a id="b05ch14p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH14.P08.S01 — Schedule predictability: less schedule capture; better compatibility with care

Schedule predictability is a primary determinant of R/B viability in low R/B households: volatile hours and last-minute changes create hidden costs (care, transport, penalties, missed appointments) and amplify lock-in.

### Required fields (template)
- `work_schedule_mode_pre` and `work_schedule_mode_post` ∈ {fixed, variable, on_call, platform_dynamic}
- `notice_time_band` (how far in advance shifts are known)
- `cancellation_rate_band` (shift cancelled / reduced hours frequency)
- `minimum_hours_or_compensation` (existence of guaranteed hours or cancellation pay)
- `care_compatibility_markers`:
  - childcare pickup compatibility, appointment flexibility, school/daycare closures handling
- `schedule_capture_markers`:
  - penalties for refusal, rating impacts, agency blacklisting risk, “always available” expectations

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The post case **MUST** show a measurable improvement in schedule predictability or a compensating rail (e.g., cancellation compensation) that neutralizes capture.
- If platform/agency dynamics remain, the post case **MUST** specify contestability rails (appeal against deactivation, transparency of penalties) that reduce arbitrary schedule discipline.
- Care compatibility **MUST** be treated as a structural constraint (time + required coordination), not as “personal preference”.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-SCH-01 (Predictability delta):** notice_time_band increases and/or cancellation_rate_band decreases, or a defined compensation rail exists.
- **DL-SCH-02 (Capture reduction):** refusal penalties/rating impacts are reduced or made contestable with time bounds.
- **DL-SCH-03 (Care compatibility):** at least one care-compatibility proxy improves or is supported by a rail (public option / scheduling right).

<a id="b05ch14p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH14.P08.S02 — Administrative time: fewer forms/calls/delays; lower “time burden”

Administrative time is treated as an **extraction surface** when it functions as gatekeeping or default-by-friction. The post case must show reductions through one-stop execution, fewer verification loops, and time-bounded decisions.

### Required fields (template)
For each core process `{benefits_or_support, dispute_or_appeal, restructuring, provider_switching}`:
- `steps_count_pre` and `steps_count_post`
- `time_burden_band_pre` and `time_burden_band_post` (per month or per event)
- `delay_band_pre` and `delay_band_post` (time to decision / time to resolution)
- `identity_dependency` (whether access requires fragile authentication)
- `repeat_verification_loops` (present/absent + frequency band)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The post case **MUST** reduce administrative time burden for at least two core processes, measured via steps/time/delay proxies.
- Time-bounded contestability **MUST** include deadline enforcement (freeze/escalation/auto-protection), not merely targets.
- Identity dependency **MUST** be mitigated by recovery/continuity rails; otherwise administrative burden is shifted, not reduced.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-ADM-TIME-01 (Two-process delta):** two processes show lower steps_count and/or lower time_burden_band and/or lower delay_band.
- **DL-ADM-TIME-02 (Loop reduction):** repeat verification loops decline or are constrained by anomaly-only triggers.
- **DL-ADM-TIME-03 (Deadline enforcement):** at least one automatic protective action exists when decision deadlines are missed.

<a id="b05ch14p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH14.P08.S03 — Health and stress: not as a KPI, but as an experience signal with procedural input

OBA does not treat health or stress as optimization metrics for governance (to avoid surveillance drift and moralizing). They can, however, be admitted as **experience signals** that inform procedure: where frictions are intolerable, where threats are abusive, and where time capture becomes coercive.

### Experience signal protocol (minimum)
- `experience_inputs[]` with, per input:
  - `signal_type` ∈ {stress_peak, sleep_disruption, anxiety_trigger, conflict_load, exhaustion, other}
  - `context_link` (what lock-in / threat / paperwork surface it relates to)
  - `frequency_band` (coarse)
  - `procedural_request` ∈ {review, audit_trigger_request, accessibility_support, dispute_assistance, protection_request}
  - `privacy_mode` ∈ {anonymous, aggregated, case_confidential}

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Experience signals **MUST NOT** be used as success KPIs, eligibility gates, or behavioural scoring inputs.
- Experience signals **MUST** have a procedural pathway: they may trigger review/audit/accessibility support, but must remain privacy-protected and non-punitive.
- If experience signals indicate provider abuse or coercive threats, the case **MUST** route them into the dispute/audit rails (link P10/P12).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-EXP-01 (Non-KPI compliance):** the case explicitly states that health/stress are not performance metrics and are not used for eligibility.
- **DL-EXP-02 (Procedural pathway):** each signal includes a procedural_request and privacy_mode.
- **DL-EXP-03 (Abuse routing):** abuse/coercion signals (if present) map to an audit/dispute path.

<a id="b05ch14p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH14.P08.S04 — Agency: choice-space grows because exit becomes real (not only “in theory”)

Agency is defined operationally: **the set of viable choices** available without punitive penalties, identity lockouts, or threat escalation. It increases when switching costs fall, contestability works, and baseline access removes coercive dependence.

### Required fields (template)
- `choice_space_pre` and `choice_space_post`:
  - number of viable providers/options per essentials domain (bands acceptable),
  - existence of a public/commons option,
  - viability constraints (eligibility gates, wait times, switching costs).
- `exit_realism_markers`:
  - switching cost cash/time,
  - penalty clauses,
  - threat vectors (eviction/disconnection/garnishment),
  - portability/continuity status.
- `decision_latency` (time required to execute a switch or dispute) pre vs post.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Agency claims **MUST** be backed by exit realism metrics; “feels more free” without measurable exit conditions is non-conform.
- The post case **MUST** show at least one domain where the household has a new viable exit path (commons exit or contestable alternative) that was not viable pre-OBA.
- If choice-space increases due to public options, the case **MUST** evidence access conditions and time-to-access (not mere policy existence).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-AGY-01 (Metric grounding):** agency is grounded in switching costs, penalties, threat vectors, and time-to-switch.
- **DL-AGY-02 (New viable exit):** at least one domain shows a newly viable exit path with evidence class.
- **DL-AGY-03 (Choice-space delta):** choice_space_post > choice_space_pre in at least one domain, or coercive dependence is materially reduced by baseline access.

---

### Output format (day structure summary)

At minimum, the post case **MUST** emit a day-structure summary (table or structured list) with:

- `dimension` | `pre_proxy` | `post_proxy` | `delta` | `measurement_method` | `uncertainty_band` | `link_to_rail`

Where dimensions include: schedule predictability, admin time burden, decision latency, and exit realism/agency.
