# B05.CH14.P03 — Lock-in map: where block power pins the household

This paragraph specifies the **lock-in map** for the baseline (pre-OBA) household. The lock-in map is a structured diagnosis of **block power surfaces**: where exit is not real (or is punitive), and where vulnerability is converted into enforceable dependence. The output is not a narrative; it is a *domain-by-domain inventory* of lock-in mechanisms, switching costs, and threat vectors that shape **L** and the household’s **R/B tail**.

---

<a id="b05ch14p03s01"></a>
## B05.CH14.P03.S01 — Housing lock-in: lease terms, deposit, waitlists, moving friction, blacklisting

Housing lock-in is typically the highest-impact block surface: it binds the household through contract terms, scarcity, and institutional gatekeeping.

### Required fields (template)
- `housing_status_pre` ∈ {private_rent, public_rent, waitlist, informal, owner_with_mortgage, temporary_shelter}
- `lease_terms` — duration, renewal, indexation, termination rights, notice periods.
- `deposit_and_advance` — deposit amount, advance rent, conditions for return, dispute process.
- `scarcity_markers` — waitlists, vacancy rate proxy, application rejection frequency (if known), required documents.
- `moving_friction` — estimated moving costs (cash + time), required references, transport/storage constraints.
- `blacklisting_risk` — eviction records, landlord databases, reference gates, arrears stigma (categorical; no personal identifiers).

### Lock-in mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Termination penalties or loss of deposit.
- “Take-it-or-leave-it” renewals under scarcity (no contestable alternative).
- Application gates (income multiples, guarantors, document burdens).
- Eviction/termination threat processes and time-to-relief (baseline).
- Location lock: distance-to-work/care creates hidden switching costs.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** quantify switching costs for housing in at least coarse bands (low/medium/high or ranges) and separate **cash** vs **time** cost.
- The case **MUST** state whether housing access is scarcity-bound (waitlist/limited supply) and how that scarcity amplifies landlord block power.
- The case **MUST** identify at least one enforceable threat vector (eviction, deposit loss, blacklist) or explicitly declare none with evidence class.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-HOU-01 (Exit realism):** a concrete exit path is described, or the lock-in is explicitly classified as “no viable exit”.
- **DL-HOU-02 (Switching cost disclosure):** cash/time switching costs are present (bands acceptable).
- **DL-HOU-03 (Threat vector visibility):** eviction/deposit/blacklist exposure is declared.

## B05.CH14.P03.S02 — Energy lock-in: disconnection threats, advance payments, penalties, opaque surcharges

Energy lock-in converts essential access into a threat-based discipline system. The key diagnostic is the disconnection/arrears regime and the fee/penalty surface.

### Required fields (template)
- `energy_provider_type` ∈ {private, municipal, coop, prepaid, landlord_included}
- `tariff_structure` — fixed vs variable components, peak pricing, indexation triggers (if any).
- `advance_payment_requirements` — deposits, prepayment, “security top-ups”, meter credit rules.
- `arrears_and_disconnection_rules` — notice periods, grace windows, reconnection fees, minimum payment plans.
- `penalty_surface` — late fees, administrative fees, “service charges”, contractual penalties.
- `opacity_markers` — unclear surcharges, non-transparent price breakdown, disputed billing frequency.

### Lock-in mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Disconnection threat and reconnection fee loop.
- Prepayment traps (running out triggers immediate deprivation).
- Penalty stacking (arrears → admin fees → higher advance → worse arrears).
- Opaque adjustments and billing dispute disadvantage.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** identify the disconnection threat regime and time-to-disconnection under arrears.
- The case **MUST** list all penalty/fee components as separate items (no lumping).
- If tariff opacity exists, the case **MUST** tag it as an opacity lock-in marker (since opacity itself creates non-contestable dependence).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-ENE-01 (Threat regime):** disconnection timing + reconnection conditions are present.
- **DL-ENE-02 (Fee itemization):** penalty surface is itemized.
- **DL-ENE-03 (Opacity tagging):** unclear surcharges are explicitly flagged when present.

## B05.CH14.P03.S03 — Digital lock-in: paywalls, device lock-in, identity lockout, platform fees

Digital lock-in is a composite block surface: access is gated by paywalls, device ecosystems, identity systems, and platform governance. It also mediates access to services (benefits, work, care scheduling), making it a multiplier of vulnerability.

### Required fields (template)
- `connectivity_status` — broadband/mobile availability, data caps, coverage issues (categorical).
- `contract_terms` — minimum term, early termination fees, bundling (router/TV/phone), penalties.
- `device_ecosystem` — carrier lock, app store lock, forced upgrades, repair barriers (categorical).
- `identity_access` — ability to authenticate to key services; failure modes (lost phone, SIM swap risk, account lockouts).
- `platform_dependency` — which platforms gate income/communication/services; associated fees/take-rates (if applicable).

### Lock-in mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Paywalls for essentials (banking, school portals, government services).
- Device lock + repair inaccessibility → forced replacement.
- Identity lockout: inability to login/authenticate blocks disputes and access.
- Platform fee/take-rate + ranking or deactivation threat (income gating).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** treat identity lockout as a first-class lock-in surface (not “tech inconvenience”), because it can block access to rights and dispute flows.
- The case **MUST** list at least one dependency chain (e.g., “platform account → income access” or “authentication → benefits access”) if present.
- Platform fees **MUST** be reported as explicit cost items when they gate work or essential services.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-DIG-01 (Access viability):** connectivity + contract terms are present.
- **DL-DIG-02 (Identity risk):** identity failure modes are declared (present/absent + category).
- **DL-DIG-03 (Dependency chain):** at least one dependency chain is described if platform/identity gating exists.

## B05.CH14.P03.S04 — Debt and collections lock-in: fee cascades, garnishment threats, exploitation of vulnerability

Debt lock-in is where “small shocks” become persistent tail events. Collections ecosystems often operate as fee multipliers and threat amplifiers, especially under information and time asymmetry.

### Required fields (template)
- `debt_classes[]` — {rent_arrears, energy_arrears, consumer_credit, payday_short_term, medical, tax, fines, informal_debt}
- `collections_stage` — {none, internal_collection, external_agency, legal_action, enforcement}
- `fee_cascade_surface` — late fees, collection fees, legal fees, interest, penalty multipliers.
- `enforcement_threats` — wage garnishment, asset seizure, bank account attachment, credit reporting, eviction acceleration.
- `vulnerability_exploitation_markers` — intimidation scripts, confusing settlement offers, deadline traps, paper overload.

### Lock-in mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Fee cascades that grow faster than repayment capacity.
- Threat asymmetry (rapid enforcement vs slow appeal/verification).
- Information asymmetry (opaque balance computation, shifting creditors).
- Time capture: forms/calls/appointments make compliance impossible → default-by-friction.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** identify whether the household is in a **fee cascade** regime (yes/no) and list the cascade components separately.
- The case **MUST** declare the enforcement threat surface and the baseline “time-to-enforcement” vs “time-to-dispute resolution” (bands acceptable).
- The case **MUST** treat paperwork/time capture as a lock-in mechanism when it functions as default-by-friction.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-DEBT-01 (Stage clarity):** collections stage is stated.
- **DL-DEBT-02 (Cascade visibility):** fee components are itemized and tagged as cascade/non-cascade.
- **DL-DEBT-03 (Threat asymmetry):** enforcement vs dispute timing is declared (even as coarse bands).

---

### Output format (lock-in map summary)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit a compact lock-in map summary table (or equivalent structured list) with the following columns per domain:

- `domain` | `primary_gatekeepers` | `exit_cost_cash` | `exit_cost_time` | `threat_vector` | `opacity_flag` | `appeal_viability` | `notes`

If the map cannot be produced from evidence classes (contracts/bills/notices), the case is non-conform under the CH14 fail-closed rule.
