# B05.CH14.P10 — Dispute-flow and errors (what if it goes wrong)

This paragraph specifies the **dispute-flow and error handling** rails for Day-in-the-Life Case I. The case is non-trivial precisely because execution can fail: wrong decisions happen, providers can abuse vulnerability, and systems can drift into privacy-violating overreach. OBA therefore requires **contestability**, **time-bounded correction**, **provisional relief**, and **transparent logs**. These rails are part of conformance: if “after” improvements rely on perfect execution, the case is non-conform.

---

<a id="b05ch14p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH14.P10.S01 — Wrong decision: fast correction, provisional relief, restatement of the calculation

Wrong decisions include incorrect eligibility outcomes, miscomputed B/T/N/L/R, wrongful penalties, or incorrect enforcement triggers. The rail is: *fast correction beats slow apology*.

### Required dispute objects (template)
- `decision_id` (opaque identifier; privacy-safe)
- `decision_type` ∈ {eligibility, tariff, arrears_action, restructuring, enforcement_trigger, portability, other}
- `error_class` ∈ {wrong_input, wrong_rule, missing_evidence, identity_failure, clerical, system_bug, provider_misreporting}
- `restatement_required` (true/false)
- `provisional_relief_required` (true/false)
- `time_bounds`:
  - `t_ack` (time to acknowledge),
  - `t_relief` (time to grant provisional relief, if required),
  - `t_fix` (time to correct),
  - `t_final` (time to finalize and communicate)
- `log_refs[]` (audit log references; redacted allowed)
- `calculation_trace_ref` (link to method_id and version of the rules used)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- A disputable decision **MUST** have an accessible initiation path (online/offline), with receipt and timestamp (`t_ack`).
- If the decision can trigger irreversible harm (eviction/disconnection/garnishment/identity lockout), **provisional relief MUST** be granted within `t_relief` while the dispute is pending.
- When the dispute concerns B/T/N/L/R, the system **MUST** produce a **restatement**: the calculation is re-run with explicit inputs, rule version, and delta explanation.
- If time bounds are missed, the system **MUST** trigger protective defaults (freeze enforcement, auto-escalation, or interim approval per policy).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-DSP-01 (Receipt):** dispute initiation yields a timestamped receipt and trackable `decision_id`.
- **DL-DSP-02 (Relief):** where harm risk exists, provisional relief is granted within the declared time bound.
- **DL-DSP-03 (Restatement):** correction includes a calculation restatement tied to `method_id` and rule version.
- **DL-DSP-04 (Deadline enforcement):** missed deadlines trigger automatic protective action.

## B05.CH14.P10.S02 — Provider abuse: anomaly → audit → contract rewrite/sanction

Provider abuse includes coercive threats, illegal/opaque fees, discriminatory gatekeeping, retaliation for disputes, and systematic exploitation of time/identity fragility. The rail is: *anomaly triggers audit; audit can rewrite the contract surface*.

### Required abuse signal protocol (template)
- `abuse_signal_id`
- `provider_class` ∈ {landlord, energy_provider, telecom, platform, collections_agency, intermediary, public_body, other}
- `abuse_type[]` ∈ {illegal_fee, opaque_surcharge, retaliation, intimidation, discriminatory_gate, denial_by_friction, wrongful_disconnection, wrongful_eviction, deceptive_terms, other}
- `anomaly_trigger` ∈ {complaint_cluster, outlier_fee_rate, outlier_disconnection_rate, dispute_win_rate_spike, audit_sampling_hit, whistle_signal}
- `evidence_class[]` (notice, bill, contract terms, call log, portal log, witness statement class)
- `immediate_protection_required` (true/false)
- `audit_path`:
  - `audit_start_time_bound`,
  - `audit_scope` (fees, enforcement practices, selection criteria, logs),
  - `audit_output` ∈ {no_issue, remediation_order, contract_rewrite_required, sanction_required}
- `remedy_actions[]` (see below)

### Remedy ladder (ordered)
1) `remediation_order` (remove fee item, correct practice, refund)
2) `contract_rewrite` (mandatory template terms, removal of clauses, transparency/portability requirements)
3) `sanction` (fines, license constraints, exclusion from procurement/market access)
4) `replacement` (public option expansion / contract termination where contestability cannot be restored)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Abuse signals **MUST** have a protected reporting path and **MUST NOT** trigger retaliation.
- When an anomaly trigger fires, an audit **MUST** begin within a declared time bound (`audit_start_time_bound`).
- If abuse is confirmed, remedies **MUST** follow the ladder and be evidenced (refunds, rewritten terms, sanctions).
- Audit outcomes **MUST** be contestable (provider can appeal) but **MUST NOT** delay immediate protection where harm risk exists.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-ABU-01 (Trigger-to-audit):** anomaly trigger produces an audit start within the time bound.
- **DL-ABU-02 (Evidence linkage):** abuse claims reference evidence classes; “everyone knows” is non-conform.
- **DL-ABU-03 (Remedy trace):** remediation/contract rewrite/sanction is logged with references and deltas (what changed).

## B05.CH14.P10.S03 — System abuse: privacy breach or overreach → hard stop and rollback

System abuse includes privacy breaches, surveillance drift, eligibility scoring using prohibited signals, or expansion of data collection beyond necessity. The rail is: *hard stop and rollback*, not “we will improve”.

### Prohibited actions (non-exhaustive)
- Using health/stress signals as eligibility gates, behavioural scores, or enforcement triggers.
- Collecting fine-grained behavioural data when coarse evidence classes suffice.
- Sharing household-level data across systems without necessity, consent basis, and strict purpose limitation.
- Black-box automated denials without explainability and contestability.

### Hard stop protocol (template)
- `incident_id`
- `incident_type` ∈ {privacy_breach, overcollection, prohibited_scoring, unauthorized_sharing, black_box_denial, logging_failure}
- `detection_source` ∈ {audit, complaint, monitoring, breach_report, whistle}
- `containment_action` (immediate stop, access revocation, key rotation, system isolation)
- `rollback_scope` (what is reverted: model/rule version, data pipeline, access permissions)
- `notification_class` (who must be notified; aggregated categories)
- `postmortem_required` (true/false) + publication requirements (aggregated)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Privacy breaches and overreach **MUST** trigger immediate containment and **MUST** suspend affected automated decisions until corrected.
- The system **MUST** support rollback to a prior known-good configuration (rules + pipelines + access control).
- Postmortems **MUST** be produced in aggregated form, including root cause and the preventive control added (without leaking personal data).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-SYS-01 (Containment):** incident triggers containment within defined time bounds.
- **DL-SYS-02 (Rollback):** rollback can be executed and is logged (rule version + effect).
- **DL-SYS-03 (No prohibited scoring):** explicit statement and audit check that prohibited signals are not used.

<a id="b05ch14p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH14.P10.S04 — Protection: no retaliation, humane procedures, transparent logs

Contestability is meaningless if people fear retaliation or cannot survive the process. Protection rails are therefore part of OBA conformance.

### Protection requirements (minimum set)
- `no_retaliation`:
  - prohibited retaliation by providers or public bodies for disputes, switching, or reporting abuse,
  - enforcement with sanctions.
- `humane_procedure`:
  - clear language, accessible channels, reasonable deadlines,
  - assistance available for low-digital-access households,
  - non-punitive defaults when proof is missing due to system fault.
- `transparent_logs`:
  - user-visible status tracking,
  - audit logs with timestamps, rule versions, and actions,
  - ability to obtain an explanation for denials and enforcement actions.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The system **MUST** provide a no-retaliation guarantee with enforceable penalties and a protected reporting channel.
- Procedures **MUST** be accessible (offline/assisted options) and must minimize repeated verification loops.
- Logs **MUST** be transparent at two levels:
  - **user view** (status, reasons, next steps),
  - **audit view** (rule versions, evidence classes, action trail).
- Any enforcement action (eviction/disconnection/garnishment/deactivation) **MUST** be accompanied by a contestable reason and a time window for challenge, unless an explicit emergency rule applies (which is separately governed).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL-PRT-01 (Retaliation guard):** retaliation is explicitly prohibited and sanctionable; reporting path exists.
- **DL-PRT-02 (Accessibility):** at least one offline/assisted dispute initiation path exists.
- **DL-PRT-03 (Two-level logging):** user view + audit view logs exist and are referenced.

---

### Output format (dispute-flow dashboard)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit a dispute-flow dashboard (table or structured list) with:

- `issue_type` | `harm_risk` | `provisional_relief` | `t_ack` | `t_relief` | `t_fix` | `t_final` | `deadline_enforcement` | `log_ref`

If dispute handling cannot be evidenced and time-bounded, the “after” case is non-conform (execution dependency without rails).
