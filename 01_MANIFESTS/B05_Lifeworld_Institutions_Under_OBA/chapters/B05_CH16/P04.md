# B05.CH16.P04 — Remedies upon breach: reallocation without system damage

This paragraph specifies the **remedy logic** applied when I-MAX or W-MAX breaches occur in the high-income/wealth case. Remedies are not
punishment theatre; they are **reallocation mechanisms** designed to reduce claim power and rentier concentration while avoiding systemic
damage (fire sales, credit contagion, arbitrary confiscation). The output is a rule-bound remedy set, a constrained choice space, and a
public consequence layer (aggregated conformance status + audit summary).

---

<a id="b05ch16p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH16.P04.S01 — Remedy set: levy/clawback, conversion to commons funding (C), divestment, lockbox/escrow

Remedies must be executable across different asset/liquidity profiles and must remain enforceable under opacity and flight risk.

### Remedy menu (fixed options; minimum set)
- `levy_or_clawback`:
  - monetary levy on breach amount or clawback of excess realization (I-MAX event)
- `conversion_to_commons_funding`:
  - conversion of excess into earmarked commons contribution (C) via defined channels
- `divestment_or_deconcentration`:
  - structured divestment to reduce W concentration or breach (W-MAX event)
- `lockbox_or_escrow`:
  - escrow/lockbox placement of disputed or breach-related amounts/assets pending resolution

### Required fields (template)
For each applied remedy:
- `remedy_id`
- `trigger_event_ref` (cap event id)
- `cap_type` ∈ {I, W}
- `remedy_type` (from menu)
- `amount_or_position_band`
- `liquidity_handling`:
  - {immediate_cash, scheduled_payment_plan, structured_divestment, escrow_hold}
- `time_to_compliance_bound`
- `evidence_class[]` (payment record, escrow agreement, divestment plan, transfer record)
- `system_risk_checks`:
  - flags for fire-sale risk, contagion risk, correlated positions (coarse)
- `uncertainty_band` (where valuation affects amount)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Remedies **MUST** be linked to specific cap events and to the UBC subject attribution.
- Illiquid exposures **MUST** have non-fire-sale compliance routes (structured divestment or scheduled plans), unless a higher-risk
  override is justified and logged.
- Under opacity/flight risk, escrow/lockbox **MUST** be available as a provisional enforcement tool (link P03.S04/P09.S02).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-RMD-01 (Event linkage):** every remedy references a specific cap event and UBC subject.
- **DL16-RMD-02 (System risk check):** structured handling exists when assets are illiquid or concentrated.
- **DL16-RMD-03 (Evidence):** each remedy has at least one evidence class proving execution.

<a id="b05ch16p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH16.P04.S02 — Choice space: multiple routes, but within fixed rules (no bespoke deals)

The system can allow multiple compliance routes to minimize collateral damage, but it cannot allow bespoke negotiation that recreates
elite carve-outs.

### Choice-space constraints (minimum)
- `fixed_rule_menu`:
  - remedy options are pre-defined and versioned; no ad hoc additions.
- `equivalence_rules`:
  - if multiple routes exist, they must be demonstrably equivalent in compliance effect (cap breach neutralised within bounds).
- `deadline_rules`:
  - time-bounded selection and execution; delay is not a bargaining tactic.
- `no_bespoke_side_letters`:
  - no private deals outside the published remedy set and precedent catalogue.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The system **MUST** allow choice only among fixed, published remedy options.
- Any “special treatment” request **MUST** be treated as a compatibility breach unless it is a versioned rule change applied generally.
- If choice is not exercised within the deadline, a default remedy **MUST** auto-apply (fail-closed against delay).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-CHO-01 (Menu integrity):** remedy types used are within the published menu.
- **DL16-CHO-02 (Deadline enforcement):** missed choice deadlines trigger default remedy action.
- **DL16-CHO-03 (No bespoke evidence):** no side-letter or discretionary carve-out artefacts exist for remedy outcomes.

<a id="b05ch16p04s03"></a>
## B05.CH16.P04.S03 — Lifeworld impact: consumption can continue within I-MAX, but claim power and rentier positions decline

The lifeworld impact at the top is bounded but not punitive. The goal is to reduce the ability to accumulate and exercise block power,
not to destroy ordinary high-quality consumption within the cap.

### Impact logic (explicit)
- `consumption_continuity`:
  - spending within I-MAX remains broadly feasible; the cap constrains marginal escalation, not normal autonomy.
- `claim_power_reduction`:
  - breach remedies reduce concentrated capital, fee stacks, and rentier positions that generate block power.
- `leverage_reduction`:
  - incentives shift away from debt-based cashouts and synthetic exposures used to circumvent caps.
- `structure_simplification_pressure`:
  - opaque complexity becomes costly due to presumptions and audit burden.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Lifeworld impact claims **MUST** be tied to measurable outputs (cap event amounts, remedy execution, concentration proxies) rather than
  sentiment.
- The case **MUST** explicitly separate consumption continuity from claim power: “still comfortable” does not imply “still dominant”.
- If remedies unintentionally choke legitimate productive activity, the case **MUST** route it to parameter tuning within rails (P13.S04),
  not bespoke exemptions.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-IMP-01 (Measurable grounding):** impact claims map to dashboard metrics (P12) and remedy logs.
- **DL16-IMP-02 (Power vs comfort separation):** the case explicitly states and evidences the reduction in claim power surfaces.
- **DL16-IMP-03 (No bespoke relief):** productive-activity protection uses general parameter rails, not private deals.

<a id="b05ch16p04s04"></a>
## B05.CH16.P04.S04 — Public consequence: conformance status and audit summary (aggregated, privacy tiers)

To prevent private bargaining and hidden enforcement, CH16 requires a public consequence layer: aggregated disclosure of conformance
status and enforcement outcomes, with privacy tiers.

### Required public outputs (aggregated)
- `conformance_status` ∈ {CONFORM, PARTIAL, NON_CONFORM, UNDER_REVIEW}
- `cap_event_summary` (counts, magnitude bands, remedy types selected)
- `audit_summary`:
  - audits initiated, finding severity distribution, opacity incidents, sanction outcomes
- `dispute_summary`:
  - dispute volumes, durations, reversal rates, precedent references (versioned)
- `privacy_tier`:
  - disclosure level categories (no personal data; aggregated release)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Public consequence outputs **MUST** be published in aggregated form; secrecy is a legitimacy breach except for narrowly defined security
  constraints.
- Conformance status **MUST** be rule-derived and versioned; it cannot be negotiated.
- Publication **MUST NOT** leak identifying details; privacy tiers control granularity, not accountability.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-PUB-01 (Status publication):** conformance status is published (aggregated) with rule/version reference.
- **DL16-PUB-02 (Audit/dispute transparency):** audit and dispute summaries exist and are versioned.
- **DL16-PUB-03 (Privacy discipline):** outputs are non-identifying and respect the declared privacy tier.

---

### Output format (remedy log)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit a remedy log (table or structured list) with:

- `cap_event_id` | `UBC_subject` | `remedy_type` | `amount_or_position_band` | `liquidity_handling` | `time_to_compliance_bound` |
  `escrow_required` | `evidence_class` | `status`

If remedies are described without execution evidence, the “after” case is NON-CONFORM.
