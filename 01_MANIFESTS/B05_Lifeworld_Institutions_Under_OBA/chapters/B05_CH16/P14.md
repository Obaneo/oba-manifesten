# B05.CH16.P14 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies **failure modes** and **hard stops** for the high-income/wealth Day-in-the-Life case. CH16 is where OBA either
proves compatibility under pressure or collapses into exceptions, shadow regimes, or illegitimate enforcement. The chapter therefore
functions as an **early-warning system** with FAIL-CLOSED behavior: carve-outs are prohibited, shadow rails trigger leakage escalation,
arbitrary/selective enforcement triggers legitimacy breach, and structural opacity triggers automatic presumptions, restrictions, and
status downgrades.

---

<a id="b05ch16p14s01"></a>
## B05.CH16.P14.S01 — Elite carve-outs: exceptions for “strategic capital” → compatibility breach

The most direct failure mode is an exception regime: special treatment for “strategic” actors that recreates the old order under new
labels.

### Carve-out patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `strategic_capital_exception` (explicit exemption language)
- `bespoke_deal_remedy` (negotiated settlement outside the fixed remedy menu)
- `grandfathering_for_elites` (legacy structures exempted without substance justification)
- `selective_non_enforcement` (rules exist but are not applied to top actors)
- `private_side_letters_with_state` (informal agreements, undisclosed accommodations)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Any carve-out attempt **MUST** trigger a hard stop: it is a **compatibility breach**.
- Remedies **MUST** remain rule-bound and versioned; bespoke deals are non-conform by definition.
- If a rule change is proposed, it **MUST** be general, published, and versioned; it cannot be applied only to the requesting actor.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-CO-01 (Menu integrity):** remedy outcomes use only the fixed remedy menu (P04); deviations → NON-CONFORM.
- **DL16-CO-02 (Publication):** any exception proposal must be publicly versioned; private carve-outs → hard stop.
- **DL16-CO-03 (Status downgrade):** carve-out detection triggers conformance status downgrade (UNDER_REVIEW/NON_CONFORM).

<a id="b05ch16p14s02"></a>
## B05.CH16.P14.S02 — Shadow regime: parallel rails (crypto/offshore) grows → leakage breach and escalation to controls

A second failure mode is the growth of a parallel regime—offshore secrecy structures, crypto rails, synthetic exposure chains—that
bypasses disclosure, attribution, and cap enforcement.

### Shadow regime indicators (minimum set; aggregated)
- `offshore_exposure_growth` (more secrecy nodes, more treaty-hub routing)
- `crypto_shadow_channel_growth` (mixers/bridges/opaque custody usage rates)
- `synthetic_exposure_growth` (TRS/CFD/option replication usage)
- `custody_migration_growth` (assets moved to opaque custodians)
- `leakage_test_failures` (Codex leakage tests breached)

### Escalation toolkit (minimum set)
- `forbidden_channel_expansion` (reclassify channels to forbidden/conditional)
- `withholding_hooks_activation` (custody/payment rail withholding)
- `approval_regimes_activation` (temporary transfer approvals)
- `audit_expansion` (mandatory audits for high-risk clusters)
- `temporary_capital_controls` with `sunset_rule` (time-bounded escalation)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- If shadow regime indicators breach thresholds, this is a **leakage breach** and escalation **MUST** occur within time bounds.
- Escalation measures **MUST** be criteria-based, logged, contestable, and sunset by default (avoid permanent emergency drift).
- The system **MUST** prefer rail-level enforcement over endless bespoke investigations.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-SHD-01 (Leakage trigger):** at least one leakage threshold exists and is monitored (P12/Codex).
- **DL16-SHD-02 (Escalation action):** threshold breach triggers a specific escalation action within time bounds.
- **DL16-SHD-03 (Sunset discipline):** escalations include sunset and renewal criteria.

## B05.CH16.P14.S03 — Overenforcement: arbitrariness or selectivity → legitimacy breach; requires transparent precedents

A third failure mode is enforcement that is arbitrary or selectively punitive. Even “tough” enforcement becomes illegitimate if rules are
opaque, inconsistent, or applied unevenly.

### Overenforcement patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `inconsistent_presumptions` (similar cases treated differently without precedent basis)
- `selective_targeting` (political/retaliatory enforcement; uneven audits)
- `opaque_rule_drift` (method changes without versioned publication)
- `punitive_delay` (slow correction used as pressure tactic)
- `disproportionate_sanctions` (sanction severity decoupled from evidence and obstruction)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Enforcement **MUST** be anchored in versioned rules and precedents; otherwise it is a legitimacy breach.
- Decisions **MUST** cite rule versions and applicable precedents (aggregated publication).
- Sanction severity **MUST** be proportional and tied to evidence and obstruction signals; “exemplary punishment” is non-conform.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-LEG-01 (Precedent citation):** decisions reference versioned precedents; missing citations raise legitimacy flags.
- **DL16-LEG-02 (Variance audit):** selective execution indicators trigger audit-on-the-auditor (P13.S02).
- **DL16-LEG-03 (Proportionality):** sanction outcomes map to severity bands and obstruction flags (P12.S03).

<a id="b05ch16p14s04"></a>
## B05.CH16.P14.S04 — Fail-closed: under structural opacity/obstruction, stricter presumptions, rail restrictions, and status downgrades auto-activate

The hard stop is fail-closed escalation: when the subject obstructs or the structure becomes opaque, the system does not pause—it becomes
more conservative and more restrictive.

### Structural opacity/obstruction triggers (minimum set)
- missing UBC disclosure or nominee/trust layers without evidence
- missing valuation support for illiquid assets
- refusal to disclose side letters, fee/carry terms, or related-party transactions
- repeated deadline breaches and selective disclosure drip-feed
- asset/structure mutation during dispute (risk escalation)
- custody migration to secrecy nodes during assessment

### Auto-activation actions (minimum set)
- `stricter_presumptions`:
  - conservative valuation/exposure bands, default UBC attribution
- `rail_restrictions`:
  - forbidden/conditional channel classification, transfer restrictions, withholding hooks
- `escrow_lockbox`:
  - mandatory escrow for disputed amounts/exposures
- `sanction_multipliers`:
  - escalation for obstruction and repeated deadline breaches
- `status_downgrade`:
  - CONFORM → UNDER_REVIEW → NON_CONFORM (rule-bound, logged)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Under structural opacity/obstruction, auto-activation **MUST** occur; discretionary hesitation is capture risk.
- Auto-activation **MUST** be logged, time-bounded, and contestable, but contestation **MUST NOT** automatically suspend measures when
  risk flags are active.
- Status downgrades **MUST** be rule-derived and published in aggregated form (privacy tiers apply).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-FC-01 (Trigger-to-action):** each trigger maps to an automatic action; missing mapping → NON-CONFORM.
- **DL16-FC-02 (No-stay under risk):** disputes do not automatically stay enforcement under opacity/flight risk (P09.S02).
- **DL16-FC-03 (Publication):** status downgrades and presumption rates are visible in aggregated dashboards (P12).

---

### Output format (hard-stop register)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit a hard-stop register (table or structured list) with:

- `failure_mode` | `trigger_indicator` | `threshold_or_condition` | `detected` | `auto_action` | `time_bound` | `status`

Where auto_action ∈ {disqualify_case, status_downgrade, escrow_lockbox, apply_stricter_presumptions, expand_forbidden_channels,
activate_withholding, launch_audit_on_auditor, publish_precedent_update}.

If hard-stop logic is absent or discretionary, CH16 is non-conform because it cannot survive adversarial pressure.
