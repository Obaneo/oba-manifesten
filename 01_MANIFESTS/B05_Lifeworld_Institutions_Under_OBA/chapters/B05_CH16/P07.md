# B05.CH16.P07 — Evasion routes II: technical shifts (platforms, bundling, digital assets)

This paragraph specifies **technical evasion routes**: shifts that use platforms, bundling, digital assets, and data extraction to create
parallel toll regimes or shadow channels that bypass caps, disclosure, or classification. These routes are treated as **rail-level**
problems: they require forbidden channel flags, tagging, leakage tests, and enforcement at the infrastructure layer (link to Codex),
not only entity-by-entity adjudication.

---

<a id="b05ch16p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH16.P07.S01 — Platform arbitrage: self-preferencing, API gating, marketplace fees as extraction

Platform arbitrage routes preserve or expand claim power by embedding extraction in infrastructure: marketplace take rates, API gating,
and self-preferencing that creates non-contestable toll positions.

### Platform arbitrage patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `self_preferencing`:
  - platform privileges own products/services in ranking, pricing, access, or interoperability.
- `API_gating`:
  - access to essential APIs restricted to create dependency; fees and terms become non-optional.
- `marketplace_fee_stack`:
  - layered fees (listing, payment, “service”, advertising-for-visibility) that behave like an extraction stack.
- `deplatforming_threat`:
  - arbitrary removal or throttling used as a disciplinary tool.
- `bundled_tie_in`:
  - forcing use of platform-adjacent services (payment, logistics, identity) as a gate.

### Detection signals (minimum)
- `take_rate_effective` increases without cost justification (stack depth grows)
- `ranking_opacity` + correlated self-preference outcomes
- `dependency_graph` shows choke points (single API, single identity provider)
- `appeals_stats` show low contestability (high denial, long disputes, inconsistent rules)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Platform toll layers **MUST** be measured as effective take rate (all mandatory fee layers), not headline commission.
- Gatekeeping surfaces (API, ranking, identity) **MUST** be contestable: reason disclosure + appeal + time bounds.
- Self-preferencing and tie-ins **MUST** be treated as block power surfaces and routed to remedies (interoperability, separation, bounded
  fees).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-PLAT-01 (Fee stack visibility):** fee layers are itemized and effective take rate is computed.
- **DL16-PLAT-02 (Chokepoint detection):** dependency_graph includes at least one choke point proxy when platform is material.
- **DL16-PLAT-03 (Contestability):** deplatforming/ranking disputes are time-bounded and logged.

## B05.CH16.P07.S02 — Crypto/DeFi rails: shadow channels, mixers, wrapped assets; payment rail governance (link to Codex)

Crypto/DeFi can create **parallel settlement rails** that bypass reporting, ownership attribution, and capital controls, enabling both W
hiding and cross-border leakage.

### Shadow channel patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `mixing_obfuscation` (mixers, tumblers, privacy pools)
- `wrapped_asset_hopping` (wrapping/bridging across chains to break traceability)
- `custodial_shelling` (custody through opaque providers or layered wallets)
- `DeFi_synthetic_exposure` (derivative exposure that replicates capped positions)
- `stablecoin_settlement` (shadow payment channels for large value transfer)

### Detection signals (minimum)
- `unlinked_wallet_clusters` associated with known UBC subjects or related vehicles (graph signatures)
- `bridge_frequency_spikes` around cap events or enforcement windows
- `counterparty_opacity` (no KYC/identity linkage; refusal to disclose)
- `asset_migration_patterns` consistent with flight risk (rapid cross-border settlement)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Shadow rails **MUST** be treated as leakage risk; where disclosure/traceability fails, forbidden channel flags apply.
- If identity linkage is missing, conservative presumptions **MUST** apply to attribution and valuation.
- Payment rail governance **MUST** support tagging, reporting hooks, and enforcement escalation (link Codex forbidden-channel framework).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-CRY-01 (Rail classification):** crypto channels are classified under channel objects (forbidden/allowed/conditional) with rationale.
- **DL16-CRY-02 (Graph signal):** at least one wallet/bridge/cluster signal is specified when crypto is present.
- **DL16-CRY-03 (Presumption under opacity):** missing identity linkage triggers presumptions + provisional restrictions.

## B05.CH16.P07.S03 — Data extraction: surveillance rent as a new cashflow; reclassify as E

Data extraction can become a durable rent stream: surveillance and behavioural targeting produce cashflows and block power not captured by
traditional “income” categories. Under OBA, surveillance rent is treated as **E-like** extraction when it functions as a toll position
or coercive gate.

### Surveillance rent patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `behavioural_targeting_rent` (adtech-driven cashflows tied to surveillance)
- `data_brokerage_fees` (sale/licensing of personal data at scale)
- `attention_gate_fees` (pay-to-be-seen structures; ranking/verification as tolls)
- `identity_control_rent` (charging for access to identity/authentication infrastructure)

### Detection signals (minimum)
- revenue share derived from surveillance-dependent mechanisms (category bands)
- growth in pay-to-access / pay-to-avoid harms (privacy as luxury)
- dependency surfaces: identity and ranking become non-optional
- complaints/audits indicate coercive or opaque data practices

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Surveillance-derived cashflows **MUST** be classified and, where they function as tolls, reclassified as E surfaces subject to bounded
  take rates and governance constraints.
- Systems **MUST NOT** treat surveillance rent as “normal profit” when it rests on coercive data capture; governance rails apply.
- Classification **MUST** be evidence-linked (revenue categories, product terms, data practice disclosures).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-DATA-01 (Cashflow classification):** at least one data rent cashflow category is identified when platforms are material.
- **DL16-DATA-02 (E tagging):** toll-like data cashflows are tagged as E-like surfaces.
- **DL16-DATA-03 (Governance linkage):** remedy rails (bounded fees, portability, forbidden practices) are specified.

## B05.CH16.P07.S04 — Remedy: forbidden channels, tagging, leakage tests, rail-level enforcement

Technical evasion requires enforcement at the rail level. Remedies must be standardized, versioned, and fail-closed under opacity.

### Remedy toolkit (minimum set)
- `forbidden_channel_flags`:
  - channels flagged as forbidden/conditional based on opacity/leakage risk (Codex channel objects).
- `tagging_and_traceability`:
  - mandatory tagging for high-risk transactions, platform fee layers, and asset migrations.
- `leakage_tests`:
  - quantitative/graph-based tests that detect shadow channel growth and bypass patterns.
- `rail_level_enforcement`:
  - withholding hooks, access restrictions, licensing constraints, procurement exclusion for non-compliant platforms,
  - escalation under systemic leakage (temporary, sunset, contestable).
- `auditpack_requirements`:
  - required evidence classes and logs for platforms and digital asset channels.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Remedies **MUST** be enforceable without bespoke investigations for every case; rail-level controls are required.
- When leakage tests trigger, enforcement **MUST** escalate (restrictions, withholding, audit expansion) within defined time bounds.
- All tagging/leakage rules **MUST** be versioned and published; hidden changes create legitimacy risk.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL16-RLTECH-01 (Channel object):** each technical route maps to a channel classification (allowed/conditional/forbidden).
- **DL16-RLTECH-02 (Leakage trigger):** at least one leakage test and escalation action is defined.
- **DL16-RLTECH-03 (Versioning):** remedy rules reference versioned method/policy identifiers.

---

### Output format (technical shift register)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit a technical shift register (table or structured list) with:

- `shift_id` | `pattern_type` | `surface` (platform/crypto/data) | `signal_set` | `channel_classification` |
  `tagging_required` | `leakage_test` | `default_presumption` | `remedy_route` | `status`

If technical evasion is described without channel classification and leakage enforcement logic, the case is NON-CONFORM.
