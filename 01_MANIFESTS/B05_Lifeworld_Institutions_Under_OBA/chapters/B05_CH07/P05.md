# B05.CH07.P05 — Land and location: scarcity, speculation, and block power

This paragraph specifies how land and location function as scarcity and block-power surfaces under OBA. Land is not produced, and
location value is often created by collective decisions (planning, zoning, infrastructure, schools, safety, commons). If windfalls
from these decisions are privately captured, land becomes a clamp amplifier: it raises housing costs, concentrates wealth, and turns
permits and access into political toll points. OBA therefore treats land value dynamics as a primary extraction (E) mechanism and
requires rails that (i) capture windfalls for public/commons benefit, (ii) prevent speculation and withholding, and (iii) make
planning and permitting contestable, time-bounded, and audit-safe. Where land governance becomes opaque or captured, OBA applies
fail-closed remedies.

---

## B05.CH07.P05.S01 — Land value as a clamp: planning/infrastructure windfalls (E mechanism)

Land value growth is requalified as E to the extent it represents private capture of collectively created location value.

### Mechanism (binding)
- Public actions (zoning changes, permitting approvals, infrastructure provision, neighbourhood safety/amenities, commons investment)
  increase location desirability and land values.
- Under scarcity, landowners can appropriate these gains without adding productive housing capacity, turning the windfall into a toll
  embedded in rent and purchase prices.
- Speculative holding and strategic withholding further restrict supply, intensifying L through higher prices and longer waits.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- **Windfall land value gains** attributable to collective decisions are classified as **E** unless captured for public/commons use via
  compliant instruments.
- Intermediary structures designed to monetise planning outcomes (options, land banking for speculation, permit hoarding) are treated
  as E surfaces and subject to look-through.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- Price-to-income divergence concentrated in high-amenity/infrastructure-upgraded zones; low build-out despite approvals; high rates of
  land flipping; and rising share of total housing cost attributable to land.

## B05.CH07.P05.S02 — Remedies: land value capture, leasehold/ground-lease rails, public land banks (parameters)

OBA permits multiple remedy instruments. Jurisdictions choose parameters, but the rails (incidence, anti-avoidance, measurability) are
binding.

### Remedy instruments (admissible; parameters required)
- **Land value capture (LVC)**: taxes/levies on land value (especially uplift from rezoning/infrastructure) with clear valuation rules,
  published schedules, and anti-avoidance.
- **Leasehold / ground-lease rails**: separate land ownership from building use rights to prevent permanent private capture of location
  value; ground rents are bounded, rule-based, and linked to public/commons investment.
- **Public land banks**: acquire, assemble, and service land for housing supply and commons delivery; prevent speculation by holding
  land in a public/commons balance sheet and releasing it under build/maintenance conditions.

### Rail requirements (binding)
- **Incidence discipline**: instruments must be designed to fall on land rents/windfalls, not to be mechanically shifted onto tenants
  through opaque add-ons (requires rent-rail interaction and disclosure).
- **Anti-avoidance**: apply look-through on ownership vehicles, nominee chains, and related-party transactions; require beneficial
  ownership registration where applicable.
- **Use-it-or-build-it**: where legal, impose time-bounded obligations on serviced land to prevent strategic withholding; failure
  triggers escalating costs or compulsory pathways (jurisdictional parameters).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate outcomes: captured value volumes, land-bank inventory and release rates, and build-out performance tied to land
  releases.

## B05.CH07.P05.S03 — Zoning/permits: process friction as hidden L; standardisation and time norms

Permitting and zoning are often the de facto throttle on supply. Under OBA, process friction is treated as hidden L.

### Process rails (binding)
- **Time norms**: jurisdictions must set maximum cycle times for major permit classes and publish performance distributions (median,
  p90/p95).
- **Standardisation**: standard permit packages, standard conditions, and standard appeal pathways reduce discretionary bottlenecks and
  corruption risk.
- **Transparency**: clear criteria for approvals/denials; decisions must reference criteria and be reproducible from recorded inputs.

### Prohibited drift (binding)
- Discretionary delay as a political tool, opaque “negotiation” of conditions, and process requirements that function primarily as
  gatekeeping or rent extraction are non-conforming.

### Fail-closed escalation (binding)
- Under persistent cycle-time breaches or backlog growth, the system must trigger capacity expansion in permitting/inspection and/or
  simplify rules toward auditable standard pathways until performance returns to norms.

## B05.CH07.P05.S04 — Anti-corruption: transparent decision logs, conflict-of-interest rules, audit of the auditor

Because land decisions create large windfalls, they attract capture. OBA therefore treats land governance as audit-critical.

### Governance minima (binding)
- **Decision logs**: publish decision records for zoning and major permits (privacy-safe), including rationale, criteria references,
  objections, and mitigation conditions.
- **Conflict-of-interest rules**: mandatory disclosures for officials, advisors, and consultants; cooling-off periods; prohibitions on
  participating in decisions with direct or indirect personal financial exposure.
- **Procurement transparency**: where infrastructure is tied to land decisions, publish procurement and change-order logs to prevent
  kickback cycles.

### Audit-of-the-auditor (binding)
- Independent audits of planning bodies, valuation models, and permitting performance; rotation and peer review; public summary
  findings.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven bribery, systematic favouritism, or persistent concealment of decision rationale triggers governance reset: suspension of
  affected decision streams, review of prior approvals, and tightened rules with enhanced disclosure (fail-closed against land
  capture).
