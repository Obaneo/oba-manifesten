# B05.CH07.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of housing-as-essential under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of housing-as-essential under OBA. Housing is treated as the
largest and most structurally binding essentials domain (L): it determines baseline stability, mobility, family formation, and access
to work, education, and commons. Because housing markets are highly susceptible to scarcity rents, asset-price dynamics, and lock-in,
OBA treats housing governance as a constitutive rail. The order objective is not “homeownership” or “market efficiency” as such; it is
**L reduction** via supply and maintenance capacity, contestability, and commons options—while prohibiting demand-stimulus channels
that inflate prices or normalise clamp.

---

## B05.CH07.P01.S01 — Purpose: reduce L in the largest essentials domain via supply/maintenance/contestability, not via asset inflation

Under OBA, housing policy exists to reduce the essentials burden (L) in the domain that most strongly constrains lifeworld freedom.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Reduce L structurally**: increase available units, improve quality, and reduce recurring costs through build capacity, renovation,
  and maintenance-first infrastructure (ΔG).
- **Increase contestability**: reduce block power in allocation, landlord/manager structures, and permit/land bottlenecks; prevent
  scarcity rents from becoming a permanent toll.
- **Prevent clamp**: design exit rails and contract discipline so mobility is possible without punitive fees, blacklisting, or
  irreversible loss of access.
- **Prefer real capacity over price support**: treat affordability as a function of supply and maintenance, not of expanded credit or
  subsidies that bid up prices.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- Any housing intervention whose primary functional effect is to **support or raise asset prices** (directly or indirectly via credit,
  subsidies, or constrained supply) is treated as order-incompatible.

---

## B05.CH07.P01.S02 — Scope: rent/ownership, build and renovation capacity, land, financing, landlord structures, contract clamp, housing commons

CH07 covers the full housing stack: not only “tenure”, but the bottlenecks and power surfaces that determine L outcomes.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Tenure**: rental markets, ownership markets, mixed models, and transitions between them (exit and portability).
- **Build and renovation capacity**: skills pipelines, standardisation, prefab, procurement rails, maintenance backlogs, and cycle
  times.
- **Land and location**: planning, permitting, infrastructure gating, land value windfalls, speculation, and land-bank/leasehold
  regimes.
- **Financing**: mortgage products and underwriting, credit rails, subsidies and their incidence, and prohibited asset-support
  channels (ΔM leakage).
- **Landlord and intermediary structures**: ownership look-through, management layers, brokerage chains, service-charge constructs,
  and fee decomposition.
- **Contract clamp**: long or rigid terms, exit penalties, income requirements, blacklisting, opaque allocation, and enforcement
  postures that create lock-in.
- **Housing commons**: public housing, co-ops, trusts, non-profit rental models, governance minima, and anti-commons-washing criteria.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH07 does not prescribe architectural styles, local cultural preferences, or micro-design choices except where they affect
  measurability, maintenance burden, safety, and baseline access.

## B05.CH07.P01.S03 — Binding: Definition Set II (L), P/E/C classification, caps (W/I), ΔM (prohibited asset-support), commons standard

Housing governance under OBA is bound to canonical definition sets and invariants. Claims that ignore these rails are non-conforming.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **Definition Set II (L)**: housing is measured as an essentials burden (cost + friction + clamp). Policies must be evaluated by their
  effect on L distributions and tails, not by nominal price indices alone.
- **P/E/C classification**: distinguish productive housing activity (P: building, maintaining, renovating, operating) from extraction
  (E: scarcity rents, fee-stacks, speculative gains, lock-in enforcement) and commons flows (C: non-profit, access-first provision).
- **Caps (W/I)**: real estate holdings and rentier income flows are within the caps base (with look-through). Concentration and
  portfolio landlordism must be contestable and deconcentratable under exceedance.
- **ΔM rails**: credit and monetary channels may not be used for asset-support (price floors, demand stimulus, speculative carry).
  ΔM is admissible only into real capacity (ΔG) and commons housing capex/maintenance where ring-fenced and auditable.
- **Commons standard**: housing commons must meet governance minima (access rules, maintenance-first budgeting, transparency,
  contestability) and must not embed self-benefit loops.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any housing programme must provide an audit pack linking: mechanism → expected incidence → L/ΔG metrics → risk of E leakage → hard
  stops and rollback path.

## B05.CH07.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: housing policy that supports price increases, or normalises lock-in, is non-conform

Because housing is a dominant L surface and a primary asset channel, OBA applies fail-closed discipline to prevent drift into
price-support and clamp.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- **Price-support mechanisms**: policies that expand purchasing power or credit in constrained supply conditions (demand stimulus) or
  otherwise stabilise prices as a policy objective.
- **Lock-in normalisation**: rising exit penalties, increased blacklisting, longer binding terms without portability, and allocation
  rules that are non-transparent or non-contestable.
- **Opacity and capture**: permitting/land decisions without decision logs, opaque landlord ownership, undisclosed service-charge
  chains, or non-auditable subsidy incidence.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Under detected non-conformance, the programme must be suspended or redesigned to restore supply/maintenance-first mechanisms and
  contestability.
- Where the channel is structurally prone to asset inflation (e.g., credit/subsidy demand stimulus), the default remedy is **hard
  prohibition** with redirection of resources to ΔG (build/renovation) and housing commons.
- Where lock-in indicators worsen, contract standards and exit rails must be tightened, and dispute/audit procedures escalated until
  clamp signals reverse.
