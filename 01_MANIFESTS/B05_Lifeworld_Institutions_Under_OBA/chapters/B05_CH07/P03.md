# B05.CH07.P03 — Baseline housing security (access without clamp)

This paragraph specifies the baseline housing security requirements under OBA: access without clamp. Because housing is a dominant
essentials domain (L), OBA treats baseline access as a constitutive safeguard: if households can be pushed into homelessness, unsafe
units, or perpetual waiting as a normal market outcome, the order is already failing. Baseline housing security is therefore defined
as (i) minimum quality and safety, (ii) bounded waiting times for access pathways, (iii) transparent allocation without hidden
gatekeeping, (iv) emergency housing capacity with rapid throughput, and (v) exit rails that preserve mobility (no punitive fees, and
portability where relevant). These requirements are parameterised: jurisdictions set numeric thresholds, but the rails (measurability,
contestability, and fail-closed discipline) are binding.

---

## B05.CH07.P03.S01 — Baseline access: minimum quality standard, maximum wait-time norms (parameters)

Baseline access is defined as a housing floor that protects health, safety, and participation, and is reachable within bounded time.

### Minimum quality standard (binding)
- Housing offered through baseline access pathways must meet a minimum standard for: structural safety, heating/ventilation, water and
  sanitation, damp/mould control, basic accessibility, and privacy.
- Units that fail minimum standards are non-admissible for baseline allocation and must trigger remediation or replacement.

### Maximum wait-time norms (parameters; binding requirement to set and publish)
- Jurisdictions must set maximum wait-time norms for baseline access (by household type and urgency class) and publish them as
  parameters.
- If maximum waits are exceeded in aggregate or in tails, the system must trigger capacity expansion measures (ΔG) and/or allocation
  redesign (fail-closed against normalised waiting clamp).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish distributions (median, p90/p95) for wait times and quality compliance, with stable definitions and time series.

---

## B05.CH07.P03.S02 — Anti-discrimination: transparent allocation, auditable rules, dispute flow

Allocation systems are high-risk surfaces for hidden block power and discrimination. Under OBA, allocation must be contestable.

### Allocation rules (binding)
- Allocation criteria must be **explicit**, written, and publicly accessible: priority classes, weighting, eligibility, and required
  documentation.
- Decision-making must be auditable: each allocation decision must map to a rule path and be reproducible from the recorded inputs
  (privacy-minimal).

### Anti-discrimination minima (binding)
- Prohibit direct and proxy discrimination (including algorithmic proxies) in baseline access pathways.
- Require periodic disparate-impact audits with published aggregate results and remediation plans.

### Dispute flow (binding)
- Applicants must have a fast objection/appeal process with strict time bounds and a neutral review layer.
- Denials must provide a reason code and the rule reference; “silent rejection” and indefinite limbo are non-conforming.

## B05.CH07.P03.S03 — Emergency housing: rapid shelter and throughput (fallback-commons link to CH04)

Emergency housing is the fail-safe layer when shocks, eviction, violence, or displacement break the normal access path. It must not be
a warehousing regime.

### Required properties (binding)
- **Rapid shelter**: immediate access capacity for acute need (same-day where feasible), with safe conditions and basic services.
- **Throughput**: emergency housing must be designed for transition into stable units; prolonged stays without pathway are treated as
  structural failure.
- **No clamp**: emergency access may not be conditional on punitive debt acceptance, coercive surveillance, or forced programme
  participation beyond safety and basic coordination.

### Fallback-commons linkage (binding)
- Emergency housing is treated as a fallback commons function (linked to the broader CH04 commons safety infrastructure): it must have
  ring-fenced funding, published capacity statistics, and predictable triggers for scaling.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish capacity and throughput metrics: occupancy, average length of stay, transition rates, and return-to-emergency rates (privacy
  safe aggregation).

---

## B05.CH07.P03.S04 — Exit rails: moving without punitive fees; portability of waitlist time/deposits where relevant

Mobility is a core lifeworld freedom. Under OBA, housing must not be designed as a lock-in apparatus.

### Exit rails (binding)
- **No punitive exit fees**: break fees, renewal traps, and discretionary “restoration” charges are prohibited beyond demonstrable,
  bounded cost recovery.
- **Deposit discipline**: deposits must be held in auditable escrow-like structures; timelines for return are strict; disputes use
  reproducible calculation packs and neutral review.
- **Portability where relevant (parameters)**: jurisdictions may implement portability of waitlist time, eligibility status, and
  deposits across providers or regions to prevent lock-in via administrative resets.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish exit-friction indicators: average moving costs attributable to fees, deposit return times, dispute volumes/outcomes, and
  incidence of blacklisting or denial due to prior moves.
