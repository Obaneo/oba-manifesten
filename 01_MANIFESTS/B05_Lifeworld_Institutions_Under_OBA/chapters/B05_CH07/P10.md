# B05.CH07.P10 — Lock-in metrics and clamp detection in housing

This paragraph specifies how OBA detects lock-in and clamp dynamics in housing and how those signals trigger escalation. In an
essentials domain, harm often does not appear first as “price”: it appears as **immobility**, coercive contract terms, opaque charges,
and exclusion mechanisms that prevent exit. OBA therefore requires a measurable lock-in layer: clamp indicators (moving friction,
penalties, blacklist/denial), fee-stack metrics (service-charge and management extraction), and market power metrics (concentration,
vacancy manipulation, collusive signals). These indicators are not “monitoring for its own sake”: they are triggers for structured
response—anomaly detection, audit packs, requalification of E components, and structural remedies when extraction is systemic.

---

<a id="b05ch07p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH07.P10.S01 — Clamp indicators: moving friction, penalties, loss of waitlist time, credit penalties, blacklisting

Clamp in housing is defined by rising exit costs and rising exclusion risk. OBA requires publication of the following indicator
families.

### Moving friction (binding)
- **All-in moving cost** distributions (median/p90/p95) attributable to fees and charges (broker fees, contract exit costs, mandatory
  cleaning/repair packages, administrative fees).
- **Time-to-move** distributions: time from search to contract and from notice to exit, including administrative delays.
- **Portability loss incidence**: share of moves that cause loss of waitlist time, eligibility status, or deposits where portability is
  relevant in the jurisdiction.

### Penalties and exit charges (binding)
- Incidence and magnitude of break fees, renewal traps, deposit forfeitures, discretionary damage claims, and “restoration” charges.
- Share of tenants facing charges above bounded cost recovery thresholds (jurisdictional parameters).

### Credit penalties and blacklisting (binding)
- Incidence of housing-related credit penalties (e.g., reporting practices, debt transfers) and denial rates tied to prior arrears or
  disputes.
- **Blacklist/denial metrics**: denial rates, stated reason-code distributions, and appeal outcomes; measure opaque registries and
  automated exclusions.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Rising tails in these indicators trigger clamp escalation procedures: contract template tightening, fee caps, and audit of providers
  with elevated incidence.

<a id="b05ch07p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH07.P10.S02 — Fee-stack metrics: management fee shares, service-cost anomalies, intermediary layers

Fee-stacks in housing often operate through “service charges” and intermediary layers that are difficult to compare across units.

### Fee-stack metrics (binding)
- **Management fee share**: management/administration charges as a share of total rent+service cost, by provider segment and region.
- **Service-charge decomposition**: itemised cost categories and variance across comparable units/buildings; flag outliers and rising
  dispersion as extraction signals.
- **Intermediary depth**: number of entities in the ownership/management/service chain (look-through), including related-party
  providers and captive vendor arrangements.
- **Related-party exposure**: share of service spend routed to affiliates of the landlord/manager.

### Anomaly indicators (binding)
- Persistent outliers in fee share, unexplained cost increases, frequent reclassification of charges, and high dispute volumes over
  service invoices.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- Non-auditable or non-cost-based charges are classified as E by default and trigger refunds/credits and contract rewrite where
  applicable (fail-closed against opaque service extraction).

<a id="b05ch07p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH07.P10.S03 — Market power metrics: concentration, vacancy manipulation, collusive pricing signals

Housing clamp is amplified when market power allows landlords to convert scarcity into toll pricing and exclusion.

### Market power metrics (binding)
- **Concentration**: ownership/management concentration by region and unit class (HHI or equivalent), including portfolio landlords and
  institutional investors (look-through).
- **Vacancy and withholding**: vacancy rates, duration of vacancies, and patterns consistent with strategic withholding or renovation
  as a pretext for rent resets.
- **Rent dispersion and synchrony**: unusually synchronised rent increases across large landlords, identical fee schedules, or repeated
  simultaneous “market adjustments” not explained by cost indices.
- **Allocation control**: dominance of a single platform/broker or gatekeeper in listings and tenant screening.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Elevated concentration or strategic withholding triggers contestability interventions: licensing constraints, disclosure expansion,
  audit of allocation/screening systems, and structural remedies where abuse is persistent.

<a id="b05ch07p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH07.P10.S04 — Escalation: anomaly → audit → requalification → structural remedies (unbundling, caps, procurement shifts)

OBA defines a structured escalation ladder so detection leads to correction rather than permanent “monitoring”.

### Step 1: anomaly detection (binding)
- Use published metrics to flag outliers by region/provider/product type (tail increases, fee dispersion spikes, denial/blacklisting
  clusters, vacancy manipulation signatures).
- Trigger a standard anomaly notice with the indicator set, thresholds breached, and requested evidence.

### Step 2: audit pack request (binding)
- Require a standard audit pack: contract templates, pricing/service-charge decomposition, ownership/management chain (UBC look-through),
  dispute logs, and enforcement/eviction pipeline data.
- Failure to provide the pack within time bounds triggers fail-closed restrictions (suspension of contested charges or rent increases).

### Step 3: requalification and remedies (binding)
- Requalify non-auditable or non-functional components as E and apply remedies: refunds/credits, fee caps, contract rewrite into
  standard templates, and restriction of abusive screening/blacklisting.

### Step 4: structural remedies (binding)
- Where extraction is systemic or concentration enables toll pricing, apply structural interventions:
  - **Unbundling**: separate ownership from management/service contracts; prohibit captive vendor chains.
  - **Caps and licensing**: tighten rent-rail caps, impose landlord licensing conditions, restrict portfolio expansion under exceedance.
  - **Procurement shifts**: redirect public/commons procurement and land releases away from captured actors; prioritise commons delivery
    and maintenance-first providers.
  - **Deconcentration**: where caps and W/I exceedance apply, trigger divestment, conversion to commons, or levies.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Repeated failure to disclose, repeated fee-stack violations, or persistent clamp indicators without remediation triggers suspension of
  the provider/product from admissible housing programmes and mandatory governance intervention (fail-closed against housing clamp).
