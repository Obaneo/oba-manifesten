# B05.CH07.P04 — Build and renovation capacity as commons infrastructure (ΔG)

This paragraph specifies housing build and renovation capacity as **commons infrastructure** under OBA (ΔG). In an essentials domain,
affordability is primarily a capacity problem: if unit output, renovation throughput, and maintenance quality cannot scale, any
demand-side relief will tend to capitalise into higher prices and higher L. OBA therefore treats build/renovation capacity as a
governance asset that must be planned, staffed, and measured like infrastructure—without requiring central planning of every project.
The rail objective is to increase throughput, reduce cycle times, prioritise maintenance-first backlog reduction, and prevent capture
in procurement and delivery organisations.

---

## B05.CH07.P04.S01 — Capacity planning without central planning: pipeline, skills, prefab, standardisation

OBA requires capacity planning as a bounded, evidence-driven pipeline approach: forecast needs, remove bottlenecks, and expand
production capability without micromanaging design choices.

### Pipeline planning (binding)
- Maintain a published, rolling pipeline of housing output and renovation work: targets, stages, expected completion windows, and
  dependency constraints (land, permits, infrastructure, labour).
- Track bottlenecks explicitly (permit cycle time, contractor availability, inspection capacity, materials) and maintain a backlog
  register.

### Skills and labour (binding)
- Establish skills pipelines (training, apprenticeships, re-skilling) sized to the required output, with published capacity metrics.
- Prevent labour scarcity from becoming a permanent throttling mechanism by ensuring predictable multi-year demand and procurement
  stability.

### Prefab and standardisation (binding)
- Standardise components and interfaces where it reduces time and cost without degrading quality: modular elements, standard
  permitting packages, repeatable designs for baseline units, and interoperable building systems.
- Use prefab/off-site construction as an admissible productivity lever where quality control and maintenance interfaces are auditable.

### Contestability rule (binding)
- Capacity expansion must not create new monopolies: procurement and delivery must be structured to keep multiple providers viable,
  avoid single-vendor lock-in, and enable entry for new builders.

## B05.CH07.P04.S02 — Maintenance-first: renovation/insulation as first priority (backlog reduction)

Under OBA, maintenance and renovation are first-class supply. They protect health, reduce L (especially energy burden), and prevent
depreciation-driven scarcity.

### Maintenance-first principle (binding)
- Treat renovation, repairs, and insulation upgrades as priority output before new build expansion where backlogs are material.
- Maintain an auditable backlog register: type, severity, health/safety risk, and expected resolution windows.

### Backlog reduction rails (binding)
- Define backlog thresholds that trigger escalation (capacity surge, funding reallocation, governance intervention).
- Prevent “planned neglect” by ring-fencing maintenance budgets (especially in commons/public stock) and prohibiting diversion to
  discretionary spending or asset-speculation motives.

### L-reduction linkage (binding)
- Prioritise interventions with high L impact: insulation, damp/mould remediation, heating system upgrades, accessibility upgrades, and
  structural repairs that reduce recurring costs and health harms.

## B05.CH07.P04.S03 — Public/commons build organisations: governance minima, procurement rails, anti-capture

Where public or commons build organisations exist (or are created), they must be designed as anti-capture infrastructure.

### Governance minima (binding)
- **Mandate clarity**: deliver ΔG output (units + renovation) with maintenance-first priority and published performance metrics.
- **Transparency**: publish procurement decisions, contract values, change orders, and delivery outcomes in privacy-safe form.
- **Audit-of-the-auditor**: independent audits of procurement, cost estimation models, and supplier relationships; rotation and peer
  review.
- **No selective enforcement**: rules for contractor performance, penalties, and change approvals must be uniform and logged.

### Procurement rails (binding)
- Use standard contract formats with clear scope, measurable deliverables, and bounded variation rules.
- Prohibit vendor lock-in patterns: proprietary standards that block future competition, captive maintenance contracts, and opaque
  sub-contractor chains.
- Require look-through on ownership and conflicts of interest in major suppliers.

### Anti-capture hard stop (binding)
- Proven corruption, systematic bid rigging, or persistent non-disclosure triggers governance reset: contract suspension, leadership
  rotation, and re-tendering under tightened rails (fail-closed against procurement capture).

## B05.CH07.P04.S04 — Measurability: output (units), quality, cycle time, cost per unit, backlog indicators

Build and renovation capacity must be governed through measurable outcomes to prevent paper targets and cosmetic compliance.

### Core metrics (binding)
- **Output**: completed units delivered and renovated units completed, by category (baseline, family, accessibility, emergency, etc.).
- **Quality**: defect rates, post-occupancy incident rates (damp/mould, safety), and inspection pass/fail distributions.
- **Cycle time**: median and tail durations from permit to completion and from work order to repair completion.
- **Cost per unit**: total delivered cost per unit (including land, infrastructure, and financing where relevant) with standardised
  accounting categories to prevent hidden transfer pricing.
- **Backlog indicators**: size, severity distribution, average age of backlog, and share exceeding SLA/time norms.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish these metrics as time series with stable definitions and product/region taxonomy; include p90/p95 tails to detect clamp risk
  and governance failure.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If output falls while costs rise and backlogs grow, demand-side interventions must be treated as non-conforming until capacity and
  bottlenecks are corrected (fail-closed against price-capitalisation of scarcity).
