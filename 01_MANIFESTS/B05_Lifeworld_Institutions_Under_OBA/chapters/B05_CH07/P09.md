# B05.CH07.P09 — Maintenance, quality, and energy performance (housing as infrastructure)

This paragraph specifies maintenance, quality, and energy performance requirements for housing under OBA, treating housing as
infrastructure rather than as a financial asset. In an essentials domain, quality failures (damp, mould, unsafe electrics, heating
breakdowns) and maintenance backlogs translate directly into higher L, health harms, and lock-in. OBA therefore binds housing
providers—private, public, and commons—to inspection cycles, repair SLAs, and backlog thresholds, with measurability and escalation
discipline. Energy performance is treated as a first-order L driver: insulation and renovation programmes are admissible as L-reduction
infrastructure (ΔG) and must be delivered with maintenance-first logic. To keep the system contestable, OBA requires incident logging
and public aggregated statistics, while enforcing privacy minimalism.

---

<a id="b05ch07p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH07.P09.S01 — Maintenance standards: inspection cycles, repair SLAs, backlog thresholds

Maintenance is a governance rail: without enforceable standards, housing drifts into planned neglect and implicit rent extraction.

### Inspection cycles (binding)
- Providers must run periodic inspections on a published schedule (jurisdictional parameters), including safety-critical checks
  (electrics, fire safety, structural issues) and health-critical checks (ventilation, moisture).
- Inspections must produce a standardised condition report with severity grading and a repair plan.

### Repair SLAs (binding)
- Repairs must be categorised by urgency (emergency / urgent / routine) with maximum resolution times (parameters required).
- Failure to meet SLAs triggers automatic escalation: temporary remedies (e.g., alternative heating), rent/charge adjustments where
  applicable, and enforcement review.

### Backlog thresholds (binding)
- Providers must maintain an auditable backlog register: age, severity, and SLA breach status.
- Jurisdictions must define backlog thresholds (parameters) that trigger interventions: capacity surge requirements, licence review,
  governance reset for commons/public providers, or mandated management changes.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate maintenance performance: SLA compliance distributions (median/p90/p95), backlog size and age, and repeat-fault
  rates (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch07p09s02"></a>
## B05.CH07.P09.S02 — Health quality: damp/mould, safety, accessibility (baseline)

Baseline quality is not discretionary. Health and safety failures are treated as immediate lifeworld harm.

### Baseline health and safety minima (binding)
- **Damp/mould**: mandatory remediation triggers and maximum time-to-remedy; providers must address root causes (ventilation, leaks,
  insulation), not cosmetic repainting.
- **Safety**: compliance with fire safety, structural safety, and electrical standards; urgent defects require emergency response
  protocols.
- **Accessibility**: baseline accessibility requirements for new builds and a progressive upgrade path for existing stock (parameters),
  with priority for households with mobility needs.

### Prohibited practices (binding)
- Retaliatory action (rent increase, eviction pressure, service withdrawal) in response to complaints or inspection findings.
- “Fix denial” via shifting responsibility to tenants where provider-controlled defects exist (structure, leaks, ventilation).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Where minimum health/safety standards cannot be maintained, the unit is non-admissible for letting/occupation until remediated, and
  the provider must supply temporary alternative accommodation or compensatory relief (jurisdictional mechanism).

<a id="b05ch07p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH07.P09.S03 — Insulation/energy: renovation programmes as L-reduction (link to energy CH08)

Energy performance is a major driver of L through ongoing utility costs and volatility exposure. OBA therefore treats insulation and
energy renovation as essential infrastructure, not as optional upgrades.

### Programme principles (binding)
- **Maintenance-first integration**: insulation upgrades must be integrated with damp remediation and structural repairs to avoid
  creating hidden defects.
- **Targeting by L impact**: prioritise buildings and households with the highest energy burden and poorest performance; use audit-safe
  targeting to avoid capture.
- **Measured outcomes**: programmes must publish pre/post performance metrics (energy rating changes, consumption reductions adjusted
  for weather where feasible) and cost per achieved improvement unit.

### Financing discipline (binding)
- Public/ΔM financing is admissible only into real renovation capacity (ΔG) with tagging, leakage tests, and ring-fenced maintenance;
  it is not admissible as a general subsidy that capitalises into higher rents/prices.

### Linkage (binding)
- Energy renovation rules in CH08 apply as the technical counterpart; CH07 specifies housing-side obligations (tenancy protection,
  maintenance integration, and anti-rent-hike discipline during upgrades).

## B05.CH07.P09.S04 — Incident logging: tenant rights, duty-to-report, public aggregated incident statistics

To prevent quiet neglect and intimidation, housing quality must be contestable through evidence and publication.

### Tenant rights and duty-to-report (binding)
- Tenants must have a clear right to report defects, receive acknowledgement, and obtain a repair timeline with reason codes for any
  denial or delay.
- Providers must log incidents and repairs in a standard format and provide tenants with access to their unit’s repair record.

### Public aggregated incident statistics (binding)
- Publish aggregated incident metrics: complaint volumes, category breakdown (damp/mould, heating, safety, accessibility), resolution
  times, SLA breach rates, and repeat incidents by provider segment and region.
- Publish enforcement actions and licence interventions in aggregate to prevent quiet continuation.

### Privacy minimalism (binding)
- Public outputs must avoid individual address disclosure and small-cell re-identification; apply suppression and bucketing rules.
- Incident logs may not be repurposed for commercial profiling, tenant steering, or retaliatory screening (hard stop).

### Hard stop (binding)
- Systematic concealment of incidents, retaliation against complainants, or persistent SLA breach without remediation triggers
  conformance suspension and mandatory governance intervention (fail-closed against neglect as a rent mechanism).
