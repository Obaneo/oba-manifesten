# B05.CH07.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for housing governance under OBA and the hard stops that apply when the system
drifts back into scarcity rents, lock-in, and asset-support dynamics. Housing is the largest essentials domain (L) and a dominant
wealth channel; small policy deviations can rapidly capitalise into higher prices, higher leverage, and deeper clamp. OBA therefore
defines explicit non-conformance signals and fail-closed responses. The objective is not to preserve “market functioning” at any
price, but to preserve **order compatibility**: housing outcomes must reduce L, expand real capacity (ΔG), protect baseline access,
and prevent toll positions from becoming permanent.

---

<a id="b05ch07p13s01"></a>
## B05.CH07.P13.S01 — Housing washing: programmes that stabilise prices via demand stimulus → prohibited channel

“Housing washing” is the pattern where a programme is framed as affordability support but functions as price stabilisation or price
support via demand stimulus.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Buyer subsidies, guarantees, tax advantages, or credit expansions deployed under constrained supply, coinciding with rising
  price-to-income and rising mortgage burden tails.
- Programmes whose success criteria implicitly or explicitly include “supporting the market”, “preventing price falls”, or
  “protecting homeowner equity”.
- Public purchases of private stock or bailout-like interventions that sustain valuations without delivering ΔG (units, renovation
  throughput) or reducing L.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Such channels are **prohibited**: they must be suspended and redirected to ΔG (build/renovation capacity) and housing commons, with
  explicit incidence and leakage tests before reintroduction (fail-closed against asset-support).

---

<a id="b05ch07p13s02"></a>
## B05.CH07.P13.S02 — Capture: construction/land lobby steers rules toward scarcity → audit trigger and governance remedies

A common failure mode is regulatory and process capture that sustains scarcity: lobbying, discretionary permitting, and procurement
arrangements that throttle output while maintaining high prices and rents.

### Capture indicators (binding)
- Permitting cycle times and backlogs worsen without corrective capacity measures; rules become more discretionary and less auditable.
- Procurement repeatedly selects a narrow supplier set with persistent cost overruns and change-order inflation.
- Zoning/land decisions concentrate windfalls and restrict serviced land release without transparent rationale.
- Exemptions, carve-outs, and “special zones” proliferate without published evidence packs and sunset discipline.

### Audit trigger (binding)
- Detection of these patterns triggers mandatory audit: decision logs, conflict-of-interest disclosures, supplier ownership look-through,
  and performance benchmarking against time norms.

### Governance remedies (binding)
- Tighten standardisation and time norms; reduce discretionary variance pathways; expand inspection/permit capacity.
- Enforce anti-corruption rails: procurement transparency, rotation, audit-of-the-auditor, and conflict-of-interest enforcement.
- Where capture persists, apply governance reset: leadership rotation, re-tendering, and rule simplification to auditable minimums
  (fail-closed against scarcity-by-design).

<a id="b05ch07p13s03"></a>
## B05.CH07.P13.S03 — Maintenance neglect: backlog grows → commons-status downgrade or landlord-licence remedies

Maintenance neglect converts housing into a hidden L amplifier and a covert extraction mechanism (collect rent while degrading quality).

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Growing maintenance backlog size/age/severity; repeated SLA breaches; rising incident rates (damp/mould, heating failures, safety
  defects).
- Budget patterns that divert maintenance funds to expansion, branding, or surplus extraction (including affiliate contracting).
- Provider segments with persistently worse quality outcomes and higher retaliation/dispute indicators.

### Remedy ladder (binding)
- **Mandatory remediation plan** with time-bounded backlog reduction targets and published progress metrics.
- **Financial remedies**: rent/charge adjustments, penalties, and ring-fencing of maintenance reserves.
- **Governance/operational remedies**: management replacement, procurement intervention, or assignment of a corrective operator.

### Status and licence consequences (binding)
- For commons providers: persistent neglect triggers **commons-status downgrade** and suspension of preferential financing channels
  until governance is reset.
- For private landlords/managers: persistent neglect triggers **licence restrictions**, letting bans for affected units, or forced
  transfer of management (fail-closed against neglect-as-business-model).

<a id="b05ch07p13s04"></a>
## B05.CH07.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: if L in housing rises or clamp indicators worsen, redesign is mandatory

OBA treats worsening lifeworld outcomes as evidence that the system is drifting toward incompatibility. In housing, the primary signal
is rising L and worsening clamp metrics.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- **Rising L**: sustained increase in housing cost burden distributions and tails (p90/p95), especially when disconnected from income
  growth and quality improvements.
- **Worsening clamp indicators**: rising moving friction, exit penalties, blacklisting/denial without due process, and rising dispute
  volumes with long resolution times.
- **Scarcity persistence**: long wait times, low throughput, and growing backlogs despite policy intervention.
- **Opacity**: inability to publish core metrics safely and reproducibly.

### Mandatory response (binding)
- Treat the current policy mix as non-conforming; initiate redesign with explicit mechanism diagnosis (supply bottlenecks, land capture,
  fee-stacks, market power).
- Suspend demand-side channels and redirect capacity and funding to ΔG (build/renovation/maintenance) and housing commons.
- Tighten contract standards, rent rails, and dispute capacity until clamp indicators reverse.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Fail-closed events and redesign decisions must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to prevent
  quiet drift and selective enforcement.
