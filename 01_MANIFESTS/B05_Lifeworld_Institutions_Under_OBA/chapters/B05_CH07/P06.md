# B05.CH07.P06 — Rental market: price discipline, contract standards, and lock-in reduction

This paragraph specifies how rental markets must operate under OBA so that rent remains a housing access price rather than an
escalating toll. Rental markets are high-risk surfaces for scarcity rents, fee-stacks, and lock-in: tenants must pay to live, and
moving is costly, so landlords and intermediaries can extract via indexation games, opaque service charges, and punitive contract
terms. OBA therefore requires **rent rails** (bounded increases and transparent cost pass-through), standard contract templates with
explicit prohibited clamp clauses, look-through on landlord and management structures to detect fee-stack cascades, and fast dispute
procedures based on reproducible calculations. Where contestability is lost, OBA applies fail-closed remedies including contract
rewrite and structural interventions.

---

## B05.CH07.P06.S01 — Rent rails: indexation, bounded increases, transparent service charges

Rent increases are admissible only as bounded, rule-based adjustments linked to measurable cost drivers, not as opportunistic scarcity
pricing.

### Rent rails (binding)
- **Indexation discipline**: rent may be indexed only to published, standard indices (jurisdictional parameter), with explicit caps on
  annual adjustment and time-bounded catch-up rules.
- **Bounded increases**: above-index increases require a defined justification class (e.g., verified renovation that improves quality
  or reduces operating costs) and must be amortised transparently over a standard period.
- **No scarcity-premium coding**: “market adjustment” clauses that function as discretionary scarcity rent extraction are prohibited.

### Service charges (binding)
- Service charges must be itemised, cost-based, and auditable (utilities, maintenance, common-area services). Bundled “admin” or
  “management” surcharges without cost basis are classified as E.
- Tenants must receive an annual reconciliation statement with reproducible calculations and supporting documentation access rights.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate rent tables: median/p90/p95 rent levels by region and unit class, indexation usage, above-index justifications
  frequency, and service-charge shares (privacy-safe aggregation).

---

## B05.CH07.P06.S02 — Contract templates: standard leases with prohibited clamp clauses

Lease terms are treated as governance rails: contract design can create clamp even when nominal rent is stable.

### Standard lease templates (binding)
- Jurisdictions must provide standard lease templates with fixed placement for key terms: rent, service charges, deposit, duration,
  renewal, repairs obligations, entry/exit rules, dispute flow, and enforcement steps.
- Non-standard clauses are permitted only in a clearly marked annex with justification and rule references.

### Prohibited clamp clauses (binding)
- **Punitive exit penalties**: break fees beyond bounded cost recovery; automatic renewal traps without clear opt-out windows.
- **Deposit abuse**: non-auditable deposit holding, discretionary deductions without evidence, or delayed return beyond strict time
  bounds.
- **Opaque service-charge powers**: discretionary charges, unilateral fee creation, or unlimited pass-through clauses.
- **Excessive gatekeeping**: income requirements not tied to affordability logic, coercive guarantor demands, and denial without reason
  codes and objection paths.
- **Blacklisting without due process**: shared registries or denial mechanisms without transparent criteria and appeal.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any landlord product that relies on prohibited clamp clauses is non-conforming and must be rewritten into standard format as a
  condition of continued letting.

## B05.CH07.P06.S03 — Landlord structures: look-through on ownership, fee-stacks, and management constructs

Rental markets drift into extraction when ownership and servicing are split into multiple layers to insert fees and obscure
accountability.

### Look-through rule (binding)
- Rental regulation applies to the **ultimate beneficial controller** and the full chain of intermediaries (owners, managers,
  servicers, brokers). Entities cannot evade rails through corporate shells, nominee arrangements, or fragmented contracting.

### Fee-stack detection (binding)
- Identify and requalify layered charges across: management fees, leasing fees, “platform” fees, maintenance markups, captive vendor
  contracts, and related-party service companies.
- Where charges are not bounded productive costs, they are classified as E and subject to caps, refunds, or prohibition.

### Accountability rule (binding)
- The party with control over allocation, pricing, and enforcement bears the burden of proof and must provide the audit pack for rent
  and service-charge claims.

## B05.CH07.P06.S04 — Dispute flow: fast procedures, reproducible calculations, remedies (refund/contract rewrite)

Dispute resolution must be fast enough to prevent lock-in and intimidation from becoming the de facto rule.

### Fast procedures (binding)
- Strict time bounds for: complaint acknowledgement, document provision, provisional relief, hearing/review, and final resolution.
- During disputes, clamp escalation is suspended: no retaliatory eviction, no fee stacking, and no adverse service withdrawal beyond
  safety necessity.

### Reproducible calculations (binding)
- All contested amounts (rent increases, service charges, deposit deductions, repair costs) must be supported by a standardised
  calculation pack: inputs, method, invoices, and rule references.
- Tenants must be able to recompute the outcome from disclosed parameters.

### Remedies (binding)
- **Refund/credit** for overcharges with statutory uplift where applicable.
- **Contract rewrite** into the standard template where non-standard clauses or prohibited clamp terms are identified.
- **Structural remedies** for repeat violations: penalties, licence restrictions, management replacement, or forced divestment where
  concentration and abuse are systemic.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate dispute statistics: volumes, common causes (rent, service charges, deposits), resolution times, outcomes, and
  repeat-offender indicators (privacy-safe aggregation).
