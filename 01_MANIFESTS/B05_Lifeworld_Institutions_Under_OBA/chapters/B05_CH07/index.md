# B05.CH07 — Housing as an essential: rent/ownership, build capacity, lock-in, commons options, and L-reduction

This chapter specifies housing as the largest **essentials (L) domain** and therefore as a primary lifeworld interface for clamp,
scarcity, and block power. Under OBA, housing policy is admissible only insofar as it reduces L through **supply and maintenance
capacity (ΔG)**, contestability, contract discipline, and commons options—not through demand stimulus or credit-driven asset inflation.
When housing becomes a rentier toll system, a fee-stack ecosystem, or a lock-in regime, it is requalified as E and must be neutralised.

CH07 binds housing governance to canonical definition sets (P/E/C; Definition Set II for L; caps W/I; ΔM rails prohibiting
asset-support; commons standards) and provides diagnostics, baseline access rules, build/renovation commons design, land scarcity
remedies, rent and ownership discipline, clamp detection metrics, publication requirements, and hard stops. It is designed to
fail-closed: programmes that stabilise or raise prices, normalise lock-in, or conceal scarcity/capture are non-conforming until
redesigned.
