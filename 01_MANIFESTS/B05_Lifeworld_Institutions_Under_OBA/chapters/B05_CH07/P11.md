# B05.CH07.P11 — Housing and caps: W-MAX effects on property and landlord positions

This paragraph specifies how OBA caps (W-MAX and I-MAX) apply to housing assets and landlord positions. Housing is a dominant wealth
store and a primary channel for accumulation under scarcity. If caps do not apply to real estate with look-through, OBA cannot
prevent portfolio landlordism, rentier toll formation, and speculative cycles from rebuilding. OBA therefore includes real estate in
the W base using auditable valuation rules, applies look-through across corporate shells and funds to the ultimate beneficial
controller (UBC), and treats rental flows as I-base income with requalification of fee/royalty constructs that function as extraction.
Where caps are exceeded, remedies are structural: deconcentration via divestment, conversion to commons, or levies—chosen to reduce L
and restore contestability rather than to preserve rentier claims.

---

<a id="b05ch07p11s01"></a>
## B05.CH07.P11.S01 — Real estate in the W base: valuation, look-through, portfolios, private funds

Real estate must be included in the wealth base (W) in a way that is hard to evade and easy to audit.

### Inclusion rule (binding)
- All real estate interests that confer economic benefit or control are included in W: direct ownership, beneficial interests through
  companies, trusts, partnerships, and fund units.
- Rights that function as ownership (long-term leaseholds, ground-lease claims with capitalisable windfalls, option positions tied to
  planning outcomes) are included where they create wealth-like exposure.

### Valuation (binding)
- Use a standard valuation basis (jurisdictional parameter) with conservative defaults where prices are volatile or illiquid.
- Portfolio and fund valuations must be reconcilable to underlying asset registers and disclosed valuation methods; discretionary
  “internal marks” without auditability are non-conforming.

### Look-through (binding)
- Apply look-through to the ultimate beneficial controller (UBC): nominee arrangements, layered holding companies, and fund-of-fund
  structures do not prevent attribution.
- Where ownership is fragmented into multiple entities with common control, holdings are consolidated for cap assessment.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Maintain a reproducible property-and-interest register for cap assessment, with linkage to beneficial ownership evidence and
  valuation method packs (privacy-minimal for public outputs; full detail for auditors).

<a id="b05ch07p11s02"></a>
## B05.CH07.P11.S02 — Rental as income flow: I-base inclusion and requalification of fees/royalties

Rental cashflows and housing-related charges are treated as income flows subject to I-base inclusion and E requalification.

### Inclusion rule (binding)
- Net rental income is included in I-base using standardised accounting categories; “management” and “service” entities under common
  control do not remove income from the base.
- Any recurrent housing charge that functions as a rent substitute (mandatory service charges, platform fees, allocation tolls) is
  included as rental-like income.

### Requalification (binding)
- Fees and royalties embedded in landlord structures (brand fees, “technology” charges, captive maintenance markups, related-party
  consulting) are requalified as rental/extraction income where their functional effect is to transfer surplus to the controlling
  party.
- Artificial cost inflation through related-party contracts is treated as avoidance and triggers adjustments.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Landlords/managers must provide an auditable fee decomposition and related-party disclosure pack for sampled audits; failure triggers
  conservative requalification as E and I-base inclusion (fail-closed against fee shifting).

<a id="b05ch07p11s03"></a>
## B05.CH07.P11.S03 — Deconcentration: remedies under exceedance (divestment, conversion to commons, levy)

When W-MAX exceedance is detected in housing positions, the remedy must reduce concentration and toll formation, not merely collect a
fine while preserving control.

### Remedy ladder (binding)
- **Divestment**: reduce holdings over a defined window to return below cap thresholds, with anti-fire-sale rules that prevent sales to
  related parties or nominee proxies.
- **Conversion to commons**: convert a portion of holdings into housing commons forms (public option, co-op, trust, non-profit) with
  bounded pricing rails and maintenance obligations; used where this directly reduces L and increases baseline access.
- **Levy**: apply escalating levies on exceedance where immediate divestment is not feasible, with proceeds ring-fenced for housing
  commons and build/renovation capacity (ΔG).

### Allocation rule (binding)
- Remedy selection is based on lifeworld impact: prefer actions that reduce L tails, shorten wait times, and reduce lock-in. Where
  portfolio control sustains toll pricing, structural deconcentration is mandatory.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate exceedance and remedy statistics: number of cases, remedy mix, units affected, and commons conversion volumes
  (privacy-safe aggregation).

## B05.CH07.P11.S04 — Failure mode: shifting into corporate shells/nominees → detection via UBC graph

A predictable avoidance pattern is shifting housing assets into corporate shells, nominee ownership, and layered funds to obscure
control. OBA treats this as a core failure mode.

### Detection via UBC graph (binding)
- Maintain a beneficial ownership and control graph (UBC graph) linking persons/entities to property interests, voting rights, economic
  benefit, and related-party relationships.
- Use anomaly detection for: rapid entity proliferation, circular ownership, repeated related-party transfers, and cross-jurisdiction
  structures with opaque beneficial ownership.

### Conformance and hard stop (binding)
- Failure to disclose beneficial ownership, or evidence of nominee concealment, triggers conservative attribution to suspected
  controllers and immediate restrictions (transaction blocks, higher levies, forced disclosures).
- Repeat avoidance attempts trigger escalated remedies: forced consolidation for cap assessment, mandatory divestment windows, and
  restrictions on acquiring additional housing assets (fail-closed against cap evasion).
