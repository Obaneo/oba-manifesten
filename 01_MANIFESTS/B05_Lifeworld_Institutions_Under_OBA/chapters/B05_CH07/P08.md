# B05.CH07.P08 — Housing commons: public option, co-ops, trusts, and non-profit rental

This paragraph specifies housing commons under OBA: institutional forms that provide housing access without converting scarcity into
private windfalls. In an essentials domain, commons options are not “nice-to-have social policy”; they are **order infrastructure**
that reduces L, increases contestability, and provides exit from private toll positions. Housing commons must therefore be designed
with explicit access rules, maintenance-first budgeting, transparency, and contestability. Financing is treated as a disciplined
channel: C-flows and (where applicable) ΔM are admissible only into real commons capacity (capex and ring-fenced maintenance), not into
asset support or self-benefit loops. Where “commons” structures embed speculation, hidden dividends, or insider extraction, they are
classified as commons-washing and are non-conforming.

---

<a id="b05ch07p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH07.P08.S01 — Commons types: public housing, housing co-ops, community land trusts, non-profit landlords

OBA recognises multiple commons forms. The defining property is not ownership label but functional non-speculation and access-first
governance.

### Commons types (admissible)
- **Public housing**: publicly owned or controlled stock with access rules and maintenance obligations treated as public service
  infrastructure.
- **Housing co-ops**: resident-governed housing with non-speculative resale rules and maintenance-first budgeting.
- **Community land trusts (CLTs)**: land held in trust with long-term, inheritable use rights and bounded resale value capture; separates
  land windfalls from private extraction.
- **Non-profit landlords**: entities prohibited from distributing profits to private owners; surplus is reinvested in maintenance and
  capacity expansion.

### Functional definition (binding)
- A housing commons must provide: stable access, bounded cost trajectories, transparent charges, and governance that prevents private
  capture of location windfalls.

---

<a id="b05ch07p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH07.P08.S02 — Governance minima: access rules, maintenance-first budget, transparency, contestability

Housing commons governance is treated as anti-capture infrastructure, because it can otherwise recreate closed insider systems.

### Access rules (binding)
- Eligibility and allocation rules must be explicit, published, and auditable; priority criteria and weighting must be reproducible.
- Dispute flow is mandatory: fast objection/appeal with reason codes and time bounds; no “silent rejection”.

### Maintenance-first budget (binding)
- Maintenance and health/safety remediation are first claim on operating budgets; deferral beyond thresholds is non-conforming.
- Ring-fence maintenance reserves and publish backlog metrics (severity distribution, age of backlog, SLA compliance).

### Transparency (binding)
- Publish annual aggregate accounts: rent/charges, operating costs, maintenance spend, capex spend, and reserve status.
- Publish governance decisions: major procurement contracts, change orders, and policy changes (privacy-safe).

### Contestability (binding)
- Ensure mechanisms for oversight and correction: independent audits, tenant/member representation, rotation of boards where relevant,
  and audit-of-the-auditor for key decisions.
- Prohibit governance structures that allow permanent insider control without review (closed boards, opaque appointment chains).

<a id="b05ch07p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH07.P08.S03 — Financing: C-flows, ΔM allowed channel (commons capex), ring-fenced maintenance

Financing of housing commons must be disciplined to prevent drift into asset support or leverage-driven extraction.

### Admissible financing channels (binding)
- **C-flows**: rents/charges set to cover bounded operating costs and maintenance-first obligations, not to maximise surplus.
- **ΔM / public credit (allowed)**: admissible only for **commons capex** (new units, renovations, infrastructure upgrades) and only
  under tagging, leakage tests, and auditable use-of-funds conditions.
- **Ring-fenced maintenance**: financing structures must include protected maintenance reserves; capex financing may not cannibalise
  maintenance.

### Prohibited channels (binding)
- Asset-price support mechanisms (buying up private stock to sustain prices without ΔG), speculative leverage, and financing that
  depends on rent escalation beyond bounded rails.
- Financing structures that hide effective dividends (affiliate service contracts, management markups, related-party lending).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish an aggregated financing table: funding sources, uses (capex vs maintenance), leverage ratios where applicable, and leakage
  test outcomes.

<a id="b05ch07p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH07.P08.S04 — Anti-commons-washing: criteria for true non-speculation and no self-benefit loops

Because “commons” labels are easily abused, OBA defines anti-washing criteria as binding.

### Commons-washing indicators (binding)
- **Speculative upside**: members/insiders can capture market appreciation or exit with windfall payouts.
- **Hidden dividends**: surplus is extracted through affiliate contracts, management companies, consulting fees, or related-party
  finance.
- **Opaque allocation**: discretionary access rules, insider preference, or exclusion without reason codes and appeals.
- **Maintenance neglect**: backlog grows while budgets are diverted to expansion, branding, or insider benefits.

### Conformance criteria (binding)
- **Non-speculation**: resale/exit rules cap upside and prevent flipping; appreciation capture is bounded and rules-based.
- **No self-benefit loops**: procurement and service contracting must be arm’s length, transparent, and audited; related-party
  transactions are disclosed and tightly constrained.
- **Access-first**: allocation and pricing serve baseline access and L reduction, not wealth-building for a subset.
- **Maintenance-first**: backlog thresholds trigger automatic escalation; persistent neglect triggers governance intervention.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven commons-washing triggers status downgrade: loss of “commons” designation, suspension of preferential financing channels, and
  mandatory governance reset (fail-closed against label capture).
