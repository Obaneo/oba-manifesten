# B05.CH07.P12 — Measurement and publication requirements (lifeworld test for housing)

This paragraph specifies the measurement and publication requirements for housing under OBA. Housing is the largest essentials domain
(L); if the system cannot measure and publish housing outcomes, clamp can expand invisibly through wait times, lock-in, fee-stacks,
maintenance neglect, and covert asset-support channels. OBA therefore requires a **public measurement layer** that makes housing costs,
scarcity, enforcement, commons performance, and contract clamp contestable as time series—while enforcing privacy minimalism so the
publication layer cannot be repurposed as a commercial profiling asset. Publication is treated as conformance infrastructure: if core
indicators cannot be published safely and reproducibly, scope must be reduced to auditable coverage (fail-closed against invisible L).

---

<a id="b05ch07p12s01"></a>
## B05.CH07.P12.S01 — Core statistics: housing cost burden, rent index, vacancy, wait times, eviction/foreclosure rates

OBA requires baseline indicators that reveal affordability, scarcity, and enforcement dynamics, including tails.

### Core statistics (binding)
- **Housing cost burden** distributions (rent+service charges or mortgage+mandatory costs as share of income), including tails
  (p90/p95) by region and household cohort.
- **Rent index** and rent dispersion: median and tail rents by unit class and region; track synchrony and above-index adjustments.
- **Vacancy rates** and vacancy duration: distinguish frictional vacancy from strategic withholding signatures where feasible.
- **Wait times** for baseline access pathways: distributions (median/p90/p95) by allocation category and region.
- **Eviction rates**: filings, orders, and executed evictions with cycle-time distributions.
- **Foreclosure rates**: arrears-to-foreclosure transitions, initiated vs completed foreclosures, and cycle-time distributions.

### Definition stability (binding)
- Event definitions (eviction, foreclosure, vacancy) must be standardised and stable across time to prevent reclassification games.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish these metrics as time series with stable taxonomy (region, unit class, provider segment) and explicit tail reporting.

<a id="b05ch07p12s02"></a>
## B05.CH07.P12.S02 — Commons metrics: units, access, maintenance backlog, incident rates, cost per unit

Housing commons must be measurable as infrastructure: output, access, quality, and maintenance discipline.

### Commons metrics (binding)
- **Units and capacity**: total commons units by type (public, co-op, CLT, non-profit), additions, retirements, and occupancy.
- **Access performance**: allocations per period, wait times, denial rates with reason-code distributions, and appeal outcomes (aggregate).
- **Maintenance backlog**: size, severity distribution, age of backlog, SLA compliance, and repeat-fault rates.
- **Incident rates**: damp/mould incidents, heating failures, safety defects, complaint volumes, and resolution-time distributions.
- **Cost per unit**: operating cost per occupied unit, maintenance spend per unit, and capex spend per delivered unit (standardised
  categories to prevent hidden transfer pricing).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Commons providers must publish annual aggregate accounts and performance tables sufficient to detect neglect, insider capture, or
  commons-washing.

<a id="b05ch07p12s03"></a>
## B05.CH07.P12.S03 — Contract clamp: portability/exit metrics, penalty incidence, dispute volumes/outcomes

Clamp often manifests through mobility restrictions and enforcement asymmetry rather than through headline rent levels.

### Clamp metrics (binding)
- **Portability/exit metrics**: share of moves incurring contract exit fees, loss of waitlist time, loss of deposits, or denial due to
  administrative resets; moving friction distributions.
- **Penalty incidence**: frequency and magnitude distributions of break fees, deposit deductions, restoration charges, and disputed
  service-charge add-ons.
- **Dispute volumes and outcomes**: disputes by category (rent increases, service charges, deposits, repairs), resolution times, outcome
  distribution (tenant wins/partial/landlord wins), and repeat-offender indicators by provider segment.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish as time series with tail emphasis (p90/p95), and include indicator linkages to escalation triggers (see P10).

<a id="b05ch07p12s04"></a>
## B05.CH07.P12.S04 — Privacy minimalism: public aggregates, suppression, no individual addresses in public outputs

The housing measurement layer must not become a vulnerability map or a commercial profiling asset.

### Privacy minima (binding)
- **Aggregates only**: publish distributions and aggregated tables; no individual-level outputs and no persistent pseudonymous IDs in
  public releases.
- **Suppression and bucketing**: apply small-cell suppression thresholds; bucket ranges (income, rent, wait times) and top-code tails
  where needed to prevent re-identification.
- **No addresses**: individual addresses, unit identifiers, and precise geolocation are prohibited in public outputs.

### Purpose limitation (binding)
- Published housing datasets may be used for order governance, audit, and public contestation only. Commercial repackaging for tenant
  screening, lead generation, or rent optimisation is prohibited.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Evidence of re-identification, address leakage, or commercial re-profiling triggers immediate suspension of affected publications
  and mandatory redesign of the data pipeline (fail-closed against surveillance rent).
