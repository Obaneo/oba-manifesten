# B05.CH02.P03 — Allocation of labour: where people move as E contracts

This paragraph specifies labour allocation dynamics under OBA when **E (extraction)** is structurally constrained and when **caps**
and **commons expansion** shift demand toward production, maintenance, and essential service capacity. The core claim is not that
“jobs are created and destroyed” in the abstract, but that the system intentionally **shrinks whole classes of activity** that exist
to defend toll positions, sustain lock-in, and manufacture claim stacks—while expanding labour needs where society must actually
produce, maintain, and govern essential capacity.

Because labour reallocation is frictionful, OBA treats transition as a **designed pathway** rather than a moral lecture: matching,
regional constraints, skill conversion, timing gaps, and household risk all need explicit rails. The **ΔM channel** (instrument and
credit architecture) is the mechanism to finance reskilling, mobility, and commons capex without recreating hidden claim-support
regimes or vendor lock-in.

---

## B05.CH02.P03.S01 — E contraction: which functions disappear (tolls, lock-in, rent-seeking, fee-stacks, bullshit work)

As E contracts, labour demand declines in roles whose function is to **extract** rather than to produce or maintain.

### Primary contraction zones (non-exhaustive)
- **Toll administration and gatekeeping**: access-brokering, priority-selling, paywall engineering, and “permission layers” whose
  purpose is to monetise scarcity rather than resolve it.
- **Lock-in engineering**: switching-cost design, portability obstruction, exclusivity contracting, loyalty-penalty systems, and
  subscription traps.
- **Rent-seeking compliance theatre**: paperwork regimes, “audit-looking” functions without audit power, and reporting designed to
  shield carve-outs rather than to reveal clamps.
- **Fee-stack optimisation**: intermediation chains that slice value via take-rates, service fees, convenience fees, and layered
  financing margins without adding output.
- **Claim-defence apparatus**: legal/financial engineering whose primary output is protecting claim positions against contestability.
- **“Bullshit work” as symptom**: roles that exist mainly to coordinate around organisational dysfunction or narrative posture,
  rather than to improve service minima, maintenance, or production.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- When an implementation claims “E contraction,” it MUST identify which labour functions are expected to shrink and how clamp
  mechanics (fee-stacks, lock-in proxies) are being neutralised rather than merely relabeled.

## B05.CH02.P03.S02 — P/C expansion: where labour is needed (essentials, infrastructure, maintenance, care, education, digital rails)

As extraction roles shrink, labour demand rises in domains that deliver **P (production)** and sustain **C (commons/collective
capacity)**.

### Primary expansion zones (non-exhaustive)
- **Essentials provisioning**: housing delivery and retrofit capacity, energy reliability, food logistics, basic financial rails,
  and access governance under explicit rules (no ad-hoc gatekeeping).
- **Infrastructure build + renewal**: transport, grids, water, digital backbone—especially **maintenance-heavy** systems where ΔG
  discipline determines lived reality.
- **Maintenance as first-class work (ΔG)**: repair, renewal, quality assurance, incident response, lifecycle engineering, and
  backlog elimination across essential systems.
- **Care and health capacity**: care work expands when coercive household clamp dynamics are reduced and when service minima are
  treated as constitutive rather than “budget residual.”
- **Education and reskilling**: vocational conversion pipelines, apprenticeship capacity, and adult learning infrastructure.
- **Digital rails (non-extractive)**: interoperability, portability, identity and access systems, open standards, audit tooling,
  and public-interest platforms that replace private gatekeeping layers.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Expansion claims MUST be backed by staffing and capacity plans tied to service minima and maintenance backlogs, not headline
  budgets or PR outputs.

## B05.CH02.P03.S03 — Transition frictions: matching, regional mismatches, skill gaps, timing

Labour does not reallocate automatically. OBA treats frictions as predictable failure points that can cause backlash, washing, or
selective execution unless designed against.

### Friction categories
- **Matching**: job-content mismatch (skills and work identity), credential barriers, and hiring risk under uncertainty.
- **Regional mismatch**: expansion demand arises where people are not (and where housing access may itself be clamped).
- **Skill gaps and conversion time**: essential trades, maintenance specialisms, care roles, and infrastructure work require
  training horizons and supervised practice.
- **Timing gaps**: E roles can collapse quickly; P/C capacity often expands slowly due to procurement, build time, and institutional
  readiness.
- **Household risk constraint**: people cannot “transition” if income volatility and essentials burdens spike during retraining.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Transition design MUST include explicit timing and risk rails (income smoothing, training stipends, predictable scheduling) and
  MUST publish the conversion pathway logic (who moves where, under what conditions, with what evidence of feasibility).
- If transition frictions are ignored, the implementation MUST be treated as incomplete and likely to induce clamp substitution
  (new gatekeeping roles replacing old ones).

## B05.CH02.P03.S04 — ΔM channel role: public credit channels for reskilling, mobility, and commons capex

The ΔM channel is the instrument layer that makes transition feasible without recreating extraction through hidden claim support.

### ΔM functions for labour reallocation
- **Reskilling finance**: predictable funding for training providers and for households in conversion (stipends, childcare support,
  tools/equipment).
- **Mobility and housing access**: relocation support and access mechanisms that prevent regional mismatch from becoming a permanent
  exclusion (without subsidising private rent extraction).
- **Commons capex**: upfront investment to expand essential capacity and maintenance readiness, with procurement rails that avoid
  vendor lock-in and black-box dependence.
- **Timing smoothing**: bridging instruments to prevent demand collapse from translating into household clamp and political sabotage.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- ΔM instruments used for labour transition MUST be traceable by channel_id and instrument_id, with explicit conditions and audit
  logs; prohibited asset/claim support flags MUST be enforced (no disguised rent underwriting).
- Any reskilling/mobility programme that cannot demonstrate measurable effects on essentials burdens (L) and household residual
  space (R/B) MUST be treated as incomplete pending lifeworld validation.
