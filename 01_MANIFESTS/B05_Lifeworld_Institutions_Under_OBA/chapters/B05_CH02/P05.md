# B05.CH02.P05 — Labour power and contract clamp (control/block in work relations)

This paragraph specifies labour power under OBA as a **contract-clamp problem**: who controls time, dependency, mobility, and
contestability in work relations. In OBA terms, labour relations are a primary site where **control** and **block power** can
re-create extraction even when macro caps exist—through non-competes, exclusivity, schedule domination, credential gating, and
benefits that lock people into employers or sectors.

The objective is to make clamp mechanics **detectable**, constrain them with **rails**, and provide **exit and contestability**
infrastructure (portability, switching-cost caps, fast disputes, and audit triggers). The focus is functional: preventing labour
markets from becoming monopsonistic toll systems that quietly increase L, compress R, and defeat agency while appearing “compliant”
in headline wage statistics.

---

## B05.CH02.P05.S01 — Clamp indicators: non-competes, exclusivity, schedule control, abuse of dependency

Labour clamp exists when a worker’s ability to exit, renegotiate, or refuse is structurally impaired. The following indicators are
treated as clamp signals because they create **dependency** and **block** rather than mutually contestable exchange.

### Clamp indicators (non-exhaustive)
- **Non-competes / non-solicits**: restrictions that limit exit options beyond narrowly justified protection of trade secrets.
- **Exclusivity and moonlighting bans**: employer-imposed limits on secondary income, portfolio work, or multi-client models.
- **Schedule control and on-call domination**: unpredictable shifts, punitive scheduling, “availability capture,” and short-notice
  changes that prevent planning and reduce outside options.
- **Dependency exploitation**: tying immigration, housing, health access, or essential benefits to continued employment without
  portable alternatives.
- **Credential and licensing choke points**: artificial gating where incumbents can block entry or mobility without output-based
  justification.
- **Retaliation surfaces**: opaque performance scoring, blacklists, reference coercion, or algorithmic de-ranking used to discipline
  voice and exit.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Any system claiming OBA-compatible work relations MUST publish a clamp register: which of the above mechanisms exist, why, and
  which measurable safeguards and appeal rails neutralise them.

## B05.CH02.P05.S02 — Wage-setting rails: transparency, bounded dispersion, no monopsony toll

Wage formation is treated as a potential **monopsony toll**: if exit is constrained, wages can be depressed below the worker’s
contribution, shifting value into claim layers. OBA requires wage-setting to be **transparent**, **contestable**, and bounded by
dispersion rails consistent with caps logic.

### Wage-setting rails (binding)
- **Transparency**: publish pay bands, job ladders, promotion criteria, and total-compensation decomposition (cash, benefits, risk,
  time requirements).
- **Bounded dispersion**: within-firm and within-sector wage dispersion MUST remain within published, auditable bounds; outliers
  require justification tied to measurable scarcity/skill contributions rather than positional power.
- **No monopsony toll**: when market structure or clamp indicators imply monopsony power, wage-setting MUST be corrected via
  countervailing mechanisms (collective bargaining recognition, sectoral floors, portability, anti-exclusivity rules).
- **Anti-discrimination enforceability**: pay equality claims must be testable with published cohort tables (join-safe), not “trust
  us” narratives.

### Audit trigger
- Persistent wage depression alongside high clamp indicators (non-competes, scheduling coercion, benefits lock-in) MUST trigger a
  labour-power audit of the employer or sector.

## B05.CH02.P05.S03 — Mobility/exit: portability of pensions/security, switching-cost caps, cross-sector mobility

Mobility is the core anti-clamp mechanism. If exit is expensive or risky, labour markets become captive. OBA therefore treats
portability and switching-cost caps as constitutive infrastructure.

### Mobility rails (binding)
- **Portability of security**: pensions, healthcare entitlements, training credits, and unemployment protections MUST be portable and
  not function as employer-specific chains.
- **Switching-cost caps**: contractual or administrative exit costs (repayment clauses, training debt, visa traps, benefit cliffs)
  MUST be capped, time-limited, and contestable.
- **Credential portability**: recognition frameworks must allow cross-sector movement with bridging modules rather than full reset
  (avoid “re-licensing as clamp”).
- **Cross-sector mobility channels**: public/commons channels (ΔM where applicable) may fund reskilling and relocation, but must be
  tied to measurable output and avoid vendor capture.

### Fail-closed rule
- If workers cannot exit without disproportionate loss of security, the work relation MUST be treated as clamp-tainted and therefore
  non-conforming until portability is restored.

## B05.CH02.P05.S04 — Dispute/contestability: fast objection procedures and audits of large employers/sectors

Clamp persists when disputes are slow, risky, or non-existent. Contestability must therefore be operational, fast, and protected
against retaliation.

### Contestability infrastructure (binding)
- **Fast objection procedure**: workers must have access to rapid dispute channels for clamp indicators (non-competes, scheduling
  abuse, retaliation, pay-band violations), with timelines and escalation ladders.
- **Anti-retaliation enforceability**: retaliation claims must be actionable with evidentiary rails (logs, decision records,
  algorithmic ranking explanations where used).
- **Sector and large-employer audits**: employers above defined thresholds (size, market share, essentiality) MUST be subject to
  periodic labour-power audits focusing on clamp surfaces and wage-setting rails.
- **Remedy-first design**: dispute outcomes must prioritise clamp neutralisation (void clauses, portability fixes, backpay where
  relevant) over symbolic penalties.

### Hard stop
- If dispute systems exist only as HR theatre (no timelines, no independent review, no enforceable remedies), the labour-power layer
  MUST be treated as non-operational and the implementation as incomplete.
