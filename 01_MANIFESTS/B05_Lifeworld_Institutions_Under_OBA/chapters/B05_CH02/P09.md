# B05.CH02.P09 — Public and commons employers: governance and quality

This paragraph specifies the employer-side requirements for **public and commons work** under OBA. As E contracts, a larger share of
employment must occur in **commons provision and maintenance**, public execution, and audited service delivery. This shift only
produces L reduction and legitimacy if public/commons employers are governed as **anti-capture institutions** with measurable
quality, contestability, and non-extractive productivity discipline.

The target is not “bigger public sector,” but **order-robust service organisations**: maintenance-first, auditable, exit-safe in
procurement, and structurally prevented from becoming new clamp surfaces (bureaucratic tolls, opaque discretion, vendor veto
layers). Public and commons employers must therefore meet governance rails comparable to those required for any other order-critical
institution: transparency, contestability, separation of functions, and sanctions/exception discipline.

---

## B05.CH02.P09.S01 — Commons work as an institutional core: maintenance teams, infrastructure, care/education, digital rails

Commons work is first-class because it directly produces **P and C** and sustains **ΔG**: maintenance and service minima are not
“overhead,” they are the condition for non-extractive access. Public and commons employers are therefore the primary carriers of
the maintenance-first transition.

### Core work domains (non-exhaustive)
- **Maintenance teams (ΔG core)**: inspection, repair, backlog reduction, lifecycle planning, incident response.
- **Infrastructure operations**: energy grids, housing retrofit capacity, water/sanitation, transport reliability.
- **Care and education capacity**: duty-of-care roles with quality rails and staffing adequacy as a measurable constraint.
- **Digital rails**: identity/registry functions, public service platforms, interoperability, security operations, audit logs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons/public employers MUST treat maintenance capacity as a protected function (ring-fenced staffing, backlog metrics, escalation
  triggers on decay).
- Where scarcity exists (queues, rationing, service minima violations), employers MUST publish the scarcity rule set and appeals path.

---

## B05.CH02.P09.S02 — Governance rails: anti-capture, transparency, procurement without vendor lock-in

Public and commons employers are capture targets because they control budgets, standards, access gates, and procurement. Governance
must therefore be designed to prevent vendor veto layers and internal patronage from recreating extraction.

### Governance rails (binding)
- **Anti-capture**: conflict-of-interest regimes, rotation where appropriate, disclosure of decision-makers and supplier ties.
- **Transparency surface**: public definitions, service standards, budgets, backlog metrics, incident registers, exception registers.
- **Separation of functions**: execution vs oversight vs audit; no self-auditing; audit-of-auditors for key regimes.
- **Procurement exitability**: interoperability requirements, data portability, open interfaces, escrow/continuity plans, avoidance of
  single-vendor dependency in order-critical systems.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any vendor providing order-critical tooling MUST be replaceable without institutional collapse (exit plan + tested migration path).
- “Black-box operation” in delivery, allocation, or measurement pipelines MUST be treated as a governance failure mode requiring
  remediation or hard stop.

## B05.CH02.P09.S03 — Quality and professional ethics: service levels, duty of care, contestability

Quality in public/commons work is not a marketing claim; it is a constitutive condition for legitimacy. Because services often
concern essentials (housing, energy, care, digital access), failure produces direct L increases and clamp dynamics.

### Quality model (binding)
- **Service levels**: explicit minima, published indicators, incident thresholds, and backlog/queue discipline.
- **Duty of care**: professional standards that constrain discretionary harm (especially in care, education, and enforcement roles).
- **Contestability**: objections/appeals, complaint handling with timelines, and review mechanisms that can trigger audits and remedy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Users MUST have a usable contestation interface (not only “feedback”): claims can trigger review, corrections, and restatements.
- Where professional discretion exists, the permissible discretion set MUST be explicit and logged; exceptions require justification,
  publication within privacy rails, and sunset discipline.

---

## B05.CH02.P09.S04 — Productivity without extraction: output metrics tied to ΔG and user experience (without surveillance)

Public and commons employers require productivity discipline, but it must not reintroduce extraction or surveillance. Productivity
must therefore be measured as **output and maintenance** relative to resources, plus **experienced service outcomes**, under privacy
minimalism and anti-gaming checks.

### Productivity discipline (binding)
- **Tie to ΔG**: backlog reduction, uptime/reliability, lifecycle compliance, preventive maintenance ratios.
- **Tie to user experience**: accessibility, queue times, failure rates, resolution times, and contestability performance.
- **Anti-surveillance**: no person tracking as the default; avoid behavioural coercion via measurement duty.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Productivity metrics MUST be plural (no single KPI governance) and MUST include anti-gaming checks and uncertainty discipline.
- Any measurement regime that requires invasive tracking or coercive monitoring to “prove productivity” MUST be treated as drifting
  toward clamp dynamics and is non-conforming unless redesigned.
