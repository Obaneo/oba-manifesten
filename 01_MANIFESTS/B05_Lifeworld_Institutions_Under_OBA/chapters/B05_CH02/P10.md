# B05.CH02.P10 — Labour productivity and technology (incl. AI)

This paragraph specifies how technology (including AI) is treated in the work layer under OBA. Technology is not assumed to be
“good” or “bad”; it is classified by function: does it increase **P (production)** and **ΔG maintenance capacity**, or does it
strengthen **E (extraction)** by deepening surveillance, manipulation, lock-in, and gatekeeping? The work layer treats productivity
as order-relevant only insofar as it (a) expands real output/maintenance and (b) improves lifeworld constraints (lower L, higher R,
more stable R/B tails) without introducing new clamp dynamics.

The paragraph therefore binds technology adoption to:
- P/E/C classification of automation effects,
- anti-surveillance and anti-manipulation rails (linked to the cognitive/information order in Book 4),
- explicit handling of “released time” as a social allocation problem (not merely profit extraction),
- and robust measurement discipline that avoids Goodhart failures and KPI theatre.

---

## B05.CH02.P10.S01 — Automation: where it increases P versus where it strengthens E (classification)

Automation is classified by the function it expands and the power relations it produces.

### P-increasing automation (indicative)
- Reduces waste, increases throughput, improves quality, and expands maintenance capacity (ΔG) without raising clamp or exclusion.
- Replaces repetitive verification, scheduling, and logistics tasks *while preserving contestability* and leaving decision rights
  auditable and appealable.

### E-strengthening automation (indicative)
- Expands surveillance and behavioural control (productivity scoring, micro-management, algorithmic discipline).
- Deepens lock-in and gatekeeping (proprietary workflows, platform dependence, vendor-controlled control planes).
- Increases fee-stack extraction by enabling finer-grained tolling (dynamic pricing, discriminatory access, pay-to-unlock).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any automation deployment MUST include a functional classification: expected P gains, expected ΔG effects, and clamp risk assessment.
- If automation primarily creates new gatekeeping or surveillance rents, it MUST be treated as E-strengthening and requires redesign,
  restriction, or prohibition under the relevant rails.

## B05.CH02.P10.S02 — AI in work: rails against surveillance, manipulation, and clamp dynamics (link to Book 4)

AI systems in work settings are high-risk clamp amplifiers: they can centralise control, create opaque ranking, and discipline workers
through non-contestable outputs. OBA therefore requires strict rails aligned with Book 4.

### AI rails (binding)
- **No surveillance rent**: AI must not require pervasive monitoring as the default input stream.
- **Contestability**: any AI-mediated decision affecting pay, scheduling, promotion, discipline, or access MUST be explainable,
  appealable, and logged; black-box decisions are non-conform.
- **Manipulation ban**: systems that nudge, pressure, or behaviourally condition workers through opaque feedback loops are treated as
  clamp mechanisms.
- **Role separation**: tool operators cannot be the sole auditors; independent review must exist for high-impact systems.
- **Vendor exitability**: procurement must avoid lock-in; models/workflows must be replaceable without institutional collapse.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- High-impact AI uses MUST have an audit pack (inputs, outputs, error modes, drift detection, incident logs, appeals process).
- If contestability and auditability cannot be guaranteed, deployment MUST fail-closed: the AI cannot be used for consequential
  decisions.

## B05.CH02.P10.S03 — Released time: reallocation to care/commons/art (link to CH03/CH13)

Productivity gains release time. In an extraction order, released time becomes unemployment risk or profit capture. In OBA, released
time is treated as an allocation problem: how to convert it into **lower L**, higher **agency**, and expanded **commons capacity**.

### Reallocation channels (indicative)
- **Care and social reproduction** (CH03): more time sovereignty, reduced schedule capture, expanded care participation without clamp.
- **Commons maintenance and participation** (ΔG): increased capacity for repair, community infrastructure, and service continuity.
- **Art/culture/status shift** (CH13): status competition reoriented toward contribution and craft rather than monetary escalation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any productivity programme MUST state how released time is handled: reduced hours, more predictable scheduling, reskilling time,
  commons staffing—rather than unstructured displacement.
- If automation increases precarity or schedule volatility, it fails the lifeworld test regardless of headline productivity gains.

## B05.CH02.P10.S04 — Measurement risk: Goodhart in productivity KPIs; mitigation via multi-metric + CSA

Productivity measurement is a prime Goodhart surface: once a KPI becomes a target, organisations optimise the metric and degrade
quality, dignity, or maintenance. Under OBA, productivity must be measured in a way that preserves agency and prevents metric capture.

### Measurement discipline (binding)
- **Multi-metric**: productivity is triangulated across output, quality, maintenance (ΔG), and experience/contestability indicators.
- **Anti-gaming checks**: publish sensitivity analyses and monitor for metric manipulation (e.g., speed up at the cost of error,
  suppress incidents, deny cases to improve throughput).
- **CSA linkage**: productivity claims must be publishable and contestable through CSA public tables and dispute flows; internal KPI
  dashboards are not sufficient evidence.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Single KPI regimes used for discipline or funding allocation MUST be treated as drift risks unless constrained by explicit rails,
  plural metrics, and contestability.
- If productivity measurement increases surveillance or coercion, it is non-conforming by definition and must be redesigned.
