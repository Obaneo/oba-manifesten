# B05.CH02.P07 — Security tied to work (and decoupling where necessary)

This paragraph specifies the security architecture around work under OBA: which rights may remain connected to employment and which
must be **portable** or **decoupled** to prevent contract clamp and dependency abuse. Security is treated as an **anti-clamp rail**:
if basic protections are employer-gated, then “work” becomes a coercive channel and labour relations drift toward control/block
dynamics even when wages appear fair.

The objective is twofold:
- preserve **agency** (exit without collapse) by making key rights follow the person,
- and preserve **order stability** by treating unemployment/transition rights as R/B stabilisers rather than as discipline devices.

This paragraph therefore binds work security to the lifeworld test: security design must measurably reduce lock-in (L), protect
residual space (R), and prevent tail-risk blowups (R/B tails), without creating new extraction channels.

---

## B05.CH02.P07.S01 — Portable security: insurance/rights follow the person, not the employer (anti-clamp)

When health insurance, pensions, training rights, housing access, or legal protection are employer-bound, switching jobs becomes a
high-friction event. This is contract clamp: exit costs become an implicit non-compete, and employers acquire hidden block power.

### Portability principles (binding)
- Core security components MUST be **person-linked** rather than employer-linked:
  - health coverage and essential protections,
  - pension accrual and vesting,
  - unemployment insurance rights and eligibility history,
  - training/reskilling entitlements,
  - basic legal assistance for labour disputes.
- Switching employers MUST not reset rights, restart waiting periods, or require discretionary “approval” transfers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any system that makes an employee forfeit accumulated rights upon exit (or makes transfer discretionary) MUST be treated as
  clamp-producing unless explicitly justified under a canonical exception regime with sunset and audit.
- Portability MUST be technically and administratively executable: published rules, standard transfer schemas, and dispute rails.

## B05.CH02.P07.S02 — Unemployment/transition: rights as stabilisers of R/B, not as discipline devices

Unemployment and transition rights exist to stabilise households against tail-risk and to prevent clamp cascades (debt spirals,
housing loss, health loss) that convert temporary job loss into long-term extraction. If benefits are designed primarily as “work
discipline,” they generate coercion and low-quality matches, and they incentivise compliance theatre rather than real transition.

### Stabiliser logic
- Benefits and transition support are an **R/B stabiliser**: they preserve residual space and prevent catastrophic downside.
- The system should optimise for **matching quality** (skills → productive roles) and **speed of reattachment** without coercion.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Eligibility and enforcement MUST be rule-based and appealable; discretionary sanctioning is a capture surface.
- Conditionality MUST be bounded: requirements may exist (e.g., participation in reskilling), but MUST not force acceptance of
  clamp-heavy contracts or unsafe work as a condition for survival.
- Any sanction regime MUST be demonstrably non-abusive: published sanction rates, cohort distributions, and audit triggers.

## B05.CH02.P07.S03 — Flex vs precarity: criteria for permissible flexibility (no hidden L)

“Flexibility” is permissible only when it does not conceal lock-in, unpaid burdens, or unilateral control over time and income.
Precarity is flexibility where L is externalised onto the worker.

### Criteria for permissible flexibility (binding)
Flex arrangements (temporary work, gig work, variable hours) may be compatible only if all of the following hold:
- **Income predictability rail**: minimum pay floors and predictable scheduling bands, or compensation for volatility.
- **Time autonomy rail**: worker retains meaningful control; on-call regimes must be compensated and capped.
- **Cost externalisation rail**: required tools, uniforms, vehicle costs, and compliance costs are not pushed onto workers as hidden L.
- **Exit rail**: no punitive rating locks, deplatforming without due process, or retaliation for switching clients/employers.
- **Classification integrity**: “independent contractor” labels cannot be used to remove protections when control/block is present
  (function over label).

### Audit triggers
- High churn, high volatility, widespread unpaid waiting time, or concentrated platform/agency power MUST trigger a clamp audit.

## B05.CH02.P07.S04 — Shock resilience: crisis protocols that preserve work continuity without reintroducing E

Crises (pandemics, energy shocks, financial shocks, supply shocks) create pressure to suspend rails, centralise discretion, and
reintroduce extraction channels “temporarily.” Shock resilience must preserve continuity without opening permanent carve-outs.

### Crisis protocol requirements (binding)
- **Continuity instruments**: wage support, reduced-hours schemes, rapid reskilling, and temporary redeployment MAY be used if they
  are rule-bound, time-limited, and auditable.
- **Sunset discipline**: any emergency measure MUST have an explicit end date, rollback path, and post-audit requirement.
- **Anti-extraction constraint**: crisis support MUST not become claim support for asset/claim holders via hidden channels
  (ΔM discipline); support must target continuity of production/maintenance and household R/B stability.
- **Selective enforcement ban**: crisis measures must publish distributional impacts; uneven protection across regions/classes is a
  legitimacy breach and must trigger escalation.

### Fail-closed
- If crisis protocols cannot be executed with transparency, auditability, and sunset/rollback discipline, the system MUST treat
  the measures as drift-risk and MUST downgrade compatibility claims pending remediation.
