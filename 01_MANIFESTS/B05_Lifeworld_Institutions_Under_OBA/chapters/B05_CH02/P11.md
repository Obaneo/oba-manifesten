# B05.CH02.P11 — Sectoral labour planning without central planning (hybrid allocation)

This paragraph specifies a **hybrid allocation** approach: sectoral labour planning that increases P/C capacity where needed without
turning into central planning or discretionary dealmaking. The design problem is structural: essentials and maintenance-first sectors
require predictable staffing and training pipelines, yet labour allocation cannot be reduced to a single planner’s optimisation
without creating capture surfaces and legitimacy collapse.

OBA therefore uses **sector canvases** and **lifeworld hotspots** (L burdens, maintenance backlogs, clamp signals) to infer labour
demand, and then uses **rule-based incentives** and **public options** to move people through the system. The approach is explicitly
anti-discretionary: decisions must be template-driven, auditable, and contestable; otherwise planning becomes a bargaining arena for
incumbents and vendors.

---

## B05.CH02.P11.S01 — Sector canvas → labour demand: bottlenecks, maintenance backlogs, L hotspots

Labour demand is derived from the sector canvas and from lifeworld observables, not from narrative urgency or lobbying pressure.

### Labour demand derivation (binding inputs)
- **Bottlenecks**: capacity constraints (skills, permits, equipment, facilities, logistics, supply chains) that limit output or
  service minima.
- **Maintenance backlogs (ΔG)**: backlog size, age, and incident correlation; backlog thresholds trigger demand.
- **L hotspots**: essentials burden spikes, lock-in/exit-friction proxies, and tail-risk signals indicating clamp harm.
- **Service minima**: defined required levels (uptime, wait times, quality standards) that imply staffing floors.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Demand claims MUST reference measurable inputs (backlog metrics, service minima breaches, L hotspot indicators) and must be tied to
  a published method_id; “we need more workers” without evidence is non-conform.
- Demand must be expressed as **roles/skill categories** with conversion pathways (links to CH02.P08 taxonomy), not only headcounts.

## B05.CH02.P11.S02 — Incentives: wage bands, training credit, mobility support, public options

Allocation is achieved through a controlled set of incentives and options that preserve freedom of choice while shifting the payoff
structure toward essentials and maintenance needs.

### Incentive toolkit (non-exhaustive; binding where used)
- **Wage bands**: sectoral bands and scarcity premiums within caps-compatible dispersion rails; no hidden perks as cap bypass.
- **Training credit**: person-linked credits aligned to shortage roles; portable across providers with published completion/placement
  outcomes.
- **Mobility support**: relocation/housing access support where regional mismatch blocks supply, with anti-extraction rails (no rent
  underwriting).
- **Public options (employment)**: commons/public employers act as stabilisers and pipelines for maintenance and essentials staffing,
  absorbing demand where private markets underprovide due to low margins or high training lead times.
- **Learn-and-work tracks**: paid apprenticeships and supervised practice to reduce training friction and household tail-risk.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Incentives must be rule-bound and published; discretionary “packages” are a capture surface.
- Any incentive must be evaluated in lifeworld terms (L/R/B effects): if it increases volatility or clamp, redesign is mandatory.

## B05.CH02.P11.S03 — Local variation: regions may adjust parameters within invariants

Sector needs and constraints vary by region (infrastructure, climate, demography, housing markets). OBA permits local parameter
variation, but only within invariant rails to prevent fragmentation, postcode lotteries, and selective enforcement.

### Parameter space (examples; permitted)
- Regional wage band adjustments within national dispersion constraints.
- Local prioritisation among shortage roles based on local backlogs and L hotspots.
- Local training provider mixes and delivery formats, provided method discipline and comparability are maintained.

### Invariants (non-negotiable)
- Canonical definitions and classification tests (P/E/C, caps logic, clamp detection).
- Publication and contestability requirements (public tables, method_id, dispute flow).
- Anti-capture and anti-discretion rules (no bespoke deals; exception discipline).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Local deviations MUST be logged as parameter changes with versioning and restatements; silent divergence is non-conform.
- Local outcomes MUST be comparable via shared schemas and cohorts (join-safe), enabling detection of selective execution.

---

## B05.CH02.P11.S04 — Fail-closed: no discretionary deals; everything via templates/auditpacks

The central anti-capture rule is fail-closed against discretionary allocation. Labour planning must not become a negotiation market
where powerful actors secure carve-outs, subsidies, or bespoke staffing pipelines.

### Fail-closed rules (binding)
- **No bespoke deals**: no individual employer/sector agreements that bypass templates, published criteria, and auditpacks.
- **Template discipline**: programmes must use standard templates for eligibility, incentive levels, provider selection, and outcomes.
- **Auditpacks**: every programme must have an audit pack specifying: inputs, decision rules, methods, output tables, dispute rails,
  thresholds for escalation, and sunset/rollback paths.
- **Hard stop**: if templates and auditpacks cannot be applied (capacity loss, legal obstruction, vendor lock-in), the programme
  must be scaled back to auditable scope or suspended.

### Conformance implication
- Any labour planning regime that depends on discretion, secrecy, or non-reproducible “expert judgement” MUST be treated as
  drift-prone and incompatible pending redesign.
