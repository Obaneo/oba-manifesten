# B05.CH02.P02 — Function of work in a caps/commons order

This paragraph specifies what “work” *does* in an OBA-compatible order once **caps** constrain status rent and **commons** carry
a larger share of essential provisioning. The function of work is no longer to justify unlimited claim stacks (“I earned it” as a
blank cheque), nor to operate as a coercive gate to survival. Instead, work is the primary interface that connects **productive
output (P)** and **maintenance (ΔG)** to **legitimate allocation**, while reducing the scope for extraction and clamp mechanics to
hide inside compensation structures, contracting chains, and labour market architecture.

In a caps/commons order, labour institutions must (1) privilege **P-linked legitimacy** over claim-linked entitlement, (2) treat
commons maintenance as first-class work (measured and staffed as ΔG, not squeezed as overhead), (3) narrow status competition so
incentives migrate toward skill, contribution, and stewardship rather than positional arms races, and (4) explicitly recognise that
time, care, and non-market contribution remain real social production—work is not the sole channel of legitimacy.

---

## B05.CH02.P02.S01 — Production (P) as primary legitimacy: tie work to output/maintenance, not to claim stacks

OBA treats **production (P)** as the primary legitimacy base for compensation and recognition. The work layer must therefore prevent
compensation regimes from drifting into **claim stacks** (rights-to-income mechanisms detached from output/maintenance) that recreate
extraction and undermine caps.

### Mechanism
- **P-linked compensation**: pay and recognition are anchored to measurable output, service quality, and maintenance obligations.
- **Anti-claim discipline**: compensation must not be predominantly generated by ownership-position claims, toll gates, or fee-stack
  engineering that persists regardless of productive contribution.
- **Contract architecture matters**: subcontracting chains, platform take-rates, and “internal markets” can convert P into E by
  inserting toll layers between work and outcome.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any compensation stack MUST be decomposable into P-linked components versus claim/toll components; dominant claim/toll components
  MUST trigger requalification or remedy.
- Any regime that relies on opaque intermediated fee-stacks between work performed and compensation received MUST be treated as a
  clamp risk and audited as such.

---

## B05.CH02.P02.S02 — Commons maintenance as a work category: maintenance is first-class (ΔG), not “overhead”

In an OBA order, commons are not slogans; they are **maintained systems**. Maintenance is therefore a first-class work category with
its own legitimacy, staffing, career paths, and measurement discipline.

### Core principle
- **ΔG is work**: sustaining capacity (repair, upkeep, quality assurance, incident response, lifecycle renewal) is productive, not
  an administrative residue to be cut during budget stress.
- **Maintenance-first allocation**: where commons provision essentials, maintenance backlogs are order failures, not optional trade-offs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Commons institutions MUST publish ΔG-relevant staffing and backlog indicators (join-safe), and MUST ring-fence minimum maintenance
  capacity as constitutive for service minima.
- Labour policies that improve headline employment while degrading maintenance capacity (outsourcing rot, churn, deskilling) MUST be
  treated as drifting and non-conforming.

## B05.CH02.P02.S03 — Limits on status competition: caps shift incentives from money/status to skill/contribution

Caps alter the incentive landscape: they compress the payoff to positional money/status escalation and redirect competition toward
competence, contribution quality, stewardship, and innovation that improves commons and reduces burdens.

### Incentive shift (intended)
- **From rent-seeking to contribution**: less reward for engineering claim positions; more reward for skill, reliability, and
  measurable improvements to outcomes and maintenance.
- **From tournament to mastery**: career ladders should privilege mastery, mentoring, and institutional memory, not purely managerial
  escalation or “bonus extraction” roles.
- **Anti-circumvention focus**: when caps exist, actors will seek substitutes (titles, perks, equity-like shadow claims, vendor
  kickbacks). Work governance must treat these as circumvention surfaces.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Work institutions MUST define and audit cap-circumvention channels in compensation and procurement-linked career tracks.
- Status systems that reward opaque gatekeeping, exclusivity control, or “access brokerage” MUST be treated as extraction-enabling
  and redesigned or sanctioned.

---

## B05.CH02.P02.S04 — Relation to time/care: work is not the only social contribution (link to CH03)

A caps/commons order cannot treat paid labour as the sole source of legitimacy, because essential production includes **care,
education, household maintenance, community stewardship, and recovery time**. If these are excluded, the work system becomes a clamp
that forces people to trade autonomy and care obligations for survival, which will reintroduce coercion and legitimacy failure.

### Interface principle
- **Contribution pluralism**: paid work, care work, commons participation, and civic contributions must be legible and compatible,
  not mutually cannibalising.
- **Time as an order variable**: scheduling volatility, unpaid overtime, and coercive availability are lifeworld clamp mechanisms
  that directly impact L and R/B.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Work policy MUST explicitly interface with time/care rules (CH03): leave, scheduling discipline, care infrastructure, and
  protection against coercive availability.
- Any labour market design that structurally penalises care obligations (forcing clamp acceptance) MUST be treated as non-conforming
  unless mitigated with explicit rails and measurable effects.
