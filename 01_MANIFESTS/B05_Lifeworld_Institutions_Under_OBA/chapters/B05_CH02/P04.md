# B05.CH02.P04 — Bullshit jobs: definitions, detection, and phase-out logic

This paragraph defines “bullshit jobs” in OBA terms as a **functional category**: work whose primary output is the preservation,
operation, or camouflage of **E (extraction)**—i.e., the maintenance of claim layers, clamp mechanics, and deception surfaces that
allow toll positions to persist. The emphasis is not moralistic (“some people do useless work”), but structural: in an order that
shrinks E, these roles become **unstable by design** and must be handled explicitly to avoid backlash, washing, and substitution
into new gatekeeping layers.

The objective is therefore twofold: (1) make the category **detectable** using observable signals (fee-stacks, lock-in proxies,
dark patterns, compliance theatre, internal tolling), and (2) specify **phase-out logic** that removes the *need* for the work by
changing rules, interfaces, and incentive surfaces—rather than merely automating, optimising, or rebranding it. Because these roles
often anchor identity and status, the paragraph also treats the social consequences as first-class transition risks, with mitigation
rails referenced to later transition chapters.

---

## B05.CH02.P04.S01 — Functional definition: work primarily maintaining E (claim layers, “clamp” dynamics, deception)

A “bullshit job” is defined by function, not by sector or personal competence. The question is: **what does the work maintain?**

### Functional definition (binding)
Work is classified as “bullshit” (E-maintenance work) when its dominant function is one or more of:
- **Claim-layer maintenance**: constructing, defending, or expanding claim stacks that extract cashflows without increasing output,
  access, or maintenance.
- **Clamp operation**: sustaining lock-in, switching costs, exclusivity, bundling traps, or hidden veto positions that reduce
  contestability.
- **Deception surface**: producing narratives, metrics, or documentation primarily intended to obscure extraction, hide failures, or
  create the appearance of accountability without audit power.
- **Toll choreography**: intermediation whose value is mainly creating additional toll points (take-rate slicing, fee layering),
  not reducing transaction costs under open, contestable rails.

### Boundary condition (important)
- “Bullshit” is **not** “non-manual” or “office work.” Many high-skill roles are P/C-critical. The test is whether the role
  primarily **neutralises contestability** or **manufactures rent**.

### Conformance implication
- Any implementation claiming E contraction MUST specify which work classes are E-maintenance and which are P/C, using functional
  criteria rather than slogans.

## B05.CH02.P04.S02 — Detection signals: fee-stack growth, compliance theatre, dark patterns, internal pass-through charging

Because organisations will re-label work to protect budgets and status, detection must use **behavioural and accounting signals**.

### Detection signals (non-exhaustive)
- **Fee-stack growth**: rising count and complexity of fees, take-rates, “service charges,” convenience add-ons, and tiered access
  pricing—especially in essentials.
- **Compliance theatre**: increasing reporting volume with declining auditability; dashboards without public tables; “certifications”
  without reproducible methods; process steps whose output is paperwork rather than remedy.
- **Dark patterns and friction design**: deliberate increases in cancellation friction, portability obstruction, confusing defaults,
  punitive timing, or “customer retention” practices that are effectively clamp maintenance.
- **Internal pass-through charging**: proliferating internal transfer prices, chargebacks, ticketing rituals, and interdepartmental
  tolling that mimic market extraction inside an organisation.
- **Exception inflation**: growing exception registers and carve-outs that require bureaucratic handling to sustain, indicating
  semantic drift rather than genuine edge cases.

### Operational test (practical)
- If the measurable effect of a process is primarily **higher friction** and **higher fee-layer density** (without improved service
  minima or maintenance), the associated work is presumptively E-maintenance.

## B05.CH02.P04.S03 — Phase-out: redesign processes/rules so the work disappears rather than becoming “more efficient”

The phase-out goal is to eliminate the **reason** the job exists. If you “optimise” an E-maintenance role, you often just make
extraction cheaper and more resilient.

### Phase-out logic (binding)
- **Remove the toll interface**: unbundle, mandate portability/interoperability, and replace permissioned gatekeeping with rule-based
  access (commons or regulated rails).
- **Replace discretion with auditable rails**: where “work” exists to negotiate exceptions, redesign rules to reduce exception
  volume, publish criteria, and enforce sunset/rollback discipline.
- **Kill dark-pattern economics**: prohibit clamp tactics and require measurable exitability; if revenue depends on friction, force a
  model change rather than “better UX.”
- **Collapse claim layers**: simplify contract stacks, ban redundant intermediaries where they function as toll points, and require
  transparent cost/quality disclosure to restore contestability.
- **Reclassify and reroute labour**: convert organisational capacity toward maintenance backlogs, service delivery, audit readiness,
  and commons operation.

### Anti-substitution rule
- Phase-out MUST include a substitution check: eliminated E-maintenance work must not reappear as “compliance,” “customer success,”
  “risk management,” or “AI governance” without measurable P/C outputs and auditability.

## B05.CH02.P04.S04 — Social consequences: identity loss, status shock, resistance; mitigation in CH13/CH18

E-maintenance roles often carry status, identity, and a sense of being “inside the machine.” Removing them can create **status shock**
and organised resistance—independent of material welfare.

### Consequence model
- **Identity loss**: “my job matters” collapse, especially in roles framed as professional guardianship.
- **Status compression**: caps reduce money-status gradients; additional loss occurs when prestige roles are revealed as clamp work.
- **Political resistance**: threatened groups may sabotage reforms, reframe them as “anti-work,” or demand carve-outs.
- **Narrative backlash**: abolition of bullshit jobs can be depicted as disrespect or humiliation if not handled with dignity rails.

### Mitigation rail (reference)
- Transition chapters (CH13/CH18) MUST include: dignity-preserving pathways, reclassification into P/C roles, retraining with income
  smoothing, and public legitimacy framing grounded in measurable lifeworld outcomes (L/R/B improvements), not moral rhetoric.
