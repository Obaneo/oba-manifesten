# B05.CH02.P08 — Reskilling and transition pathways (personal migration through the order)

This paragraph specifies how people move through the order when E contracts and P/C expands: reskilling is treated as a **migration
pathway** with rails, instruments, and measurement—not as a motivational program. The purpose is to convert structural change into
**agency-preserving transition**: people must be able to exit shrinking E-functions without falling into clamp dynamics, and must
be able to enter expanding P/C-functions without being forced into low-quality matches.

Reskilling is therefore binding for OBA compatibility at the work layer:
- it must reduce lock-in burdens (L) by lowering switching costs and dependency,
- stabilise R/B tails during transition,
- and measurably increase productive/commons capacity (P and C, with ΔG maintenance discipline).

Narrative claims (“people were helped”) are insufficient. Transition pathways must be **auditable pipelines** with cohort-level
flows, wage paths, and R/B effects.

---

## B05.CH02.P08.S01 — Skill taxonomy: mapping E-functions to P/C-functions (transferable skills)

A reskilling regime requires a skill taxonomy that recognises that many E-roles contain transferable competence once the claim-stack
function is removed. The goal is to map **functions** (what the work does) rather than titles.

### Mapping logic (functional)
- Identify E-functions that primarily maintain clamp/claim layers (e.g., toll enforcement, fee-stack optimisation, compliance
  theatre, dark-pattern conversion), and extract the underlying competencies:
  - process design, coordination, verification, customer support, risk handling, logistics, software operations, negotiation,
    documentation, quality assurance.
- Map these competencies to P/C target functions where they create output/maintenance:
  - essentials delivery, infrastructure operations, maintenance planning (ΔG), care logistics, education support, audit execution,
    digital public rails, commons governance operations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Transition programs MUST publish the skill taxonomy: categories, mapping rules, and target pathways (not ad-hoc casework only).
- Mapping MUST be role-neutral and portable: individuals can present verified skill profiles without employer gatekeeping.

## B05.CH02.P08.S02 — Transition instruments: training credit, learn-and-work tracks, wage insurance, relocation support

Transition must be instrumented with repeatable tools that remove the main frictions: time-to-skill, income risk, mobility costs,
and employer risk aversion.

### Instrument set (non-exhaustive; binding where used)
- **Training credit**: person-linked entitlements usable across accredited providers; funding follows the learner, not institutions.
- **Learn-and-work tracks**: paid apprenticeships / dual pathways with certified competence outputs (not “internship exploitation”).
- **Wage insurance**: temporary top-ups when moving into socially needed roles with lower initial pay, to prevent clamp via income shock.
- **Relocation support**: support for regional mismatch (housing, transport, family logistics) with anti-fraud rails and sunset terms.
- **Credential conversion**: fast recognition for adjacent roles (bridge modules + exams), avoiding redundant schooling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Instruments MUST be portable, rule-based, and appealable (no discretionary patronage pipelines).
- Provider capture MUST be prevented: publish completion rates, placement rates, and wage trajectories by provider/cohort.

## B05.CH02.P08.S03 — Prioritisation: essentials and maintenance-first sectors first (ΔG and L reduction)

Prioritisation is not ideology; it is an order constraint: OBA compatibility requires that essentials and maintenance capacity expands
first because these directly reduce burdens (L) and sustain commons viability (ΔG).

### Priority rules (binding)
- First priority pathways MUST target:
  - essentials sectors (housing delivery/retrofit, energy operations, care capacity, food/logistics, basic digital rails),
  - infrastructure and maintenance (ΔG) roles (asset upkeep, inspection, repair, lifecycle planning),
  - commons operations (service delivery + governance execution + audit support where relevant).
- “High-status” reskilling that does not reduce L or increase ΔG MUST not crowd out essentials/maintenance capacity during scarcity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A transition plan MUST publish an explicit priority ordering and justify it via L reduction and ΔG maintenance requirements.
- If prioritisation is absent, the system drifts toward status allocation and provider capture (non-conforming risk).

## B05.CH02.P08.S04 — Measurability: outflow/inflow, wage paths, R/B effects per cohort (no storytelling)

Reskilling is valid only if it produces measurable migration: people exit E-shrinking roles and enter P/C-expanding roles with
stabilised life outcomes.

### Required measurements (binding; cohort-level)
- **Flows**: outflow from shrinking categories; inflow into target categories; time-to-placement; drop-out reasons.
- **Wage paths**: wage trajectory pre/post transition; dispersion; volatility; incidence of clamp contracts post-placement.
- **R/B effects**: residual space (R) and R/B tail-risk before/after; shocks during training; stabiliser usage.
- **L indicators**: essentials burden changes, mobility costs, switching cost proxies, dependency indicators.
- **Distribution**: breakdown by region, age, prior sector, household type (within join-safety constraints).

### Publication and audit rails (binding)
- Outputs MUST be reproducible: method_id, definition-set references, revision/restatement policy.
- If the above measurements cannot be produced reliably, transition claims MUST be treated as **non-evidenced** and therefore
  incomplete (fail-closed against “success stories”).
