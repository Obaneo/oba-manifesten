# B05.CH02.P12 — Migration, labour migration, and international tension (lifeworld impact)

This paragraph specifies labour migration under OBA as an **allocation mechanism** with explicit lifeworld constraints. Migration is
not treated as a moral debate but as a structural interface: if essentials and maintenance-first sectors face capacity shortages,
labour migration can reduce backlogs and lower L. However, without strict rails it becomes an extraction channel: exploitation,
race-to-the-bottom wage pressure, and “L shifting” onto migrants and low-capacity cohorts.

The paragraph therefore binds labour migration to: (1) measurable criteria tied to essentials backlogs and service minima, (2)
anti-exploitation and rights portability, (3) geopolitically aware scenarios and mitigations (Book 2), and (4) cross-border contract
and platform compliance via Codex interoperability so that evasion and intermediary fee-stacks do not become the dominant migration
industry.

---

## B05.CH02.P12.S01 — Labour migration as an allocation mechanism: criteria tied to essentials/backlogs

Under OBA, labour migration is permitted as a targeted capacity instrument where domestic supply cannot meet essential needs within
acceptable time and L constraints.

### Admissibility criteria (binding)
Labour migration programmes MUST be justified by measurable indicators such as:
- **Essentials backlogs**: maintenance backlog thresholds, staffing ratios, queue/wait-time breaches in care/education, energy and
  infrastructure incident patterns.
- **Service minima breaches**: inability to meet baseline access norms (CH01 lifeworld criteria), with published evidence.
- **Time-to-train constraints**: where domestic training lead time is structurally longer than acceptable harm horizons.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Programme scope MUST be role/skill specific (taxonomy-linked) and time-bounded where possible, with explicit review cadence.
- Allocation must be rule-based and published (criteria, quotas/targets if used, employer eligibility, audit triggers); discretionary
  “shortage claims” without evidence are non-conform.

---

## B05.CH02.P12.S02 — Anti-exploitation: minima, rights portability, no race-to-the-bottom via L shifting

Labour migration is a primary exploitation surface. OBA treats exploitation not only as a moral wrong, but as a system failure that
creates hidden L, clamps agency, and degrades labour power for all.

### Anti-exploitation rails (binding)
- **Minimum rails**: enforceable wage floors/bands, maximum hours, safe working conditions, and duty-of-care standards.
- **Rights portability**: security rights follow the person (health coverage, injury protection, pension accrual, dispute access),
  independent of employer or intermediary.
- **No employer-tied dependency traps**: residency/visa/work permits must not become coercive levers; exit and job switching must be
  feasible without catastrophic loss.
- **Ban migration-industry fee stacks**: recruitment fees, debt-bondage-like arrangements, forced “accommodation packages,” and
  payroll skimming are treated as E and prohibited/requalified.
- **Dispute and protection**: fast objection procedures, anti-retaliation protections, access to legal assistance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If migration increases the prevalence of clamp indicators (exclusivity, confiscation of documents, threat-based scheduling),
  the programme is non-conforming and must be suspended or redesigned.
- Migration must not be used to depress domestic wage-setting via a race-to-the-bottom; wage rails and dispersion bounds apply.

## B05.CH02.P12.S03 — Brain drain and geopolitics: scenarios and mitigation (link to Book 2)

Labour migration has cross-border consequences: source countries can experience capacity loss (“brain drain”), and migration flows can
become geopolitical bargaining chips or sources of retaliation.

### Scenario frame (binding references)
- Use Book 2 scenario logic: geopolitical tension, retaliation, treaty friction, sanctions, and cross-border bargaining over labour
  and essentials supply chains.
- Identify high-risk domains: healthcare staffing, engineering/critical infrastructure, digital security, education capacity.

### Mitigation directions (non-exhaustive)
- **Reciprocity and training offsets**: fund training capacity in source contexts or implement bilateral capacity agreements.
- **Ethical recruitment constraints**: prohibit targeting critical shortages in vulnerable systems unless compensated/offset.
- **Contingency planning**: diversify supply and training pipelines to avoid single-source dependency.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any labour migration programme in critical sectors MUST include a geopolitical risk note and mitigation plan consistent with Book 2
  (scenario-aware, not “assume cooperation”).

## B05.CH02.P12.S04 — Cross-border compliance: contracts, intermediaries, platforms (Codex interoperability)

Cross-border labour relationships are mediated by contracts, agencies, platforms, and financial rails. Without interoperability and
auditability, evasion becomes the default (misclassification, offshore payroll, fee-stack intermediaries, platform take-rate abuse).

### Compliance rails (binding)
- **Contract standardisation**: use canonical contract templates where possible; where not possible, require mapping into canonical
  fields (role, pay, hours, fees, termination, portability, dispute).
- **Intermediary control**: agencies and platforms are treated as capture surfaces; require disclosure of fee stacks, take-rates,
  and control/block mechanisms.
- **Payment rail traceability**: compensation flows must be traceable to prevent skimming and hidden deductions (within privacy rails).
- **Codex interoperability**: cross-border arrangements must be representable in Codex schemas and pass validators (including
  prohibited clause detection and fee-stack checks).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If cross-border labour cannot be audited due to opaque intermediaries or non-interoperable data, the arrangement MUST be treated
  as non-conforming (fail-closed) until brought into an auditable schema regime.
- Platforms or intermediaries that enforce clamp via deplatforming, rating coercion, or identity lockout without due process MUST be
  treated as block-power loci and subjected to structural remedies (unbundling, take-rate caps, public options where essential).
