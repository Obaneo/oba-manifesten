# B05.CH02.P13 — Measurement and publication requirements for work (lifeworld test)

This paragraph specifies the **measurement and publication surface** required to execute “work under OBA” as a lifeworld test. Work
policy is non-evidence without public, reproducible outputs that show whether labour reallocation, wage formation, portability, and
anti-clamp rails are actually functioning. The required outputs must support evaluation of:
- **allocation** (E→P/C migration and sector capacity),
- **power relations** (contract clamp and monopsony signals),
- **lifeworld impacts** (L, R, and R/B tails by cohort),
- and **commons viability** (ΔG staffing and maintenance outcomes).

Publication is bounded by privacy minimalism: no person-tracking systems. The objective is contestability and auditability under
aggregation and sampling, not surveillance.

---

## B05.CH02.P13.S01 — Core statistics: job transitions E→P/C, wage paths, contract-clamp indices, skills coverage

The work layer MUST publish core labour-market and transition statistics sufficient to test whether E contraction and P/C expansion
are producing real migration and improved contestability.

### Required core statistics (minimum; binding)

**1) Transition flows (E→P/C)**
- Outflow/inflow rates between functional categories (E-maintenance → P/C roles), with explicit taxonomy reference and version.
- Time-to-transition distributions (median + tails), plus drop-out reasons (aggregated taxonomy).

**2) Wage paths and compensation structure**
- Wage trajectories pre/post transition (bands/quantiles), volatility measures, and decomposition where relevant (cash vs variable vs
  in-kind baseline impacts).
- Pay dispersion indicators (within-firm and sector) with bounds and exception reporting.

**3) Contract-clamp indices**
- Prevalence of non-competes, exclusivity, scheduling volatility, unpaid on-call, portability barriers, retaliation indicators
  (complaints/appeals), platform deplatforming incidents where relevant.
- Switching-cost proxies (exit fees, benefit cliffs, training-debt clauses) by sector and cohort.

**4) Skills coverage**
- Coverage of training/reskilling pipelines relative to shortage roles: seats, completion rates, placement rates, and verified skill
  profiles produced (aggregated).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Statistics MUST be tied to a published method_id and taxonomy version; “job categories” without functional mapping are insufficient.
- Missing clamp metrics (no data on non-competes/scheduling/portability) is treated as test failure: power cannot be inferred from
  wages alone.

## B05.CH02.P13.S02 — Cohort profiles: R/B effects by cohort and region, tail-risk for precarious work

Work is a lifeworld variable: its primary failure modes occur in tails (precarity, volatility, clamp). Publication must therefore be
distributional and cohort-based, not median-only.

### Required cohort outputs (minimum; binding)
- Cohort profiles that link work conditions to lifeworld outcomes:
  - **R/B effects**: residual space and tail-risk proxies for working households, by region and cohort.
  - **Volatility**: income volatility, schedule volatility, and shock incidence (arrears, benefit cliffs, emergency borrowing proxies
    where published elsewhere).
  - **Precarious work tail-risk**: concentration of clamp indicators in specific cohorts/sectors; time-to-recovery after shocks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Cohort reporting MUST be sufficient to detect selective execution and postcode/class divergence (within join-safety constraints).
- If only averages are published, the work layer is non-evidenced and must be treated as incomplete.

## B05.CH02.P13.S03 — Commons workforce metrics: maintenance backlogs, service levels, staffing ratios

Because commons/public employers are core to the transition and to L reduction, the work layer MUST publish workforce metrics that
show whether essential systems can be maintained (ΔG positive).

### Required commons workforce metrics (minimum; binding)
- **Staffing ratios** by domain (care, education, infrastructure, digital rails, housing maintenance) against published minima.
- **Maintenance backlogs**: size/age distributions, backlog breach counts, and incident correlations (aggregated).
- **Service levels**: uptime/availability, wait times, response times, and resolution times, aligned to service minima definitions.
- **Churn and burnout proxies**: turnover rates, vacancy duration, overtime prevalence (aggregated) as early-warning for capacity loss.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If commons staffing/backlog metrics are not published, “commons expansion” is non-auditable and must be treated as claim-only.
- If backlogs grow structurally (ΔG negative) without transparent correction, escalation is mandatory.

## B05.CH02.P13.S04 — Privacy minimalism: no person-tracking systems; use aggregates and sampling

Work measurement is high-risk for surveillance drift. The lifeworld layer requires evidence without turning workers into monitored
objects.

### Privacy rails (binding)
- **No person tracking**: no individual-level longitudinal worker dossiers as the default measurement system.
- **Aggregation first**: publish cohort-level tables and sector aggregates; use suppression for small-n.
- **Sampling**: use surveys and audits with published sampling frames and uncertainty bands to estimate clamp prevalence and
  experience indicators without continuous tracking.
- **Join safety**: prevent linkage across published tables that re-identifies individuals or micro-groups.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If the only way to produce metrics is invasive tracking, the measurement design is non-conforming and must be redesigned to a
  minimal, auditable scope.
- Privacy compliance is not optional: failure to maintain privacy rails triggers fail-closed (no compatibility claim).
