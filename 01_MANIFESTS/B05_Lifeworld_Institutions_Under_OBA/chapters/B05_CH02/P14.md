# B05.CH02.P14 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies failure modes of the “work under OBA” layer and defines hard-stop rules. Work reforms operate directly on
identity, status, and daily survival interfaces; they therefore generate predictable backlash incentives and drift pathways even when
macro logic is sound. Failure modes here are treated as **systemic**: they recur because actors adapt, institutions protect
themselves, and affected groups respond to clamp and uncertainty.

The hard-stop principle is lifeworld-based: if work policy increases **lock-in** or worsens **R/B tails** (volatility, downside
events, precarity), it must be redesigned regardless of intent. OBA does not permit “temporary harm” justified by promises; temporary
harm is allowed only under explicit shock regimes with buffers, sunsets, and measurable correction paths.

---

## B05.CH02.P14.S01 — “Bullshit job” moral panic: scapegoating instead of structure; mitigate via functional classification

A primary failure mode is moral panic: “bullshit jobs” becomes a cultural weapon used to shame people or target professions, rather
than a functional diagnosis of E-maintenance work. This converts structural reform into status warfare and undermines legitimacy.

### Failure mechanism
- **Scapegoating**: targeting visible groups (often administrative or service roles) without proving E function.
- **Identity humiliation**: people experience reform as contempt, not as structural redesign; resistance hardens.
- **Functional blindness**: true E-maintenance roles are protected by re-labeling while symbolic targets are attacked.

### Mitigation (binding)
- Use **functional classification** (P/E/C and clamp tests), not moral labels. The question is what the work maintains (output/
  maintenance vs claim/clamp/deception).
- Publish classification criteria and audit packs; avoid “list of bad jobs” rhetoric.
- Where roles are phased out, pair with dignity-preserving transition rails (reskilling pathways, income smoothing, status-neutral
  messaging anchored in lifeworld outcomes).

## B05.CH02.P14.S02 — Transition-induced precarity: temporary shocks without buffers → sabotage risk (link to CH18)

Transition is frictionful. If E contraction outpaces P/C absorption, households experience volatility spikes, benefit cliffs, and
essentials clamp (L rises). This produces predictable sabotage incentives: non-compliance, strike waves, political backlash, and
selective execution.

### Failure mechanism
- **Timing gap**: job loss occurs faster than training/placement; households hit tail-risk.
- **Buffer absence**: insufficient unemployment/transition stabilisers, childcare/time support, debt restructuring, or relocation
  assistance.
- **Clamp substitution**: desperate households accept worse contracts, creating new clamp equilibria.

### Mitigation (binding)
- Transition must be buffered (CH02.P07 + CH02.P08): portable security, wage insurance, training stipends, and predictable schedules.
- Shock protocols must be explicit: if volatility spikes, scale-down/slow-down rules activate and priority shifts to stabilisation.
- Link to CH18: transition-induced precarity is a sabotage precursor and must be treated as an early-warning trigger.

## B05.CH02.P14.S03 — Bureaucratic labour planning: centralisation without contestability → capture risk

A second systemic failure mode is planning drift: labour allocation becomes a centralised bureaucratic machine with discretion, opaque
criteria, and vendor dependence. This recreates gatekeeping and provides new capture surfaces.

### Failure mechanism
- **Discretion creep**: exceptions proliferate; selection becomes patronage or lobbying.
- **Opaque optimisation**: “expert models” without audit packs decide priorities; public contestability collapses.
- **Vendor lock-in**: planning systems become proprietary; tooling becomes a veto layer.

### Mitigation (binding)
- Hybrid allocation must remain template-based (CH02.P11): rule-bound incentives, published criteria, audit packs, and dispute rails.
- Maintain separation of functions: planning inputs, decision rights, and audit must not be concentrated in one actor.
- Procurement exitability is constitutive: no black-box planning institutions.

## B05.CH02.P14.S04 — Fail-closed: if work policy worsens R/B tails or raises lock-in, redesign is mandatory

Work policy is judged on lifeworld outcomes and power relations. Hard-stop rules prevent “successful on paper” reforms that degrade
agency.

### Hard-stop conditions (binding)
An implementation MUST trigger redesign (or suspend scaling) if any of the following occur without an external shock explanation:
- **R/B tails worsen**: increased volatility, increased share in precarious conditions, longer time-to-recovery, or rising
  share_below_alpha linked to work transitions.
- **Lock-in rises**: increased non-compete/exclusivity prevalence, increased scheduling coercion, higher switching costs, more
  employer-tied security dependency, rising retaliation indicators.
- **Selective harm**: cohort/region splits where certain groups experience worse tails or more clamp without transparent correction.

### Required response (binding)
- Diagnose via published tables (CH02.P13) and CSA-style contestability where applicable; produce restatements.
- Execute remedy templates: portability fixes, wage rails, clamp clause invalidation, buffer expansion, program redesign.
- If measurement cannot verify outcomes, fail-closed: no compatibility claim and no further rollout until observability is restored.
