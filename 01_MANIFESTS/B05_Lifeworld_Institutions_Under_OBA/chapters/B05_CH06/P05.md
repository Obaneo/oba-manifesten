# B05.CH06.P05 — Consumer credit and “emergency loans”

This paragraph specifies admissibility rules and enforcement logic for consumer credit and “emergency loans” under OBA. Consumer
credit is a high-frequency lifeworld channel: it directly mediates small shocks, liquidity gaps, and time misalignments. Without
rails, it becomes a predictable extraction surface: hidden APR, junk fees, rollovers, and penalty cascades convert minor shortfalls
into long-term clamp.

OBA permits consumer credit only when it is transparent, bounded, and non-coercive. It prohibits rollover-based products and opaque
fee bundles, requires a low-friction public/commons alternative for emergency liquidity (micro-buffer + emergency credit), and uses
repeat borrowing and fee-stack growth as anomaly triggers for audits and redesign.

---

## B05.CH06.P05.S01 — Minimum admissibility: transparent APR, fee caps, no penalty cascades

Consumer credit is admissible only if the borrower can understand the cost and exit without being trapped by penalties.

### Minimum admissibility (binding)
- **Transparent all-in APR**: disclose the effective annual cost including *all* fees and mandatory add-ons, in a standard format.
- **Fee caps**: cap origination, servicing, late fees, and collections add-ons; ban junk fee multiplication by “administrative” relabels.
- **No penalty cascades**: penalties may exist only as bounded, single-step consequences; compounding fee ladders are prohibited.
- **Repayment clarity**: clear schedule, total cost, and early repayment terms; no “surprise” repricing or hidden triggers.
- **Exitability**: early repayment allowed; switching/refinancing not blocked by punitive friction.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- If any cost component cannot be stated up-front in a standard all-in APR, the product is treated as non-conforming until redesigned.

---

## B05.CH06.P05.S02 — Prohibited products: payday-style rollovers, opaque fee bundles, tied products

Some consumer credit structures are structurally parasitic because profitability depends on distress and repeat cycles.

### Prohibited by design (binding)
- **Payday-style rollovers**: products that expect repeated extensions or refinancing as the normal path of repayment.
- **Opaque fee bundles**: “membership fees,” “service plans,” “credit protection,” or layered charges that prevent a clear all-in APR.
- **Tied products**: credit contingent on purchasing insurance, subscriptions, devices, or services that function as extraction layers.
- **Penalty-first design**: terms where late fees/penalties are a primary revenue driver rather than a bounded deterrent.
- **Coercive security/guarantees**: disproportionate collateralisation or guarantor coercion for small loans.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- These products are non-conforming and must be banned or forced to redesign into admissible standard formats.

---

## B05.CH06.P05.S03 — Alternative: public/commons micro-buffer and emergency credit (fallback) with minimal friction

If emergency liquidity is left to the market, parasitic products return because demand is inelastic under distress. OBA therefore
requires a public/commons alternative that is *easier* than payday credit.

### Required alternative (binding)
- **Micro-buffer**: a small automatic buffer mechanism (e.g., payroll-linked or benefits-linked) that reduces the need for borrowing.
- **Emergency credit**: low-cost, standardised, transparent loans for shocks (repairs, utilities, travel disruptions), with bounded cost
  and humane repayment options.
- **Minimal friction**: fast eligibility, low proof burden, predictable terms; administration must not become a toll (hidden L).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Emergency credit must not be conditional on behavioural micromanagement, surveillance, or humiliating procedures (anti-clamp).
- The alternative must publish performance metrics (uptake, time-to-disbursement, arrears outcomes) aggregated and join-safe.

---

## B05.CH06.P05.S04 — Detection: repeat borrowing, fee-stack growth, default clustering (anomaly protocols)

OBA treats consumer credit as a monitored clamp surface with early-warning indicators.

### Anomaly indicators (minimum)
- **Repeat borrowing**: frequent re-borrowing within short windows; serial use to cover essentials.
- **Fee-stack growth**: rising share of lender revenue from fees/penalties versus interest; increasing ancillary charges.
- **Default clustering**: concentrated defaults by cohort/region/product variant; spikes following marketing changes.
- **Refinancing friction signals**: borrowers unable to refinance into cheaper products despite stable income profiles.

### Response protocol (binding)
- Indicators trigger audit packs and product review: pricing decomposition, contract terms, sales/UX flows, and enforcement pathways.
- Where anomalies persist, remedies escalate: fee caps tightened, clauses prohibited, marketing/UX constrained, product redesign mandated,
  or product banned.
