# B05.CH06.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of lifeworld debt/credit

This paragraph defines the purpose, scope, and normative status of debt/credit in the lifeworld layer under OBA. Credit is treated
as an **order interface**: it determines whether households and productive actors can bridge time, absorb shocks, and invest in P and
commons capacity without being captured by claim stacks, fee-stacks, or contract clamps. In most real systems, debt is the mechanism
through which scarcity, power asymmetry, and opacity are converted into **lock-in** and **lifeworld burdens (L)**.

Under OBA, the central distinction is functional: credit is admissible only when it serves productive allocation with bounded returns,
transparent terms, and contestable enforcement. When credit functions as extraction—via rollovers, penalties, opaque spreads,
securitised opacity, or coercive collections—it is requalified as E and must be neutralised through redesign, restriction, or ban.
This chapter is therefore normative: it defines the rails that keep credit compatible with caps, commons, ΔM discipline, and lifeworld
R/B stability.

---

## B05.CH06.P01.S01 — Purpose: credit as a productive allocation interface, not as a claim stack or clamp mechanism

Credit is necessary in any complex economy because time, shocks, and investment needs are real. The purpose under OBA is to make credit
a **productive interface** rather than a claim amplifier.

### Purpose statement (binding)
- **Allocation**: enable P and commons investment where cashflow timing would otherwise block productive action.
- **Stabilisation**: dampen R/B tails by preventing small shocks from turning into arrears cascades and long-term lock-in.
- **Exit power**: prevent dependence from becoming coercion; credit must not function as an ongoing clamp on labour, housing, or
  essential access.

### Non-admissible purpose (binding)
- Credit may not be used primarily to support asset prices, preserve rentier cashflows, or build multi-layer claim stacks where
  profits depend on information asymmetry or penalties.

## B05.CH06.P01.S02 — Scope: household debt, consumer credit, mortgages, student finance, SME credit, collections, restructuring

CH06 applies to all lifeworld-relevant credit domains where clamp risk is high and incidence is experienced directly.

### Included domains (scope)
- **Household debt**: unsecured and secured household liabilities, including arrears and repayment plans.
- **Consumer credit**: instalment credit, overdrafts, revolving credit, BNPL-style products, emergency lending.
- **Mortgages and housing finance**: including refinancing, fees, foreclosure/eviction pathways, and credit-linked subsidies.
- **Student finance**: education-related lending and income-contingent repayment regimes.
- **SME credit**: working capital, capex finance, and restructuring for productive firms (not financial engineering).
- **Collections and insolvency**: the enforcement/collections industry, insolvency procedures, and reset mechanisms.
- **Restructuring**: templates, criteria, processes, and dispute flows for resolving unsustainable debt without endless clamp.

### Boundary note
- Sector-specific housing supply and commons design live primarily in CH07; CH06 governs the credit channel and its admissibility.

## B05.CH06.P01.S03 — Binding: Definition Set I (P/E/C), II (B,T,N,L,R), VI (ΔM), caps (W/I), anti-avoidance and audit standards

This chapter does not introduce new economic definitions. It binds credit policy to canonical definition sets and enforcement rails.

### Binding references (normative)
- **P/E/C (Definition Set I)**: classify credit products and revenue components (interest/fees) by function; requalify extraction.
- **B,T,N,L,R (Definition Set II)**: evaluate lifeworld impact (burdens, residual space, tails) as the primary success criterion.
- **ΔM rails (Definition Set VI)**: prevent monetary and credit channels from becoming asset/claim support; leakage tests apply.
- **Caps (W/I)**: prevent credit structures from enabling cap evasion via deferral, leverage, or synthetic exposure.
- **Anti-avoidance**: apply substance-over-form; detect relabelling and routing through intermediaries.
- **Audit standards**: require disclosure, reproducibility, decision logs, and contestability for product admissibility and enforcement.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any credit regime must be able to point to definition-set IDs and method references for classifications and metrics used in policy,
  enforcement, and publication.

---

## B05.CH06.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: opaque credit products, fee-stacks, and lock-in contracts are non-conform until made auditable

Credit is a high-entropy domain: without fail-closed rules, complexity and opacity become default and clamp dynamics return.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- **Opaque products** (unclear APR, hidden fees, discretionary pricing, non-reproducible terms) are non-conforming.
- **Fee-stacks** (layered junk fees, penalty cascades, mandatory intermediaries, collections add-ons) are non-conforming.
- **Lock-in contracts** (punitive exit/refinance barriers, cross-default traps, coercive clauses) are non-conforming.

### Required remediation (binding)
- Products must be redesigned into standard, auditable formats (all-in APR disclosure, bounded fees, clear triggers, portability).
- If auditability cannot be achieved (e.g., securitisation without disclosure, proprietary black-box scoring as decisive), the product
  or channel must be restricted, forced to redesign, or banned.
