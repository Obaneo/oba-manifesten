# B05.CH06.P06 — Student finance and human capital: limits on debt discipline

This paragraph specifies how student finance is treated under OBA and sets limits on “debt discipline” as a governance mechanism.
Education is a capacity domain: it builds productive capability (P) and supports mobility/exit power. When financed through heavy
private debt with clamp dynamics, education becomes a coercive allocator: it steers career choices, amplifies tail-risk, and
reproduces inequality via time and debt burdens.

OBA therefore treats education primarily as a **commons-like capacity system** with baseline access, and treats debt (where used) as a
secondary instrument that must be bounded, income-contingent, and non-coercive. Student finance must stabilise R/B tails, not
discipline behaviour; and it must include automatic shock handling to prevent labour-market volatility from becoming lifelong clamp.

---

## B05.CH06.P06.S01 — Education as a commons: baseline access reduces the need for high debt

The first-order design choice is structural: if education is essential capacity, the system should not require large debt positions to
access it.

### Commons framing (binding)
- **Baseline access**: a defined minimum of education access is provided through public/commons rails, reducing dependence on borrowing.
- **Capacity focus**: funding prioritises training capacity, quality, and continuity (ΔG-like), not credential scarcity.
- **Anti-toll**: fee-stacks (brokers, mandatory intermediaries, “admin fees”) are treated as E and eliminated.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any student finance regime must show that baseline access is real (coverage and affordability), otherwise debt becomes a de facto gate
  and creates clamp by design.

---

## B05.CH06.P06.S02 — Repayment: income-contingent, bounded, without lifelong clamp or compounding fees

Where debt exists, repayment must be a stabiliser—not a lifelong extraction channel.

### Repayment rails (binding)
- **Income-contingent**: repayments scale with income and pause at low income thresholds; no forced default through inability to pay.
- **Bounded total burden**: caps on total repayment, duration, and effective cost; no indefinite balances.
- **No compounding fee stacks**: late fees, penalty interest, and administrative charges are bounded and cannot create cascades.
- **Exitability**: clear settlement options; portability across jobs and borders within defined rules; no blacklisting as governance tool.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Student debt must not function as a permanent claim on future labour; if it does, it is parasitic and requires redesign or conversion
  into a non-clamping public instrument.

---

## B05.CH06.P06.S03 — Labour-market shocks: automatic pauses/forgiveness under evidenced tail-risk events

Education-to-work pathways are exposed to macro volatility (recessions, sector collapses, health shocks). OBA requires automatic
shock-handling to prevent education debt from amplifying tail-risk.

### Shock rails (binding)
- **Automatic pauses** under evidenced labour-market shock conditions (unemployment, disability, major income loss).
- **Forgiveness/conversion mechanisms** when shocks are persistent or when repayment would violate R/B tail constraints.
- **No discretionary humiliation**: relief must be rule-based, fast, and low-friction; administration cannot become a toll layer.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Shock handling must be auditable and measurable (uptake, pause frequency, outcomes) and must demonstrably stabilise tails.

---

## B05.CH06.P06.S04 — Failure mode: debt as disciplining career choices → agency loss and misallocation

A core failure mode is using debt as behavioural control: pushing graduates into higher-paying sectors regardless of social need, fit,
or long-run productivity, and deterring risk-taking and mobility.

### Mechanism
- **Career steering**: repayment pressure forces choices toward immediate income maximisation rather than skill development or public
  value.
- **Agency loss**: debt constrains exit power (job switching, entrepreneurship, care time), increasing clamp in labour relations.
- **Misallocation**: socially necessary sectors (care, teaching, maintenance) become under-supplied because debt makes them infeasible.

### Hard rule (binding)
- If student finance predictably steers cohorts away from essential/maintenance roles or increases labour clamp, the regime is
  non-conforming and must be redesigned (increase baseline access, adjust repayment rails, or convert debt into commons funding).
