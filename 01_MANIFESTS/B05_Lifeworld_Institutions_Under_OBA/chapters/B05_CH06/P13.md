# B05.CH06.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for credit and debt governance under OBA and the hard stops that apply when the
system drifts back into extraction (E), opacity, and asset-support dynamics. Credit is a high-leverage surface: small design changes
can recreate clamp, rebuild fee-stacks, or re-inflate asset prices. OBA therefore defines explicit non-conformance signals and
fail-closed responses. The objective is not to preserve “credit availability” at any price, but to preserve **order compatibility**:
credit must expand productive/commons capacity, stabilise tails, and reduce L—not reconstitute rentier cashflows and bubbles.

---

## B05.CH06.P13.S01 — Debt shifting: public buffers shrink while private debt grows (masking) → non-conform

OBA treats “debt shifting” as a structural masking failure: public stabilisers are withdrawn or hollowed out while households are
pushed into private credit to fund essentials, smoothing statistics while deepening clamp.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Declining public buffers (commons access, stabilisation programmes, essential support) coinciding with rising household debt for
  essentials (housing, utilities, health, education) and increased arrears sensitivity to small shocks.
- Policy packages that reduce public expenditure but rely on expanded private credit as the de facto replacement for baseline
  lifeworld capacity.
- Increased share of income devoted to debt service without corresponding improvements in ΔG or reductions in L.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Masking via debt shifting is **non-conform**: affected programmes must be redesigned to restore public buffer capacity or to expand
  commons substitution, and to reduce the essentials-credit load.
- Where debt shifting is detected, any public credit facility that indirectly enables the shift must tighten eligibility or suspend
  the channel (fail-closed against private clamp substitution).

---

## B05.CH06.P13.S02 — Shadow-credit regrowth: BNPL/fintech fee-stacks → detection and requalification

Shadow credit regrows where products are marketed as “not credit” while functionally acting as debt with enforcement and fee-stacks.

### Detection indicators (binding)
- Products that avoid credit regulation by labelling (e.g., “pay later”, “subscription”, “membership”) while imposing repayment
  schedules, penalties, lockouts, or collections actions.
- Revenue dominated by late fees, reactivation fees, merchant kickbacks, yield-based steering, or cross-subsidies that require
  distress or rollovers to be profitable.
- Opaque underwriting and pricing models that cannot produce P/E decomposition and all-in cost disclosure.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- If a product functions as credit, it is **requalified as credit** and must meet all OBA credit rails: all-in APR, fee caps, standard
  contracts, dispute flow, restructuring availability, and publication metrics.
- Where economics rely on fee-stacks or opacity, the product is requalified as **E-dominant** and must be redesigned or prohibited.

### Enforcement posture (binding)
- Regulators and rail operators must maintain a “shadow-credit watchlist” taxonomy and require periodic audits targeted at fast-moving
  fintech segments (fail-closed against label arbitrage).

## B05.CH06.P13.S03 — Asset inflation via credit: relapse into housing bubbles → prohibited channel and hard stop

Credit used primarily to inflate asset prices is a direct relapse into OOA-style accumulation dynamics and is incompatible with OBA.

### Prohibited channel (binding)
- Credit programmes and underwriting practices whose functional effect is to raise asset prices (especially housing) without expanding
  ΔG (real housing capacity, maintenance, infrastructure) or reducing L (housing cost burden).
- Investor/landlord finance structures that amplify speculative demand, collateral loops, or rent extraction while constraining
  lifeworld access.

### Bubble detection (binding)
- Rapid divergence of house prices from income and rent fundamentals; rising mortgage burdens in tails (p90/p95); growth of highly
  leveraged investor share; and increased refinancing churn driven by price appreciation rather than productive investment.
- Expanded securitisation or credit easing that coincides with asset price inflation and declining affordability.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Under detected asset-support dynamics, the channel is **prohibited**: tighten credit standards, suspend affected facilities, and
  impose constraints on investor-oriented lending. Where needed, redirect capacity toward commons housing supply, maintenance, and
  affordability stabilisation rather than demand-side leverage.

---

## B05.CH06.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: under opacity, securitisation without disclosure, or repeat fee-stacks, the credit product is banned or forced to redesign

OBA applies fail-closed when contestability is lost. Credit products must be auditable end-to-end; otherwise they are treated as
extraction infrastructure.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- **Opacity**: missing all-in APR, non-reproducible ledgers, undisclosed intermediaries, or non-standard clauses without annex +
  justification.
- **Securitisation without disclosure**: structures that break traceability of terms, servicing, fee decomposition, or risk allocation;
  inability to audit underlying pools and enforcement practices.
- **Repeat fee-stacks**: recurring evidence that profitability depends on layered fees, penalties, rollovers, or distress monetisation.
- **Selective enforcement** or unexplained lockouts that function as block power without due process.

### Mandatory actions (binding)
- **Immediate suspension** of new issuance for the product class until redesign and conformance re-certification.
- **Forced redesign** into standard templates with bounded fees and transparent pricing decomposition; if redesign fails, **ban** the
  product.
- **Remediation** for affected borrowers: restatements, refunds/credits for E components, contract adjustments to admissible P ranges,
  and restructuring access.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Fail-closed actions, bans, and redesign mandates must be recorded in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to
  prevent quiet continuation through rebranding or platform migration.
