# B05.CH06.P09 — Interest, fees, and price discipline (what is allowed and what is not)

This paragraph specifies how interest and fees on credit products must be structured and disclosed under OBA. Price is not treated as
a neutral market signal: in credit markets it is a primary channel for extraction (E), opacity, steering, and distress monetisation.
OBA therefore decomposes the credit price into a **functional cost component** (P) and an **E component**, and requires contract formats
and disclosures that make the decomposition contestable. Where contestability is lost, OBA applies conservatism: fail-closed
classification as E with mandatory remediation.

---

## B05.CH06.P09.S01 — Functional cost component: funding cost + risk + operations (P)

Under OBA, a credit price is admissible only to the extent it reflects auditable functional costs that support productive capacity,
maintenance, and solvency restoration.

### Functional components (binding)
- **Funding cost**: benchmark-referenced cost of funds (defined by the jurisdiction’s standard benchmark set) plus verifiable funding
  frictions (e.g., term liquidity), stated explicitly.
- **Risk premium**: based on transparent risk classes and loss estimates that are reviewable, reproducible, and linked to realised
  performance (with periodic recalibration and public summary tables).
- **Operations cost**: bounded, itemised cost-to-serve and servicing costs, with standard categories and caps where scale economies
  exist.

### Prohibited contamination of P (binding)
- Risk pricing may not embed **behavioural profiling rents** or unrelated monetisation of customer data.
- “Operational cost” may not be used as a bucket for margin padding, cross-subsidies, or affiliate profit shifting.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Lenders must publish (at least in aggregate) the mapping from product class → allowed components → typical ranges, and maintain a
  reproducible method pack for audits.

---

## B05.CH06.P09.S02 — E component: excess spread, junk fees, steering, kickbacks, opacity premiums

Any price element whose functional effect is not funding/risk/operations is treated as extraction (E), irrespective of label.

### E indicators (binding)
- **Excess spread**: margin above the maximum admissible P range for the risk class without auditable justification.
- **Junk fees**: layered servicing charges, penalty cascades, “convenience” or processing fees that do not reflect real costs, and
  fees whose primary function is to exploit inattention or lock-in.
- **Steering / yield-based incentives**: compensation tied to placing borrowers into higher-cost products than they qualify for.
- **Kickbacks / affiliate leakage**: referral payments, broker chains, or bundled add-ons that route value to intermediaries without
  increasing P capacity or reducing L.
- **Opacity premiums**: price increases made possible only by non-standard contracts, undisclosed add-ons, or complex fee schedules
  that prevent like-for-like comparison.

### Enforcement posture (binding)
- Where a product’s economics rely on E indicators, it must be redesigned, restricted, or prohibited; continued issuance is treated as
  a conformance breach.

---

## B05.CH06.P09.S03 — Fee caps and disclosure: mandatory all-in APR, standard contract formats

Because comparability is a public good in credit markets, disclosure is treated as a rail, not a consumer “choice” feature.

### Mandatory all-in disclosure (binding)
- **All-in APR** must include *all* compulsory charges: origination, servicing, mandatory insurance, add-ons required for approval,
  late/penalty fees (modelled as standard scenarios), and broker/intermediary compensation.
- **Total cost of credit** must be displayed in absolute terms alongside APR, for standardised scenarios.
- **Fee table** must show: name, trigger, amount, maximum, and whether it is optional (with default = not included unless explicitly
  opted in).

### Standard formats (binding)
- Credit contracts must use standard templates per product category (mortgage, consumer instalment, revolving, SME credit), with fixed
  placement for key terms (rate, fees, enforcement, restructuring options).
- Non-standard clauses are permitted only via a clearly marked annex with an explicit justification field and an audit reference.

### Fee caps (binding)
- Caps apply to: origination, servicing, penalty fees, and add-on markups; caps are set by product category and adjusted only through
  a published, reviewable rule process (with sunset-bounded exceptions).
- Where scale economies exist (e.g., automated payments, digital servicing), caps must tighten over time unless justified.

---

## B05.CH06.P09.S04 — Fail-closed: missing disclosure → classify as E + sanctions/contract adjustment

If disclosure or comparability fails, OBA assumes adverse selection and applies conservatism: the burden is on the issuer to prove P.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- Missing or non-comparable **all-in APR**, incomplete fee tables, undisclosed intermediaries, opaque add-ons, or non-standard clauses
  without annex + justification.
- Inability to reproduce the pricing decomposition (funding/risk/ops) for a sampled contract during audit.

### Mandatory classification and remedies (binding)
- The non-disclosed portion is **classified as E by default**.
- **Contract adjustment** applies automatically: reduce price terms to the maximum admissible P range for the verified risk class and
  refund/credit the difference (plus statutory uplift where applicable).
- **Sanctions** scale with severity and recurrence: fines, restitution orders, product suspension, and—in repeat cases—licence
  restrictions for the issuing entity and involved intermediaries.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Enforcement actions and adjustments must be recorded in an exception/sanctions register (privacy-safe aggregation) to prevent quiet
  settlements from becoming the true operating model.
