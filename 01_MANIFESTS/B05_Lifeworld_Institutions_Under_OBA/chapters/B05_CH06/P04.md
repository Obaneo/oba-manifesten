# B05.CH06.P04 — Mortgages and housing: credit as a channel of asset inflation (or not)

This paragraph specifies when housing credit is productive and when it becomes a channel of **asset inflation** that raises lifeworld
burdens (L) and produces clamp. Housing is an essential with structural scarcity; therefore credit expansion does not primarily
increase “housing consumption,” but often increases **prices**. The result is higher rents, higher mortgage burdens, greater
dependency on refinancing cycles, and a political economy that defends asset values at the expense of access and stability.

OBA treats housing credit as a high-risk domain. The admissible path is to impose **price discipline** and redirect policy from demand
stimulus toward **supply and commons capacity**. Mortgages are productive only when they are boring: transparent, portable, bounded,
and not dependent on speculative price dynamics. Any policy that supports housing prices through credit or ΔM channels is a prohibited
asset/claim-support route.

---

## B05.CH06.P04.S01 — Mechanism: cheap credit + scarcity → price inflation → higher L (lifeworld harm)

The core mechanism is structural: when supply is constrained, credit abundance capitalises into asset values.

### Inflation channel
- **Cheap credit** increases purchasing power at the margin.
- **Scarcity** (land, permits, build capacity, infrastructure) prevents proportional supply response.
- **Prices rise**; households must borrow more for the same shelter.
- **L rises** through higher monthly burdens, higher deposits, higher rent pass-through, and increased volatility under rate resets.

### Lifeworld harm (binding interpretation)
- Higher housing debt is not neutral if it raises essentials burdens, increases lock-in, and makes households reliant on asset inflation
  or refinancing as survival strategy.
- Housing-credit inflation is therefore treated as a lifeworld failure mode, even if aggregate “wealth” measures rise.

---

## B05.CH06.P04.S02 — OBA rail: shift toward building/supply and commons housing instead of demand stimulus

OBA’s housing-credit rail is to address scarcity directly and to provide non-speculative exit options.

### Policy rail (binding)
- **No demand stimulus** as primary tool: subsidies, tax advantages, or credit loosening that mainly raise prices are disallowed.
- **Supply/maintenance first**: expand build and renovation capacity; reduce bottlenecks; shorten cycle times (link CH07).
- **Commons housing**: develop public/commons housing pipelines as a structural alternative to rentier pricing and speculative upside.
- **Price discipline**: constrain mark-ups and rent escalation where scarcity creates toll power; treat fee-stacks as E.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any housing finance policy must demonstrate that its primary effect is ΔG/supply/quality improvement or L reduction—not price support.

---

## B05.CH06.P04.S03 — Productive mortgage: only under price discipline, bounded LTV, transparent fees, portability

A mortgage can be productive when it finances stable shelter access without creating clamp or speculative dependence.

### Admissibility criteria (productive mortgage; binding)
- **Price discipline context**: credit does not primarily drive asset inflation; scarcity is being addressed and speculative channels are constrained.
- **Bounded LTV / risk allocation**: leverage limits; clear stress testing; conversion options for rate shocks without coercive terms.
- **All-in transparency**: standard contract formats; full disclosure of fees and spreads; prohibition of junk fee bundles.
- **Portability and refinance access**: switching costs capped; refinancing processes standardised; no punitive break fees.
- **Fair enforcement**: foreclosure is last resort; restructuring pathways exist and are fast; baseline access is preserved (link P10/P11, CH04).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Mortgage products must be auditable end-to-end, including servicing, repricing triggers, arrears handling, and foreclosure timelines.

---

## B05.CH06.P04.S04 — Hard stop: policy that supports housing prices via credit = prohibited channel (ΔM leakage)

Housing-price support through credit is a prohibited channel because it converts public policy (including ΔM) into claim support.

### Hard stop rule (binding)
- Any policy whose primary or systematic effect is to **stabilise or raise housing prices** via credit availability, guarantees,
  purchase programmes, or monetary channels is **non-conforming** (asset/claim-support leakage).
- “Stability” rhetoric does not override functional effect: if the channel props up prices rather than delivering supply/ΔG and L
  reduction, it is prohibited.

### Required response
- Redirect instruments to supply and commons capacity.
- If leakage cannot be prevented or measured, restrict the programme to auditable channels or unwind (fail-closed).
