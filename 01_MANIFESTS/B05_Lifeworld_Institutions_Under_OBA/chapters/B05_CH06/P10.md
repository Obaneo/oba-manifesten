# B05.CH06.P10 — Restructuring: when legitimate and how executed

This paragraph specifies when debt restructuring is legitimate under OBA and how it must be executed so that it restores lifeworld
solvency rather than extending clamp. In an OBA order, restructuring is not a discretionary “mercy” product nor a stigma mechanism: it
is a **governance rail** for correcting parasitic credit, unsustainable terms, and shock-broken tails that collapse R/B. Because
distress extraction is a primary E surface, OBA treats restructuring capacity as mandatory infrastructure with fast triage, standard
templates, and contestable evidence. The objective is to restore a viable R/B profile and reduce L and clamp dynamics, not to maximise
recovery through prolonged enforcement.

---

## B05.CH06.P10.S01 — Criteria: debt is parasitic or unsustainable, or a shock has broken R/B tails

Restructuring is legitimate when the current contract terms are functionally incompatible with a stable lifeworld baseline.

### Legitimate triggers (binding)
- **Parasitic debt (E-embedded)**: pricing or enforcement relies on junk fees, penalty cascades, opacity premiums, steering, or other
  E components such that repayment primarily services extraction rather than principal risk or functional cost.
- **Unsustainable debt service**: the verified repayment path requires persistent R/B collapse (R/B below the minimum rail) or requires
  indefinite sacrifice of essentials (L escalation) to remain current.
- **Shock-broken tails**: a documented exogenous shock (job loss, illness, family breakup, regional collapse, disaster) has materially
  changed cashflows and broken the tails assumed at origination, causing R/B failure.
- **Contract clamp**: lock-in terms (variable rate jumps, cross-default triggers, collateral loops, auto-debit traps) prevent realistic
  stabilisation even when underlying capacity exists.

### Prohibited gatekeeping (binding)
- Restructuring may not be denied solely on stigma, “fault” narratives, or refusal to accept unrelated add-ons (insurance, bundled
  products, paid advisory services).
- Where the issuer cannot reproduce the original affordability and pricing decomposition evidence, conservatism applies in favour of
  restructuring (fail-closed against clamp).

---

## B05.CH06.P10.S02 — Instruments: rate reduction, fee waiver, principal write-down, maturity extension, conversion

OBA permits a bounded set of restructuring instruments, chosen to match the structural cause of unsustainability and to restore a
stable payment path.

### Permitted instruments (binding)
- **Rate reduction**: reduce the interest rate to the maximum admissible P range for the verified risk class; remove opacity premiums
  and excess spread.
- **Fee waiver / fee reset**: waive junk fees, penalty cascades, and servicing add-ons; convert remaining fees into transparent, capped
  schedules.
- **Principal write-down**: reduce principal where the claim is inflated by E components, mispricing, predatory terms, or where asset
  valuation/collateral cycles at origination were structurally non-viable.
- **Maturity extension**: extend term to reduce monthly service load where the underlying principal is legitimate and the extension
  does not increase total extraction (E) through added fees.
- **Conversion**: convert to a standardised product class with compliant disclosure and bounded enforcement (e.g., convert revolving to
  instalment with fixed schedule), or convert private claims into structured public facility terms where the channel is admissible.

### Selection rule (binding)
- Instrument choice must **target the dominant failure mechanism** (rate shock, fee-stack, principal inflation, income shock, L
  escalation, clamp terms) and must be documented in a brief rationale note linked to the evidence set.

## B05.CH06.P10.S03 — Process: fast triage, standard templates, evidence set, objection/appeal (no endless clamp)

Restructuring must be delivered as fast, standard infrastructure, not as a negotiator’s battlefield.

### Fast triage (binding)
- Requests must be triaged within strict time bounds: acknowledgement, provisional relief decision, and final offer windows must be
  defined per product class.
- During triage, **clamp escalation is suspended**: no penalty stacking, no adverse enforcement intensification, and no new fees for
  “processing”.

### Standard templates (binding)
- Restructuring offers must use standard contract templates with fixed placement for: revised rate, revised fees, revised principal,
  term, enforcement posture, dispute rights, and re-default handling.
- All offers must present at least two compliant options (where feasible): a minimal adjustment and a stabilisation option that meets
  the R/B rail.

### Evidence set (binding)
- Minimum evidence includes: current income and essential costs (L), existing obligations, shock documentation (if applicable), and the
  issuer’s reproducible pricing decomposition (P vs E) and enforcement ledger.
- Evidence demands must be proportional; missing non-essential documentation cannot be used to prolong clamp.

### Objection and appeal (binding)
- Borrowers must have a fast objection path with a neutral review layer.
- Appeals must be time-bounded; the system must not allow “endless clamp” via repeated re-documentation loops.

## B05.CH06.P10.S04 — Moral-hazard control: conditions target structural causes (L/price/contract clamp), not punishment

OBA controls moral hazard by correcting upstream incentives and contract structures, not by punitive hardship.

### Permitted conditions (binding)
- **Structural stabilisation**: require conversion to standard product formats; removal of auto-debit traps; caps on variable-rate
  jumps; elimination of cross-default triggers that amplify tails.
- **Affordability alignment**: require payment schedules consistent with verified R/B minima, and automatic re-evaluation triggers when
  shocks recur.
- **Issuer-side conditions**: where the lender’s product design or enforcement model contributed to clamp, impose remediation on the
  issuer (fee refunds, product redesign, tighter underwriting standards, reduced enforcement rights).

### Prohibited conditions (binding)
- **Punitive add-ons**: mandatory paid counselling, forced ancillary products, “monitoring” fees, or behavioural surveillance beyond
  order necessity.
- **Stigma enforcement**: conditions designed to shame, exclude, or extract leverage rather than restore solvency.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If the restructuring programme increases total extraction (E) or extends clamp duration without improving R/B, it is non-conform and
  must be redesigned; repeated violations trigger sanctions and programme suspension (fail-closed against distress monetisation).
