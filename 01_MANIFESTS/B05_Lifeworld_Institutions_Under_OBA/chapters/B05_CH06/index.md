# B05.CH06 — Debt and credit in the lifeworld: productive credit, parasitic debt, and restructuring

This chapter defines debt and credit as a **lifeworld interface** where clamp dynamics, fee-stacks, and asset-price channels often
form. Under OBA, credit is admissible only as a **productive allocation mechanism** (financing P or commons capacity with bounded
returns and auditable terms). When credit becomes a **claim stack** or a **lock-in tool**—through opaque pricing, penalty cascades,
collections ecosystems, or housing-price support—it is requalified as E and must be neutralised.

The chapter binds credit policy to canonical definition sets (P/E/C; B,T,N,L,R; ΔM rails; caps) and specifies admissibility,
detection, restructuring and dispute-flow logic, and publication requirements. It is designed to fail-closed: opaque products and
non-auditable securitisation are treated as non-conforming until corrected.
