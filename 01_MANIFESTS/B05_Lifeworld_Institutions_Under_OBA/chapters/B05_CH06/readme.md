# B05.CH06 — Debt and credit in the lifeworld: productive credit, parasitic debt, and restructuring

This chapter specifies how **debt and credit** function under OBA as a **lifeworld interface**: the place where essentials (housing,
energy, health, education) meet contract design, pricing power, and enforcement. Credit is admissible only as a **productive
allocation mechanism**—financing P or commons capacity with bounded returns and auditable terms. When credit becomes a **claim
stack**, a **fee-stack ecosystem**, or a **clamp mechanism** (lock-in, penalty cascades, collections coercion, asset-price support),
it is requalified as E and must be neutralised.

CH06 is designed to be **anti-washing** and **fail-closed**. Opaque credit products, non-disclosed fee structures, and non-auditable
securitisation are treated as **non-conforming** until made auditable or replaced. The chapter also defines when and how
**restructuring** is legitimate, how disputes are handled without endless clamp, and what publication is required to keep the credit
layer contestable and non-captured.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing credit regulation**: use CH06 to define admissibility rules (APR disclosure, fee caps, prohibited clauses),
  detect clamp patterns (refinancing friction, cross-default, collections fee-stacks), and decide when products must be banned or
  redesigned.
- If you are **designing housing/education policy**: use CH06 to prevent credit from becoming a demand-stimulus asset-support channel
  (housing bubbles, debt discipline as coercion); route finance toward supply/commons capacity instead.
- If you are **building public credit rails**: implement tagging, traceability, dispute flows, and governance constraints; treat rail
  operators as audit-critical institutions.
- If you are **auditing lifeworld outcomes**: use the measurement and publication requirements (P12) to track arrears, defaults, fee
  shares, refinancing friction, foreclosure/eviction rates, and post-restructuring tail outcomes.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA-compatible design of credit markets, public credit infrastructure, and collections /
insolvency regimes. In particular, the admissibility rules, disclosure requirements, fee-stack prohibitions, housing-credit hard
stops, restructuring templates, and publication discipline are treated as **constitutive** safeguards against clamp dynamics.

If CH06 constraints are weakened—by opacity, vendor black boxes, discretionary settlements, or tolerated fee-stacks—the lifeworld
layer will register predictable failure signals (arrears cascades, lock-in, asset inflation, coercive collections). In that case,
the implementation must be treated as drifting toward incompatibility and must trigger redesign, scope restriction to auditable
coverage, and (where necessary) product bans or forced contract rewrites.
