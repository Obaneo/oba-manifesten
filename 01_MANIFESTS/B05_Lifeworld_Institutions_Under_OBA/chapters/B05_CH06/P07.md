# B05.CH06.P07 — SMEs and productive enterprise credit

This paragraph specifies admissible credit for SMEs and productive enterprises under OBA. The goal is to finance **production and
maintenance capacity** (P and commons-adjacent ΔG) rather than financial engineering, asset stripping, or fee extraction. SME credit
is treated as a core allocation interface: it determines whether productive firms can invest, hold inventory, innovate, and survive
shocks without being converted into extraction machines for lenders, intermediaries, or private-equity-style governance layers.

OBA permits enterprise credit when it is cashflow-grounded, transparent, and bounded in return. It prohibits covenant structures that
function as hidden takeover rights or allow extraction via fees, forced sales, and priority stacking. In crises, OBA prefers
restructuring over liquidation when liquidation would destroy productive capacity and worsen lifeworld outcomes.

---

## B05.CH06.P07.S01 — Purpose: P capex, inventory, innovation, maintenance — not financial engineering

SME credit is admissible only when it funds productive activity and real capacity.

### Admissible purposes (binding)
- **Capex**: equipment, tools, production lines, facilities upgrades, digitisation that supports P (not surveillance rent).
- **Inventory and working capital**: bridging cashflow gaps for inputs, wages, and delivery cycles.
- **Innovation**: R&D and capability building with identifiable output pathways.
- **Maintenance**: repair, replacement, resilience upgrades (ΔG-like discipline in firms).

### Non-admissible purposes (binding)
- **Financial engineering**: leverage used primarily to extract distributions, arbitrage tax/caps, or generate fee stacks.
- **Asset extraction**: lending that anticipates collateral liquidation as the core profitability path.
- **Opaque intermediated stacks**: broker layers and syndication structures whose primary function is fee extraction.

---

## B05.CH06.P07.S02 — Bounded returns: interest/fees bounded; covenants against asset stripping and fee extraction

Enterprise credit must not create a parallel extraction regime inside productive firms.

### Bounded returns (binding)
- Interest/fees must be bounded to functional components (funding cost + risk + operations) with caps on excess spread and junk fees.
- All lender compensation must be disclosed; side letters and hidden kickbacks are non-conforming.

### Covenant rails (binding)
- Covenants may protect solvency, but must not function as:
  - **hidden control rights** enabling de facto takeover for extraction,
  - **forced asset sales** unrelated to solvency restoration,
  - **fee triggers** (event-of-default fees, waiver fees) that turn covenant breaches into profit centres,
  - **priority stacking** that subordinates workers, suppliers, or essential obligations in opaque ways.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- If profitability relies materially on covenant-trigger fees or asset stripping, the credit product is requalified as E and must be redesigned or prohibited.

---

## B05.CH06.P07.S03 — Transparency: use-of-funds tagging, cashflow-based underwriting, auditability

To prevent drift into extraction, enterprise credit requires explicit evidence discipline.

### Transparency rails (binding)
- **Use-of-funds tagging**: loans must specify permitted purposes and forbid diversion into distributions or claim support.
- **Cashflow-based underwriting**: primary serviceability assessed on operating cashflows and productive plan, not on collateral alone.
- **Auditability**: publishable (at least to auditors/regulators) documentation of pricing decomposition, covenant triggers, and default paths.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Credit facilities must support reproducible review: standard contract formats, traceable amendments, and decision logs for exceptions.
- Where opacity prevents auditability (complex securitisation, shadow vehicles, undisclosed intermediation), fail-closed applies.

---

## B05.CH06.P07.S04 — Crisis: restructuring over liquidation when P capacity would otherwise be lost

In downturns, SME failures can be systemic. Liquidation often destroys productive capacity and worsens lifeworld burdens via job loss,
supply-chain disruption, and regional decline.

### Crisis rule (binding)
- Where a firm is **operationally viable** but temporarily insolvent due to a shock, restructuring is the preferred path.
- Liquidation is admissible when viability is absent or when restructuring would preserve an extraction structure rather than P.

### Restructuring principles (binding)
- Focus on restoring productive capacity: rate reductions, maturity extensions, fee waivers, partial write-downs, or conversion where needed.
- Prevent creditor-driven asset stripping during distress; enforce moratoria where necessary to enable a fair triage.
- Ensure dispute/appeal rails and time-bounded processes to avoid endless clamp through “temporary” extensions.
