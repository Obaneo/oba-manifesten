# B05.CH06.P11 — Insolvency, collections, and the “debt industry”

This paragraph specifies how insolvency and collections must operate under OBA from a lifeworld and anti-extraction perspective.
Collections is not a neutral enforcement utility: it frequently becomes an **E ecosystem** that monetises distress through fee-stacks,
penalty cascades, opaque calculations, and intimidation. OBA therefore treats the “debt industry” as a high-stakes capture surface for
block power and information abuse. The order objective is a dignified reset with fast cycle time, protection of basic assets and
commons access, and dispute mechanisms that are reproducible and auditable. Where traceability is lost or coercion substitutes for
lawful enforcement, OBA applies fail-closed restrictions and requalification.

---

## B05.CH06.P11.S01 — Collections as an E ecosystem: requalify and cap fee-stacks and penalties

Collections-related pricing and practices are admissible only as bounded functional cost recovery, not as a profit model.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- Any revenue whose functional effect is to profit from distress—rather than recover legitimate principal and bounded P costs—is
  **classified as E** irrespective of contract labels or intermediary structure.
- This includes: layered “collection fees”, “legal fees” not tied to real cost, penalty interest cascades, repeated processing charges,
  and add-ons required to negotiate.

### Caps and limits (binding)
- **Fee caps** apply across the entire enforcement chain (issuer → servicer → collector → legal representative). Caps are cumulative:
  intermediaries cannot each apply a maximum independently.
- **Penalty discipline**: penalty rates must be bounded, time-limited, and may not compound into self-perpetuating clamp. Default
  penalties must not exceed the maximum admissible P range for the verified risk class.
- **Contact discipline**: frequency and channels of contact are capped to prevent harassment dynamics and to keep disputes resolvable.

### Transparency requirement (binding)
- Every enforcement action must link to a reproducible ledger: principal, interest, fees (by category), payments, adjustments, and the
  legal basis for each charge.

---

## B05.CH06.P11.S02 — Insolvency procedures: dignified reset, fast cycle time, protection of basic assets/commons access

Insolvency is treated as a necessary safety valve for tail-risk reality, not as moral failure. The procedure must restore agency and
order participation.

### Procedure objectives (binding)
- **Dignified reset**: enable a clear “after” state where individuals can re-enter ordinary economic life without perpetual clamp.
- **Fast cycle time**: strict time bounds for decisioning and discharge; long-duration limbo is treated as lifeworld harm and a source
  of informal extraction.
- **Predictable outcomes**: standardised rules reduce bargaining asymmetries and opportunistic pressure.

### Protected baseline (binding)
- **Basic assets** necessary for life and participation (essential household goods, tools of work, minimal mobility) are protected.
- **Commons access** (health, education, core utilities where applicable, and essential payment rail access) must not be blocked by
  insolvency status, except under narrow, reviewable fraud criteria.
- **Income floor protection**: enforcement cannot push the person below the minimum lifeworld baseline (R/B rail), and essential
  expenses (L) have priority classification.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Insolvency administrators must publish aggregated statistics: cycle times, discharge rates, protected-asset decisions, and appeal
  outcomes (privacy-safe aggregation).

---

## B05.CH06.P11.S03 — Prohibited practices: intimidation, data extraction, exploitation of vulnerability

Because collections actors wield coercive leverage, OBA explicitly prohibits practices that convert vulnerability into profit.

### Prohibited practices (binding)
- **Intimidation and harassment**: threats beyond lawful remedies, repeated high-frequency contact, contacting employers/family as
  pressure tactics, and any deceptive representation of authority or legal status.
- **Data extraction**: coercing expanded data disclosure (contacts, behavioural data, device access, social graphs) beyond what is
  strictly necessary to verify the claim and execute a lawful process.
- **Vulnerability exploitation**: targeting cognitively impaired, ill, elderly, or otherwise vulnerable people with complex offers,
  time pressure, or “settlement” structures designed to re-lock them into worse clamp.
- **Pay-to-negotiate**: charging fees to access restructuring, complaint handling, or basic documentation.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven intimidation, deceptive authority claims, or coercive data extraction triggers immediate activity suspension for the actor and
  mandatory review of all affected cases (fail-closed against coercive enforcement).

---

## B05.CH06.P11.S04 — Dispute flow: reproducible calculations, audit packs, correction/restatement

Disputes must be resolvable through evidence and reproducible arithmetic, not through asymmetric persistence.

### Reproducible calculation (binding)
- Every claim must be presented in a standardised calculation pack: principal ledger, interest method, fee schedule, penalty rule,
  payment history, and all adjustments with timestamps.
- Calculations must be machine-checkable and independently recomputable from disclosed parameters.

### Audit packs (binding)
- For sampled cases and contested cases, collectors/issuers must provide an audit pack that includes: contract template references,
  pricing decomposition (P vs E), enforcement actions, and legal basis mappings.

### Correction and restatement (binding)
- When errors are identified, the issuer/collector must issue a **restatement**: corrected ledger, corrected balance, and corrected
  enforcement status, with a clear diff from the prior state.
- Where errors created over-collection, mandatory refund/credit applies with statutory uplift; repeat errors trigger sanctions.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Missing documentation, non-reproducible calculations, or opaque fee attribution causes the disputed portion to be classified as E by
  default and suspended from enforcement until corrected (fail-closed against ledger opacity).
