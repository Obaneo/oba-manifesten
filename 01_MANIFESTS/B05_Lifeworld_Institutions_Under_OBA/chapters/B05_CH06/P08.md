# B05.CH06.P08 — Public credit infrastructure and banks (lifeworld perspective)

This paragraph specifies how public credit infrastructure, banks, and payment rails must operate under OBA from a lifeworld
perspective. Credit infrastructure is not merely financial plumbing: it is a **governance rail** that determines which activities
receive financing, under what terms, and with what enforcement posture. Because it can rapidly become a channel for claim/asset
support, opaque intermediation, and selective power, it is treated as a high-stakes surface for ΔM leakage, fee-stack regrowth, and
capture.

OBA therefore requires channel discipline: explicit allowed purposes, leakage tests, and auditable tagging for public facilities; hard
constraints on commercial bank behaviour where they recreate extraction; payment rails designed for dispute and safety without
surveillance rent; and governance arrangements that make the rail operator itself contestable (audit-of-the-auditor), with public
incident discipline.

---

## B05.CH06.P08.S01 — Public banks/facilities: channel discipline, allowed purposes, leakage tests

Public credit facilities are admissible only as disciplined channels into P and commons capacity, not as general asset-price support.

### Channel discipline (binding)
- **Allowed purposes**: finance P capex, maintenance, commons infrastructure, and stabilisation instruments that reduce L and tails.
- **Prohibited purposes**: asset/claim support (price floors, bailout of rentier cashflows, speculative carry structures), and any
  programme whose functional effect is to raise asset values without ΔG or L reduction.
- **Tagging**: every facility must tag flows by purpose class and link to audit packs (method/definition-set references).

### Leakage tests (binding)
- Facilities must run explicit leakage tests: where funds can be routed into claims, fees, collateral loops, or speculative positions.
- Under detected leakage or opacity, conservatism applies: tighten eligibility, cap amounts, or suspend the channel (fail-closed).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Public facilities must publish aggregated programme tables: volumes, purposes, outcomes (ΔG/L/RB proxies), and exception registers
  with sunset discipline.

## B05.CH06.P08.S02 — Commercial banks: permitted within rails; prohibited fee-stacks, opaque securitisation, shadow conduits

Commercial banking can operate in an OBA order, but only within rails that prevent extraction and opacity from becoming the core model.

### Permitted within rails
- Standard lending with transparent pricing and bounded fees.
- Auditable securitisation only where full disclosure and risk allocation are clear and contestable.

### Prohibited patterns (binding)
- **Fee-stacks**: junk fees, penalty cascades, forced add-ons, and layered servicing charges as primary profit mechanisms.
- **Opaque securitisation**: structures that break traceability of underlying terms, servicing practices, and fee decomposition.
- **Shadow conduits**: off-balance-sheet vehicles and intermediation chains that exist primarily to evade caps, disclosure, or risk
  rules.
- **Predatory enforcement**: collections models that profit from distress rather than restoring solvency.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Where product economics rely on opacity, fee-stacks, or distress extraction, the activity is requalified as E and must be redesigned,
  restricted, or prohibited.

## B05.CH06.P08.S03 — Payment rails: tagging, dispute flow, fraud controls without surveillance rent

Payment rails are the operational substrate for credit and enforcement. If the rail becomes a profiling machine or a selective veto
layer, lifeworld clamp follows.

### Required functions (binding)
- **Tagging support**: enable purpose tagging and traceability where needed for allowed channels, without turning all transactions into
  behavioural surveillance.
- **Dispute flow**: fast objection procedures for erroneous debits, fraud, merchant abuse, and collections actions; time-bounded
  resolution and reversible states where appropriate.
- **Fraud controls**: robust security and anomaly detection that is proportional and contestable.

### Prohibited drift (binding)
- **Surveillance rent**: monetising behavioural data, profiling for steering, or expanding data collection beyond order necessity.
- **Selective exclusion**: account lockouts or payment denial without due process, especially where essentials access depends on the rail.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Rails must publish incident statistics (aggregated): downtime, fraud rates, dispute volumes, resolution times, lockout events.

---

## B05.CH06.P08.S04 — Governance: audit of the rail operator, no selective enforcement, public incident logs

Because rail operators wield block power, their governance must be designed as anti-capture infrastructure.

### Governance minima (binding)
- **Audit-of-the-auditor**: independent audits of rail operator decisions, models, and procurement; rotation and peer review.
- **No selective enforcement**: uniform rule application; exception handling must be logged, published (at least in aggregate), and
  sunset-bounded.
- **Public incident logs**: operational incidents, security events, major rule changes, and systemic errors must be recorded and
  disclosed in a reproducible way (privacy-safe aggregation).

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven selective enforcement, unverifiable black-box decisioning, or persistent incident concealment triggers conformance
  suspension and mandatory governance reset (fail-closed against rail capture).
