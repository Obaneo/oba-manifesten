# B05.CH15.P06 — Housing and maintenance (after): less asset stress, more quality security

This paragraph specifies the **post-OBA housing and maintenance** state for the middle-household case. The goal is not to “make housing
an investment again”, but to reduce **asset stress** (rate-reset volatility, refinancing traps, maintenance spikes) while increasing
**quality security** (stable, healthy housing; predictable costs; contestable service pathways). The output is a set of enforceable rails
and measurable deltas linked to baseline frictions (P03) and the case dashboard (P11).

---

<a id="b05ch15p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P06.S01 — Mortgage without a bubble: demand stimulus banned; focus on supply/renovation/commons options

OBA treats housing inflation driven by demand stimulus as a destabilizing transfer mechanism. In the “after” case, the housing regime
shifts away from asset-price support and toward supply, renovation capacity, and commons/public options.

### Post-OBA housing regime (minimum rails)
- `no_demand_stimulus_for_prices`:
  - rules that prohibit policies primarily designed to raise house prices (asset-support bias),
  - separation between household security support and asset-price support.
- `supply_and_renovation_focus`:
  - capacity build for construction and renovation (commons procurement, workforce pipelines),
  - streamlined but contestable permitting with anti-capture rails.
- `commons_housing_options`:
  - expanded public/commons rental and cooperative options as real exits from private scarcity pricing,
  - coverage and access-time bands (operational, not symbolic).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The “after” case **MUST** represent housing support as **cost/quality security**, not price appreciation.
- If commons options are invoked, they **MUST** be operationally evidenced (coverage + time-to-access bands).
- Supply/renovation measures **MUST** include anti-capture constraints (transparent procurement, audit trails, contestability).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-HOU-A1 (No asset-support framing):** the regime avoids price-pumping instruments as the primary “solution”.
- **DL15-HOU-A2 (Operational commons option):** commons housing includes access timelines and eligibility clarity.
- **DL15-HOU-A3 (Supply capacity evidence):** renovation/supply capacity is tied to an operational mechanism (program/rails), not slogans.

<a id="b05ch15p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH15.P06.S02 — Maintenance regime: renovation/insulation via commons capacity; fewer unexpected cost spikes

Maintenance spikes are a major middle-household tail driver. Post-OBA, the regime shifts from ad-hoc crisis spending to predictable
maintenance capacity and standardized pathways.

### Post-OBA maintenance rails (minimum set)
- `predictable_maintenance_pathway`:
  - standardized maintenance/repair pathways with transparent pricing,
  - priority rules for safety-critical repairs.
- `commons_renovation_capacity`:
  - insulation/energy-efficiency upgrades via commons capacity (procurement + certified workforce),
  - reduced reliance on “one-off contractor scarcity pricing”.
- `spike_smoothing_mechanisms`:
  - repair reserve mechanisms or amortized upgrade finance with bounded terms,
  - prohibition of punitive fee stacks in repair financing channels (no cascade products).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The “after” case **MUST** reduce maintenance spike exposure via either (a) reduced spike frequency/severity, or (b) smoothing rails that
  prevent cascades.
- Maintenance pathways **MUST** be contestable and auditable (price transparency, complaint/appeal).
- Where financing exists, it **MUST NOT** reintroduce fee cascades or predatory rollover structures.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-MNT-01 (Spike delta):** maintenance spike volatility proxies decline (frequency/severity bands) or are neutralized by smoothing.
- **DL15-MNT-02 (Transparency):** maintenance pricing/terms are itemized and contestable.
- **DL15-MNT-03 (No cascade finance):** financing terms show no fee-stack cascade surfaces.

## B05.CH15.P06.S03 — Moving: lower switching costs; transparent contracts; better work/home matching

Mobility between homes is essential for work matching and family stability, but moving is typically high-friction. Post-OBA, moving must
be feasible without punitive switching costs and opaque contract traps.

### Post-OBA moving rails (minimum set)
- `switching_cost_reduction`:
  - reduced transaction fees and administrative steps,
  - bounded deposit rules and fast deposit return pathways (time-bounded disputes).
- `transparent_contract_templates`:
  - standardized, plain-language lease/mortgage servicing clauses,
  - prohibition of hidden fees and retaliatory blacklisting for lawful switching/disputes.
- `coordination_support`:
  - alignment support for childcare/school transitions and address/identity updates,
  - one-stop interface for moving-related administrative updates.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- A “moving improvement” claim **MUST** be backed by switching cost deltas (cash/time) and reduced coordination steps.
- Contract transparency **MUST** include itemized fees and enforceable dispute-safe rules.
- The regime **MUST** reduce blacklisting and retaliation risk as a block-power surface.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-MOV-01 (Switching delta):** moving cash/time bands decline or are supported by concrete rails that neutralize the barrier.
- **DL15-MOV-02 (Deposit/dispute speed):** deposit return and dispute timelines are shortened or protected by provisional relief/freeze.
- **DL15-MOV-03 (No retaliation):** blacklisting/retaliation protections exist and are enforceable.

## B05.CH15.P06.S04 — Measurable effect: housing burden down, arrears down, dispute times down

The housing “after” state must produce measurable deltas in the dashboard. This section defines the minimum measurable effects required
to claim that housing normality improved.

### Required measurable outputs (minimum)
- `housing_burden_share_pre/post` (share of N consumed by housing + housing-linked utilities; bands acceptable)
- `housing_cost_volatility_pre/post`:
  - rate-reset exposure markers,
  - maintenance spike frequency/severity bands,
  - surprise fee incidence.
- `arrears_or_late_payment_pre/post` (incidence band)
- `dispute_duration_pre/post` for:
  - mortgage servicing issues (if applicable),
  - landlord/lease disputes,
  - maintenance/repair disputes.
- `switching_cost_moving_pre/post` (cash/time bands)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The “after” case **MUST** reduce at least one of: housing burden share, volatility proxies, arrears incidence, or dispute durations,
  without worsening any tail indicators (FAIL-CLOSED applies).
- If a metric does not improve, the case **MUST** disclose why and whether the residual friction triggers redesign (link P13/P14).
- Outputs **MUST** carry method provenance and uncertainty bands (link P11).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-HOU-M1 (Delta presence):** pre/post values exist for burden, volatility proxies, arrears, and dispute durations.
- **DL15-HOU-M2 (No tail regression):** arrears or threat timelines do not worsen post.
- **DL15-HOU-M3 (Provenance):** metrics reference `method_id` and uncertainty bands via the dashboard.
