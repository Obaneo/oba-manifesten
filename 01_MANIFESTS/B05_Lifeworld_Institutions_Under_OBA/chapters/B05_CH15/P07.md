# B05.CH15.P07 — Work and time (after): less schedule capture, more agency

This paragraph specifies the **post-OBA work and time** state for the middle-household case. The aim is not to prescribe lifestyle or
reduce work effort by decree; it is to reduce **schedule capture** and **time volatility** so that work, care, participation, and basic
administration can coexist without chronic coordination failure. Post-OBA, work quality shifts through rails: higher predictability,
portability of rights, and a reduced status race under caps—while actively monitoring for new non-monetary gatekeeping.

---

<a id="b05ch15p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P07.S01 — Workweek/schedules: higher predictability; room for care and participation

Schedule predictability is a primary time-security output. The “after” case must show reduced last-minute changes, reduced coercive
availability expectations, and improved care compatibility.

### Post-OBA schedule rails (minimum set)
- `notice_minimums`:
  - minimum notice time rules for shifts and changes.
- `cancellation_compensation`:
  - compensation when shifts are cancelled or reduced on short notice.
- `refusal_protection`:
  - constraints on punitive refusal (no rating penalties, blacklisting, or hidden sanctions).
- `care_compatibility_support`:
  - predictable windows or accommodations for childcare/school constraints,
  - portable leave/care rights linkage (see P05).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The “after” case **MUST** improve at least one schedule predictability proxy (notice time, change frequency, cancellation rate) or
  provide compensating rails that neutralize the harm (e.g., cancellation pay).
- Schedule rails **MUST** be enforceable (complaint path + time-bounded correction); “guidelines” are non-conform.
- Care compatibility **MUST** be treated as a structural constraint and must be expressed via measurable proxies (hours gap, penalty
  incidence, forced unpaid time).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-SCH-A1 (Predictability delta):** notice_time_band increases and/or last_minute_change_band decreases, or compensation rail exists.
- **DL15-SCH-A2 (No punitive refusal):** refusal protection is declared and evidenced by rule + remedy path.
- **DL15-SCH-A3 (Care proxy):** at least one care-compatibility proxy improves or is supported by a rail.

<a id="b05ch15p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH15.P07.S02 — Career path: less rat race via caps; focus on craft and contribution

Caps reduce the marginal payoff of winner-take-all status competition. In the middle-household lifeworld, the key effect is not “everyone
stops competing”, but that career progression becomes less dominated by positional escalation and more aligned with competence and
contribution.

### Post-OBA career alignment mechanisms (minimum set)
- `bounded_compensation_escalation`:
  - reduction of extreme status payoffs that drive unhealthy escalation incentives.
- `competence_signalling`:
  - recognition tied to demonstrable skill, reliability, and workmanship.
- `contribution_signalling`:
  - prestige and progression tied to maintaining commons infrastructure and continuity outputs (ΔG as domain contribution).
- `procedural_certification` (when credentials exist):
  - criteria that are testable, contestable, and non-exclusionary by default.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Career “alignment” claims **MUST NOT** rely on moral language; they must reference concrete institutional signals (criteria, pathways,
  portability).
- Where credentials or progression gates exist, they **MUST** be procedurally testable and contestable (appeal paths + transparency).
- The system **MUST** monitor for migration of status competition into non-monetary gatekeeping (see S04).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-CAR-01 (Signal grounding):** career-path claims reference criteria or pathway rules, not narrative impressions.
- **DL15-CAR-02 (Contestability):** credential/progression gates include appeal paths and disclosure of criteria.
- **DL15-CAR-03 (Portability):** skill/credential portability exists where relevant (no closed-institution monopoly on recognition).

<a id="b05ch15p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH15.P07.S03 — Overtime/bonus: less incentive toward burnout; income spikes dampened via security/buffers

Middle-household time poverty is often driven by the interaction of overtime culture, variable bonus dependence, and insecurity. Post-OBA,
the goal is to reduce the need for burnout-driven spikes by stabilizing essentials and providing buffers.

### Post-OBA mechanisms (minimum set)
- `variable_income_dampening`:
  - reduced dependence on overtime/bonuses to cover essentials due to lower L volatility (see P05/P06/P08/P09/P10).
- `buffer_substitution`:
  - emergency buffers and fallback commons reduce reliance on overtime to self-insure.
- `overtime_norm_rails`:
  - rules limiting coercive overtime expectations and protecting refusal without retaliation.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The “after” case **MUST** show that reduced overtime/bonus dependence is a structural outcome (L volatility down + buffers up), not a
  cultural sermon.
- If overtime remains common, the case **MUST** state whether it is voluntary and non-coercive, with measurable proxies.
- Income spike dampening **MUST** not create hidden losses; it must be accompanied by stability gains (R/B and time security).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-OT-01 (Dependence delta):** share of N dependent on overtime/bonus declines, or L volatility declines enough to neutralize need.
- **DL15-OT-02 (Coercion check):** refusal penalties are absent or contestable with time bounds.
- **DL15-OT-03 (Stability coupling):** any reduction in spikes is paired with improved stability metrics (R/B, arrears incidence, recovery).

## B05.CH15.P07.S04 — Failure mode: new status barriers (credentials/networks) → detection via drop-off metrics

Under caps and stabilized essentials, status competition can migrate into non-monetary arenas: credentials, networks, invitation-only
paths, and platform visibility. This is a predictable failure mode and must be monitored.

### High-risk status-barrier arenas (minimum set)
- `credential_gates` (licenses, micro-credentials, “elite programmes”)
- `network_access` (boards, closed referral systems, invitation-only jobs)
- `platform_visibility` (ranking systems, verified badges, algorithmic promotion)
- `institutional_curation` (who defines “merit” and controls progression narratives)

### Detection requirements (minimum)
- `drop_off_metrics[]` (aggregated):
  - application-to-acceptance conversion by cohort proxy,
  - credential inflation rate (rising required badges),
  - appeal/complaint rates for gate decisions,
  - concentration metrics for awarding bodies or gatekeepers,
  - portability success rates (credential transfer / recognition).
- `audit_triggers`:
  - thresholds for anomaly clusters (e.g., sudden drop in conversion, rising credential requirements without performance justification).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Status-barrier monitoring **MUST** be privacy-preserving and aggregated; it **MUST NOT** become behavioural scoring.
- When drop-off anomalies trigger, the system **MUST** route into audit and remedy rails: transparency, appeal rights, portability, and
  where necessary public option/procurement restrictions.
- If gatekeeping cannot be made contestable, the relevant credential/network mechanism is **NON-CONFORM** as an E-like toll surface.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-STAT-01 (Drop-off instrumentation):** at least two drop-off metrics are defined and reported in aggregated form.
- **DL15-STAT-02 (Trigger-to-audit):** anomaly triggers produce an audit start within a declared time bound.
- **DL15-STAT-03 (Remedy ladder):** remedies are logged (criteria disclosure, appeal, portability, contract rewrite, or replacement).

---

### Output format (work/time summary)

At minimum, the post-case **MUST** emit a work/time summary (table or structured list) with:

- `proxy` | `pre_value_band` | `post_value_band` | `delta` | `method_id_ref` | `uncertainty_band` | `rail_link`

Where proxies include: schedule notice time, last-minute change frequency, administrative time, commute variance (if work travel is
material), overtime/bonus dependence, and refusal penalty incidence.
