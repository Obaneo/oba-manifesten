# B05.CH15.P14 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies **failure modes** and **hard stops** for the middle-household Day-in-the-Life case. CH15 is vulnerable to a
specific kind of washing: the system can claim “better normality” while households experience no visible reduction in L volatility or
time capture. CH15 therefore functions as an **early-warning system**: it detects cosmetic gains, new gatekeeping in public services,
selective execution by locality, and regressions in volatility/tails. These are **conformance breakers** with FAIL-CLOSED behaviour.

---

<a id="b05ch15p14s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P14.S01 — Cosmetic gains: higher net but unchanged L/lock-in → legitimacy loss

Cosmetic gains occur when N rises (or taxes shift) but **L**, lock-ins, and time capture remain effectively unchanged. The household then
experiences “nothing improved”, which is a legitimacy failure and a signal that the rails are mis-specified or captured.

### Cosmetic gain indicators (minimum set)
- `N_up_L_flat`:
  - N increases but L share and L volatility do not materially decline (within uncertainty).
- `R_up_time_flat`:
  - R increases but time proxies (admin time, schedule predictability, commute variance) do not improve.
- `lock_in_unchanged`:
  - switching costs, penalty incidence, and wait-time gates remain materially unchanged.
- `opacity_persists`:
  - fee stacks remain non-itemized; dispute outcomes remain slow/opaque.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- If cosmetic indicators trigger, the case **MUST** be treated as a legitimacy loss and must route into redesign (rails targeting L
  volatility, lock-ins, and time capture), not narrative explanation.
- “Success” claims **MUST NOT** be based solely on higher N; CH15 conformance requires structural friction reduction.
- Dashboard outputs (P11) **MUST** be used to adjudicate cosmetic gains; if the dashboard is missing, the case is NON-CONFORM.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-COS-01 (L delta check):** if L share/volatility deltas are within uncertainty (no visible improvement), cosmetic gain is flagged.
- **DL15-COS-02 (Time delta check):** if time proxies show no improvement, cosmetic gain is flagged.
- **DL15-COS-03 (Lock-in delta check):** if switching/wait-time/penalty proxies show no improvement, cosmetic gain is flagged.

<a id="b05ch15p14s02"></a>
## B05.CH15.P14.S02 — New lock-in: public services with digital exclusion or bureaucratic toll → NON-CONFORM

A core failure mode is the creation of new block power inside public systems: digital-only access, heavy proof burdens, denial-by-friction,
opaque criteria, or punitive enforcement. OBA treats this as a conformance failure, not a “trade-off”.

### New lock-in indicators (minimum set)
- `digital_exclusion` (no offline/assisted path; identity lockout blocks access)
- `bureaucratic_toll` (steps/time burden rises; repeated verification loops)
- `opaque_criteria` (non-explainable decisions; discretionary gatekeeping)
- `punitive_enforcement` (fast sanctions, slow correction; no provisional relief)
- `surveillance_drift` (disproportionate monitoring of certain households)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- If new lock-in indicators are present, the system is **NON-CONFORM** unless remedied via:
  - open criteria + appeal,
  - reduced proof burden,
  - identity recovery and portability,
  - and dispute-safe enforcement constraints.
- Public services **MUST NOT** use behavioural scoring or health/stress signals for eligibility/enforcement.
- Any automation affecting access **MUST** be contestable with logged reasons and time-bounded correction (link P12).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-NLI-01 (Access paths):** offline/assisted access exists for critical services; absence → NON-CONFORM.
- **DL15-NLI-02 (Admin toll delta):** admin steps/time must not increase post; increase without mitigation → NON-CONFORM.
- **DL15-NLI-03 (Provisional relief):** where harm risk exists, provisional relief exists; absence → NON-CONFORM.

<a id="b05ch15p14s03"></a>
## B05.CH15.P14.S03 — Selective execution: local postcode differences without accountability → contestability breach

Selective execution occurs when outcomes depend on locality/provider discretion without transparency, producing unequal rights and
postcode lotteries. This breaches contestability and undermines claims of “shared rails”.

### Selective execution indicators (minimum set)
Material variance across localities/providers in:
- wait times (childcare, healthcare pathways),
- transport reliability and coverage,
- dispute decision times and reversal rates,
- proof burden and verification loops,
- enforcement aggressiveness (penalties, service cutoffs, administrative sanctions),
- access coverage to public options.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The system **MUST** publish aggregated comparative metrics across localities/providers for key dashboard indicators (P11).
- Differences **MUST** be explainable by declared parameters (capacity constraints, transition phases) and must have remediation plans.
- Unexplained variance beyond tolerance **MUST** trigger audit, capacity injection, contract rewrite, or public option expansion.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-SEL-01 (Comparative publication):** comparative metrics exist for at least two domains (care, mobility, disputes).
- **DL15-SEL-02 (Rule/version control):** rule/method versions are consistent or differences are declared and justified.
- **DL15-SEL-03 (Variance trigger):** variance beyond tolerance routes into mandatory remedy actions.

<a id="b05ch15p14s04"></a>
## B05.CH15.P14.S04 — FAIL-CLOSED: if “after” worsens volatility or tails (more arrears, more time poverty), redesign is mandatory

The hard stop is outcome-based: if “after” worsens volatility or tail indicators, OBA does not accept narrative justification. It triggers
mandatory redesign.

### Tail/volatility regression conditions (hard stop)
A FAIL-CLOSED trigger occurs if, post intervention:
- `L_volatility_proxies` worsen (more reset exposure, more surprise charges, higher penalty incidence), or
- `arrears_or_late_payments` increase in housing/energy/childcare, or
- `time_poverty_proxies` worsen (admin time rises, schedule predictability worsens, commute variance worsens), or
- `dispute_duration` increases without compensating provisional relief, or
- `fallback_commons_use` increases due to system failure rather than intended crisis coverage, or
- uncertainty bands include meaningful worsening and no external-scope cause is explicitly documented.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- If a regression trigger occurs, the system **MUST** treat the intervention set as insufficient and **MUST** redesign the responsible
  rail(s).
- Redesign **MUST** be traceable: which friction surface caused regression, which rail is changed, and how the change will be re-tested.
- The system **MUST NOT** claim conformance on an “after” state that fails tail/volatility tests, even if some averages improve.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-FC-01 (Regression gate):** any regression condition → FAIL-CLOSED (no conformance claim).
- **DL15-FC-02 (Attribution):** regression is attributed to a friction surface (P03) and a redesign action is logged.
- **DL15-FC-03 (Re-test):** redesigned rails trigger re-run of dashboard deltas and risk events (P04/P11).

---

### Output format (early-warning register)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit an early-warning register (table or structured list) with:

- `failure_mode` | `trigger_indicator` | `threshold_or_condition` | `detected` | `impact_surface` | `required_action` | `status`

Where required_action ∈ {disqualify_case, launch_audit, contract_rewrite, capacity_injection, expand_public_option, rollback, redesign_rail,
publish_variance_report}.
