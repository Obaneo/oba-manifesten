# B05.CH15.P12 — Dispute-flow and exceptions: how the middle household corrects system errors

This paragraph specifies the **dispute-flow and exception handling** rails for Day-in-the-Life Case II (middle household). Middle
households often “absorb” errors quietly—lost time, administrative loops, and friction—until a shock turns the friction into a tail
event. OBA therefore requires **contestability**, **time-bounded correction**, **provisional relief**, and **transparent logs**, plus hard
stops against privacy overreach. These rails are part of conformance: if the “after” improvements depend on perfect execution, the case
is non-conform.

---

<a id="b05ch15p12s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P12.S01 — Wrong decision: fast correction + provisional relief + restatement

Wrong decisions include incorrect eligibility outcomes, miscomputed B/T/N/L/R, wrongful fees or penalties, incorrect claim outcomes, and
erroneous enforcement actions. The rail is: *fast correction beats slow apology*.

### Required dispute objects (template)
- `decision_id` (opaque identifier; privacy-safe)
- `decision_type` ∈ {eligibility, tariff, claim, childcare_placement, education_support, mobility_penalty, platform_fee, identity_lock,
  mortgage_servicing, other}
- `error_class` ∈ {wrong_input, wrong_rule, missing_evidence, identity_failure, clerical, system_bug, provider_misreporting}
- `restatement_required` (true/false)
- `provisional_relief_required` (true/false)
- `time_bounds`:
  - `t_ack` (time to acknowledge),
  - `t_relief` (time to grant provisional relief, if required),
  - `t_fix` (time to correct),
  - `t_final` (time to finalize and communicate)
- `log_refs[]` (audit log references; redacted allowed)
- `calculation_trace_ref` (link to `method_id` + rule version used)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- A disputable decision **MUST** have an accessible initiation path (online/offline) with a timestamped receipt (`t_ack`) and tracking.
- If the decision can trigger irreversible harm or cascading costs (eviction/disconnection/garnishment/identity lockout/major penalty
  escalation), **provisional relief MUST** be granted within `t_relief` while the dispute is pending.
- When the dispute concerns B/T/N/L/R or a computed fee/take-rate, the system **MUST** produce a **restatement**: re-run calculation with
  explicit inputs, rule version, and delta explanation.
- If time bounds are missed, the system **MUST** trigger protective defaults (freeze enforcement, auto-escalation, interim approval per
  policy).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-DSP-01 (Receipt):** dispute initiation yields a timestamped receipt and trackable `decision_id`.
- **DL15-DSP-02 (Relief):** where harm risk exists, provisional relief is granted within time bounds.
- **DL15-DSP-03 (Restatement):** corrections include a calculation restatement tied to `method_id` and rule version.
- **DL15-DSP-04 (Deadline enforcement):** missed deadlines trigger an automatic protective action.

## B05.CH15.P12.S02 — Provider abuse: anomaly → audit → contract remedies

Provider abuse includes coercive or retaliatory practices, illegal/opaque fees, discriminatory gatekeeping, denial-by-friction, and
systematic exploitation of time/identity fragility. The rail is: *anomaly triggers audit; audit can rewrite the contract surface*.

### Required abuse signal protocol (template)
- `abuse_signal_id`
- `provider_class` ∈ {landlord, mortgage_servicer, childcare_provider, school_admin, healthcare_provider, insurer, telecom, platform,
  transport_operator, parking_toll_operator, intermediary, public_body, other}
- `abuse_type[]` ∈ {illegal_fee, opaque_surcharge, retaliation, intimidation, discriminatory_gate, denial_by_friction, wrongful_penalty,
  deceptive_terms, abusive_claim_delay, other}
- `anomaly_trigger` ∈ {complaint_cluster, outlier_fee_rate, outlier_denial_rate, dispute_win_rate_spike, audit_sampling_hit, whistle_signal}
- `evidence_class[]` (contract, invoice, notice, portal logs, call logs; redacted allowed)
- `immediate_protection_required` (true/false)
- `audit_path`:
  - `audit_start_time_bound`,
  - `audit_scope` (fees, criteria, enforcement practices, logs),
  - `audit_output` ∈ {no_issue, remediation_order, contract_rewrite_required, sanction_required}
- `remedy_actions[]` (see remedy ladder)

### Remedy ladder (ordered)
1) `remediation_order` (remove fee item, correct practice, refund/credit)
2) `contract_rewrite` (mandatory template terms, removal of clauses, transparency/portability requirements)
3) `sanction` (fines, license constraints, exclusion from procurement/market access)
4) `replacement` (public option expansion / contract termination where contestability cannot be restored)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Abuse signals **MUST** have a protected reporting path and **MUST NOT** trigger retaliation.
- When an anomaly trigger fires, an audit **MUST** begin within `audit_start_time_bound`.
- If abuse is confirmed, remedies **MUST** follow the ladder and be evidenced (what changed + when + why).
- Audit outcomes **MUST** be contestable, but **MUST NOT** delay immediate protection where harm risk exists.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-ABU-01 (Trigger-to-audit):** anomaly trigger starts audit within time bound.
- **DL15-ABU-02 (Evidence linkage):** abuse claims reference evidence classes; “common knowledge” is non-conform.
- **DL15-ABU-03 (Remedy trace):** remedy actions are logged with deltas (fee removal, clause rewrite, sanction).

## B05.CH15.P12.S03 — Privacy overreach: hard stop and rollback; public incident log

System abuse includes privacy breaches, surveillance drift, prohibited scoring, and data collection expansion beyond necessity. The rail is
**HARD STOP and rollback**, not “we will improve”.

### Prohibited actions (non-exhaustive)
- Using health or stress signals as eligibility gates, behavioural scores, or enforcement triggers.
- Collecting fine-grained behavioural data when coarse evidence classes suffice.
- Sharing household-level data across systems without necessity, purpose limitation, and contestability.
- Black-box automated denials without explainability and appeal.

### Hard stop protocol (template)
- `incident_id`
- `incident_type` ∈ {privacy_breach, overcollection, prohibited_scoring, unauthorized_sharing, black_box_denial, logging_failure}
- `detection_source` ∈ {audit, complaint, monitoring, breach_report, whistle}
- `containment_action` (stop, isolation, access revocation, key rotation)
- `rollback_scope` (rule version, pipeline, access control, data retention changes)
- `public_incident_log_entry`:
  - aggregated description,
  - impact scope category,
  - mitigation and preventive control added (no personal data)
- `postmortem_required` (true/false)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Privacy breaches and overreach **MUST** trigger immediate containment and **MUST** suspend affected automated decisions until corrected.
- The system **MUST** support rollback to a prior known-good configuration (rules + pipelines + access control) and must log the rollback.
- A public incident log **MUST** exist in aggregated form; secrecy is a legitimacy breach unless narrowly justified by security.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-PRV-01 (Containment):** incident triggers containment within defined time bounds.
- **DL15-PRV-02 (Rollback):** rollback can be executed and is logged (rule version + effect).
- **DL15-PRV-03 (Public log):** a public (aggregated) incident log entry exists for each qualifying incident.

<a id="b05ch15p12s04"></a>
## B05.CH15.P12.S04 — Protection: no retaliation; transparent logs and appeals stats

Contestability is meaningless if households fear retaliation or cannot survive the process. Protection rails are part of OBA conformance.

### Protection requirements (minimum set)
- `no_retaliation`:
  - prohibited retaliation by providers/public bodies for disputes, switching, or reporting,
  - enforceable penalties and protected channels.
- `humane_procedure`:
  - clear language, accessible channels, reasonable deadlines,
  - assisted/offline options,
  - non-punitive defaults when proof is missing due to system fault.
- `transparent_logs`:
  - user-visible status tracking,
  - audit logs with timestamps, rule versions, and action trails.
- `appeals_stats_publication` (aggregated):
  - appeal volumes, decision times, reversal rates, error classes (privacy-safe).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The system **MUST** provide enforceable no-retaliation guarantees with sanctions and protected reporting.
- Logs **MUST** exist at two levels:
  - **user view** (status, reasons, next steps),
  - **audit view** (rule versions, evidence classes, action trail).
- Appeals statistics **MUST** be published in aggregated form to detect selective execution and systematic error.
- Any enforcement action (eviction/disconnection/garnishment/deactivation/major penalties) **MUST** include a contestable reason and a
  time window for challenge, unless an explicit emergency rule applies (separately governed).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-PRT-01 (Retaliation guard):** retaliation prohibition + sanction mechanism + reporting channel exist.
- **DL15-PRT-02 (Two-level logging):** user view + audit view logs exist and are referenced.
- **DL15-PRT-03 (Appeals stats):** aggregated appeals stats exist and are linkable to time bounds and reversal rates.

---

### Output format (dispute-flow dashboard)

At minimum, the case **MUST** emit a dispute-flow dashboard (table or structured list) with:

- `issue_type` | `harm_risk` | `provisional_relief` | `t_ack` | `t_relief` | `t_fix` | `t_final` | `deadline_enforcement` | `log_ref`

If dispute handling cannot be evidenced and time-bounded, the “after” case is non-conform (execution dependency without rails).
