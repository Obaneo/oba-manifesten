# B05.CH15.P05 — OBA intervention set (after): how “normality” shifts

This paragraph specifies the **OBA intervention set** used to generate the **post-OBA (“after”)** middle-household case. The intervention
set is not a wishlist; it is a **rails package** that must produce measurable changes in (i) **L level and L volatility**, (ii) **time
security** (predictability, lower admin-time burden, lower commute variance where applicable), and (iii) **shock recovery** (faster
procedures, fewer tail cascades). The output is an auditable list of interventions with linkage to baseline frictions (P03), risk events
(P04), and the case dashboard (P11).

---

<a id="b05ch15p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P05.S01 — Essentials baseline: price rails + public options; less volatility in L

The middle-household “normality shift” begins with **volatility reduction** in recurring essentials: predictable prices, fewer penalty
surfaces, and real public/commons options when markets remain scarcity-bound or non-contestable.

### Intervention components (minimum set)
- `price_rails_essentials`:
  - transparent itemized tariffs for energy and core utilities,
  - bounded renewal/indexation rules for essential contracts,
  - limits on penalty fees and cancellation traps that function as tolls.
- `public_options_essentials`:
  - operational public/commons options where contestability fails (energy baseline, childcare baseline, digital baseline, mobility
    baseline as applicable),
  - coverage + access timelines (not “exists in law”).
- `fee_stack_reduction`:
  - prohibition/caps for non-optional intermediary fees in essentials,
  - unbundling and plain-language disclosure requirements where bundles hide toll layers.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Each baseline/rail element **MUST** map to a baseline friction surface (P03) and an L volatility driver (reset/indexation/penalty).
- Public options **MUST** be operationally evidenced (coverage + time-to-access bands) and **MUST** include dispute-safe protection.
- Fee-stack reduction **MUST** be expressed as concrete prohibitions/caps with evidence requirements (what must disappear from bills and
  contracts).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-EB-01 (Volatility delta):** post-case shows reduced L volatility proxies (reset exposure, penalty incidence, surprise charges).
- **DL15-EB-02 (Operational public option):** coverage + time-to-access bands exist where public option is claimed.
- **DL15-EB-03 (Bill/contract delta):** item-level fee/penalty removals or caps are visible in post-case evidence classes.

<a id="b05ch15p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH15.P05.S02 — Time security: schedule rails, portability of leave/care rights, lower administrative toll

Time security is treated as a structural output. The intervention set must reduce schedule capture, preserve care compatibility, and
collapse administrative time as an extraction surface.

### Intervention components (minimum set)
- `schedule_rails`:
  - minimum notice rules,
  - cancellation compensation,
  - constraints on punitive refusal/availability expectations.
- `portability_of_rights`:
  - portable leave/care rights across employers/insurers/public bodies,
  - continuity during transitions (no “reset” of entitlements due to job change).
- `admin_toll_reduction`:
  - one-stop interface for benefits/claims/disputes/transitions,
  - fewer verification loops (anomaly-triggered only),
  - time-bounded decisions with deadline enforcement (freeze/escalation/auto-protection).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Time security interventions **MUST** be measured via explicit proxies (schedule notice, admin steps/time, dispute durations).
- Portability **MUST** include identity recovery and continuity (no lockout of rights due to authentication failure).
- Deadline enforcement **MUST** be a rule with protective action, not a performance target.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-TS-01 (Predictability delta):** schedule notice improves and/or cancellation compensation exists with enforcement.
- **DL15-TS-02 (Admin-time delta):** admin steps/time bands decline for at least two core processes (claims/disputes/switching).
- **DL15-TS-03 (Portability continuity):** a rights portability pathway exists and is evidenced (rule + procedure).

<a id="b05ch15p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH15.P05.S03 — Security/buffers: better shock absorption via insurance and fallback commons; faster procedures

Middle-household security depends less on emergency credit and more on the quality and speed of insurance/claims systems and fallback
commons under large shocks. The intervention set must reduce pathway friction and tail extension.

### Intervention components (minimum set)
- `insurance_and_claims_rails`:
  - standardized claim pathways and evidence classes,
  - time-bounded claim decisions,
  - prohibition of adversarial delay as a cost strategy (paperwork as toll).
- `fallback_commons`:
  - continuity of essentials and temporary relief during severe events,
  - suspension of punitive enforcement during contested periods and crisis windows.
- `fast_procedures`:
  - rapid triage lanes for high-impact events,
  - provisional relief for irreversibility risks (eviction, disconnection, account lockout, enforcement actions).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Security mechanisms **MUST** be faster than the baseline tail timeline; slow relief is non-functional.
- Claim/dispute pathways **MUST** be measurable (decision times, appeal rates, error correction times).
- Fallback commons **MUST** be operationally evidenced (coverage + time-to-access bands) and bounded to avoid overreach.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-SB-01 (Speed delta):** claim/dispute decision times decline (bands acceptable) or provisional relief neutralizes tail.
- **DL15-SB-02 (Paperwork toll reduction):** verification loops decline or are limited to anomaly triggers.
- **DL15-SB-03 (Fallback operationality):** fallback commons has coverage + access-time bands and an activation rule.

<a id="b05ch15p05s04"></a>
## B05.CH15.P05.S04 — Work transition: reskilling rails and mobility support without a precarious dip

A core middle-household risk is a forced work transition. OBA must make transitions survivable without pushing the household into
precarity through time capture, proof burdens, or delayed support.

### Intervention components (minimum set)
- `reskilling_rails`:
  - fast eligibility decisions,
  - low proof burden (standard evidence classes),
  - support continuity during training (no income cliff).
- `mobility_support`:
  - commute and relocation support where job matching requires it,
  - reduced switching costs for housing and childcare reconfiguration.
- `transition_continuity`:
  - portability of leave/care rights and benefits across employers,
  - guaranteed dispute-safe status while decisions are pending.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Reskilling access **MUST** be time-bounded; long wait times recreate the very dip OBA claims to prevent.
- Mobility support **MUST** be expressed as concrete mechanisms (cost/time reduction, public transport support, switching support), not
  general aspirations.
- Transition continuity **MUST** include provisional relief when enforcement would create irreversible harm.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-WTR-01 (Time-to-entry):** time to reskilling entry/approval is bounded and lower than baseline pathway friction bands.
- **DL15-WTR-02 (No cliff):** the transition does not create a net income cliff (N) beyond declared tolerance, or compensating buffer
  rails are evidenced.
- **DL15-WTR-03 (Mobility feasibility):** relocation/commute adjustment is feasible under cash/time constraints (bands declared).

---

### Output format (intervention set summary)

At minimum, the post-case **MUST** emit an intervention set summary (table or structured list) with:

- `intervention_id` | `target_domain` | `target_friction_surface` | `mechanism` | `expected_delta` | `evidence_required` | `time_bound`

If expected deltas are stated without measurement linkage (P11) and evidence requirements, the “after” becomes non-conform storytelling
under CH15 fail-closed rules.
