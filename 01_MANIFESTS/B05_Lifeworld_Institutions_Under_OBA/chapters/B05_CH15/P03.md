# B05.CH15.P03 — Friction map: where the middle household gets stuck (pre-OBA)

This paragraph specifies the **friction map** for the baseline (pre-OBA) middle-household case. The friction map is a structured
diagnosis of where “normal life” is consumed by **non-obvious constraints**: refinancing traps, wait-time lock-ins, schedule mismatches,
mobility dependence, subscription bundling, identity gating, and administrative toll. The output is not narrative; it is a
domain-by-domain inventory of friction mechanisms and measurable proxies that later enable before/after deltas.

---

<a id="b05ch15p03s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P03.S01 — Housing/mortgage: refinancing friction, rate-reset risk, moving friction, maintenance costs

Housing for the middle household often functions as a volatility amplifier: rate resets, refinancing friction, and maintenance spikes
convert “asset ownership” into recurring stress, time loss, and constrained mobility.

### Required fields (template)
- `housing_mode_pre` ∈ {mortgage_owner, private_rent, public_rent, mixed}
- `rate_reset_risk`:
  - `rate_type` ∈ {fixed, variable, hybrid}
  - `reset_frequency_band` (if variable/hybrid)
  - `payment_sensitivity_band` (Δ payment per rate move; coarse)
- `refinancing_friction`:
  - eligibility gates, documentation steps, fees, processing time band
- `moving_friction`:
  - cash/time switching costs, transaction fees, school/care disruption markers
- `maintenance_spike_exposure`:
  - typical spike categories (roof, heating, insulation, appliances),
  - spike frequency band and cost band (coarse)

### Friction mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Rate-reset exposure + slow renegotiation (T and L volatility).
- Refinancing as gatekeeping (proof burden, fees, delays).
- Moving as high switching cost (cash + time + coordination).
- Maintenance spikes as quasi-shocks (often financed via expensive credit channels).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** represent housing burden as both level and volatility drivers (rate resets, maintenance spikes).
- Refinancing and moving frictions **MUST** include measurable proxies (steps/time bands + fee bands).
- If housing risk is “low”, the case **MUST** justify that claim with declared proxies (e.g., fixed rate long horizon, low maintenance
  exposure, low switching need).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-HOUF-01 (Rate risk visibility):** rate type and reset exposure are declared (or explicitly N/A with rationale).
- **DL15-HOUF-02 (Switching costs):** moving friction includes cash + time bands.
- **DL15-HOUF-03 (Refi burden):** refinancing friction includes steps/time/fees proxies.

## B05.CH15.P03.S02 — Childcare/education: wait times, schedule mismatches, quality dispersion, administrative burden

Childcare and education are core time-security infrastructures. Friction here typically manifests as wait lists, mismatch between
operating hours and work schedules, quality dispersion that creates “pay-to-win” dynamics, and heavy administration.

### Required fields (template)
- `childcare_access_pre`:
  - availability status (secured / waitlist / partial),
  - `wait_time_band`,
  - `operating_hours_fit` (fit vs work schedule; categorical + rationale)
- `education_access_pre`:
  - catchment rules, admissions friction (if applicable),
  - quality dispersion markers (proxies; not subjective ratings as truth)
- `schedule_mismatch_markers`:
  - pickup/drop-off constraints, closure days, last-minute changes, penalty fees
- `admin_burden_pre`:
  - steps/time bands for applications, subsidies, billing, disputes
- `cost_volatility_pre`:
  - variable fees, penalties, seasonal/closure-induced extra costs

### Friction mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Wait-time lock-in: no alternative without private tolls.
- Schedule mismatch: creates commute compression and unpaid coordination load.
- Quality dispersion + scarcity: produces implicit “access markets” (tutoring, fee-based advantages).
- Administration as toll: forms, verification loops, and delayed decisions.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Wait times **MUST** be treated as a lock-in mechanism (time scarcity + forced dependence) and must be measured as bands.
- Schedule mismatch **MUST** be represented as a measurable proxy (hours gap band, penalty incidence, forced unpaid time).
- Quality dispersion claims **MUST** be grounded in observable proxies (e.g., incident reporting, staffing ratios, published outcomes),
  not informal reputation.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-CHD-01 (Wait time):** wait_time_band exists (or explicit “no wait time” with evidence class).
- **DL15-CHD-02 (Mismatch proxy):** at least one schedule mismatch proxy exists.
- **DL15-CHD-03 (Admin burden):** application/management steps/time bands exist.

## B05.CH15.P03.S03 — Mobility: car lock-in, fuel/parking, unreliable public transport, time loss

Mobility friction is a major time-security determinant. Middle households often appear “fine” financially while losing large time blocks
through unreliable travel, forced car dependence, and fee stacks (parking, tolls, insurance).

### Required fields (template)
- `commute_mode_pre` ∈ {car, public_transport, mixed, active_mobility}
- `commute_time_band_pre` + `commute_variance_band_pre`
- `car_lock_in_markers` (if car-dependent):
  - financing/lease constraints, repair/maintenance exposure, insurance costs, replacement urgency
- `public_transport_quality_markers` (if used/available):
  - coverage, reliability incidents, cancellations, missed connections
- `fee_stack_pre`:
  - parking/tolls/road fees, congestion charges, ticketing penalties
- `access_constraint_markers`:
  - “must drive” constraints due to schedule or location (school/care/work)

### Friction mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Forced car dependence (no viable alternative) + repair shocks.
- Time variance: unpredictability cascades into childcare penalties and missed appointments.
- Fee stacks: tolls/parking function as hidden E-like layers.
- Public transport unreliability as time tax.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Mobility friction **MUST** report commute variance; average commute time alone is non-conform.
- If car lock-in exists, the case **MUST** disclose the main lock-in mechanism (finance, repair access, insurance, replacement urgency).
- Fee stacks (parking/tolls) **MUST** be itemized when materially present; lumping hides toll layers.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-MOB-01 (Variance):** commute_variance_band exists.
- **DL15-MOB-02 (Mode constraints):** forced dependence constraints are declared when present.
- **DL15-MOB-03 (Fee itemization):** at least one fee stack component is itemized if fees are material.

## B05.CH15.P03.S04 — Digital friction: subscription bundles, vendor lock-in, identity processes, platform fees

Digital friction is not “tech inconvenience” but an enabling infrastructure that gates work, schooling, care coordination, and dispute
flows. Middle households often face bundle creep, vendor lock-in, and identity processes that convert small failures into large time
loss.

### Required fields (template)
- `subscription_stack_pre`:
  - number of recurring subscriptions (bands),
  - bundle constraints (cancellation friction, penalties, forced upgrades)
- `vendor_lock_in_markers`:
  - proprietary ecosystems, data export barriers, repair barriers, device lock
- `identity_process_friction`:
  - authentication dependencies, recovery path viability, lockout incidence band
- `platform_fee_exposure`:
  - app store fees, payment processing fees, platform take rates when they gate income/services
- `digital_admin_burden_pre`:
  - steps/time bands to manage accounts, disputes, cancellations, portability actions

### Friction mechanisms (minimum inventory)
- Bundle creep: recurring costs rise via bundled services and friction to cancel.
- Vendor lock-in: portability barriers create switching costs and forced upgrades.
- Identity lockout: blocks access to rights and dispute processes.
- Platform fees: toll layers on commerce/work/service access.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Digital friction **MUST** treat identity recovery as a first-class constraint; without recovery, “portability” is cosmetic.
- Subscription bundles **MUST** be decomposed where possible; inability to decompose **MUST** be tagged as an opacity marker.
- Platform fees **MUST** be reported as explicit cost items when they gate essentials or income.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-DIG-01 (Identity viability):** identity recovery viability is declared (present/absent + time band).
- **DL15-DIG-02 (Bundle decomposition):** subscription stack is itemized or explicitly flagged opaque.
- **DL15-DIG-03 (Admin time):** digital admin burden includes measurable proxies (steps/time band).

---

### Output format (friction map summary)

At minimum, the baseline case **MUST** emit a friction map summary table (or structured list) with the following columns per domain:

- `domain` | `primary_gatekeepers` | `friction_type` | `time_cost_band` | `cash_cost_band` | `variance_or_wait_band` | `opacity_flag` | `contestability_viability` | `evidence_class`

If the friction map cannot be produced with evidence linkage (contracts, bills, schedules, notices, logs as applicable), the case is
NON-CONFORM under CH15 fail-closed rules.
