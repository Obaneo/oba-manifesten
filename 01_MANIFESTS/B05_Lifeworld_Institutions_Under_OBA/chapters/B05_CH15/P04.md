# B05.CH15.P04 — Risk events: typical middle-class shocks

This paragraph specifies the **risk events** used in the pre-OBA baseline case to expose how “normal” households become fragile: not
primarily through chronic crisis, but through **pathway friction** and **volatility channels** that turn shocks into prolonged time loss,
rising L, and degraded R/B. Risk events are not story beats; they are **standardized stressors** used to (i) reveal friction multipliers,
(ii) quantify recovery time and volatility impact, and (iii) establish measurable *before/after* improvements under OBA rails.

---

<a id="b05ch15p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P04.S01 — Health shock: temporary incapacity + wait time/pathway friction

A middle-household health shock is typically *temporary incapacity* rather than immediate destitution. The primary tail driver is often
system friction: unclear pathways, wait times, administrative burden, and income/leave coordination failure.

### Standard risk event definition
- `risk_event_type`: `health_shock_temporary`
- `trigger_event`: {short_term_incapacity, injury, acute_episode}
- `duration_band`: time unable to work at full capacity
- `income_impact`: B reduction band + any sick-pay/leave replacement band
- `pathway_friction`:
  - `wait_time_band` (appointments/diagnostics),
  - `referral_steps_band`,
  - `documentation_burden_band`

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Leave/benefit coordination friction: delays, clawback risk, proof loops.
- Healthcare access pathway: wait time → prolonged incapacity → more admin interactions.
- Secondary cost channel: transport/medication/copays (if present), plus time loss.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The event **MUST** be represented as a **timeline** (incapacity duration + wait times + decision times), not a single cost.
- The event **MUST** record pathway friction proxies (wait time, steps, admin time), since these are the structural multipliers.
- If income replacement exists, the event **MUST** state timing and clawback exposure (T interaction).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-HLT-01 (Timeline):** duration_band + wait_time_band are present.
- **DL15-HLT-02 (Pathway proxy):** referral/documentation burden proxy exists.
- **DL15-HLT-03 (T coupling):** timing of replacement (or lack) is declared.

## B05.CH15.P04.S02 — Work transition: sector shift, reorganization, skills mismatch

Work transitions are a major middle-household risk: the household may remain employed, but experiences income volatility, time capture,
and a skills mismatch that forces reskilling under stress.

### Standard risk event definition
- `risk_event_type`: `work_transition`
- `trigger_event`: {reorg, redundancy_risk, role_change, sector_downturn, contract_change}
- `income_impact`: B/N change band (including loss of variable components)
- `time_impact`: additional hours (job search, training) + schedule unpredictability band
- `skills_gap_marker`: present/absent + category (certification required, tooling shift, language/regulation)

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Skills mismatch → reskilling time burden → short-term income dip.
- HR/admin friction: severance processes, benefits transitions, documentation loops.
- Geographic/mobility friction: commuting changes, relocation feasibility, childcare mismatch.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The event **MUST** separate (i) income change, (ii) time burden change, and (iii) administrative friction (three-channel model).
- Skills gap **MUST** be declared as a measurable proxy (category + time-to-qualification band if known).
- If the transition involves public systems (benefits/training), the event **MUST** record wait times and decision times.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-WRK-01 (Three-channel):** income/time/admin impacts are all declared (bands acceptable).
- **DL15-WRK-02 (Skills proxy):** skills_gap_marker exists (and time band if applicable).
- **DL15-WRK-03 (Wait times):** where public pathways exist, wait/decision bands are declared.

## B05.CH15.P04.S03 — Price shock: energy, food, interest rates; compounding into rising L

Middle households are often vulnerable to *volatility* rather than absolute scarcity: energy/food spikes and interest-rate changes
compound into rising L and reduced R, especially through mortgages and debt service.

### Standard risk event definition
- `risk_event_type`: `price_shock_bundle`
- `trigger_event`: {energy_spike, food_spike, interest_rate_increase, insurance_premium_jump}
- `price_delta_band` per domain
- `pass_through_timing` (when bills reset vs pay cycle; T interaction)
- `contract_reset_markers`:
  - mortgage reset dates, tariff renewal dates, premium renewal dates

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Contract reset + lag: rising costs arrive before adjustments in N, creating temporary tail pressure.
- Fee/penalty channel: missed payment → late fees → administrative time → compounded stress.
- Substitution limits: switching frictions, opacity, bundles that prevent effective switching.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The event **MUST** report both level change and volatility mechanism (reset dates, indexation triggers).
- The event **MUST** separate base price change from fee/penalty spillovers (if missed payments occur).
- Switching feasibility **MUST** be declared (can the household switch providers/contracts within a realistic time window?).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-PRC-01 (Reset visibility):** reset/renewal markers are declared for affected domains.
- **DL15-PRC-02 (Base vs fees):** base deltas are separated from penalties/fees.
- **DL15-PRC-03 (Switch feasibility):** feasibility of switching is declared (present/absent + time band).

## B05.CH15.P04.S04 — Care shock: sudden informal care, divorce, death; system friction as multiplier

Care shocks are classic middle-household tail events: sudden obligations and coordination load increase time poverty, reduce effective work
capacity, and amplify administrative burden. System friction (legal, childcare, benefits, housing) often becomes the multiplier.

### Standard risk event definition
- `risk_event_type`: `care_shock`
- `trigger_event`: {sudden_informal_care, divorce, bereavement, custody_change}
- `time_impact`: additional care hours band + coordination/admin time band
- `income_impact`: B/N reduction band (reduced hours, leave use, job loss risk)
- `system_pathways_involved[]`: {family_law, benefits, housing, childcare, healthcare, schooling, debt_restructuring}

### Propagation channels (minimum inventory)
- Care time expansion → schedule conflict → income dip + higher childcare costs.
- Legal/administrative pathways → paperwork and delays → prolonged uncertainty.
- Housing and mobility reconfiguration → switching costs + commute variance.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The event **MUST** treat system friction as a first-class multiplier (steps/time/delay proxies), not as “bad luck”.
- The event **MUST** represent both income and time impacts, including administrative time.
- Where disputes/enforcement are possible (custody, housing, debt), provisional relief availability **MUST** be declared.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-CARE-01 (Time+income):** time_impact and income_impact are both declared.
- **DL15-CARE-02 (Pathway proxy):** at least one system friction proxy exists (steps/time/delay).
- **DL15-CARE-03 (Protection):** presence/absence of provisional relief is explicitly declared where harm risk exists.

---

### Output format (risk event summary)

At minimum, the baseline case **MUST** emit a risk event summary table (or structured list) with:

- `risk_event_type` | `trigger_event` | `B/N impact` | `T interaction` | `L impact` | `time_impact` | `pathway_friction_proxy` | `recovery_time_band`

Risk events must be linkable to the friction map (P03) and to post-case intervention deltas (P05/P11); otherwise they degrade into
storytelling and become non-conform under CH15 fail-closed rules.
