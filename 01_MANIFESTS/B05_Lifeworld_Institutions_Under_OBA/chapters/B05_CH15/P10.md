# B05.CH15.P10 — Digital lifeworld (after): less subscription lock-in and platform toll

This paragraph specifies the **post-OBA digital lifeworld** state for the middle-household case. The goal is not to abolish digital
markets; it is to remove **subscription lock-in**, reduce **vendor dependency**, bound **platform tolls**, and prevent **identity
lockout** from blocking essentials, work, education, care coordination, or dispute flows. The output is a set of enforceable rails and
measurable deltas linked to baseline digital frictions (P03) and the case dashboard (P11).

---

<a id="b05ch15p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH15.P10.S01 — Baseline digital services: interoperability/portability; less vendor lock-in

Post-OBA, digital “baseline services” function like infrastructure: they must be interoperable, portable, and accessible without forcing
a household into closed ecosystems.

### Post-OBA baseline digital rails (minimum set)
- `interoperability_requirements`:
  - open standards for core functions (identity, messaging, document exchange, payments where relevant),
  - API or export/import requirements sufficient for real switching.
- `portability_requirements`:
  - data export in usable formats,
  - migration support (import paths) to prevent “export but unusable” compliance.
- `bundle_and_subscription_constraints`:
  - clear unbundling rules,
  - cancellation friction constraints,
  - prohibition of forced upgrade traps that function as recurring tolls.
- `universal_access_support`:
  - offline/assisted paths for critical services to avoid digital-only exclusion.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Interoperability **MUST** be operational (tested via successful exchanges/migrations), not symbolic “open policy statements”.
- Portability **MUST** include both export and import viability; export-only is cosmetic.
- Baseline digital access **MUST NOT** be gated behind paid tiers that block essential access (schools, government services, healthcare
  portals, dispute flows).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-DIG-A1 (Interoperability test):** at least one interoperability action is demonstrated (exchange or migration) or the claim fails.
- **DL15-DIG-A2 (Portability viability):** export + import (or equivalent migration) is possible within a declared time band.
- **DL15-DIG-A3 (Unbundling):** bundles are decomposable or explicitly tagged as opaque/non-conform surfaces.

<a id="b05ch15p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH15.P10.S02 — Platform fees: bounded take rates; transparency and dispute-flow

Platform fees are treated as toll surfaces when they become non-optional for access to work, commerce, communication, or essential
services. Post-OBA, take rates must be bounded and transparent, and enforcement must be contestable.

### Post-OBA platform toll rails (minimum set)
- `bounded_take_rates`:
  - maximum take-rate bands per platform class (where platforms gate access),
  - prohibition of hidden fee layering (payment processing + “service fees” + priority fees).
- `fee_itemization`:
  - mandatory itemized fee disclosure at transaction and monthly levels.
- `ranking_and_access_due_process`:
  - explainable ranking/deactivation impacts when they affect income or essential access,
  - dispute/appeal pathways with time-bounded decisions and provisional access where harm risk exists.
- `anti-retaliation`:
  - switching or disputing **MUST NOT** trigger punitive shadow bans or access degradation.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Platform fees **MUST** be itemized and measurable; “platform costs” lump sums are non-conform.
- Take-rate bounding **MUST** apply to the effective fee stack (all mandatory layers), not only a headline commission.
- If platform access decisions can remove livelihood or essential access, due process **MUST** exist (reason + appeal + time bounds +
  provisional relief where appropriate).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-PLT-01 (Effective take-rate):** effective take-rate is computed from itemized layers pre/post.
- **DL15-PLT-02 (Hidden fee ban):** hidden fee layers are absent or explicitly flagged as non-conform.
- **DL15-PLT-03 (Due process):** dispute-flow exists for deactivation/ranking and is time-bounded.

<a id="b05ch15p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH15.P10.S03 — Identity: recovery and due process; no lockout from essentials

Identity failure is a systemic tail driver: account loss or authentication failure can block banking, work tools, school portals,
healthcare portals, and dispute initiation. Post-OBA, identity must have recovery guarantees and due process.

### Post-OBA identity rails (minimum set)
- `recovery_guarantees`:
  - account recovery paths with time bounds,
  - alternative verification paths when devices are lost or numbers change.
- `no_lockout_from_essentials`:
  - essential service access cannot be permanently blocked by a single authentication failure,
  - offline/assisted recovery pathways for critical services.
- `due_process_for_restrictions`:
  - reason disclosure for account restrictions,
  - appeal path with time bounds,
  - proportionality (temporary limitations over permanent bans where appropriate).
- `portability_of_identity`:
  - ability to switch identity providers or authentication methods without losing service access.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Identity recovery **MUST** be faster than the baseline harm timeline (e.g., before missed bills or work lockout triggers cascades).
- Essential access **MUST NOT** depend on a single point of failure (single device, single platform account).
- Account restriction decisions **MUST** be contestable and logged; black-box lockouts are non-conform.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-ID-01 (Time-bounded recovery):** recovery time bounds exist and are shorter than declared harm thresholds.
- **DL15-ID-02 (No single point of failure):** at least one alternative recovery/authentication path exists.
- **DL15-ID-03 (Restriction contestability):** reason + appeal + time bounds exist for restrictions.

<a id="b05ch15p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH15.P10.S04 — Measurable effect: portability success rates up, lockout incidents down, fee shares down

This section defines the minimum measurable outputs required to claim digital normality improved.

### Required measurable outputs (minimum)
- `portability_success_rate_pre/post` (aggregated; export+import completion rate)
- `time_to_portability_pre/post` (band)
- `lockout_incidence_pre/post` (identity/account lockouts impacting essentials; aggregated)
- `recovery_time_band_pre/post` (for critical accounts)
- `subscription_stack_size_pre/post` (count band + cost share)
- `platform_fee_share_pre/post` (share of relevant transactions or share of L where applicable)
- `dispute_duration_pre/post` for:
  - billing disputes,
  - deactivation/ranking disputes,
  - identity restriction disputes.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Digital improvement claims **MUST** include at least one portability metric, one lockout metric, and one fee metric.
- Reporting **MUST** be privacy-preserving and aggregated; it **MUST NOT** become behavioural scoring.
- Outputs **MUST** link to `method_id` and include uncertainty bands (via P11).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DL15-DIG-M1 (Portability delta):** portability success rates rise and/or time-to-portability declines.
- **DL15-DIG-M2 (Lockout delta):** lockout incidence declines and recovery times improve (bands acceptable).
- **DL15-DIG-M3 (Fee delta):** platform fee share declines or is bounded with effective itemized proof.

---

### Output format (digital summary)

At minimum, the post-case **MUST** emit a digital summary (table or structured list) with:

- `surface` | `pre_proxy` | `post_proxy` | `delta` | `method_id_ref` | `uncertainty_band` | `rail_link`

Where surfaces include: subscriptions, portability, identity recovery, platform fees, and dispute durations.
