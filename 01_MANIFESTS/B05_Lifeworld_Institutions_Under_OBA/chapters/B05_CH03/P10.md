# B05.CH03.P10 — Evolutionary infrastructure: learning care institutions without drift

This paragraph specifies how care institutions learn and adapt **without semantic drift** and without turning iterative improvement
into discretionary experimentation on households. Care systems are complex, context-sensitive, and exposed to shifting demographics,
labour markets, and shocks. OBA therefore requires evolutionary infrastructure: controlled change mechanisms, version control,
published benchmarks, and hard rollback triggers when lifeworld harms appear.

The aim is to make care institutions **correctable** while keeping them **bounded by invariants**: maintenance-first capacity,
contestability, privacy minimalism, and anti-capture rails. “Learning” is not allowed to expand data appetite, increase coercion, or
introduce vendor lock-in; those are drift patterns, not improvements.

---

## B05.CH03.P10.S01 — Pilots and scaling: evidence, audit, rollout, rollback (version control)

Change must be modular and reversible. Care reforms cannot be justified by intent; they must be tested under bounded risk.

### Pilot discipline (binding)
- **Pilot scope**: clearly defined intervention, target cohorts/regions, duration, and success/failure criteria.
- **Evidence pack**: baseline measures + method_id + uncertainty bands + expected causal chain (what frictions are removed, what L/R/B
  tail effect is expected).
- **Audit requirement**: independent review of outcomes and failure modes; publish aggregated results and restatements if errors occur.
- **Rollout gates**: scaling is permitted only if lifeworld indicators improve (access, waiting time, time burden, tail-risk) without
  privacy drift or clamp.
- **Rollback discipline**: predefined triggers that require rollback (worsened access/quality, rising admin toll, privacy incidents,
  selective execution patterns).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any scaled reform MUST cite a pilot/audit lineage (version history). “Nationwide rollout without pilot evidence” is non-conforming
  unless justified as an emergency regime with sunset and post-mortem.
- Changes MUST be versioned: what changed, when, why, and how compatibility is preserved.

## B05.CH03.P10.S02 — Maintenance-first in care: staffing and training as infrastructure, not a residual

Care institutions fail primarily through capacity decay: understaffing, burnout, training bottlenecks, and backlog growth. OBA treats
staffing and training as infrastructure (ΔG analogue), not as adjustable overhead.

### Maintenance-first components (binding)
- **Staffing minima**: ratios/skill mix aligned to service minima; persistent breach triggers escalation and remediation.
- **Training pipeline**: intake capacity, supervision capacity, and career ladders to sustain long-run staffing.
- **Retention and workload discipline**: burnout proxies and churn are treated as early-warning indicators for capacity collapse.
- **Backlog management**: waiting times and unmet needs are treated as backlog states requiring active reduction plans.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Budgets must be ring-fenced for staffing/training and backlog reduction; hollowing out capacity to “save money” is non-conforming.
- If staffing shortages are structural, the system must treat this as a capacity breach (not a normal state) and activate emergency
  capacity plans with sunset and audit.

## B05.CH03.P10.S03 — Feedback into the Codex: benchmarks (waiting time/quality) with versioning and restatements

Care governance requires shared benchmarks and schemas to avoid local semantic drift and dashboard theatre. The Codex is the canonical
mechanism for making benchmarks reproducible and machine-auditable.

### Codex feedback loop (binding)
- **Benchmarks**: waiting time norms, access coverage minima, staffing ratio bands, quality/incident categories.
- **Versioning**: benchmark changes must be versioned, with migration notes and backward compatibility where applicable.
- **Restatements**: when methods change or errors are found, outputs must be restated and previous claims corrected publicly.
- **Interoperability**: local implementations must map to shared schema fields so results can be compared and selective execution
  detected (within privacy rails).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any public claim about care performance MUST reference benchmark versions and method_id; otherwise it is non-reproducible.
- If local systems cannot produce outputs in Codex-compatible formats, scaling is blocked (fail-closed) until interoperability exists.

## B05.CH03.P10.S04 — Audit of the guardian: external review of care and community governance

Care institutions and their auditors are themselves capture targets. “Audit of the auditor” is therefore constitutive: without
external review, discretion expands, exceptions grow, and reporting becomes self-protective.

### External review requirements (binding)
- **Meta-audits**: periodic audits of auditors and governance bodies; rotation rules where appropriate.
- **Independence**: separation from operators and vendors; conflict-of-interest disclosures and cooling-off constraints.
- **Transparency**: publish audit summaries, exception registers (aggregated), remediation progress, and escalation decisions.
- **User-side voice**: structured input channels (including qualitative experience evidence) that can trigger audit review, without
  allowing anecdote capture.

### Hard stop
- Proven selective enforcement, suppressed findings, or capture signals require immediate escalation: governance reset protocols,
  suspension of compatibility claims, and redesign of the oversight architecture.
