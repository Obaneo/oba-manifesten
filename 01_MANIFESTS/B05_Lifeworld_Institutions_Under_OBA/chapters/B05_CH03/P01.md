# B05.CH03.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of time/care/social reproduction under OBA

This paragraph defines why time/care/social reproduction is treated as an **order-critical** component of OBA’s lifeworld layer.
Without explicit care capacity and time sovereignty, the order will reproduce hidden burdens (L), clamp dynamics, and unstable R/B
tails even if macro rules are formally correct. Care and time are not residual “private domains”; they are **infrastructure for
people’s capacity** to work, participate, learn, recover, and sustain communities.

CH03 is therefore normative: it specifies the rails by which institutions must prevent care externalisation, prevent administrative
tolling, maintain continuity and quality, and preserve autonomy under privacy-minimal measurement. The chapter is not a moral program
to prescribe family roles; it is a structural interface layer that turns “people can live” into a testable condition.

---

## B05.CH03.P01.S01 — Purpose: make reproductive capacity explicit without totalising measurement

The purpose is to make **reproductive capacity** (care, recovery, social cohesion, capability to function) explicit as a constraint on
the order—without converting it into a behavioural scoring regime.

### Purpose statement
- **Explicit constraint**: time and care capacity limit what labour markets and institutions can demand without producing clamp and
  collapse.
- **Anti-totalisation**: care/wellbeing must not be reduced to a single KPI or used to justify surveillance or behavioural coercion.
- **Testability without domination**: institutions must produce bounded signals (access, waiting time, staffing, continuity, friction)
  and contestable procedures, not omniscient measurement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any care/time policy MUST define what is being protected (capacity, continuity, autonomy) and which bounded signals demonstrate it.
- Any regime that requires pervasive monitoring to “prove wellbeing” is non-conforming by design and must be redesigned.

---

## B05.CH03.P01.S02 — Scope: care work (formal/informal), time use, childcare, informal caregiving, community work

CH03 covers the full care/time system as it appears in the lifeworld—not only paid care services.

### In scope
- **Formal care work**: childcare, education support, healthcare and long-term care services, professional caregiving.
- **Informal care and caregiving**: unpaid household care, elder care, disability support, crisis support networks.
- **Time use and scheduling**: workweek length, schedule predictability, commute and travel time, administrative time burden.
- **Community capacity**: neighbourhood associations, cultural carriers, mutual aid, local commons that sustain social reproduction.

### Out of scope (but referenced)
- Canonical definitions and measurement primitives (Kernel Spec / Book 1), and the institutional democracy layer (Book 3), except as
  needed for contestability and audit rails.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Policies must specify which scope element they target (childcare access, scheduling rights, caregiver support, community commons),
  and how effects will be tested in lifeworld terms (L/R/B and friction).

---

## B05.CH03.P01.S03 — Binding: maintenance-first (ΔG) as the guiding principle for people/relationships (lifeworld analogue)

The binding principle is **maintenance-first**: care systems must be designed for continuity, staffing adequacy, and backlog control,
analogous to ΔG maintenance discipline in physical commons.

### Maintenance-first logic (lifeworld analogue)
- **Continuity**: stable access pathways; care does not collapse into crisis-only service.
- **Staffing and training**: capacity is infrastructure; under-staffing is ΔG-negative decay.
- **Backlog discipline**: waiting times and unmet needs are treated as measurable backlog states that trigger escalation.
- **Incident discipline**: safety and quality failures must be logged, investigated, and corrected (not hidden to protect metrics).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any care commons MUST publish service minima and capacity indicators (coverage, waiting times, staffing ratios) and define escalation
  triggers.
- Any reform that “saves money” by increasing unpaid household burden is treated as L externalisation and is non-conforming unless
  explicitly buffered and shown to reduce tails.

---

## B05.CH03.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: policy that externalises care to households without buffers is non-conform in the lifeworld layer

Care externalisation is a primary drift mode: institutions claim efficiency while shifting time costs, mental load, and crisis risk to
households—especially to low R/B cohorts. This increases hidden L, reduces autonomy, and destabilises tails.

### Externalisation patterns (non-exhaustive)
- Reduced service coverage or increased waiting times without compensating buffers.
- Administrative friction (“prove need”) that converts rights into time burdens.
- Work schedules that assume unlimited household elasticity (unpaid coordination and emergency caregiving).
- Private fee-stacks around care access (insurance clamp, brokerage, paid priority).

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If care/time policy increases household burden or worsens R/B tails (time poverty, crisis dropouts, burnout) without explicit
  buffers, it MUST be treated as **non-conforming**.
- If bounded measurement cannot be executed without privacy breach or capacity collapse, the policy cannot claim compatibility and
  must fail-closed pending redesign.
