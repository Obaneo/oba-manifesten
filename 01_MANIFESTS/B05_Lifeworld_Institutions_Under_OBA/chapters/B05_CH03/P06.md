# B05.CH03.P06 — Community infrastructure: neighbourhood, associations, cultural carriers

This paragraph specifies community infrastructure as a **capacity layer** of the lifeworld: the spaces, associations, and cultural
carriers that maintain social cohesion, informal support networks, and the practical ability to participate. Under OBA, community
infrastructure is not ornamental “culture policy”; it is a stabiliser of R/B tails and a reducer of hidden L because it lowers the
coordination costs of daily life (childcare swapping, mutual aid, local problem-solving) and provides non-commercial access to
learning, activity, and belonging.

Community commons must be designed as **open access by default** within explicit boundary rules, protected against capture, and
measured only via minimal signals sufficient to maintain accountability without turning community life into a profiling surface.

---

## B05.CH03.P06.S01 — Community commons: spaces, sport, libraries, makerspaces as social “capacity”

Community commons are treated as social infrastructure that produces capability, resilience, and continuity.

### Commons domains (non-exhaustive)
- **Libraries and learning spaces**: open knowledge access, quiet work capacity, digital inclusion points.
- **Sport and activity infrastructure**: low-barrier physical health and social ties (a stabiliser, not a KPI target).
- **Community centers and neighbourhood hubs**: meeting space, association support, local service desks.
- **Makerspaces and repair hubs**: practical capacity for maintenance, learning-by-doing, repair culture (ΔG analogue in social form).
- **Cultural carriers**: local stages, rehearsal rooms, language/community groups, intergenerational knowledge transfer.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Community commons must have explicit governance: access rules, maintenance budgets, incident discipline, and contestability.
- Where provision is scarce, prioritisation rules must be explicit and auditable; informal gatekeeping is non-conforming.

---

## B05.CH03.P06.S02 — Access and safety: open by default with explicit boundary rules (abuse prevention)

Open access does not mean “no rules.” It means default inclusion with clearly defined boundaries that protect users and prevent abuse
without discretionary exclusion or covert profiling.

### Access and safety design (binding)
- **Open by default**: baseline ability to enter and use commons services without membership paywalls or status gating.
- **Explicit boundary rules**: behaviour rules, safety protocols, and escalation ladders are published and consistently applied.
- **Abuse prevention**: clear sanctions for harassment, violence, vandalism, and systematic exclusion attempts.
- **Due process**: exclusions (temporary or permanent) must be logged, justified, time-bounded where possible, and appealable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Safety must not be achieved by blanket surveillance or identity scoring. If safety requires invasive monitoring, the design is
  non-conforming and must be redesigned (minimalism + proportionality).
- “Soft bans” (informal exclusion without procedure) are treated as clamp dynamics and must be eliminated via procedural rails.

## B05.CH03.P06.S03 — Anti-capture: no political or commercial hijacking of community provisions

Community commons are vulnerable to capture: political branding, ideological gatekeeping, commercial sponsorship as veto power, or
vendor lock-in in operations. Capture converts commons into factional instruments and destroys legitimacy.

### Capture mechanisms (indicative)
- **Political hijacking**: using commons access as a loyalty test or propaganda channel; selective exclusion based on viewpoint.
- **Commercial hijacking**: sponsorship that controls programming, access, or data; monetisation via fee-stacks or “premium tiers.”
- **Governance capture**: boards dominated by insiders, donors, or vendors; opaque procurement and exceptions.

### Anti-capture rails (binding)
- Publish governance roles and decision logs; rotate roles where appropriate; enforce conflict-of-interest rules.
- Prohibit conditioning access on political alignment or commercial consent.
- Procurement must preserve exitability: no single vendor may become an operational veto layer.

---

## B05.CH03.P06.S04 — Measurement: minimal signals (participation, accessibility, continuity) without personal profiling

Community infrastructure requires accountability, but measurement must not turn community life into a data factory.

### Minimal signal set (binding)
- **Participation**: usage volumes and broad participation counts (aggregated), not individual behaviour tracking.
- **Accessibility**: opening hours, geographic coverage, physical accessibility, language support, cost barriers.
- **Continuity**: uptime/availability of spaces, maintenance backlogs, closure incidents, staffing adequacy (aggregated).
- **Safety incidents**: incident counts and resolution times (aggregated), with confidentiality protections.

### Privacy rails (binding)
- No personal dossiers, no behavioural scoring, no commercial profiling pipelines.
- Use aggregation, sampling, and suppression for small-n; maintain join safety across published tables.

### Conformance rule
- If measurement drifts toward surveillance or profiling (even “for good reasons”), it becomes an extraction/capture surface and is
  non-conforming; redesign is mandatory.
