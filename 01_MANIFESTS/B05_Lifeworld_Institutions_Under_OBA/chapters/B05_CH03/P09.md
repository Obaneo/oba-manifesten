# B05.CH03.P09 — Epistemic limits and privacy rails: boundaries of measuring care and wellbeing

This paragraph defines epistemic and privacy boundaries for CH03. Time/care/wellbeing are high-salience targets for measurement
totalisation: institutions and vendors are incentivised to turn care into a data factory and then govern by optimisation against
scores. Under OBA, that drift is treated as a clamp mechanism: it creates surveillance rent, behavioural coercion, and loss of agency,
while often degrading care quality through Goodhart effects.

The chapter therefore binds measurement to a strict doctrine:
- metrics are **bounded signals**, not substitutes for agency;
- experience evidence is allowed but must be **procedurally structured** and adversarially reviewable;
- care data is high-risk and requires **strict data minimalism** and join-safety;
- paternalistic optimisation is a systemic risk and is mitigated by plurality, freedom of choice, and contestability.

---

## B05.CH03.P09.S01 — No reduction to KPI: metrics are bounded signals, not substitutes for agency

Care and wellbeing cannot be collapsed into a single scalar without distorting behaviour and legitimising coercion.

### Principle
- Metrics are **indicators** used to detect failures (access gaps, backlog growth, incident spikes, time burdens), not a target state
  for “optimising people.”
- The lifeworld layer requires plural signals because people’s constraints differ, and because single-axis optimisation invites gaming.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No single KPI may be used as the decisive governance target for care/wellbeing. Any KPI must be embedded in a multi-metric frame
  with anti-gaming checks and uncertainty discipline.
- Policies that attempt to “improve wellbeing” by forcing behavioural compliance with monitored KPIs are non-conforming.

---

## B05.CH03.P09.S02 — Experience inputs: structured qualitative reporting with testable procedure and adversarial review

Experience evidence is admissible because many harms appear first as lived signals (humiliation, access friction, informal gatekeeping,
time burden). But experience evidence must not degrade into anecdote politics or narrative capture.

### Procedure for experience inputs (binding)
- **Structured reporting**: fixed questionnaires or protocols tied to defined constructs (time burden, access friction, dignity
  breaches, service continuity) rather than open-ended storytelling.
- **Sampling discipline**: defined sampling frames and frequency; avoid self-selected-only “feedback” as the sole evidence source.
- **Adversarial review**: allow challenge, contradiction analysis, and cross-checks against quantitative indicators and logs.
- **Traceability**: method_id for how qualitative evidence is collected, coded, and used in decisions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Experience evidence must trigger testable hypotheses and audits (e.g., “administrative toll increased”) rather than becoming a
  substitute for measurement linkage.
- If experience evidence is used for PR without audit linkage, it is narrative capture and non-conforming.

## B05.CH03.P09.S03 — Privacy: care data as high-risk; strict data minimalism

Care and wellbeing data is inherently sensitive and re-identification-prone. OBA treats care data as a high-risk asset that must not
become a rent stream, governance weapon, or profiling base.

### Privacy rails (binding)
- **Data minimalism**: collect only what is necessary for service delivery, entitlement decisions, and audit—nothing else.
- **No person-tracking as default**: avoid longitudinal personal dossiers for “optimisation” or “risk scoring.”
- **Tiered disclosure**: public outputs are aggregated and join-safe; regulated microdata (if any) is controlled and purpose-limited;
  PII remains confidential with strict retention caps.
- **Suppression and join safety**: small-n suppression and linkage controls across published tables are mandatory.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a care programme requires broad data collection beyond necessity, it is presumed to be drifting into surveillance rent and must
  be redesigned or halted.
- Commercial reuse of care data (profiling, targeting, pricing discrimination) is categorically non-conforming.

## B05.CH03.P09.S04 — Risk: paternalistic optimisation; mitigation via plurality, freedom of choice, and contestability

Even well-intended systems drift toward paternalism: “we know what’s best,” enforced through metrics, nudges, and eligibility controls.
This is an epistemic failure: it treats people as objects to be shaped rather than as agents with contestable claims.

### Paternalism risk mechanisms
- **Optimisation creep**: goals expand from service delivery to behaviour management.
- **Scoring regimes**: risk scores and compliance points become gatekeepers of access.
- **Discretion expansion**: caseworkers or algorithms gain unreviewable authority over intimate domains.

### Mitigation (binding)
- **Plurality**: allow multiple legitimate care arrangements; avoid single-path enforcement.
- **Freedom of choice**: design institutions to expand options and reduce frictions, not to impose “correct behaviour.”
- **Contestability**: strong appeal rights, transparent criteria, and audit-of-auditor structures prevent silent coercion.

### Hard stop
- If paternalistic optimisation appears (behavioural scoring, coercive monitoring, unappealable exclusions), the regime is
  non-conforming and must trigger rollback/redesign.
