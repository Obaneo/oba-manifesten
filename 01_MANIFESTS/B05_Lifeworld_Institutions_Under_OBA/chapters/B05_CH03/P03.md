# B05.CH03.P03 — Workweek, scheduling, and time sovereignty

This paragraph specifies time sovereignty as an order constraint: workweek design and scheduling rules determine whether households
can execute care, maintain health, and participate in community life without clamp. Under OBA, time capture is treated as a hidden L
mechanism and a form of block power: even if wages rise, unpredictable schedules and coerced availability can destroy autonomy and
increase costs (childcare, transport, administration) while making exit harder.

The objective is to (1) reduce wasteful E-hours and reallocate time toward P/C work and maintenance, (2) detect and neutralise
schedule capture as a clamp mechanism, (3) define permissible flexibility without hidden burdens, and (4) make time rights portable so
that employers cannot use time as a control lever.

---

## B05.CH03.P03.S01 — Shorter workweek logic: shift from E-hours to P/C-hours and maintenance time

A shorter workweek is not a lifestyle slogan; it is a structural reallocation claim. The order produces large volumes of labour time
spent sustaining E (tolls, lock-in, compliance theatre, churn). When E contracts, a portion of labour time can be released and
redeployed toward (a) P/C output and maintenance, and (b) household/community maintenance time.

### Logic of reduction
- **E-hours contraction**: remove roles and processes whose primary function is clamp/claim maintenance.
- **P/C-hours prioritisation**: expand labour in essentials, infrastructure, care, education, and digital rails.
- **Maintenance time restoration**: enable household maintenance, caregiving, recovery, and civic participation without punishment.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Workweek reduction policies MUST specify the functional source of released hours (which E processes disappear) and the target
  allocation (which P/C capacities expand).
- If reduced hours are achieved by intensifying pace, increasing surveillance, or shifting unpaid work onto households, the policy is
  non-conforming (it increases hidden L).

---

## B05.CH03.P03.S02 — Schedule control as block power: detection of “schedule capture” and remedies

Schedule control is a primary block-power surface because it determines whether a person can plan, switch jobs, access childcare, or
maintain a second income stream. “Schedule capture” occurs when employers unilaterally control availability and thereby create
dependency.

### Schedule capture indicators (non-exhaustive)
- Short-notice shifts, frequent last-minute cancellations, punitive “availability” requirements.
- Unpaid on-call time, fragmented shifts, artificial split-shifts that increase travel/childcare burdens.
- Algorithmic scheduling without contestability (black-box rosters), retaliation via roster assignment.
- Scheduling used as discipline for voice/complaints or as a barrier to job switching.

### Remedies (binding)
- **Notice periods and compensation**: minimum notice requirements; compensation for cancellations and on-call obligations.
- **Predictability rights**: right to stable hours bands; limits on roster volatility; documented exceptions only.
- **Contestability**: workers can dispute scheduling abuse; logs and decision records must exist (no black-box rostering).
- **Anti-retaliation**: enforceable protections for complaints and job switching.

## B05.CH03.P03.S03 — Flexibility criteria: allowed only if predictable and without hidden L (childcare/transport/variable costs)

Flexibility is permitted only when it does not externalise costs and risk onto households. Under OBA, “flexibility” becomes
non-conforming when it is simply a transfer of volatility from firms to workers.

### Permissible flexibility criteria (binding)
A flexible arrangement is compatible only if all of the following hold:
- **Predictability**: schedules are published within stable horizons; variability is bounded; deviations are compensated.
- **Cost neutrality**: extra childcare, transport, or coordination costs created by variability are not shifted onto workers without
  compensation or support.
- **Income stability**: minimum guaranteed hours or income floors exist; variable hours cannot create tail-risk without buffers.
- **Exit safety**: flexibility must not be paired with clamp clauses (exclusivity, retaliation, rating coercion, deplatforming).

### Audit trigger
- If flexible work correlates with higher arrears, higher care breakdown, or higher churn (tail concentration), the flexibility model
  is presumed to be volatility-shifting and must be redesigned.

---

## B05.CH03.P03.S04 — Portability of time rights: leave, sabbaticals, care days as personal rights (not employer-bound)

Time rights are a core anti-clamp mechanism. If leave, care days, or sabbaticals are employer-controlled privileges, they become a
discipline tool and create exit friction.

### Portability principle (binding)
- Time rights (leave, care days, sick days, sabbaticals where applicable) must be **person-linked entitlements** with rules for
  accrual and transfer across employers.
- Use must be protected against retaliation and must not require discretionary approval beyond bounded operational constraints.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Portability rules MUST be simple, standardised, and auditable; complex employer-specific schemes are clamp surfaces.
- If portability is not feasible in a domain, the system MUST provide equivalent protections via public/commons rights so that exit
  does not imply loss of time sovereignty.
