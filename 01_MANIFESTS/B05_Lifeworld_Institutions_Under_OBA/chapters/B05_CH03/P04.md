# B05.CH03.P04 — Childcare and early-education commons

This paragraph specifies childcare and early education as **commons infrastructure**: an essential capacity system that stabilises
households, reduces hidden burdens (L), and improves labour mobility and time sovereignty. In OBA terms, childcare is not a private
consumption good; it is a lifeworld constraint that determines whether “work policy” is real or cosmetic. If childcare access is
scarce, expensive, or administratively clamped, households cannot execute exit, reskilling, or schedule predictability—even if wages
rise.

Childcare commons must therefore satisfy four conditions: (1) a baseline access design (parameterisable), (2) quality and maintenance
discipline (ΔG analogue), (3) price and fee-stack discipline so childcare reduces L rather than becoming a toll market, and (4)
integration with work without creating surveillance rents or employer-controlled access gates.

---

## B05.CH03.P04.S01 — Baseline access: universal or need-based access (parameterisable)

Baseline access defines the minimum entitlement: who is eligible, under what conditions, and how scarcity is managed.

### Access models (parameter space)
- **Universal baseline**: access for all children as a default right, with rules for capacity prioritisation under scarcity.
- **Need-based baseline**: eligibility tied to income, care burden, disability, or work/study status, with explicit anti-stigma rails.

The choice is parameterisable, but the rails are invariant: access must be rule-based, non-discriminatory, and contestable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Access criteria MUST be explicit, machine-readable where possible, and appealable; “caseworker discretion” is a clamp surface.
- Scarcity rules (waitlists, prioritisation) MUST be published and auditable; hidden prioritisation is non-conforming.
- Administrative proof burden MUST be minimised; proof-heavy access regimes are treated as hidden L and must be redesigned.

---

## B05.CH03.P04.S02 — Quality and maintenance: staffing ratios, training, continuity, incident logs

Childcare quality is an infrastructure property: it depends on staffing adequacy, training pipelines, continuity, and incident
discipline. Understaffing is ΔG-negative decay that produces both harm and loss of legitimacy.

### Quality/maintenance components (testable)
- **Staffing ratios and skill mix**: minimum ratios, supervision coverage, and training levels by age group.
- **Training and retention**: pipeline capacity, career ladders, turnover controls, burnout risk proxies.
- **Continuity**: stable caregiver assignment and low churn; continuity is treated as a quality dimension.
- **Incident discipline**: safety incidents, complaints, and capacity failures are logged with correction and restatement discipline.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Childcare commons MUST publish staffing ratios, turnover, waiting times, and incident summaries in aggregated form with method_id.
- Persistent understaffing or backlog growth triggers escalation: capacity breach → remediation plan → status downgrade if unresolved.

## B05.CH03.P04.S03 — Price discipline: childcare as L-reduction; prevention of private fee-stacks

Childcare is a major L component. The order objective is to reduce burdens and clamp, not to subsidise a private fee-stack ecosystem.

### Price discipline rules (binding)
- **Baseline affordability**: childcare costs must not structurally force households into time poverty, debt, or labour market exit.
- **No fee-stacks**: prohibit mandatory intermediary layers (brokerage, “management fees,” paid priority, opaque surcharges) that
  recreate toll positions around access.
- **Transparent tariffs**: where co-payments exist, they must be rule-based, published, and non-regressive (anti-tail harm).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any private provider operating within the childcare system MUST disclose all-in fees and must be constrained by anti-fee-stack rules.
- If childcare affordability worsens or becomes more volatile, the system fails the lifeworld test regardless of headline wage changes.

## B05.CH03.P04.S04 — Integration with work: schedules, travel time, employer duties without surveillance rents

Childcare must interface with work realities: opening hours, commute patterns, shift work, and irregular schedules. Integration must
expand choice space without turning childcare into an employer-controlled gate or a surveillance channel.

### Integration requirements (binding)
- **Schedule compatibility**: opening hours and flexibility must align with typical work patterns while meeting predictability rails
  (avoid “availability capture” of carers and parents).
- **Travel time minimisation**: location planning must consider commute and regional access; long travel time is hidden L.
- **Employer duties without gatekeeping**: employers may have duties (predictable scheduling, contributions where designed), but must
  not control childcare access or use childcare data to discipline workers.
- **No surveillance rents**: childcare systems must not become data factories (tracking parents/workers/children beyond necessity).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any integration mechanism MUST be privacy-minimal and must forbid secondary use of childcare data for employment discipline,
  profiling, or commercial targeting.
- If integration depends on employer-specific arrangements that create lock-in (childcare tied to employer), it is a clamp mechanism
  and must be treated as non-conforming unless replaced by portable/public access.
