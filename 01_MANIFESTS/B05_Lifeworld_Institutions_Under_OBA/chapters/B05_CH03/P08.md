# B05.CH03.P08 — Inequality and the distribution of reproductive labour

This paragraph specifies how inequality is reproduced through the distribution of reproductive labour (care, coordination, household
maintenance) and how OBA treats this as a structural lifeworld mechanism rather than as a moral or cultural blame game. Under OBA,
inequality is not only income dispersion; it is the unequal exposure to **time clamp** and **burden clamp (L)** created by contracts,
essential markets, and administrative systems. These clamps determine who has residual space (R) to build capability and who lives in
continuous triage.

The objective is to (1) make the mechanism explicit (time clamp and L clamp), (2) measure distribution without prescribing roles or
imposing behavioural ideals, (3) specify remedies as structural rails (rights, childcare, schedule sovereignty, portability, buffers),
and (4) prevent moralising/disciplining policy that increases shame and control while leaving frictions intact.

---

## B05.CH03.P08.S01 — Mechanism: time clamp and L clamp reproduce inequality via households and contracts

Inequality reproduces through cumulative frictions and asymmetric exit power. Time clamp and L clamp concentrate in households with
low capacity and high dependency (children, disability, caregiving obligations) and are amplified by contract structures.

### Mechanism model (non-exhaustive)
- **Time clamp**: schedule capture, unpredictable hours, long commutes, waiting times, and administrative burdens reduce planning
  capacity and limit labour mobility.
- **L clamp**: high essentials burdens (housing, energy, childcare, health access) force households into low-choice equilibria,
  increasing susceptibility to debt and coercive contracts.
- **Contract transmission**: non-competes, exclusivity, employer-tied benefits, and fee-stacked “services” convert dependency into
  durable lock-in.
- **Household multiplication**: care burdens amplify frictions (a missed shift → childcare scramble → arrears → debt → deeper clamp).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any inequality diagnosis under OBA MUST include time and L mechanisms (schedule volatility, wait times, admin friction, essentials
  burden volatility), not only wage/income distributions.
- Policies that ignore time clamp predictably shift burden onto households and are non-conforming in the lifeworld layer.

---

## B05.CH03.P08.S02 — Non-prescriptive approach: measure distribution without role prescriptions

OBA prohibits prescribing who should do care. The system must detect distributional imbalances and clamp patterns without enforcing a
single family model or cultural norm.

### Measurement stance (binding)
- Measure **distribution of burdens** and **constraints**, not “correct roles.”
- Use bounded signals:
  - time poverty proxies (schedule predictability, commuting time, admin burden, waiting times),
  - care access gaps (coverage, lead times, drop-offs),
  - cost burdens linked to care (fees, co-pays, transport, unpaid time burdens via surveys).
- Use cohort-level outputs with join-safety and suppression; avoid person-level behavioural dossiers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Measurement must be privacy-minimal and contestable; if distribution analysis requires invasive profiling, it must be redesigned.
- Reports must not embed normative judgments (“good parent behaviour”); they must point to structural frictions and clamp points.

## B05.CH03.P08.S03 — Remedies: rights, childcare, schedule room, portability, income buffers

Remedies must expand choice space by removing frictions and improving exit power, not by telling households how to behave.

### Remedy set (binding directions)
- **Care rights**: baseline entitlements with low proof burden and anti-stigma rails (CH03.P07).
- **Childcare commons**: access, quality, and affordability as L-reduction infrastructure (CH03.P04).
- **Schedule room**: predictability rights, limits on schedule capture, compensated variability, portability of time rights (CH03.P03).
- **Portability**: rights follow the person across employers/regions; care access and leave rights cannot be employer-gated.
- **Income and shock buffers**: stabilisers for care shocks and transition events (link CH04); prevent debt cascades and forced exits.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Remedies must be evaluated on tails: reductions in time poverty and care-shock cascades, not only average uptake.
- If a remedy increases administrative friction or conditionality burden, it is drifting into clamp and must be redesigned.

---

## B05.CH03.P08.S04 — Failure mode: moralising/disciplining instead of removing structural frictions

A core failure mode is to treat unequal care burdens as a behavioural problem: policies that discipline households (surveillance,
conditionality, stigma) while leaving time and L clamps untouched.

### Failure mechanism
- **Stigma governance**: shame-based eligibility and compliance tests reduce uptake and increase hidden burdens.
- **Behavioural scoring drift**: monitoring of parenting/care choices becomes a control layer and a data factory.
- **Mis-targeting**: “family values” narratives substitute for capacity and access reforms; frictions remain.

### Hard rule (binding)
- Any care policy that relies on coercion, profiling, or stigma to manage demand is non-conforming in CH03.
- If distributional inequality persists because frictions are left intact, the system must treat this as structural failure and
  prioritise friction removal over messaging or discipline.
