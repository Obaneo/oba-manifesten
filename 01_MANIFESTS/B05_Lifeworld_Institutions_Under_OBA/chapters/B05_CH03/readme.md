# B05.CH03 — Time, care, and social reproduction: maintaining people and communities as capacity (not a residual)

This chapter specifies **time/care/social reproduction** as an **order-critical lifeworld layer**. Under OBA, care capacity and time
sovereignty are not “private residuals” to be externalised to households after markets and institutions take what they need. They are
infrastructure: if care collapses or time is captured, the order reproduces clamp dynamics, hidden burdens (L), and unstable R/B
tails—regardless of how well the macro framework performs on paper.

CH03 is intentionally **anti-moralising** and **anti-totalising**:
- It refuses reduction of care/wellbeing to a single KPI.
- It treats measurement as bounded signals under strict privacy minimalism.
- It requires that institutions reduce frictions and expand choice space, rather than prescribe roles or enforce behavioural scoring.

The chapter binds care/time to **maintenance-first logic** (a lifeworld analogue of ΔG): continuity, staffing, and backlog discipline
are treated as constitutive. If care access and quality cannot be maintained without clamp or surveillance, the implementation must
fail-closed.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing labour and time rules**: use CH03 to constrain workweek design, scheduling rights, and portability of time
  rights so that “flexibility” cannot become schedule capture and hidden L.
- If you are **building childcare/care commons**: use CH03 as the template for baseline access, maintenance-first capacity planning,
  quality/incident discipline, and contestability/appeal rails.
- If you are **auditing lifeworld outcomes**: use CH03’s measurement requirements and failure modes to detect care-washing, privacy
  drift, administrative toll growth, and selective execution.
- If you are **sequencing transition**: treat care capacity and time sovereignty as stabilisers of R/B tails; if they worsen, expect
  sabotage dynamics and legitimacy collapse.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any implementation claiming lifeworld compatibility. If care/time policy increases hidden burdens
(L), worsens R/B tails (time poverty, care shocks, dropout cascades), or requires surveillance/data maximalism to function, it must be
treated as **non-conforming** and must trigger redesign. Measurement must remain privacy-minimal and contestable; where that cannot
be achieved, the implementation must fail-closed (no compatibility claim).
