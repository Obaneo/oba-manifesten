# B05.CH03.P11 — Measurement and publication requirements (lifeworld test for time/care)

This paragraph specifies the measurement and publication surface required to execute CH03 as a lifeworld test. Time/care policy is
not evidenced by intention, budgets, or service promises; it is evidenced by public, reproducible indicators showing whether
households can actually access care, whether capacity is maintained, and whether time burdens and tail risks decline.

Measurement must satisfy two constraints simultaneously:
1) **Sufficient observability** to detect care-washing, capacity decay, and selective execution; and  
2) **Strict privacy minimalism** because care data is high-risk and easily becomes a surveillance rent channel.

The outputs below are therefore mandatory at an aggregated, join-safe level with explicit method_id, benchmark versions, uncertainty
bands where relevant, and a restatement policy.

---

## B05.CH03.P11.S01 — Core statistics: access (coverage), waiting times, quality proxies, incident rates, staffing ratios

Care systems must publish core operational indicators that demonstrate access and maintenance-first capacity.

### Required core statistics (minimum; binding)

**Access / coverage**
- Coverage rates by service domain (childcare, respite, home care, long-term care pathways), with eligibility categories (aggregated).
- Drop-off rates (eligible but not served) and reasons taxonomy (aggregated).

**Waiting times / backlogs**
- Waiting time distributions (median + tails) per pathway; breach counts against published maxima.
- Backlog size/age distributions where applicable; escalation actions triggered.

**Quality proxies**
- Continuity proxies (staff turnover, continuity of caregiver/teacher assignment where relevant).
- Outcome-adjacent proxies suitable for publication (e.g., re-admission proxies in care contexts) only where method discipline exists.
- Complaint/appeal volumes and resolution times.

**Incident rates**
- Safety incidents and serious adverse events (aggregated categories), reporting completeness, and remediation status.

**Staffing ratios**
- Staffing ratios and vacancy rates against minima; overtime prevalence as a capacity stress proxy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- “Dashboard-only” internal reporting is insufficient: these statistics must be publishable (aggregated) with definitions and method_id.
- Persistent breaches of waiting time or staffing minima without a published remediation plan trigger capacity-breach escalation.

## B05.CH03.P11.S02 — Time-friction proxies: schedule predictability, travel time, administrative burden (survey-based)

Time burdens are a primary hidden L mechanism; therefore the care/time layer must publish time-friction proxies.

### Required time-friction proxies (minimum; binding)
- **Schedule predictability**: volatility indicators (short-notice changes, on-call prevalence, cancellation rates) at sector/cohort
  levels (aggregated; derived from labour reporting where available).
- **Travel time**: typical travel time to childcare/care access points by region; access deserts detection (aggregated).
- **Administrative burden**: time spent on applications, re-assessments, appeals; number of required interactions; lead time to decision
  (survey-based, sampled, with uncertainty bands).
- **Waiting-time-to-time-cost mapping**: treat queues and appointment delays as time rationing; publish distributions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Time-friction measurement MUST be bounded and privacy-minimal (sampling/surveys + aggregates). If the only method is invasive
  tracking, the design is non-conforming and must be redesigned.
- If time-friction rises while nominal access/coverage claims improve, the system must treat this as likely care-washing or hidden L
  shifting and trigger audit.

## B05.CH03.P11.S03 — Cohort profiles: impact on R/B tails (care costs and time-poverty correlates)

The lifeworld test is distributional. Care/time policy must demonstrate tail stabilisation, not only average service use.

### Required cohort profiles (minimum; binding)
- R/B tail proxies correlated with care:
  - time poverty prevalence (survey-based),
  - care cost burdens (fees, co-pays, transport costs) as a share of available resources,
  - crisis indicators (forced job exits due to care gaps, arrears triggered by care shocks—aggregated proxies where available).
- Breakdowns by cohort and region within join-safety constraints (e.g., household type, caregiving burden category, disability-related
  needs categories, not identity profiling).
- Trend indicators: whether tails stabilise (lower volatility, fewer crisis dropouts) following interventions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Cohort visibility is mandatory to detect selective execution; “national averages” are insufficient evidence.
- If cohort tails worsen (more time poverty, higher care-related dropouts) without external shock explanation, redesign is mandatory.

## B05.CH03.P11.S04 — Privacy minimalism: aggregates, suppression, no personal dossiers beyond care necessity

Care measurement is high-risk. The publication surface must be designed to prevent re-identification and surveillance drift.

### Privacy rails (binding)
- **Aggregates first**: publish only cohort/region aggregates; apply small-n suppression.
- **Join safety**: prevent linkage across tables that re-identifies individuals or micro-groups.
- **No personal dossiers**: no individual longitudinal care profiles beyond strict service necessity; no behavioural scoring regimes.
- **Purpose limitation**: data used for care delivery and audit only; prohibit secondary commercial reuse.
- **Retention caps**: limit how long identifiable care data is stored; publish retention policies.

### Conformance rule
- If privacy rails cannot be maintained (due to tooling, vendor demands, or policy design), the measurement system must fail-closed:
  no compatibility claim and no scaling until redesigned.
