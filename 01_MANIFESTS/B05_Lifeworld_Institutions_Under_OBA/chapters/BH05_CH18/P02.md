# B05.CH18.P02 — Failure-mode taxonomy: five classes and their causal logic

This paragraph defines the failure-mode taxonomy used throughout CH18 and specifies the causal logic that makes each class structurally
predictable. The taxonomy is not psychological labeling; it is an operational classification system that links lifeworld symptoms to
mechanisms and therefore to measurable indicators, escalation paths, and repair actions. “Perception” is treated as a signal when it
consistently maps to friction and tail dynamics; it is not dismissed as propaganda.

---

<a id="b05ch18p02s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P02.S01 — Resentment: experienced injustice/friction despite macro claims (perception ≠ propaganda, but a signal)

### Core causal logic
Resentment emerges when households and workers experience persistent **high L**, **high volatility/tails**, or **access humiliation**
while the system claims success via macro dashboards or abstract principles. The mismatch produces disbelief and antagonism. This is not a
communications failure by default; it is often a **measurement–experience divergence** caused by cohort fractures or hidden time burdens.

### Mechanism chain (canonical)
`L stays high/volatile OR time burden rises OR access humiliates`  
→ `cohort-level tail events persist (arrears/lockouts/wait spikes)`  
→ `dashboard claims lose credibility`  
→ `resentment and non-cooperation increase`  
→ `implementation becomes harder; sabotage/evasion susceptibility rises`

### Required classification fields (template)
- `resentment_trigger_surface` ∈ {L_level, L_volatility, tails, admin_friction, status_shock, symbolism_over_materiality}
- `affected_cohorts[]` (aggregated)
- `evidence_proxies[]` (friction indices, drop-off, appeal rates, wait-time spikes, cohort fractures)
- `repair_path_ref` (L-first correction, friction reduction, restatements; P05/P20)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Resentment **MUST** be treated as an early-warning signal when it aligns with measurable friction/tail proxies.
- Systems **MUST NOT** treat resentment as “misinformation by default”; doing so is a drift/capture risk (epistemic closure).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **FM-RES-01 (Proxy linkage):** resentment signals are linked to at least one measurable friction/tail proxy.
- **FM-RES-02 (Cohort specificity):** affected cohorts are specified; “people are angry” is non-conform.

## B05.CH18.P02.S02 — Sabotage: active interference via non-compliance, strikes, understaffing, slow-roll, vandalism

### Core causal logic
Sabotage emerges when key actors perceive loss (status, autonomy, rents) or overload (capacity collapse) and respond by degrading
execution—sometimes subtly (paper compliance), sometimes overtly (strikes, vandalism). Sabotage is amplified by weak logging, weak
separation of powers, and poor capacity protection (ΔG of governance).

### Mechanism chain (canonical)
`incentive threat or overload`  
→ `execution degraded (slow-roll, data quality decay, understaffing)`  
→ `cycle times/backlogs rise; incidents rise`  
→ `lifeworld tails worsen`  
→ `legitimacy falls; conflict escalates`  
→ `hard stops or emergency measures become likely`

### Required classification fields (template)
- `sabotage_type` ∈ {operational, labor, political, market}
- `integrity_signal_set[]` (log anomalies, missing lineage, silent edits, signature mismatches)
- `performance_fractures[]` (cycle time spikes, backlog spikes without external shock)
- `escalation_path_ref` (forensic audit, governance reset, temporary intervention; P07/P08/P20)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Sabotage signals **MUST** trigger integrity and forensic audit pathways; treating them as “noise” is non-conform.
- Capacity protection (ΔG of governance) **MUST** be ringfenced; otherwise sabotage is structurally incentivized.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **FM-SAB-01 (Integrity link):** sabotage classification includes integrity indicators and logging references.
- **FM-SAB-02 (External shock check):** performance fractures are evaluated against external shock baselines.

<a id="b05ch18p02s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P02.S03 — Evasion: extraction shifts into new forms/channels (legal/technical/financial/international)

### Core causal logic
Evasion is systemic under any constraint regime: caps and rails change payoffs, so actors search for new claim routes and semantic
arbitrage. Evasion is not “criminal exception”; it is an expected adaptation pressure requiring versioned taxonomies and channel
governance.

### Mechanism chain (canonical)
`caps/rails constrain old extraction routes`  
→ `semantic/structural/channel innovation`  
→ `new fee stacks, new contracts, new rails (platform/fintech/crypto), new offshore structures`  
→ `extraction persists under new labels`  
→ `lifeworld L/tails reappear; legitimacy erodes`

### Required classification fields (template)
- `shift_family` ∈ {legal, technical, financial, international}
- `pattern_type` (reclassification, bundling, API gating, fee-stack depth, synthetics, treaty shopping, nominee layers)
- `signal_set[]` (contract signatures, graph signatures, timing signatures)
- `remedy_route_ref` (reclassification, channel controls, presumptions under opacity; P11)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Evasion **MUST** be modeled and detected via shift taxonomy; “we assume compliance” is non-conform.
- Under opacity/obstruction, presumptions **MUST** fail-closed; otherwise evasion wins by withholding evidence.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **FM-EVA-01 (Taxonomy):** evasion is classified using shift_family + pattern_type + signals.
- **FM-EVA-02 (Remedy routing):** each detected pattern maps to a remedy route and auditpack.

<a id="b05ch18p02s04"></a>
## B05.CH18.P02.S04 — Drift/capture: institutions shift toward self-preservation, data maximalism, vendor lock-in, exception culture

### Core causal logic
Drift/capture is the slow failure mode: institutions evolve toward self-preservation and risk avoidance, inflating procedures and data
collection, creating exception cultures, and becoming vendor-dependent. Contestability weakens and measurement becomes performative. Over
time this recreates E surfaces (bureaucratic tolls, vendor rents) and produces legitimacy fracture.

### Mechanism chain (canonical)
`measurement becomes goal (Goodhart) + fear of error`  
→ `procedure inflation + data maximalism`  
→ `administrative L rises; access becomes gatekeeping ecosystem`  
→ `tooling complexity + vendor lock-in`  
→ `exceptions proliferate; selective execution grows`  
→ `capture forms (economic/political/epistemic/cultural) consolidate`

### Required classification fields (template)
- `drift_capture_surface` ∈ {metric_drift, data_maximalism, procedure_inflation, tooling_capture, exception_culture, selective_execution}
- `evidence_signals[]` (cycle time up, drop-off up, appeal backlog up, privacy incidents, suppressed findings, vendor dominance graphs)
- `correction_path_ref` (simplification sprints, data-minimalism audits, unbundling tooling, audit-on-auditor; P13/P15)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Drift and capture **MUST** be treated as predictable system dynamics; “good intentions” are not a safeguard.
- Data maximalism **MUST** be treated as a failure mode, not as progress; microdata publication is prohibited.
- Exception culture **MUST** be tracked and bounded; repeated exceptions are a capture red flag.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **FM-DRF-01 (Administrative L):** drift classification includes admin burden proxies (cycle time, paperwork, drop-off).
- **FM-DRF-02 (Vendor dominance):** tooling/procurement capture includes vendor dominance indicators and contract/change logs.

---

### Output format (taxonomy matrix)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** maintain a taxonomy matrix (table or structured list) with:

- `failure_mode_class` | `core_mechanism` | `primary_proxies` | `escalation_trigger` | `repair_path_ref`

Where classes include resentment, sabotage, evasion, bureaucratic drift, capture (drift and capture may share a section but must remain
distinguishable in outputs).
