# B05.CH18.P13 — Bureaucratic drift: detection and correction

This paragraph specifies **early-warning detection** and **structural correction** for bureaucratic drift. Drift is detected when
administrative L rises, when dashboards proliferate without redesign or restatements, and when privacy scope creeps. Correction is not
culture change; it is a technical intervention: simplification sprints, data-minimalism audits, procedure deprecation, and tooling
unbundling—executed with time bounds, audit logs, and recertification gates.

---

<a id="b05ch18p13s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P13.S01 — L via administration: rising cycle time, paperwork burden, drop-off, appeal backlog

### Core detection logic
Administrative load is lifeworld cost. Drift is detected when processes become slower, more document-heavy, and more failure-prone—forcing
people into intermediaries or abandonment.

### Required admin-L indicators (minimum set; aggregated)
- `cycle_time_band` by process class (applications, switching, disputes, procurement approvals)
- `paperwork_burden_proxy`:
  - step counts, document count bands, repeated-proof incidence
- `drop_off_rate_band`:
  - started vs completed processes
- `appeal_backlog_band` and `appeal_duration_band`
- `interim_relief_delay_band` (if relief exists)
- `intermediary_dependency_proxy`:
  - share of cases needing assistance or third-party help (aggregated category)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Administrative L indicators **MUST** be monitored and published (aggregated). Rising admin L is a breach signal, not “process noise”.
- If admin L rises beyond thresholds, scaling **MUST** be blocked for the affected domain until simplification is executed and re-tested.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-DET-01 (Coverage):** at least two process classes have cycle time + drop-off + appeal duration measures.
- **DRF-DET-02 (Thresholds):** admin-L thresholds exist that trigger mandatory correction.

<a id="b05ch18p13s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P13.S02 — “Dashboard without remedy”: lots of reporting, little redesign, few restatements

### Core detection logic
Drift is detected when institutions optimize for reporting outputs rather than repair. The signature is an expanding dashboard with
unchanged procedures, persistent failures, and missing restatements.

### Required “dashboard without remedy” indicators (minimum set)
- `reporting_volume_proxy` (count of dashboards/reports produced; category bands)
- `redesign_action_rate`:
  - number of procedure/contract/tool changes per reporting cycle (bands)
- `restatement_rate`:
  - frequency of restatements when errors are found (should exist; zero is suspicious)
- `incident_to_repair_lag_band`:
  - time from incident detection to implemented redesign
- `open_issues_backlog_band`:
  - persistent known issues without remediation

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Reporting without redesign is non-conform: dashboards **MUST** be linked to a repair pipeline.
- Restatements **MUST** occur when errors are found; absence indicates suppression or denial.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-DET-03 (Repair coupling):** each reporting cycle links to at least one redesign action or a justified “no change” with evidence.
- **DRF-DET-04 (Restatement discipline):** restatement policy exists and produces non-zero outputs when errors occur.

<a id="b05ch18p13s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P13.S03 — Privacy incidents: scope creep, secondary use, identity-linkage without necessity

### Core detection logic
Data maximalism is a drift vector. Drift is detected via privacy incidents and scope creep: expanded identity linkage, secondary use, and
join-unsafe datasets.

### Required privacy drift indicators (minimum set; aggregated)
- `privacy_incident_count_band` by class:
  - scope creep, secondary use, breach, unauthorized access, join-safety violation
- `data_field_growth_proxy`:
  - growth in required fields/documents over time
- `identity_linkage_expansion_proxy`:
  - number of datasets joined to identity (categorical)
- `secondary_use_register_entries`:
  - recorded secondary uses and their justifications
- `necessity_justification_failure_count`:
  - data expansions without logged necessity rationale

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Privacy incidents **MUST** trigger hard stop/rollback actions when integrity or join safety is breached (P20 stop rule B).
- Identity linkage **MUST** be necessity-bounded; “nice to have” linkage is non-conform.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-DET-05 (Incident log):** privacy incidents are logged with classification and time-to-repair.
- **DRF-DET-06 (Necessity gate):** data expansions require logged necessity; missing logs trigger non-conformance flags.

<a id="b05ch18p13s04"></a>
## B05.CH18.P13.S04 — Correction: simplification sprints, data-minimalism audits, deprecate procedures, tooling unbundling

### Core correction logic
Correction is executed, not debated. Drift is corrected via time-bounded sprints that remove steps, reduce proofs, narrow data collection,
and simplify tooling and procurement dependencies.

### Required correction actions (minimum set)
- `simplification_sprints`:
  - remove steps, reduce document requirements, standardize templates, remove exceptions
- `data_minimalism_audits`:
  - field-by-field necessity review; delete non-essential collection; enforce join-safety
- `procedure_deprecation`:
  - sunset legacy procedures and contract forms; migrate to standard templates
- `tooling_unbundling`:
  - replace monolithic vendor systems with modular, auditable components; enforce open standards and export
- `recertification`:
  - re-run lifeworld metrics and admin-L indicators post-correction; publish restatement

### Required sprint fields (template)
- `sprint_id`
- `scope` (processes/tools/data fields)
- `targets` (cycle time, drop-off, appeal backlog, data fields removed)
- `time_bounds` (start/end)
- `owner_roles[]`
- `decision_log_ref` + `restatement_ref`
- `retest_results_ref`

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Correction actions **MUST** be time-bounded and measured; “we will simplify” is non-conform.
- Procedures and tools **MUST** be deprecated when they cause rising admin L or privacy risk; indefinite coexistence sustains drift.
- After correction, results **MUST** be restated and re-tested; otherwise drift is only renamed.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-COR-01 (Sprint execution):** at least one simplification sprint is executed when thresholds breach.
- **DRF-COR-02 (Field reduction):** data-minimalism audits show concrete field removals or justified retention.
- **DRF-COR-03 (Recertification):** scaling resumes only after retest results are within thresholds.

---

### Output format (drift dashboard + correction log)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** emit:

1) **Admin-L drift dashboard**
- `process_type` | `cycle_time_band` | `drop_off_rate_band` | `appeal_backlog_band` | `paperwork_proxy` | `threshold_breached`

2) **Repair coupling dashboard**
- `reporting_cycle` | `reports_published` | `redesign_actions` | `restatements` | `incident_to_repair_lag_band`

3) **Privacy drift dashboard**
- `incident_class` | `count_band` | `time_to_repair_band` | `identity_linkage_proxy` | `necessity_failures`

4) **Correction sprint log**
- `sprint_id` | `scope` | `targets` | `time_bounds` | `outputs` (procedure deprecated/tool unbundled/data removed) | `retest_ref`

If these cannot be produced, drift correction is not operational and CH18 is non-conform.
