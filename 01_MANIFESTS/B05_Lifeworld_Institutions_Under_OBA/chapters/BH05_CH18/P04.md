# B05.CH18.P04 — Resentment: detection and indicators (early warning)

This paragraph specifies **early-warning detection** for resentment failure modes. Resentment is not measured as sentiment; it is inferred
from **cohort fractures**, **friction proxies**, and **dashboard–experience divergence**. The goal is to detect resentment before it becomes
political rupture or sabotage fuel. Indicators must be aggregated, privacy-safe, and procedurally collected. They must trigger repair
paths (P05) and stop rules (P20) when repeated.

---

<a id="b05ch18p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P04.S01 — Cohort fractures: certain regions/occupations see L rise or tails worsen relative to median gains

### Core detection logic
Resentment concentrates where reforms do not deliver. Cohort fractures are the primary early-warning signal: some regions, occupations, or
household cohorts experience rising L, higher volatility, or worse tails while medians improve.

### Required cohort-fracture indicators (minimum set)
For each cohort dimension (region / occupation / household type / dependency):
- `L_component_delta_band` vs median (housing, energy, care, digital)
- `RB_delta_band` vs median
- tail indicators vs median:
  - `arrears_incidence_band`
  - `lockout_incidence_band` (eviction/disconnection/account lockout)
  - `wait_time_exceedance_band`
  - `cost_spike_incidence_band`
- `time_friction_delta_band` (admin time, dispute duration)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Cohort fracture indicators **MUST** be computed and published in aggregated form; aggregate-only reporting is non-conform.
- If fractures persist beyond thresholds, the system **MUST** route to L-first correction and friction reduction (P05) and may block
  scaling (P20).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-DET-01 (Cohort splits):** at least two cohort dimensions are reported (region + occupation recommended).
- **RES-DET-02 (Tail comparison):** at least one tail indicator is compared vs median, not only level.

<a id="b05ch18p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P04.S02 — Friction indicators: cycle times, drop-off, appeal rates, wait-time spikes, erroneous decisions

### Core detection logic
Friction is resentment’s engine. If access requires time, repeated proof, digital hurdles, or humiliating procedures, resentment rises
even if formal rights exist.

### Required friction indicators (minimum set)
- `cycle_time_band` for key processes (applications, switching, claims, disputes)
- `drop_off_rate_band` (started vs completed processes; aggregated)
- `appeal_volume_band` and `appeal_backlog_band`
- `wait_time_spike_frequency_band` (queue exceedances beyond benchmark)
- `error_rate_band`:
  - erroneous decisions identified via reversals/restatements
- `interim_relief_usage_rate_band` (if relief exists; absence is a signal itself)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Friction indicators **MUST** be tracked as lifeworld L; rising friction is a breach signal, not “process noise”.
- When friction spikes, the system **MUST** trigger simplification and interim relief pathways (P05) and publish restatements.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-DET-03 (Process coverage):** at least two key process types have cycle time + drop-off indicators.
- **RES-DET-04 (Error visibility):** error/reversal/restatement rates are measured and published (aggregated).

## B05.CH18.P04.S03 — “Non-belief” as a signal: mismatch between dashboards and experience reporting (procedurally collected)

### Core detection logic
Non-belief is not treated as propaganda by default. It is treated as a **mismatch signal**: dashboards claim improvement while
procedurally collected experience signals report persistent harm or access failure.

### Required mismatch indicators (minimum set)
- `dashboard_claim_set`:
  - which metrics are publicly claimed to have improved (L shares, tails, time friction)
- `experience_signal_set` (procedurally collected; not social media scraping):
  - challenge/objection usage rates
  - complaint sampling outcomes (structured)
  - “unable to access” reports (reason-coded)
  - service incident reporting completeness
- `mismatch_score_band`:
  - method-defined discrepancy measure between claimed deltas and experience signals

### Procedural constraints (binding)
- Experience inputs **MUST** be sampling-based or aggregated.
- Experience inputs **MUST NOT** be used for behavioural scoring or eligibility scoring.
- Collection must be necessity-bounded and logged (privacy rails).

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- If mismatch persists, the system **MUST** trigger forensic review: either the dashboard is wrong (measurement error/drift) or the
  experience is capturing an unmeasured tail (hidden friction).
- The response **MUST** be repair (restatement, redesign), not communications management.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-DET-05 (Procedural collection):** experience signals come from defined processes (appeals, sampling), not ad hoc sentiment mining.
- **RES-DET-06 (Repair trigger):** mismatch above threshold triggers restatement and redesign actions.

## B05.CH18.P04.S04 — Media/platform signals: rising misinformation susceptibility at concrete friction points (no moralizing)

### Core detection logic
Misinformation susceptibility is treated as an indicator of **concrete friction points** where trust is broken and explanatory gaps open.
The aim is not moral judgment; it is to identify which lifeworld surfaces are producing vulnerability.

### Allowed media/platform indicators (aggregated; minimal)
- `topic_linked_susceptibility_proxy`:
  - increases in false-claim uptake **specifically tied** to concrete friction domains (e.g., benefits delays, energy billing, housing
    allocation, identity lockouts)
- `platform_dispute_cluster_proxy`:
  - clustering of disputes/complaints around certain platforms/providers
- `narrative_gap_proxy`:
  - high incidence of “cannot understand decision” or “no reason given” reports

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Media/platform indicators **MUST** be anchored to concrete friction points; broad “misinformation is rising” is non-actionable.
- Indicators **MUST** not justify censorship-by-default; the remedy is to fix friction, publish reasons, and provide contestability and
  repair paths.
- Use only aggregated/procedurally derived signals; do not create surveillance via social listening.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-DET-07 (Domain anchoring):** signals are tied to a sector/process domain with a repair path.
- **RES-DET-08 (No moralizing):** outputs are framed as vulnerability at friction points, not as moral evaluation of cohorts.

---

### Output format (resentment early-warning dashboard)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** emit a resentment dashboard (table(s) or structured lists) with:

1) **Cohort fracture table**
- `cohort_dimension` | `cohort_id` | `L_delta_vs_median` | `RB_delta_vs_median` | `tail_delta_vs_median` | `time_friction_delta`

2) **Friction table**
- `process_type` | `cycle_time_band` | `drop_off_rate_band` | `appeal_rate_band` | `error_rate_band` | `wait_time_spike_freq`

3) **Mismatch table**
- `dashboard_claim_metric` | `experience_signal` | `mismatch_score_band` | `trigger_action`

4) **Domain-linked vulnerability table**
- `domain` | `signal_proxy` | `candidate_friction_point` | `repair_path_ref`

If these cannot be produced, resentment detection is not operational and CH18 is non-conform.
