# B05.CH18.P05 — Resentment: mitigation (what works structurally)

This paragraph specifies **structural mitigations** for resentment failure modes. The design principle is simple: resentment does not
resolve through narrative management; it resolves when the lifeworld constraint actually moves (L down, tails down, time burden down) and
when repair is visible and contestable. Mitigation therefore prioritizes L-first correction, friction reduction, transparency with repair,
and plurality/contestability infrastructure.

---

<a id="b05ch18p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P05.S01 — L-first correction: accelerate essentials price rails/commons exits before additional redistribution narratives

### Core mitigation logic
When resentment arises from unchanged essentials L or worsening volatility, the fastest legitimacy repair is **L-first**: reduce the
binding constraint in essentials through price rails and real exit options (commons/public alternatives). Redistribution narratives without
L movement are read as symbolic and are brittle.

### Required actions (minimum set)
- accelerate `essentials_price_rails` in the domains driving cohort fractures (housing/energy/digital/care)
- expand `commons_exits`:
  - concrete switching paths out of lock-in contracts
  - portability and anti-punitive fee enforcement
- prioritize `tail_stabilization`:
  - prevent arrears cascades and lockouts with interim protections
- publish `cohort_targeting_notes`:
  - explain which cohorts are being repaired first and why (non-PR; rule-based)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- If cohort fractures show L/tails worsening, L-first correction **MUST** take precedence over additional redistribution complexity.
- Commons exits **MUST** be real (measurable switching cost reduction), not theoretical rights.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MIT-01 (L movement):** mitigation plan targets a specific L component and specifies measurable deltas.
- **RES-MIT-02 (Exit realism):** plan includes portability/switching metrics and enforcement owners.

<a id="b05ch18p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P05.S02 — Friction reduction: one-stop shop, standard procedures, minimal evidentiary burden, fast interim relief

### Core mitigation logic
Administrative friction is hidden L. If access is slow or humiliating, resentment persists even with improved formal entitlements.
Mitigation requires procedural simplification and time-bounded correction with interim relief.

### Required actions (minimum set)
- `one_stop_interface`:
  - unified entry point across essential domains (with offline/assisted channels)
- `standard_procedures`:
  - standard templates, default rules, fewer exceptions; reduce discretion surfaces
- `minimal_evidence_burden`:
  - accept standardized evidence classes; avoid repeated proofs; “once-only” principle where possible
- `time_bounds`:
  - strict cycle time limits for decisions and disputes
- `fast_interim_relief`:
  - provisional relief where delay causes irreversible harm (disconnection/eviction/identity lockout)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Friction reduction **MUST** be executed as a design change (procedure and system), not as staff exhortation.
- Interim relief **MUST** be available and time-bounded; otherwise time bounds become coercive gates.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MIT-03 (Cycle-time targets):** plan includes cycle time and drop-off reduction targets (bands) with method binding.
- **RES-MIT-04 (Interim relief):** provisional relief rules exist for high-harm domains and are logged.

<a id="b05ch18p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P05.S03 — Transparency with repair: public incident logs + restatements + visible repair path (not “PR”)

### Core mitigation logic
Trust is rebuilt when failures are visible and corrected. Transparency must be coupled to repair: incident disclosure, restatements, and
tracked remediation. “Transparency” without repair becomes cynicism fuel.

### Required actions (minimum set)
- `public_incident_logs` (aggregated):
  - errors, outages, privacy incidents, decision reversals, backlog spikes
- `restatements`:
  - corrected calculations, corrected eligibility decisions, corrected datasets/methods (versioned)
- `repair_path_visibility`:
  - what changed, why, version id, expected deltas, retest results
- `no_pr_substitution`:
  - explicit rule: communications cannot replace repair outputs

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Incident logs **MUST** be published in aggregated form with reason codes and time-to-repair.
- Restatements **MUST** be versioned and linked to method_id; silent edits are an integrity breach.
- Repair paths **MUST** be visible and trackable; otherwise resentment converts into sabotage risk.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MIT-05 (Incident publication):** incident logs exist and include time-to-repair bands.
- **RES-MIT-06 (Restatement discipline):** restatement process exists and prohibits silent edits.

## B05.CH18.P05.S04 — Plurality and contestability: strengthen dissent/challenge infrastructure rather than communications management

### Core mitigation logic
Systems fail when dissent is pathologized. Resentment escalates when people cannot challenge decisions, when critique is treated as
disinformation, or when institutions become epistemically closed. Mitigation requires strong contestability infrastructure and plurality
of challenge channels.

### Required actions (minimum set)
- strengthen `objection_and_appeal_infrastructure`:
  - low-friction challenge paths, independent ombuds, anti-retaliation enforcement
- publish `appeals_stats` (aggregated):
  - volumes, reversal rates, cycle times, cohort patterns
- ensure `independent_audit_and_meta_audit`:
  - audit-on-the-auditor to prevent suppression and selective execution
- maintain `plurality_of_interfaces`:
  - multiple access channels (offline/assisted), multiple accountability routes (not one gatekeeper)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Contestability **MUST** be treated as legitimacy infrastructure, not as an “added cost”.
- Communications management **MUST NOT** be used to suppress dissent; it is a capture vector.
- Anti-retaliation protections **MUST** be enforceable; otherwise contestability is performative.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MIT-07 (Independent arbiter):** an independent ombuds/arbiter exists with time-bounded correction powers.
- **RES-MIT-08 (Plurality):** multiple access and challenge channels exist; single-gate systems are resentment amplifiers.

---

### Output format (resentment mitigation plan)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** be able to emit a resentment mitigation plan (table or structured list) with:

- `resentment_surface` (L/tails/time/status/symbolism) | `trigger_indicator_ref` | `structural_action` | `owner_role` |
  `time_bound` | `expected_delta_proxy` | `publication_artifact` (incident log/restatement/audit note)

If mitigations are not operationalized into owners, time bounds, and expected deltas, the system is non-conform.
