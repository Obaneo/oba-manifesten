# B05.CH18.P16 — Human agency: why failure modes cannot be “measured away”

This paragraph states a core constraint for lifeworld governance: **human agency is causal**. People react to clamps, shame, uncertainty,
and perceived injustice. Those reactions generate resentment, sabotage, evasion, and drift—not as “noise” but as predictable causal
responses. Therefore failure modes cannot be eliminated by more measurement, more monitoring, or behavioral scoring. Attempts to “optimize”
behavior via totalization create new E axes (surveillance rents, gatekeeping scores) and fracture legitimacy. The correct implication is:
expand choice and exit, keep systems contestable, and use experience signals as procedurally embedded qualitative input alongside
quantitative metrics.

---

<a id="b05ch18p16s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P16.S01 — Agency as a constraint: people react to clamps, shame, uncertainty; that is causality, not noise

### Core mechanism
Implementations produce behavior. When people face clamps (hard constraints), shame gates (humiliation), or uncertainty (unpredictable
rules/timing), they adapt: withdraw, resist, game, sabotage, or seek alternative channels. Treating these reactions as “irrationality” is
a category error: the reaction is part of the system’s causal model.

### Canonical causal chain
`clamp + humiliation + uncertainty`  
→ `adaptive response` (avoidance/gaming/resistance)  
→ `failure mode activation` (resentment/sabotage/evasion/drift)  
→ `lifeworld integrity degrades unless design changes`

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Systems **MUST** model agency as a constraint in design; assume strategic and dignity-protecting behavior.
- Any intervention that increases humiliation or uncertainty **MUST** be treated as a risk amplifier and monitored.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **AG-01 (Agency modeled):** designs include friction/shame/uncertainty surfaces as explicit risk variables.
- **AG-02 (Tail linkage):** agency reactions are linked to tails/time burdens, not only to averages.

<a id="b05ch18p16s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P16.S02 — Limits on nudging/scoring: behavioral steering via monitoring creates new E axes and legitimacy fracture

### Core mechanism
When institutions respond to failure modes by expanding monitoring and behavioral nudges, they often create a new extraction axis: access
becomes conditional on “good behavior” measured by scores. This produces surveillance drift, gatekeeping, and legitimacy rupture—especially
for vulnerable cohorts.

### Typical harmful forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `behavioral_scoring` (eligibility or priority tied to monitored behavior)
- `nudge_overreach` (manipulative choice architecture replaces rights and exits)
- `identity_linkage_totalization` (join everything to identity “for compliance”)
- `punishment_by_metric` (service denial via low scores or flags)
- `surveillance_rent` (vendors monetize monitoring infrastructure)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Behavioral scoring for essentials **MUST NOT** be used; it is a new gatekeeping toll regime.
- Monitoring **MUST** remain necessity-bounded, aggregated, and privacy-safe; microdata publication is prohibited.
- “Nudges” **MUST NOT** substitute for structural fixes (L reduction, exit rails, contestability).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **AG-03 (No scoring):** explicit prohibition of behavioral scoring exists and is auditable.
- **AG-04 (Privacy rails):** join safety and data-minimalism rules are enforced (violations trigger rollback).

<a id="b05ch18p16s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P16.S03 — Experience signals: procedurally embedded qualitative input is required alongside quantitative metrics

### Core mechanism
Quantitative dashboards cannot fully capture humiliation, uncertainty, and procedural hostility. Yet ignoring experience produces blind
spots that become resentment and sabotage fuel. The solution is not sentiment mining; it is **procedural embedding** of experience signals
as contestable inputs.

### Allowed experience signal channels (minimum set)
- `appeal_and_objection_logs` (reason-coded; aggregated)
- `structured_complaint_sampling` (method-defined sampling, not open scraping)
- `incident_reports` (service failures, lockouts, delays; aggregated)
- `explanation_failure_reports` (“no reason given”, “cannot understand decision”; reason-coded)

### Procedural constraints (binding)
- Experience signals **MUST** be:
  - aggregated/sampled,
  - reason-coded,
  - tied to repair triggers,
  - and contestable (ability to challenge classification).
- Experience signals **MUST NOT** be used as eligibility scoring or as punitive surveillance.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Implementations **MUST** embed experience inputs procedurally; otherwise they become ad hoc and politicized.
- Experience inputs **MUST** route to redesign and interim relief, not to comms.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **AG-05 (Procedural embedding):** experience channels have method_id and sampling rules.
- **AG-06 (Repair routing):** experience thresholds map to repair actions and time bounds.

## B05.CH18.P16.S04 — Implication: design must expand choice/exit; do not attempt to “optimize” behavior via totalization

### Core implication
If agency is causal and scoring creates new E axes, then the correct design strategy is: expand **choice space**, make **exit real**, and
reduce reliance on coercive compliance. Systems should remove lock-ins, reduce friction, and make contestability and portability
constitutive. Totalization—trying to “optimize society” via universal measurement and steering—produces drift/capture.

### Required design implications (minimum set)
- `exit_rails`:
  - portability, switching assistance, public options/commons exits
- `friction_minimization`:
  - one-stop interfaces, standard procedures, minimal evidence burden
- `contestability_first`:
  - time-bounded appeals, interim relief, anti-retaliation
- `data_minimalism`:
  - collect only what is necessary for auditability; enforce join safety
- `fail_closed_rules`:
  - when integrity/privacy/selection breaches occur, rollback and governance reset are mandatory

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Implementations **MUST** prioritize exit/choice and contestability over behavioral steering.
- Any attempt at totalization (expanding monitoring to steer behavior) **MUST** be treated as a drift/capture risk and blocked unless
  proven necessary under strict rails (rare).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **AG-07 (Exit realism):** exit rails exist and are measurable (switching success/time).
- **AG-08 (Totalization block):** proposals that expand monitoring beyond necessity trigger data-minimalism audit and can be vetoed.

### Output format (agency constraints annex)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** emit an agency constraints annex (table or structured list) with:

- `agency_surface` (clamp/shame/uncertainty) | `risk_indicator_ref` | `prohibited_response` (scoring/totalization) |
  `allowed_response` (exit/contestability/friction reduction) | `repair_trigger_ref`

If agency is not formalized, implementations will default to monitoring-and-scoring escalations, reproducing the failure modes CH18 is
meant to stop.
