# B05.CH18.P08 — Sabotage: mitigation and hard stops

This paragraph specifies **structural mitigations** and **hard stops** for sabotage failure modes. Sabotage is not solved by exhortation;
it is solved by design: capacity protection, separation of powers, procurement discipline, and fail-closed integrity rules. CH18 treats
proven sabotage signatures (especially selective enforcement and integrity breaches) as disqualifying: conformance claims must be
suspended immediately until forensic repair and recertification.

---

<a id="b05ch18p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P08.S01 — Capacity-first: ringfence execution capacity and maintenance budgets (ΔG of governance)

### Core mitigation logic
Many sabotage patterns are enabled by overload. If execution capacity and maintenance are not protected, the system becomes fragile and
easily disrupted by slow-roll, attrition, and backlog cascades. ΔG of governance is a real constraint.

### Required capacity-first actions (minimum set)
- `ringfence_maintenance_budgets`:
  - protected budgets for maintenance/backlog reduction in key services and governance systems (logs, audits, dispute handling)
- `ringfence_execution_capacity`:
  - staffing minima for frontline processing, technical ops, and audit/ombuds functions
- `capacity_backlog_thresholds`:
  - backlog/cycle-time thresholds that auto-trigger capacity injection or scope restriction (fail-closed to auditable coverage)
- `surge_protocol`:
  - surge staffing procedures under spikes; avoid chronic emergency mode

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Maintenance budgets **MUST** be protected; hollowing maintenance is sabotage-by-decay.
- If capacity thresholds are breached, scaling **MUST** be blocked or scope reduced until capacity is restored.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MIT-01 (Ringfence present):** ringfenced budgets and staffing minima are explicitly defined.
- **SAB-MIT-02 (Auto-triggers):** backlog/cycle-time thresholds map to automatic actions (capacity injection or scope restriction).

<a id="b05ch18p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P08.S02 — Separation of powers: strictly separate decision/execution/oversight/appeal; rotate key roles

### Core mitigation logic
Sabotage is enabled when power is concentrated: the same actor decides, executes, audits, and controls appeals. Separation reduces both
corruption and paper compliance.

### Required separation actions (minimum set)
- `role_separation`:
  - decision/mandate ≠ execution ≠ oversight/audit ≠ appeal/ombuds
- `rotation_rules`:
  - rotation cadence for auditors and key approvers; cooling-off periods to reduce capture
- `access_controls`:
  - least-privilege controls on logs, methods, and rule changes; signed changes only
- `exception_controls`:
  - exceptions require dual control, time bounds, and publication in aggregated form

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Separation of powers **MUST** be structural, not nominal: distinct roles, distinct controls, distinct reporting.
- Rotation **MUST** exist; indefinite relationships are capture/sabotage risks.
- Unsigned or unlogged changes to rules/methods **MUST** be treated as integrity breaches.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MIT-03 (Separation):** the four role classes (decision/execution/oversight/appeal) are explicitly distinct and enforceable.
- **SAB-MIT-04 (Change control):** rule/method changes require signing + log entry + version bump.

<a id="b05ch18p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P08.S03 — Procurement rails: standardize vendor lock-in triggers (escrow/open standards) and exit assistance

### Core mitigation logic
Vendor lock-in becomes a sabotage vector: vendors can slow-roll interoperability, withhold logs, or create proprietary dependencies that
break contestability and raise administrative L. Procurement must be treated as an integrity rail.

### Required procurement actions (minimum set)
- `open_standards_mandate`:
  - interoperability standards and portability requirements as contract minima
- `escrow_and_source_access`:
  - escrow/continuity mechanisms for critical tooling; auditable interfaces
- `anti_lock_in_clauses`:
  - exit rights, data export, interface stability, ban on punitive termination fees
- `change_order_controls`:
  - change-order logs, caps, and audits to prevent stealth rent extraction
- `exit_assistance`:
  - operational support to migrate away from vendors without service collapse

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Procurement **MUST** prevent single-vendor dependency on critical rails (logs, identity, dispute systems).
- Vendors **MUST** be audit-compatible; refusal to provide evidence packs or logs is a breach.
- If lock-in triggers fire, the system **MUST** initiate exit assistance and/or contract remedies.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MIT-05 (Portability):** contracts include export/interop clauses and auditable interfaces.
- **SAB-MIT-06 (Change-order visibility):** change orders are logged and reviewable.

<a id="b05ch18p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH18.P08.S04 — Hard stop: proven selective enforcement or integrity breach → immediate suspension of conformance claims

### Hard stop logic (FAIL-CLOSED)
Some sabotage signatures are disqualifying because they destroy the truth condition: you cannot claim conformance without integrity and
non-selectivity.

### Hard stop conditions (minimum)
- `proven_selective_enforcement`:
  - systematic differences by cohort/region/position without declared rule basis and without remediation
- `integrity_breach`:
  - silent edits, unsigned changes, missing lineage, tamper-evidence failure, suppressed incident disclosure
- `audit_suppression`:
  - suppressed findings, blocked audits, intimidation of auditors/ombuds
- `procurement_capture_breach`:
  - vendor dominance with opaque tooling that defeats audit/contestability

### Mandatory actions (binding)
When hard stop triggers:
- `immediate_conformance_suspension`:
  - halt public conformance claims for the affected domain
- `containment`:
  - freeze high-risk changes; preserve logs; restrict channels if needed
- `forensic_audit`:
  - independent audit with published aggregated summary
- `restatement_and_rollback`:
  - rollback or restate affected methods/data/decisions; versioned
- `governance_reset`:
  - rotate roles, rewrite procurement/contracts, reissue procedures
- `recertification`:
  - scaling and conformance claims only resume after recertification evidence

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Hard stop actions **MUST** be automatic and time-bounded; discretion here is capture risk.
- Conformance suspension **MUST** be public (aggregated) with reason codes; secrecy destroys credibility.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-HS-01 (Auto-activation):** hard stop triggers map to automatic suspension + audit + restatement.
- **SAB-HS-02 (Publication):** suspension and audit summaries are published in aggregated form.
- **SAB-HS-03 (Recertification):** resumption requires evidence of integrity restoration and non-selective enforcement.

---

### Output format (sabotage hard-stop register)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** emit a sabotage hard-stop register (table or structured list) with:

- `hard_stop_trigger` | `detected` | `domain_scope` | `action_taken` | `audit_ref` | `restatement_or_rollback_ref` |
  `governance_reset_ref` | `recertification_status`

If hard-stop behavior is not explicit and automatic, sabotage cannot be contained and CH18 is non-conform.
