# B05.CH18.P10 — Evasion: detection (shift taxonomy in a lifeworld context)

This paragraph specifies **evasion detection** as a standardized shift taxonomy applied in a lifeworld context. Detection must not be
limited to “rich people behavior”; it must cover sector implementations, household-facing contracts, and institutional procedures where
extraction can reappear as fees, lock-ins, and clamps. The taxonomy is operational: each shift family is defined by observable signatures
(contracts, graphs, timing, and channel patterns) and routed to auditpacks and remedies (P11). Detection is aggregated and privacy-safe.

---

<a id="b05ch18p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P10.S01 — Legal shifts: reclassification into services/licenses/consultancy; franchise and royalty stacks

### Shift family definition
Legal shifts change **contract form and labels** while preserving economic substance and dependency. In lifeworld terms, this often
appears as new “service fees”, “licenses”, “consultancy”, or franchise-style royalty stacks layered onto essentials.

### Common legal shift patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `rent_to_service_fee` (housing, utilities, platforms)
- `wage_to_consultancy_or_license` (employment compensation relabeling)
- `product_to_subscription_license` (access converted into license with recurring toll)
- `franchise_royalty_stack` (mandatory royalties + fees + supply lock-ins)
- `dividend_to_loan_or_advance` (cash extraction relabeled as credit)

### Detection signatures (minimum)
- `contract_signature`:
  - sudden contract-type migration with same parties/service
  - non-arm’s-length pricing; opaque addenda; unilateral term change rights
- `cashflow_signature`:
  - stable net outflows despite label changes
  - circular flows to related entities
- `timing_signature`:
  - shifts clustered around cap/rail changes or enforcement windows
- `dependency_signature`:
  - service becomes non-optional; exit costs rise

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Legal shifts **MUST** be treated as candidates for substance-over-form reclassification.
- Franchise/royalty stacks **MUST** be audited as E-like toll layers when they attach to dependency.
- Missing contract evidence triggers presumptions (fail-closed against opacity).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVD-LGL-01 (Contract evidence):** detection requires contract template/clauses as evidence class.
- **EVD-LGL-02 (Stack visibility):** royalty/fee stack depth is itemized (effective rate), not headline-only.

## B05.CH18.P10.S02 — Technical shifts: bundling, dark patterns, API gating, pay-to-unlock, identity as a clamp

### Shift family definition
Technical shifts change **access and interface mechanics**: bundling, UI dark patterns, gating APIs, paywalls, identity lockouts, and
pay-to-unlock features that convert dependency into toll extraction or block power.

### Common technical shift patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `bundle_tie_in` (forced bundles for essentials access)
- `dark_pattern_upgrade` (misleading discounts, trap trials, frictionful cancellation)
- `API_gating` / `interop_block` (platform choke points)
- `pay_to_unlock` (fees to regain access, speed, visibility, or compliance)
- `identity_clamp` (lockout from essentials via identity failure or discretionary enforcement)

### Detection signatures (minimum)
- `interface_signature`:
  - increased steps to cancel/switch, asymmetric friction (join easy, leave hard)
- `pricing_signature`:
  - new mandatory fee layers; “optional” features required in practice
- `incident_signature`:
  - lockout incidents, recovery time spikes, dispute spikes
- `dependency_graph_signature`:
  - single chokepoint for access (API, identity provider, verification badge)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Technical shifts **MUST** be handled at rail level (channel governance, portability mandates, forbidden practices).
- Identity as a clamp **MUST NOT** be allowed for essentials: recovery and due process are constitutive.
- Dark patterns **MUST** be treated as E mechanisms and routed to prohibited clauses/standard templates.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVD-TEC-01 (Exit friction):** switching/cancellation time proxies are measured and compared pre/post.
- **EVD-TEC-02 (Lockout discipline):** lockout incidence and recovery time are tracked; spikes trigger audit/remedy.

## B05.CH18.P10.S03 — Financial shifts: securitization, fee stacks, synthetics, leverage as hidden exposure

### Shift family definition
Financial shifts preserve exposure and extraction through leverage, securitization, fee layering, and derivatives—often invisible at the
lifeworld interface but reflected in higher L (fees, pricing power) and fragility (tails).

### Common financial shift patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `fee_stack_deepening` (management/servicing/administration layers)
- `securitization_wrapping` (cashflows repackaged into vehicles; incentives distort)
- `leverage_cashout` (debt used to extract without “income” recognition)
- `synthetic_exposure_replication` (TRS/CFD/options replicating controlled exposure)
- `servicing_clamp` (collections/servicing fees become toll regime)

### Detection signatures (minimum)
- `fee_depth_proxy` and `effective_fee_rate_proxy`
- `servicing_fee_incidence` + escalation ladders
- `leverage_spike_signatures` around policy windows
- `custody_mismatch_signatures` (exposure without title)
- `fragility_signatures` (cost spikes, arrears cascades under small shocks)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Financial shifts **MUST** be detected via exposure look-through and fee stack measurement (all-layer effective rates).
- Where evidence is incomplete, conservative valuation/exposure presumptions apply.
- Servicing/collections stacks **MUST** be treated as E surfaces in lifeworld sectors (bounded/rewritten where needed).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVD-FIN-01 (All-layer fees):** effective fee rates include all mandatory layers.
- **EVD-FIN-02 (Exposure look-through):** synthetics/leverage are mapped to economic exposure (bands acceptable).

## B05.CH18.P10.S04 — International shifts: treaty shopping, IP-box, nominee ownership, offshore conduits (UBC graphs)

### Shift family definition
International shifts move control, attribution, or channels across borders to exploit mismatches and defeat domestic rails. The lifeworld
symptom is persistent extraction with weakened auditability and rising opacity.

### Common international shift patterns (minimum taxonomy)
- `treaty_hub_routing` (withholding avoidance and profit routing)
- `IP_box_licensing` (royalties drain profits; mailbox substance)
- `nominee_layering` (beneficial ownership hidden via trustees/nominees)
- `offshore_conduit_flows` (funds routed through secrecy nodes)
- `redomiciliation_moves` (control center relocation; asset migration)

### Detection signatures (minimum)
- `UBC_graph_signatures`:
  - increased layering depth, secrecy jurisdictions, circular holdings
- `substance_mismatch_signatures`:
  - profit vs activity mismatch in hubs (proxy-based)
- `withholding_avoidance_signatures`:
  - abrupt drop in withholding, shift in payer entities
- `mobility_timing_signatures`:
  - relocation clustered around enforcement/cap events

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- International shifts **MUST** be analyzed using UBC look-through graphs; formal residency labels are insufficient.
- Denial-of-benefits and withholding hooks **MUST** be available under mismatch/opacity.
- Mobility is allowed, but not as arbitrage: obligations follow exposure/control.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVD-INT-01 (UBC graph):** detection includes at least one UBC graph signature when offshore exposure exists.
- **EVD-INT-02 (DoB/withholding readiness):** denial-of-benefits and withholding hooks are specified as remedies.

---

### Output format (shift detection register)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** emit a shift detection register (table or structured list) with:

- `shift_id` | `shift_family` (legal/technical/financial/international) | `pattern_type` | `signature_indicators` |
  `evidence_pack_ref` | `default_presumption` | `remedy_route_ref` | `status`

If shifts are described without signatures, evidence packs, and remedy routing, detection is non-operational and CH18 is non-conform.
