# B05.CH18.P06 — Sabotage: mechanism and forms

This paragraph specifies the **mechanisms and forms** of sabotage failure modes in the lifeworld. Sabotage is defined as **active
interference** with execution quality, integrity, or capacity—ranging from subtle paper compliance to overt disruption. It is structurally
predictable when incentives, status, or rents are threatened, or when execution capacity collapses under overload. Sabotage must be
treated as an integrity and governance problem, not as a “communications” problem.

---

<a id="b05ch18p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P06.S01 — Operational sabotage: paper compliance, deliberate degradation of data quality, execution delays

### Core mechanism
Operational sabotage occurs when executors maintain the appearance of compliance while degrading the actual system: slowing workflows,
breaking data lineage, increasing error rates, or exploiting procedural complexity to stall outcomes.

### Common operational sabotage forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `paper_compliance`:
  - procedures followed formally while outcomes fail (slow-roll, selective processing)
- `data_quality_degradation`:
  - missing fields, corrupted logs, inconsistent identifiers, “lost” documents
- `execution_delay`:
  - deliberate backlog creation, cycle-time inflation, silent queue prioritization
- `silent_edits`:
  - unlogged changes to rules, methods, or data (integrity breach)
- `evidence_withholding`:
  - refusing to produce auditpacks or producing incomplete packs

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Operational sabotage **MUST** be detectable via integrity indicators (logging anomalies, lineage breaks) and must trigger forensic audit.
- Silent edits **MUST** be treated as a hard integrity breach (stop rule B linkage; P20).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MEC-01 (Integrity discipline):** operational sabotage includes explicit log/lineage breach signatures.
- **SAB-MEC-02 (Outcome linkage):** sabotage forms are linked to lifeworld deltas (cycle time up, errors up, tails up).

<a id="b05ch18p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P06.S02 — Labor conflict: failures in key capacities (healthcare, grid ops, administration) in response to overload or status loss

### Core mechanism
Labor conflict becomes sabotage when key capacities degrade because workers experience overload, loss of autonomy/status, or perceived
injustice in implementation. This is not “bad attitude”; it is capacity collapse under misaligned incentives or inadequate maintenance
(ΔG of governance).

### Common labor sabotage forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `understaffing_pressure` (attrition, hiring freezes, burnout cascades)
- `strike_or_work_slowdown` (explicit or implicit)
- `refusal_of_discretionary_effort` (minimum-contract behavior in high-discretion roles)
- `knowledge_withholding` (loss of tacit knowledge due to churn or deliberate non-transfer)
- `compliance_overload` (forms and audits consume operational time)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Implementations **MUST** treat capacity as a primary constraint: ringfence maintenance budgets and execution staffing (P08.S01).
- If labor conflict produces repeated service failures, scaling **MUST** be blocked until capacity and procedure design are repaired.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MEC-03 (Capacity-first):** capacity indicators (staffing, backlog, burnout proxies) are tracked and linked to governance repair.
- **SAB-MEC-04 (ΔG linkage):** labor failure is linked to maintenance/continuity deficits, not moralized.

<a id="b05ch18p06s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P06.S03 — Political sabotage: carve-outs, exceptions, maintenance budget hollowing, vetoes on interop/standards

### Core mechanism
Political sabotage occurs when decision-makers or veto holders undermine compatibility by inserting carve-outs, hollowing maintenance, or
blocking interoperability and standards required for contestability and exit.

### Common political sabotage forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `carve_out_insertion` (exceptions for strategic actors/regions/positions)
- `exception_culture_growth` (temporary exceptions become permanent)
- `maintenance_budget_hollowing` (ΔG collapse by starving ops/repair)
- `veto_on_interop` (blocking open standards, portability, data minimalism rails)
- `selective_enforcement_directives` (pressure to under-enforce against certain positions)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Carve-outs and selective enforcement **MUST** be treated as compatibility breaches (hard stop linkage; P15/P20).
- Maintenance budgets **MUST** be ringfenced; cutting maintenance while claiming success is sabotage-by-decay.
- Interop/standards vetoes **MUST** be logged as governance breaches because they block exit and contestability.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MEC-05 (Exception visibility):** exception registers exist and are publishable (aggregated).
- **SAB-MEC-06 (Maintenance integrity):** maintenance budget deltas are tracked; hollowing triggers escalation.

<a id="b05ch18p06s04"></a>
## B05.CH18.P06.S04 — Market sabotage: price manipulation, supply withholding, intensified lock-in by dominant providers

### Core mechanism
Market sabotage occurs when dominant providers respond to rails by manipulating prices, withholding supply, deepening lock-ins, or
increasing fee stacks to preserve rents. This is predictable where market power exists and contestability is weak.

### Common market sabotage forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `price_spike_or_spread_widening` (unexplained margin spikes)
- `supply_withholding` (capacity withheld to keep scarcity binding)
- `lock_in_intensification` (longer contracts, higher penalties, bundling)
- `fee_stack_deepening` (new mandatory fees layered on top of base price)
- `collusion_signals` (parallel pricing, coordinated contract terms)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Market sabotage **MUST** trigger competition and procurement rails (audits, contract rewrite, public option expansion).
- Lock-in intensification **MUST** be treated as an E-surface escalation and routed to portability mandates and prohibited clauses.
- If market sabotage raises essentials L or tails, scaling **MUST** be blocked until remedies restore contestability.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SAB-MEC-07 (Margin/fee visibility):** effective fee stacks and margin proxies are tracked (aggregated).
- **SAB-MEC-08 (Contestability response):** detected sabotage maps to a remedy path (audit → rewrite → replacement).
