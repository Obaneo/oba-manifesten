# B05.CH18.P03 — Resentment: mechanism (why it emerges)

This paragraph specifies the **mechanisms** that generate resentment in the lifeworld under an OBA implementation. Resentment is treated
as a causal response to experienced constraint, humiliation, and volatility—not as propaganda by default. It arises when the system’s
claimed improvements fail to materialize in **L**, **tails**, and **time burden**, or when status pathways are disrupted without legitimate
replacements. The mechanisms below are written so they can be connected to measurable indicators (P04) and structural mitigation (P05).

---

<a id="b05ch18p03s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P03.S01 — L stays high or unpredictable: essentials costs do not visibly fall, or volatility/tails rise

### Core mechanism
Resentment emerges when **essentials-L** remains high or volatile relative to expectations created by policy claims. Even if averages
improve, cohort-level tails (arrears, lockouts, cost spikes) can worsen. Households experience the system as unfair or deceptive because
the “real constraint” did not move.

### Canonical causal chain
`L level or volatility unchanged` OR `tails worsen`  
→ `R and RB do not visibly improve (within uncertainty)`  
→ `household feels trapped despite “reform”`  
→ `credibility collapses; resentment rises`

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Implementations **MUST** treat persistent L and worsening tails as resentment generators, regardless of macro narrative.
- Improvements **MUST** be visible beyond uncertainty bands for target cohorts; otherwise claims are cosmetic.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MEC-01 (Tail sensitivity):** mechanism explicitly references tails (arrears/lockouts/cost spikes) not only averages.
- **RES-MEC-02 (Cohort divergence):** mechanism requires cohort-level evaluation (fractures), not aggregate-only.

---

<a id="b05ch18p03s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P03.S02 — Administrative friction: “rights” exist, but access requires time, shame, or digital hurdles

### Core mechanism
Rights can exist on paper while access is practically blocked by **time cost**, **proof burden**, and **humiliation** (shame gates, stigma
procedures). Digital-only processes add identity lockout and competence barriers. This creates resentment because the system claims
fairness but imposes coercive friction on the people who need it.

### Canonical causal chain
`procedural complexity + proof burden + long cycle times`  
→ `drop-off + appeals + repeated errors`  
→ `access becomes a gatekeeping ecosystem`  
→ `resentment rises (system seen as hostile)`

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Administrative L (time burden, steps, cycle times) **MUST** be treated as real L and measured.
- Digital-only access **MUST NOT** be allowed for essentials; assisted/offline paths are required.
- Interim relief **MUST** exist where delays create irreversible harm.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MEC-03 (Time-as-L):** mechanism explicitly includes administrative time burden as hidden L.
- **RES-MEC-04 (Humiliation gate):** mechanism includes shame/stigma pathways as functional barriers (must be routable to procedures).

<a id="b05ch18p03s03"></a>
## B05.CH18.P03.S03 — Symbolism > materiality: narratives/rituals without measurable improvement (ΔG/L/RB)

### Core mechanism
Resentment is amplified when the system produces **symbolic rituals** (campaigns, slogans, KPI dashboards, ceremonies) while failing to
deliver measurable improvements in **ΔG (maintenance/continuity)**, **L**, or **RB**. People read this as deception: “they talk, but
nothing gets fixed.”

### Canonical causal chain
`high visibility narrative output` + `low visible repair`  
→ `dashboard without remedy`  
→ `non-belief`  
→ `resentment + susceptibility to counter-narratives increase`

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Symbolic outputs **MUST NOT** be treated as success evidence; only lifeworld deltas count.
- Incident logs and restatements **MUST** exist to show repair is real (not messaging).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MEC-05 (ΔG linkage):** mechanism links resentment to perceived lack of maintenance/repair capacity (ΔG).
- **RES-MEC-06 (Repair visibility):** mechanism requires visible repair paths (incident logs, restatements) to counter symbolism drift.

<a id="b05ch18p03s04"></a>
## B05.CH18.P03.S04 — Status shock: old status pathways vanish without new legitimate arenas (link to CH13)

### Core mechanism
When caps and rails weaken traditional status ladders (money-based escalation, rentier hierarchy), people can experience status loss or
status ambiguity. If the system does not provide legitimate status arenas (competence, contribution, craftsmanship, commons maintenance),
status competition migrates into gatekeeping or nihilism. This can produce resentment both among former winners and among those who see
new elites forming.

### Canonical causal chain
`old status ladder disrupted`  
→ `status ambiguity / perceived loss`  
→ `search for new gatekeeping surfaces (credentials, networks, platforms)`  
→ `resentment and polarizing narratives increase`

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Implementations **MUST** anticipate status migration and prevent new gatekeeping elites (CH13 rails apply).
- Legitimate status signals **MUST** be contestable and non-exclusionary by default (avoid credential inflation).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **RES-MEC-07 (Status migration):** mechanism explicitly includes migration into credential/network/platform gates.
- **RES-MEC-08 (CH13 linkage):** resentment mitigation must reference CH13 contestability/anti-gatekeeping rails.
