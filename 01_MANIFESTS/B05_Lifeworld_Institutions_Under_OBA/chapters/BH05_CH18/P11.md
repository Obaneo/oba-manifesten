# B05.CH18.P11 — Evasion: remedies (reclassification and channel control)

This paragraph specifies the **remedy toolkit** for evasion failure modes. Remedies operate at four layers: (i) reclassification
(substance-over-form and look-through), (ii) contract remediation (prohibited clauses, standard templates, unbundling, take-rate caps,
portability mandates), (iii) channel remediation (tagging, leakage tests, forbidden channels, rail-level enforcement with due process),
and (iv) versioning (rapid Codex updates and transparent precedents). Remedies must be rule-bound, auditable, and FAIL-CLOSED under
opacity/obstruction.

---

<a id="b05ch18p11s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P11.S01 — Reclassification: substance-over-form, look-through, presumptions under opacity (fail-closed)

### Core remedy logic
Reclassification remedies neutralize semantic arbitrage by enforcing economic reality: control/benefit/block and exposure determine
classification, not labels.

### Required reclassification tools (minimum set)
- `substance_over_form`:
  - relabeled flows are reclassified into economically nearest class
- `UBC_look_through`:
  - attribution follows UBC paths across vehicles/nominees/trusts
- `aggregation_rules`:
  - fragmentation does not defeat thresholds (portfolio/property/contract aggregation)
- `conservative_presumptions_under_opacity`:
  - missing evidence triggers default attribution/valuation/exposure assumptions
- `provisional_enforcement`:
  - escrow/lockbox, restrictions, or withholding under opacity/flight risk (time-bounded)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Reclassification **MUST** be reproducible: rules and thresholds are method-bound and versioned.
- Under opacity/obstruction, presumptions **MUST** fail-closed (enforcement does not pause due to missing disclosure).
- Presumptions **MUST** be contestable, but disputes **MUST NOT** auto-stay enforcement under risk flags.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVA-RMD-01 (Default mapping):** each detected shift maps to a default reclassification/presumption route.
- **EVA-RMD-02 (Opacity trigger):** missing evidence triggers conservative presumptions + provisional enforcement (logged).

<a id="b05ch18p11s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P11.S02 — Contract remedies: prohibited clauses, standard templates, unbundling, take-rate caps, portability mandates

### Core remedy logic
Many lifeworld evasion routes are contract-coded: fees, lock-ins, penalties, and gatekeeping are embedded in terms. Contract remedies
rewrite the substrate, reducing discretion and preventing reappearance of E surfaces.

### Required contract remedy tools (minimum set)
- `prohibited_clauses`:
  - ban punitive termination fees, unilateral term changes, dark pattern cancellation, identity lockout from essentials
- `standard_templates`:
  - mandatory contract templates for essentials services; versioned and auditable
- `unbundling_rules`:
  - prohibit forced bundles and tie-ins that create non-optional tolls
- `take_rate_caps`:
  - cap effective take rates (all mandatory fee layers) for platform/marketplace toll surfaces
- `portability_mandates`:
  - data/service portability, switching assistance, continuity requirements; time-bounded execution
- `dispute_safe_defaults`:
  - interim relief and non-retaliation rules during disputes (no “punishment by delay”)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Contract remedies **MUST** be implemented as template changes, not as case-by-case negotiation.
- Take-rate caps **MUST** use effective all-layer measures; headline-only caps are evadable.
- Portability **MUST** be measurable (success rate, time-to-portability, switching cost proxies).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVA-CTR-01 (Template enforcement):** remedies are expressed as standard templates/prohibited clauses with versioning.
- **EVA-CTR-02 (Portability metrics):** portability mandates include measurable success/time proxies.

## B05.CH18.P11.S03 — Channel remedies: tagging, leakage tests, forbidden channels, rail governance with due process

### Core remedy logic
Evasion also happens through channels: new rails and intermediaries bypass enforcement. Channel remedies constrain the infrastructure:
tagging for traceability, leakage tests for early detection, forbidden channels for high-risk routes, and rail-level enforcement.

### Required channel remedy tools (minimum set)
- `tagging_rules`:
  - flow_id/contract_id/service_id tagging; line-item tagging where feasible
- `leakage_tests`:
  - tests for leakage into claims/fees; triggers for audit and channel reclassification
- `forbidden_channel_flags`:
  - classify channels as allowed/conditional/forbidden (Codex channel objects)
- `rail_level_enforcement`:
  - withholding hooks, access restrictions, licensing constraints, procurement exclusions (time-bounded)
- `due_process_rails`:
  - reasons, appeal paths, time bounds, publication (aggregated) for channel restrictions
- `sunset_and_rollback`:
  - controls sunset by default; rollback if harms or integrity breaches occur

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Channel remedies **MUST** be enforceable at rail level; entity-by-entity enforcement alone is too slow under adaptation.
- Restrictions **MUST** be contestable and logged; but disputes **MUST NOT** automatically suspend controls under leakage/flight risk.
- Leakage triggers **MUST** map to mandatory actions within time bounds (audit start, contract rewrite, channel reclassification).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVA-CH-01 (Channel object):** each major channel has an allowed/conditional/forbidden classification with criteria.
- **EVA-CH-02 (Trigger-to-action):** leakage tests map to mandatory actions and deadlines.

## B05.CH18.P11.S04 — Versioning: rapid updates via Codex + transparent precedent registry (without core break)

### Core remedy logic
Evasion evolves. The system needs rapid updates, but must avoid semantic core drift. Codex provides a fast update layer; the Kernspec
remains slow-changing and canonical.

### Required versioning mechanisms (minimum set)
- `codex_patch_cycle`:
  - detect → hypothesis → intervention → audit → public restatement → deprecate (linked to P17)
- `precedent_registry`:
  - versioned, published (aggregated) registry of classification decisions and remedies
- `backward_compatibility_rules`:
  - how new rules apply to existing contracts/channels (look-back, deprecation timelines)
- `restatement_discipline`:
  - metric restatements when methods change; no silent edits
- `core_break_prohibition`:
  - Codex updates must not redefine the semantic core; core changes require Kernspec-level process

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Versioning **MUST** be transparent: changes published with rationale and expected effects.
- Rapid updates **MUST** preserve contestability and auditability; “secret patches” are integrity breaches.
- Core break must be avoided: fast updates live in Codex; Kernspec updates are slow and explicit.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **EVA-VRS-01 (Precedent publication):** precedent registry exists and is versioned.
- **EVA-VRS-02 (Restatement):** method changes trigger restatement and publication of deltas.
- **EVA-VRS-03 (Core integrity):** patch process references Codex vs Kernspec boundaries.

---

### Output format (remedy routing table)

At minimum, CH18 **MUST** emit a remedy routing table (table or structured list) with:

- `shift_family` | `pattern_type` | `default_reclassification` | `contract_remedy` | `channel_remedy` |
  `presumption_under_opacity` | `auditpack_ref` | `precedent_ref` | `version`

If remedies cannot be routed deterministically, evasion becomes discretionary and capture-prone.
