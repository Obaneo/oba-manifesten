# B05.CH18.P12 — Bureaucratic drift: mechanism (how “good intentions” fail)

This paragraph specifies the mechanisms of **bureaucratic drift**: how implementations degrade even under “good intentions”. Drift is not
a moral failure; it is a predictable dynamic when institutions optimize for measurable outputs, legal defensibility, and self-preservation.
The result is Goodharted metrics, data maximalism, procedure inflation, and tooling capture—raising administrative L, weakening
contestability, and recreating extraction surfaces (bureaucratic tolls and vendor rents).

---

<a id="b05ch18p12s01"></a>
## B05.CH18.P12.S01 — Metric drift: measurement becomes the goal; Goodhart shifts behavior toward scores instead of lifeworld gains

### Core mechanism
When a metric becomes the target, it stops being a good measure (Goodhart). Institutions begin to optimize for dashboard appearance rather
than lifeworld outcomes. This produces “performance theater”: better scores, unchanged (or worse) lived reality.

### Canonical causal chain
`metric selected for accountability`  
→ `metric becomes target`  
→ `gaming/selection effects + tunnel vision`  
→ `dashboard improves; tails/time burden persist`  
→ `resentment rises; legitimacy fractures`

### Typical forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `threshold_gaming` (optimize just above thresholds; ignore tails)
- `selection_gaming` (exclude hard cases; cohort fractures increase)
- `proxy_substitution` (measure what is easy, not what matters)
- `reporting_without_remedy` (publish dashboards; do not redesign)
- `KPI_pressure_on_frontline` (perverse incentives; paper compliance)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Metrics **MUST** be treated as instruments, not goals; lifeworld integrity (L/tails/time) remains the objective.
- Single-KPI optimization **MUST NOT** be allowed; triangulation is required.
- If metrics improve while tails/time do not, this **MUST** trigger redesign and restatement rather than comms.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-MET-01 (Triangulation):** at least two independent proxies exist for key outcomes (avoid single-metric capture).
- **DRF-MET-02 (Tail discipline):** tails and cohort fractures are tracked alongside medians.

<a id="b05ch18p12s02"></a>
## B05.CH18.P12.S02 — Over-measurement and data maximalism: privacy breach + execution load (administrative L) rises

### Core mechanism
In response to accountability pressure, institutions over-collect data. This increases privacy risk and expands the administrative burden
on users and staff. Data maximalism becomes a new gate: “prove yourself” systems create friction and exclusion.

### Canonical causal chain
`fear of error + desire for control`  
→ `more data demanded`  
→ `forms and verification multiply`  
→ `administrative L rises; drop-off rises`  
→ `privacy incidents occur; trust collapses`

### Typical forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `microdata_aspiration` (desire for household/person-level tracking)
- `identity_linkage_expansion` (join everything to identity)
- `verification_overreach` (repeated proofs; “once-only” principle violated)
- `secondary_use_drift` (data reused beyond stated purpose)
- `surveillance_normalization` (continuous monitoring as default)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Microdata publication is prohibited; data collection **MUST** be necessity-bounded and privacy-rail compliant.
- Join safety **MUST** be enforced by design; identity-linkage requires necessity justification and audit.
- If administrative L rises due to measurement, the system is non-conform and must simplify.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-DAT-01 (Necessity test):** each added data field has logged necessity justification.
- **DRF-DAT-02 (Join safety):** allowed joins are explicit; join-unsafe linking is prohibited.

## B05.CH18.P12.S03 — Procedure inflation: exceptions, counters, evidentiary burden grow; access becomes a gatekeeping ecosystem

### Core mechanism
Procedures expand to manage risk. Exceptions proliferate, evidence requirements grow, and access becomes conditional on navigating a
bureaucratic maze. This recreates E-like extraction: time and compliance become tolls.

### Canonical causal chain
`rule complexity + risk aversion`  
→ `procedure layers + exceptions grow`  
→ `case-by-case discretion increases`  
→ `gatekeeping ecosystem forms (consultants, intermediaries, status filters)`  
→ `access inequality + resentment increase`

### Typical forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `exception_culture` (special cases become the norm)
- `evidence_bloat` (more documents, more steps, more counters)
- `cycle_time_inflation` (slow decisions become normal)
- `intermediary_dependency` (people need help to navigate; new toll layer)
- `retaliation_risk` (challenging decisions becomes costly)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Procedure complexity **MUST** be bounded; simplification sprints are required when burdens rise.
- Exceptions **MUST** be time-bounded and published in aggregated registers; repeated exceptions are capture red flags.
- Evidence burden **MUST** be minimized; standardized evidence classes should replace bespoke proofs.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-PROC-01 (Burden metrics):** cycle times, step counts, and drop-off are tracked as administrative L.
- **DRF-PROC-02 (Exception register):** exceptions are logged, time-bounded, and monitored for repetition patterns.

<a id="b05ch18p12s04"></a>
## B05.CH18.P12.S04 — Tooling capture: systems become complex and vendor-dependent; contestability disappears

### Core mechanism
Tooling capture occurs when implementation depends on proprietary systems that are not auditable, not portable, and not contestable. Over
time, the system becomes “owned” by vendors or internal tool bureaucracies; rule changes become opaque and appeals become meaningless.

### Canonical causal chain
`complexity + outsourcing + procurement shortcuts`  
→ `proprietary tooling dominates`  
→ `auditability declines; logs become inaccessible`  
→ `contestability disappears; vendor rents rise`  
→ `capture consolidates; lifeworld integrity degrades`

### Typical forms (minimum taxonomy)
- `vendor_lock_in` (no exit; punitive termination; closed formats)
- `opaque_algorithms` (ranking/eligibility engines without reasons)
- `method_black_boxing` (method_id replaced by vendor “model”)
- `audit_incompatibility` (cannot produce evidence packs; cannot restate)
- `procurement_capture` (change orders as rent channel)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Tooling **MUST** be audit-compatible and portable; open standards are required where systems are essential.
- Decisions **MUST** be reason-giving and contestable; black-box enforcement is non-conform.
- Vendor dependence **MUST** have exit assistance and escrow/continuity mechanisms for critical rails.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **DRF-TOOL-01 (Auditability):** tooling can produce required logs/evidence packs and support restatements.
- **DRF-TOOL-02 (Portability):** contracts include interoperability/export and exit rights.
