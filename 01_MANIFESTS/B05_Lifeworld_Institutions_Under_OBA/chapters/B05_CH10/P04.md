# B05.CH10.P04 — Identity: design without a central clamp (anti-block)

This paragraph specifies identity governance under OBA: identity is treated as a rail for participation, and therefore as a potential
clamp and block-power surface. If identity becomes omniscient (too many attributes, cross-domain linkability) or centralised (single
provider or mandatory vendor), it becomes a political exclusion switch and a surveillance substrate. OBA therefore requires identity
design that is minimal, purpose-limited, multi-provider and portable, with humane recovery procedures and emergency access for
essentials. Identity mechanisms must support attributable claims where needed (proofs for specific purposes) without creating a single
global identifier that can be used to track and exclude. Where identity systems drift into surveillance or selective exclusion,
fail-closed hard stops apply.

---

<a id="b05ch10p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P04.S01 — Identity as rail: minimal attributes, purpose limitation, no omniscient ID

Identity should enable access while minimising data and preventing cross-context surveillance.

### Minimal attributes (binding)
- Collect and store only attributes necessary for the specific access purpose (age threshold, residency, entitlement category), not
  full identity dossiers.
- Prefer attribute proofs over persistent identifiers where feasible (prove “over 18” rather than reveal date of birth).

### Purpose limitation (binding)
- Identity credentials may be used only for explicit purposes; re-use for unrelated profiling, marketing, or behavioural scoring is
  prohibited.
- Cross-service correlation of identity events (logins, transactions) is prohibited unless strictly required for fraud prevention and
  is audited and minimised.

### No omniscient ID (binding)
- Prohibit designs that create a single, globally linkable identifier across domains and providers.
- Where a stable identifier is unavoidable within a domain, require domain separation and strict join controls to prevent universal
  tracking.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Identity systems must publish an attribute map: what is collected, why, retention periods, and join limits (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch10p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P04.S02 — Recovery: lockout prevention, humane recovery procedures, emergency access for essentials

Lockouts are a primary block-power harm. Recovery must be designed as a baseline guarantee.

### Lockout prevention (binding)
- Require multiple recovery methods (not single-device dependent), including offline recovery where feasible.
- Prohibit designs where loss of a phone or token permanently blocks access to essential services.

### Humane recovery procedures (binding)
- Recovery must be time-bounded and accessible (language/disability accommodations).
- Provide clear reason codes for lockouts and recovery denial; allow appeals and second-opinion review for contested cases.

### Emergency access (binding)
- Define emergency access mechanisms for essential functions (public services access, baseline payment ability, baseline communication)
  during recovery windows, with strict fraud controls and logging.
- Emergency access must be minimal and purpose-limited: sufficient to prevent life exclusion, not a full reissue of identity.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated lockout and recovery metrics: lockout incidents, median/p90 recovery times, recovery denial rates, appeal outcomes
  (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch10p04s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P04.S03 — Portability: multiple providers, open standards, attributable claims

Identity must remain contestable. Portability prevents a single provider from becoming a choke point.

### Multi-provider requirement (binding)
- Baseline identity must be supportable by multiple providers (public and/or licensed private), with non-discriminatory acceptance by
  essential services.
- Providers may not impose exclusivity requirements that prevent users from maintaining alternative credentials.

### Open standards (binding)
- Identity credential formats and verification protocols must be open and documented; proprietary verification stacks are
  non-conforming.
- Provide standard migration tooling: export credentials/claims and transfer to another provider within time-bounded windows.

### Attributable claims (binding)
- Where accountability is required (e.g., legal transactions), identity must support attributable claims without expanding into a
  universal tracking ID.
- Claims must be auditable: verification events are logged (privacy-minimal), and disputes can be resolved through reproducible rules.

<a id="b05ch10p04s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P04.S04 — Hard stop: identity as a surveillance mechanism or a politically usable exclusion switch

Identity is governance-critical; misuse must trigger fail-closed intervention.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- Identity used as a **surveillance mechanism**: cross-context tracking, behavioural profiling, or broad data enrichment.
- Identity used as a **politically usable exclusion switch**: selective lockouts, unequal recovery, or denial without due process and
  appeal.
- Vendor capture: exclusive proprietary identity stacks, paywalled verification, or inability to migrate away from a provider.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Suspend affected identity pathways and revert to minimal baseline credentials while redesign occurs.
- Mandate interoperability and multi-provider support; require independent audits and publish incident findings in aggregate.
- Where misuse is systemic, deploy a public option identity rail under stricter governance (fail-closed against identity clamp).
