# B05.CH10.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of the digital lifeworld under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of the digital lifeworld under OBA. Digital services are treated as
essentials infrastructure: identity, communication, platform access, and the ability to pay increasingly determine participation in
work, education, healthcare, public services, and social life. This makes digital systems a dominant L surface through lock-in,
exclusion, administrative friction, and cognitive harm. It also makes them high-risk extraction (E) surfaces via surveillance rent,
platform tolls (take-rates and fee-stacks), and opaque ranking and moderation. OBA therefore treats digital governance as
constitutive: baseline digital services must be accessible without personal profiling dependency, vendor lock-in, or selectively usable
block power, and the system must remain contestable through interoperability, auditability, and public options where needed.

---

<a id="b05ch10p01s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P01.S01 — Purpose: digital baseline services as essentials without surveillance rent, lock-in, or platform tolls

Under OBA, the purpose of digital lifeworld governance is to provide baseline services that enable participation, while preventing
digital systems from becoming toll or clamp infrastructure.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Baseline participation**: ensure access to identity, communication, information, and payment ability sufficient for ordinary life
  participation.
- **No surveillance rent**: prohibit monetisation of behavioural and identity data beyond order necessity; prevent profiling-based
  business models from becoming prerequisites for essentials.
- **No lock-in**: ensure real exit options through portability, open standards, and multi-provider availability.
- **No platform tolls**: prevent take-rate extraction and fee-stack ecosystems where access to markets and audiences becomes “pay to
  reach”.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- Policies or systems whose functional objective is to preserve incumbent platform rents, vendor dependence, or profiling
  infrastructures—rather than baseline access and contestability—are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch10p01s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P01.S02 — Scope: identity/auth, communication, app ecosystems, platforms/marketplaces, payment rails, data rights, public options

CH10 covers the full digital stack that determines participation and allocative power.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Identity and authentication**: identity providers, credential systems, recovery processes, and access gating mechanisms.
- **Communication**: messaging, email, social communication layers, and moderation/governance procedures.
- **App ecosystems**: app stores, OS-level gatekeeping, bundling, default settings, and distribution control.
- **Platforms and marketplaces**: marketplaces, social platforms, creator platforms, and intermediated ad-tech layers; ranking and
  discovery systems.
- **Payment rails and fintech**: accounts, card networks, payment gateways, fraud/AML layers, and lockout/dispute processes.
- **Data rights**: data minimisation, purpose limitation, portability, access logs, third-party disclosures, and incident discipline.
- **Public options**: triggers, governance minima, procurement rails, and funding models for commons digital alternatives.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH10 does not prescribe specific UI design styles or product features except where they affect auditability, portability, lock-in,
  and surveillance risk.

<a id="b05ch10p01s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P01.S03 — Binding: commons standard (access/maintenance/ΔG), P/E/C (data extraction as E), control/block tests, audit and interoperability rails

Digital lifeworld governance under OBA is bound to canonical rails. Claims that ignore these rails are non-conforming.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **Commons standard (access/maintenance/ΔG)**: baseline digital services must meet reliability/availability minima (uptime, recovery,
  incident response) and have maintenance-first budgeting; critical interfaces are treated as commons capacity.
- **P/E/C classification**: productive digital services (P) are distinguished from extraction (E) such as surveillance rent, take-rate
  tolls, and paywalled interoperability; commons flows (C) include public options and non-profit/federated infrastructures.
- **Control/block tests**: detect and constrain block power (deplatforming, identity lockout, API gating, payment denial) as
  governance-critical leverage.
- **Audit rails**: decision rules for ranking, moderation, and access denial must be reproducible, logged, and contestable; exceptions
  must be registered and sunset-bounded.
- **Interoperability rails**: open standards, portability, non-discriminatory API access, and exit tooling are required to keep
  services contestable.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Major rule changes (identity, ranking, moderation, fees) must provide audit packs linking: mechanism → expected L/E incidence → block
  power risk → portability and appeal safeguards → rollback/sunset discipline.

<a id="b05ch10p01s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: systems that depend on personal profiling, opaque ranking, or vendor lock-in are non-conform until redesigned

Because digital systems are high-leverage and fast-moving, OBA applies fail-closed discipline when contestability is lost.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- **Personal profiling dependency**: baseline services require behavioural profiling, cross-site tracking, or forced-consent data
  extraction.
- **Opaque ranking/moderation**: discovery and enforcement decisions cannot be reproduced; shadow bans without auditable reason codes;
  payola-like influence hidden in ranking.
- **Vendor lock-in**: proprietary formats, paywalled interoperability, exclusive app distribution control, or closed identity stacks
  that prevent switching.
- **Selective block power**: lockouts, API denial, or payment exclusion without due process and appeal.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Treat the system/pathway as non-conforming until redesigned: enforce interoperability and portability, require auditability and
  contestability, cap and disclose fee structures, and restrict profiling-based models from baseline functions.
- Where contestability cannot be restored, trigger structural remedies: unbundling, mandated open interfaces, and/or deployment of a
  public option (fail-closed against digital clamp and toll capture).
