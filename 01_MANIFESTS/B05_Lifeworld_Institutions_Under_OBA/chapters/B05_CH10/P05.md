# B05.CH10.P05 — Communication commons: messaging, email, social layers

This paragraph specifies communication under OBA as commons-like infrastructure: messaging, email, and social communication layers
enable participation and therefore cannot be allowed to become surveillance rent systems, lock-in traps, or arbitrary exclusion
surfaces. Communication systems are also high-risk for abuse (spam, fraud, harassment), but abuse handling must not become a pretext
for total monitoring or opaque discretionary moderation. OBA therefore requires interoperability (open protocols and federation),
portability where feasible, end-to-end security with metadata minimalism, and moderation/governance procedures that are transparent,
contestable, and auditable. Where communication systems exercise block power without due process (deplatforming, shadowbans),
fail-closed remedies apply.

---

<a id="b05ch10p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P05.S01 — Interoperability: open protocols, federation, portability of contacts/messages where feasible

Interoperability prevents communication from becoming a closed network toll and preserves contestability.

### Open protocols and federation (binding)
- Baseline communication services must support open, documented protocols where feasible (email-class interoperability or federated
  messaging), avoiding single-vendor closed networks as the default.
- Federation rules must be transparent: how servers/providers interconnect, how abuse handling is coordinated, and how disputes are
  resolved.

### Portability (binding)
- Users must be able to export contacts, address books, and message history in standard formats (privacy-safe and encrypted where
  needed).
- Migration tools must be provided with time-bounded switching windows; portability may not be paywalled.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Providers must publish interoperability and portability method notes: supported standards, export formats, retention rules, and
  limits.

---

<a id="b05ch10p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P05.S02 — Spam/abuse handling: boundary rules without total monitoring; transparent moderation procedures

Abuse control is necessary, but it must be bounded and contestable; it cannot justify pervasive surveillance.

### Boundary rules (binding)
- Apply layered anti-abuse controls: rate limits, reputation signals, proof-of-work or challenge mechanisms where appropriate, and
  user-controlled filtering.
- Abuse detection must be proportional and purpose-limited; content inspection at scale is prohibited unless explicitly justified and
  governed (and must not be the default for baseline channels).

### Transparent moderation procedures (binding)
- Providers must publish moderation policies and enforcement categories (spam, fraud, harassment) with reason codes.
- Enforcement actions must be logged (privacy-minimal) and subject to time-bounded review.

### Prohibited drift (binding)
- “Safety” measures that expand into broad behavioural profiling, cross-service tracking, or commercial data enrichment are treated as
  surveillance rent and are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch10p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P05.S03 — End-to-end security: encryption, metadata minimalism, key-management governance

Security is a constitutive property of communication commons. OBA treats metadata as sensitive.

### Encryption requirements (binding)
- Support end-to-end encryption for messaging where feasible; for email-like systems, support strong transport encryption and optional
  end-to-end schemes with usable key handling.
- Encryption should not be conditional on paid tiers for baseline safety.

### Metadata minimalism (binding)
- Minimise metadata retention (who contacted whom, when, from where) consistent with anti-abuse and operational needs.
- Prohibit monetisation of communication metadata; prohibit sale or sharing for ad targeting and profiling.

### Key-management governance (binding)
- Key recovery and device migration must be humane and secure; avoid designs that force permanent lockout.
- Governance for key escrow (if any) must be explicit, limited, audited, and legally constrained; “silent escrow” is prohibited.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated security metrics: incident counts (breaches, phishing waves), recovery performance, and abuse rates (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch10p05s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P05.S04 — Contestability: objection/appeal for moderation, deplatforming, and shadowbans (auditable)

Because communication providers wield block power, enforcement must be contestable and auditable.

### Contestability minima (binding)
- Provide time-bounded objection and appeal procedures for moderation actions, deplatforming, and account restrictions.
- Users must receive reason codes and rule references; “silent suppression” and shadowbans without auditable disclosure are
  non-conforming.

### Auditability (binding)
- Providers must maintain audit logs for enforcement actions (privacy-minimal), sufficient to reproduce decisions and detect bias or
  selective enforcement.
- Publish aggregated transparency reports: action volumes, categories, appeal rates, reversal rates, and median/p90 appeal resolution
  times.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven selective enforcement, non-auditable shadow suppression, or persistent denial of appeal rights triggers conformance
  intervention: rule redesign, independent audit, and where needed structural remedies (interoperability mandates or public option
  deployment) (fail-closed against communication block power).
