# B05.CH10.P08 — Public options: when and how (digital commons)

This paragraph specifies when public options are required in the digital lifeworld under OBA and how they must be designed. OBA does
not presume that all digital services must be publicly operated, but it treats certain functions as essentials and certain market
structures as predictably non-contestable (network effects, app-store gatekeeping, platform toll economics, block power). Where
contestability cannot be restored through interoperability and behavioural constraints, OBA requires a **public option**: a commons
alternative that provides baseline functionality without surveillance rent, lock-in, or arbitrary exclusion. Public options must be
designed as anti-capture infrastructure: transparent governance, audit-of-the-auditor, procurement rails that prevent vendor lock-in,
and ring-fenced maintenance funding. ΔM-linked finance is admissible only into real commons capacity and maintenance, not into claim
support or vendor rents.

---

<a id="b05ch10p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P08.S01 — Public option triggers: structural lock-in, take-rate abuse, block power, essential dependence

Public options are not ideological; they are triggered by structural conditions and observable failure signals.

### Trigger conditions (binding)
- **Structural lock-in**: portability and interoperability cannot produce real exit options; switching costs remain high and dependency
  is durable.
- **Take-rate abuse**: effective take-rates and fee-stacks rise persistently in dominant platforms, and behavioural remedies do not
  reverse extraction.
- **Block power**: deplatforming, shadow bans, identity lockouts, API gating, or payment denial occurs without due process, producing
  coercive leverage.
- **Essential dependence**: a platform/service becomes de facto required for access to work, education, public services, or economic
  participation, and exclusion becomes lifeworld harm.

### Trigger evidence (binding)
- Triggers must be evidenced through published metrics (lockout incidents, portability success, take-rate distributions, appeal
  outcomes) and documented audits (see P11/P12 measurement discipline).

<a id="b05ch10p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P08.S02 — Governance minima: transparency, audit of the auditor, no vendor capture

A public option that is vendor-captured or opaque recreates the same toll structure under a different label. Governance is therefore
constitutive.

### Governance minima (binding)
- **Transparency**: publish decision logs for policy changes, moderation/ranking rules (if applicable), procurement awards, and major
  incidents (privacy-safe).
- **Audit-of-the-auditor**: independent audits of governance, code changes, procurement, and security; rotation and peer review.
- **User councils**: structured representation of users (including accessibility groups) with formal rights to raise disputes and
  trigger audits.
- **No selective enforcement**: uniform rule application; exceptions must be logged and sunset-bounded.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven selective enforcement, incident concealment, or governance opacity triggers governance reset and potential operator смена
  (fail-closed against public-option capture).

<a id="b05ch10p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P08.S03 — Procurement rails: open-source/escrow triggers, open standards, exit assistance

Public options must not become long-term vendor hostages. Procurement is therefore bound to openness and exit.

### Open standards (binding)
- Require open protocols and documented APIs; avoid proprietary formats and closed identity/communication stacks.
- Interoperability with private providers is a design goal where feasible (federation, standard exports), preventing “public lock-in”.

### Open-source / escrow triggers (binding)
- For critical components, require open-source by default or escrow/continuity arrangements that allow independent maintenance and
  migration if vendors fail or become abusive.
- Prohibit exclusive maintenance rights; ensure multi-vendor support pathways.

### Exit assistance (binding)
- Public options must provide migration tooling both into and out of the public option (portability works both ways).
- Switching windows and export formats must be documented and time-bounded.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish procurement logs (aggregate): suppliers, contract durations, change orders, standards compliance, and exit-cost estimates.

<a id="b05ch10p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P08.S04 — Funding: commons funds, ΔM allowed channels, ring-fenced maintenance budgets

Funding must sustain reliability and security over time. Underfunded public options degrade into failure and invite privatisation.

### Funding architecture (binding)
- **Commons funds**: dedicated funding streams for baseline operations, security, and user support.
- **Ring-fenced maintenance budgets**: maintenance-first funding for uptime, security patching, incident response, and capacity
  upgrades; no diversion to unrelated programmes.
- **ΔM allowed channels**: ΔM-linked finance is admissible only into real infrastructure capacity (ΔG) and maintenance/resilience, with
  tagging, leakage tests, and audit packs.

### Prohibited funding uses (binding)
- Subsidising vendor rents through opaque long-term contracts; claim/asset support for incumbent private platforms; funding schemes
  that require surveillance monetisation to “balance the budget”.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate accounts: operating spend, maintenance spend, incident response spend, capex upgrades, and performance metrics
  (uptime, recovery times, security incidents) as time series.
