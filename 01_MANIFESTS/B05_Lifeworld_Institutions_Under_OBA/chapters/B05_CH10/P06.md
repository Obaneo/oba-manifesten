# B05.CH10.P06 — Platforms and marketplaces: take-rate discipline and open exit

This paragraph specifies how platforms and marketplaces must operate under OBA so that they do not become toll gates on economic and
social participation. Platforms can create value by reducing search costs and providing trusted infrastructure, but under network
effects they can also dominate access and extract via take-rates, fee-stacks, and pay-to-reach ranking. They can also exercise block
power through opaque moderation and API gating. OBA therefore requires take-rate caps with full fee-stack disclosure, auditability and
contestability of ranking and algorithmic rules, non-discriminatory API access without self-preferencing, and structural remedies when
contestability fails. Where platform toll economics dominate, OBA treats the layer as E and requires unbundling and/or a public option
next to the platform.

---

<a id="b05ch10p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P06.S01 — Take-rate caps: bounded platform margins; fee-stack disclosure mandatory

Platform margins are bounded under OBA when platforms are functionally unavoidable access points.

### Take-rate discipline (binding)
- Platforms must disclose an **all-in take-rate**: total platform extraction as a percentage of transaction value or creator revenue,
  including all fees and required add-ons.
- Take-rates are capped (jurisdictional parameters) for dominant platforms and for categories where users lack real exit options.

### Fee-stack disclosure (binding)
- All fees must be itemised and standardised: listing, payment processing, withdrawal, dispute handling, promotion/boosting, and
  subscription fees.
- Bundled “service” charges without cost basis are classified as E.

### Prohibited fee patterns (binding)
- Penalty cascades, junk fees, forced bundles, and pay-to-unlock access to baseline platform functions are prohibited.
- Price discrimination via hidden fee schedules or undisclosed fee changes is non-conforming.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated take-rate and fee-stack statistics by category, including distributions and changes over time (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch10p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P06.S02 — Ranking/algorithms: auditability, explainability of rules, prohibited opaque payola

Ranking and discovery are governance functions: they allocate attention and economic opportunity.

### Auditability requirements (binding)
- Platforms must document ranking objectives, major feature classes, and update cadence; maintain versioned rule descriptions.
- For adverse outcomes (visibility suppression, demonetisation), provide reason codes and the applicable rule basis.

### Explainability minima (binding)
- Provide meaningful explanations for why content/products are ranked or suppressed in broad terms that are contestable.
- Maintain reproducible audit procedures: authorised auditors can verify that rules were applied as described.

### Prohibited opaque payola (binding)
- Paid influence on ranking must be explicitly labeled and separated; covert “boosting” through undisclosed payments or partner status
  is prohibited.
- Self-preferencing through ranking (platform’s own products/services advantaged without disclosure and objective criteria) is
  non-conforming.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Persistent opaque ranking, shadow suppression without auditable disclosure, or payola-like influence triggers structural remedies
  (fail-closed against attention toll capture).

<a id="b05ch10p06s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P06.S03 — API access: non-discriminatory access, no self-preferencing via gating

API access and interoperability are core levers of contestability.

### Non-discriminatory access (binding)
- Provide API access on equal terms to third-party tools and services where APIs exist; prohibit selective gating that suppresses
  competitors or favours affiliated services.
- Rate limits and security controls must be justified, documented, and applied uniformly.

### Anti-self-preferencing (binding)
- Platforms may not use privileged internal access to outcompete third parties while denying equivalent interfaces externally.
- Changes that break third-party interoperability must include notice, transition windows, and documented security justification.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish API governance policies: access criteria, reasons for denial, deprecation schedules, and dispute/appeal processes.

<a id="b05ch10p06s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P06.S04 — Remedies: unbundling, interoperability mandates, public option next to the platform (contestable core)

When platform power becomes structural and abusive, behavioural rules are insufficient. OBA provides a remedy ladder.

### Remedy ladder (binding)
- **Contract remedies**: enforce fee caps, remove junk fees, mandate disclosure, and require transparent, labeled ranking.
- **Interoperability mandates**: require open standards, federation where feasible, and portability of listings, reputations, and
  content.
- **Unbundling**: separate marketplace core from payment, logistics, advertising, or app-store control to prevent cross-subsidy and
  self-preferencing.
- **Public option**: deploy a public/commons alternative next to the platform when essential dependence and abuse persist; public option
  must meet governance minima (no vendor capture, open standards, maintenance-first).

### Allocation rule (binding)
- Remedy selection prioritises restoring exit options and reducing take-rate extraction. Where lock-in and block power persist,
  structural remedies are mandatory.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Remedies, mandates, and enforcement actions must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to prevent
  quiet continuation via rebranding or interface games.
