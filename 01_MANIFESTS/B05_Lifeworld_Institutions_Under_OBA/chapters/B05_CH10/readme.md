# B05.CH10 — Digital lifeworld: identity, communication, platforms, data rights, and public options

This chapter specifies the **digital lifeworld** under OBA as an essentials interface: identity, communication, platform access, and
the ability to pay increasingly determine participation in work, education, services, and social life. Digital systems therefore
constitute a major L surface through lock-in, exclusion, administrative friction, and cognitive harm. They are also highly
susceptible to extraction (E) via surveillance rent, platform tolls (take-rates and fee-stacks), and ad-tech intermediation. Under
OBA, baseline digital services must exist without dependency on personal profiling, opaque ranking, vendor lock-in, or selectively
usable block power. Where contestability cannot be maintained, **public options** and **unbundling** become mandatory.

CH10 is designed to be **anti-washing** and **fail-closed**. Systems that depend on profiling “for safety”, opaque ranking and shadow
bans, proprietary identity stacks that create lockout clamp, or procurement that normalises vendor lock-in are treated as
**non-conforming** until redesigned. The chapter therefore defines diagnostics, baseline access packages, identity rails that avoid a
central clamp, communication commons, marketplace discipline, data rights and audits, public option triggers and governance minima,
payment-rail constraints, creator-economy protections, publication requirements, and hard stops to keep the digital layer contestable
and non-captured.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing digital public services**: use CH10 to define baseline packages (identity, messaging/email, web access,
  payment ability), protect accessibility, and prevent lockouts from becoming life-exclusion switches.
- If you are **designing identity systems**: use CH10 to avoid “omniscient ID”, enforce minimal attributes and purpose limitation,
  ensure humane recovery, and require multi-provider portability.
- If you are **regulating platforms/marketplaces**: use CH10 to cap take-rates, mandate fee-stack disclosure, require auditability and
  contestability for ranking and moderation, and enforce open API access without self-preferencing.
- If you are **designing data governance**: use CH10 to enforce data minimalism, purpose limitation, portability, and evidence packs
  (data maps, DPIA-style audits, incident logs) while prohibiting “paid privacy” and forced-consent tolls.
- If you are **building public options**: use CH10 to set triggers (structural lock-in, toll abuse, block power), governance minima
  (audit-of-the-auditor, no vendor capture), procurement rails (open standards, escrow/open-source triggers), and ring-fenced
  maintenance budgets.
- If you are **auditing lifeworld outcomes**: use P11 and P12 to track lockouts, portability success, take-rate and ad-tech extraction,
  moderation appeals, and cognitive safety signals.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA-compatible design of identity rails, communication infrastructure, platforms and
marketplaces, digital payment access, and data governance. In particular, baseline access without surveillance rent, portability and
interoperability, bounded take-rates and fee-stack disclosure, contestable moderation and ranking, non-discriminatory identity and
payment access, and public option triggers are treated as **constitutive** safeguards against digital lock-in and platform toll
capture.

If CH10 constraints are weakened—by expanding profiling “for safety”, tolerating opaque ranking and shadow banning, permitting vendor
lock-in procurement, or allowing identity/payment lockouts without due process—the lifeworld layer will register predictable failure
signals: higher switching costs, lower portability success, higher take-rates, more lockout incidents, and rising cognitive harm and
manipulation signals. In that case, the implementation must be treated as drifting toward incompatibility and must trigger redesign,
scope restriction to auditable coverage, procurement and governance resets, and (where necessary) mandatory unbundling or public
option deployment.
