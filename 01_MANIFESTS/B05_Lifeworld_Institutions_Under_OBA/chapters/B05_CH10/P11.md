# B05.CH10.P11 — Measurement and publication requirements (digital lifeworld test)

This paragraph specifies measurement and publication requirements for the digital lifeworld under OBA. Digital drift is often hidden:
baseline access can erode via lockouts and rising prices; portability can become nominal while switching fails in practice; take-rates
and fee-stacks can grow through opaque intermediaries; and manipulation can expand without visible accountability. OBA therefore
requires a **public measurement layer** that makes access, lock-in, extraction, and cognitive safety contestable as time series, while
enforcing privacy minimalism (no person-tracking). Publication is treated as conformance infrastructure: if core indicators cannot be
published safely and reproducibly, the system must reduce scope to auditable coverage and trigger structural remedies (fail-closed
against invisible digital L and E).

---

<a id="b05ch10p11s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P11.S01 — Access metrics: coverage, affordability, uptime, lockout incidents, recovery times

Access measurement must reveal whether baseline digital services are reachable in practice.

### Access statistics (binding)
- **Coverage**: share of population with baseline package access (identity, messaging/email, basic web access, ability to pay, access
  to public services), by region and cohort.
- **Affordability**: baseline cost burden indicators (median/p90/p95), including regressive fixed-charge shares and data-poor group
  impacts.
- **Uptime/latency**: service-level metrics for baseline functions (identity auth, messaging/email delivery, payment rails), including
  outage durations and tail recovery times.
- **Lockout incidents**: account lockouts, freezes, suspensions in baseline systems, with reason-code distributions.
- **Recovery times**: median/p90/p95 recovery times for lockouts, including appeal outcomes and reversal rates.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish as time series with stable definitions; include incident post-mortems in aggregated form for major outages and lockout spikes.

<a id="b05ch10p11s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P11.S02 — Lock-in metrics: switching costs, portability success rates, dependency graphs, proprietary format shares

Lock-in measurement must reveal whether exit is real or merely formal.

### Lock-in statistics (binding)
- **Switching costs**: time and monetary costs to switch providers/platforms (median/p90/p95), including loss-of-functionality proxies
  (contacts lost, history loss).
- **Portability success rates**: portability request volumes, completion rates, median/p90 completion times, and failure causes.
- **Dependency graphs**: concentration and dependency indicators for critical functions (identity providers, app stores, dominant
  platforms, payment processors), including single-point-of-failure risk.
- **Proprietary format shares**: share of activity/content stored in non-portable formats; prevalence of closed protocols and
  paywalled APIs.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Worsening lock-in indicators trigger interoperability mandates and public option readiness; persistent failure triggers structural
  remedies (unbundling) (fail-closed against digital clamp).

<a id="b05ch10p11s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P11.S03 — Extraction metrics: take-rates, fee-stack depth, ad-tech intermediary shares

Extraction measurement must reveal toll economics and how value is distributed across intermediaries.

### Extraction statistics (binding)
- **Take-rates**: all-in take-rate distributions by platform category (marketplaces, creator platforms, app stores), including changes
  over time.
- **Fee-stack depth**: number and size of fee categories per transaction; share of revenue attributable to non-transparent or
  non-comparable fees.
- **Ad-tech intermediary shares**: where relevant, the share of spend captured by intermediaries between advertiser and publisher/
  creator, including opacity indicators.
- **Pay-to-reach indicators**: share of creators/sellers requiring paid boosting to reach baseline visibility; spend shares on
  promotion relative to revenue.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated tables and distributions; require standard definitions so platforms cannot reclassify fees to evade measurement.

<a id="b05ch10p11s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P11.S04 — Cognitive safety: manipulation incidents, moderation appeal stats, transparency-report minima

Cognitive safety measurement must reveal whether digital environments are imposing lifeworld burden through manipulation and opaque
governance.

### Cognitive safety indicators (binding)
- **Manipulation incidents**: categories of confirmed manipulation (dark pattern violations, coerced consent, misleading ads,
  cancellation traps), enforcement actions, and remediation completion.
- **Moderation and ranking transparency**: volumes of removals, demonetisations, visibility suppressions, and account restrictions by
  category, including reason-code distributions.
- **Appeal stats**: appeal volumes, reversal rates, median/p90 resolution times, and repeat-offender platform segments.
- **Transparency-report minima**: publish rule changes, major ranking updates, paid influence disclosures, and content governance
  changes with versioning.

### Privacy minimalism (binding)
- Measurement must not require person-tracking. Use aggregates, sampling, and privacy-safe audits rather than behavioural surveillance.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Refusal to publish transparency minima, persistent opaque shadow suppression, or repeated dark-pattern violations triggers
  conformance intervention and structural remedies (fail-closed against manipulation as governance).
