# B05.CH10 — Digital lifeworld: identity, communication, platforms, data rights, and public options

This chapter specifies the **digital lifeworld** under OBA as an essentials interface: identity, communication, platform access, and
payment ability increasingly determine participation in work, education, services, and social life. Digital systems can therefore
become a dominant L surface through lock-in, exclusion, administrative friction, and cognitive harm. They are also highly susceptible
to extraction (E) via surveillance rent, platform tolls (take-rates and fee-stacks), and ad-tech intermediation. OBA treats the
digital layer as governance-critical infrastructure: baseline digital services must exist without personal profiling dependency,
vendor lock-in, or opaque block power. Where contestability cannot be maintained, public options and unbundling become mandatory.

CH10 binds digital governance to canonical rails (commons standards for access/maintenance/ΔG; P/E/C classification with data
extraction as E; control/benefit/block tests; auditability and interoperability; privacy minimalism). It provides diagnostics,
baseline access packages, identity rails that avoid a central clamp, communication commons, platform and marketplace discipline,
data rights, public option triggers and governance minima, payment rails constraints, creator-economy protections, publication
requirements, and hard stops. It is designed to fail-closed: systems that depend on personal profiling, opaque ranking, vendor lock-in,
or selective deplatforming are non-conforming until redesigned.
