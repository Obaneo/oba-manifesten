# B05.CH10.P09 — Payment rails and fintech in the lifeworld

This paragraph specifies payment rails and fintech under OBA as lifeworld-critical infrastructure. Payment ability is now a baseline
participation requirement: without it, households can be excluded from work, housing, energy, food, and public services. Payment
systems therefore wield block power and are high-risk surfaces for fee-stacks (junk fees, opaque spreads, interchange layering) and
for overreach under fraud/AML regimes that can become de facto surveillance or arbitrary exclusion. OBA therefore requires baseline
payment access without lockout clamp, caps and disclosure for fees and spreads, proportional fraud/AML controls with contestability,
and hard stops against selective or politically usable rail governance.

---

<a id="b05ch10p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P09.S01 — Payment access: no one may be excluded from essentials due to provider lockouts

Baseline payment access is treated as an essentials right. Exclusion is permitted only under narrow, auditable conditions.

### Baseline access rule (binding)
- Every person must have access to a baseline payment function sufficient for essentials transactions (receive wages/benefits, pay
  essentials bills, basic transfers).
- Baseline access must not depend on proprietary smartphones, invasive tracking, or paid tiers.

### Lockout prevention (binding)
- Account freezes, de-risking, and lockouts must be time-bounded, reason-coded, and appealable.
- Providers must offer humane recovery procedures and interim baseline functionality for essentials where feasible.

### Prohibited exclusion patterns (binding)
- Indefinite freezes without explanation; exclusion by association; blanket deplatforming of lawful users; and discriminatory
  de-risking patterns.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated lockout metrics: freeze counts, median/p90 durations, reason-code distributions, appeal rates, reversal rates,
  and recovery times (privacy-safe aggregation).

---

<a id="b05ch10p09s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P09.S02 — Anti-fee-stacks: junk fees, interchange stacking, opaque spreads; disclosure and caps

Payment systems are prone to fee-stack growth because users cannot easily avoid the rail. OBA treats this as extraction (E).

### Disclosure (binding)
- Providers must disclose **all-in costs** for baseline payment services: account fees, transfer fees, FX spreads, card fees, cash-out
  fees, and merchant surcharges where applicable.
- Disclosures must be comparable and standardised; fee changes require notice and versioning.

### Caps (binding)
- Apply fee caps for baseline functions (jurisdictional parameters), including caps on interchange-related pass-through and on FX
  spreads for common corridors.
- Cumulative caps apply across intermediaries: processors, gateways, and fintech layers cannot each charge a “maximum” separately.

### Prohibited fee patterns (binding)
- Junk fees (maintenance fees without service, inactivity traps), penalty cascades, opaque spreads, forced add-ons, and pay-to-fix
  dispute fees.
- Pricing discrimination via hidden fee schedules or “default premium” settings.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- Non-auditable or non-cost-based charges are classified as E and subject to refund/credit and contract rewrite (fail-closed against
  fee-stack opacity).

<a id="b05ch10p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P09.S03 — Fraud/AML without overreach: minimalism, proportionality, contestability

Fraud and AML controls are necessary, but they must not become blanket surveillance or arbitrary exclusion.

### Minimalism and proportionality (binding)
- Collect only data necessary for fraud/AML purpose; prohibit expanding data collection “just in case”.
- Apply risk-based controls proportional to transaction type and amount; avoid treating low-value essentials transactions as high-risk
  by default.

### Contestability (binding)
- Users must receive reason codes for adverse actions (holds, freezes, limits) consistent with lawful constraints; provide a fast
  appeal/review pathway and time bounds.
- Automated decisions must be auditable and reproducible; black-box scoring without audit access is non-conforming.

### Prohibited overreach (binding)
- Persistent de-risking of entire categories without evidence; cross-domain profiling; and using AML as a pretext to monetise data or
  steer users.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated fraud/AML metrics: hold/freeze rates, false-positive rates (where measurable), appeal outcomes, and resolution
  times (privacy-safe aggregation).

---

<a id="b05ch10p09s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P09.S04 — Hard stop: rail governance that becomes selective or politically usable without due process

Payment rails are governance-critical. Selective enforcement becomes a political power surface and is incompatible with OBA.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- Lockouts or payment denial without due process, reason codes, and appeal.
- Selective enforcement against disfavoured groups, regions, or lawful speech/association.
- Opaque “risk” models used to justify permanent exclusion without auditability.
- Vendor/platform capture of rail governance (exclusive gatekeepers that can unilaterally set exclusion policy).

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Trigger governance intervention: independent audit, rule redesign, and structural remedies (unbundling gatekeepers, mandating open
  access, or deploying a public option payment rail).
- Repeat violations trigger licence restrictions and mandatory structural separation between rail operation and discretionary policy
  functions (fail-closed against politically usable payment exclusion).
