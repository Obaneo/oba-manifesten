# B05.CH10.P02 — Diagnostics: how digital becomes L (and where E dominates)

This paragraph specifies the diagnostic model for the digital lifeworld under OBA: how digital systems become major essentials burdens
(L), and where extraction (E) tends to dominate. Digital becomes L when participation requires lock-in ecosystems (closed network
effects, proprietary formats, app-store gatekeeping), when platform tolls raise the cost of reaching markets and audiences, when block
power can exclude people from identity, communication, or payments, and when cognitive manipulation increases hidden burdens through
time theft, misinformed decisions, and coerced consent. E dominates when business models rely on surveillance rent, take-rate
extraction, fee-stacks, ad-tech intermediation, and paywalled interoperability. The diagnostic purpose is operational: it defines the
mechanism families that later paragraphs must neutralise via portability, interoperability, anti-toll constraints, contestable
moderation/ranking, and public option triggers.

---

<a id="b05ch10p02s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P02.S01 — Lock-in mechanisms: closed network effects, proprietary formats, app stores, bundling

Lock-in is the primary mechanism that converts digital services into clamp: exit becomes costly or impossible.

### Lock-in mechanisms (binding)
- **Closed network effects**: value depends on being inside a specific network; switching means losing contacts, reputation, history,
  and reach.
- **Proprietary formats**: data and content stored in formats that cannot be exported or used elsewhere without loss.
- **App stores and OS gatekeeping**: distribution control used to extract rents, block competitors, or impose unilateral rules and fees.
- **Bundling/defaults**: tying essential functions to proprietary ecosystems (identity, payments, messaging) and using defaults to
  prevent practical choice.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- High switching costs; low portability success rates; long-term dependency on a single vendor/platform; and “must-have” apps that
  cannot be replaced without functional loss.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Where lock-in is structural, interoperability and public option triggers must be considered; voluntary “choice” framing is treated as
  non-credible.

<a id="b05ch10p02s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P02.S02 — Platform toll: take-rates, fee-stacks, ad-tech intermediation, “pay to reach”

Platforms become tolls when they control access to markets, audiences, or visibility and monetise that control.

### Toll mechanisms (binding)
- **Take-rates**: platform margins extracted from transactions, subscriptions, or creator revenue shares.
- **Fee-stacks**: layered fees (processing, listing, “promotion”, withdrawal, dispute handling) that cumulatively raise effective
  take-rates.
- **Ad-tech intermediation**: chains of intermediaries capturing value between advertisers and publishers/creators through opacity and
  auctions that are not contestable.
- **Pay to reach**: ranking systems that require payment or “boosting” to reach audiences, effectively converting visibility into a
  toll.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- Rising effective take-rates; proliferation of fee categories; opaque revenue shares; high dependence on paid promotion for basic
  reach; and increasing spend on intermediaries without improved service quality.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- Where platform economics rely on toll extraction rather than on bounded service provision, the platform layer is classified as E and
  triggers take-rate caps, disclosure mandates, and structural remedies.

<a id="b05ch10p02s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P02.S03 — Block power: deplatforming, API gating, identity lockout, payment denial

Block power is the ability to exclude participants from essentials functions and thereby exert coercive leverage.

### Block power surfaces (binding)
- **Deplatforming and shadow banning**: removal or invisibility without auditable reasons and appeals.
- **API gating**: restricting interoperability and third-party tools to preserve control, self-preference, or suppress competition.
- **Identity lockout**: account suspension or recovery failure that prevents access to public services, work, or communications.
- **Payment denial**: payment processor lockouts, account freezes, or sudden limits that block participation in markets and essentials.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- Lockout incident volumes and recovery times; opaque enforcement rates; unexplained ranking suppression; and reliance on discretionary
  terms-of-service as the real governance layer.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- High block power without due process and contestability triggers anti-block requirements and public option readiness (fail-closed
  against selective exclusion).

<a id="b05ch10p02s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P02.S04 — Cognitive harm: manipulation, dark patterns, information asymmetry as lifeworld-L

Digital systems can impose lifeworld burden by exploiting cognition: steering, addiction loops, coerced consent, and asymmetric
information.

### Cognitive harm mechanisms (binding)
- **Manipulation**: feed shaping, recommender systems optimised for engagement at the expense of user intent and agency.
- **Dark patterns**: UI tactics that coerce consent, prevent cancellation, steer purchases, or obscure privacy settings.
- **Information asymmetry**: opaque ranking and pricing, non-transparent ad disclosures, and hidden incentives that distort decisions.
- **Time theft**: design that extracts attention as a resource, increasing indirect L through lost time and degraded decision quality.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- High complaint volumes about cancellation/consent, widespread misunderstanding of terms, rising dependence on paid boosting to reach,
  and measurable increases in time spent without user-intent alignment (where measurement is ethical and privacy-safe).

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Where cognitive harm is systemic, the system must be treated as an L amplifier; remedies include transparency requirements,
  prohibition of specific dark patterns, and constraints on opaque ranking/engagement optimisation (fail-closed against manipulation as a
  business model).
