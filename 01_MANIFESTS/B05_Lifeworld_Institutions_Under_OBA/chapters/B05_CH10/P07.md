# B05.CH10.P07 — Data rights: minimalism, portability, and usage limits

This paragraph specifies data rights under OBA: minimalism, portability, and strict usage limits to prevent data from becoming rent.
In the digital lifeworld, data collection and behavioural profiling are often the core extraction (E) mechanism, enabling surveillance
rent, discrimination, manipulation, and lock-in. OBA therefore treats data governance as a constitutive rail: collect the minimum
necessary data, use it only for service delivery and explicitly bounded extensions, make portability real through standard exports and
migration tooling, and prohibit data toll patterns such as “paid privacy”, dark patterns, and forced consent. To keep enforcement
auditable, OBA requires evidence packs (data maps, DPIA-style assessments), incident logging, and transparent third-party disclosures.
Where systems cannot operate without expansive profiling, they are non-conforming until redesigned (fail-closed against data rent).

---

<a id="b05ch10p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P07.S01 — Purpose limitation: data use only for service delivery + explicit, bounded extensions

Data use must be purpose-limited. “Collect and monetise later” is prohibited.

### Purpose limitation (binding)
- Default purpose is **service delivery**: authentication, delivery of requested functions, security, fraud prevention (bounded), and
  operational reliability.
- Any extension beyond delivery requires explicit justification, bounded scope, and expiry/sunset rules (e.g., limited research use
  under governance, safety monitoring under minimisation).

### Prohibited purposes (binding)
- Behavioural profiling for ad targeting, price discrimination, ranking manipulation, and cross-service enrichment.
- Selling or licensing personal datasets; using data as leverage for continued access.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Providers must publish a purpose register: data categories → purposes → retention periods → third-party sharing status.

<a id="b05ch10p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P07.S02 — Data portability: standard exports, migration tooling, time-bounded switching windows

Portability is the core anti-lock-in mechanism. Rights without tooling are non-operational.

### Portability minima (binding)
- **Standard exports**: provide exports in open formats for profiles, content, contacts, purchase histories, and transaction records
  where applicable.
- **Migration tooling**: provide user-facing migration tools and API-based transfers where feasible; exports must be usable, not “data
  dumps” designed to frustrate exit.
- **Time-bounded switching windows**: define maximum timelines for completing portability requests and account migrations.

### Anti-paywall rule (binding)
- Portability may not be paywalled, delayed for leverage, or degraded in fidelity for non-paying users.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated portability performance metrics: request volumes, median/p90 completion times, success rates, and common failure
  causes (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch10p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P07.S03 — Prohibited data toll: paid “privacy”, dark patterns, forced consent, data as rent

OBA treats “data as rent” as extraction: users pay with privacy, attention, or coerced consent.

### Prohibited patterns (binding)
- **Paid privacy**: baseline services require surveillance, while opting out requires payment or loss of functionality.
- **Forced consent**: bundling unrelated data uses into mandatory terms; consent that cannot be refused without exclusion from
  essentials.
- **Dark patterns**: misleading UI for consent, cancellation, portability, or privacy settings; friction to decline tracking.
- **Data as rent**: using access to user data as a condition for participation or as a bargaining chip for dispute resolution.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Where prohibited patterns are detected, the data use is suspended by default; services must continue at baseline functionality
  without surveillance dependency, or the product is non-conforming until redesigned.

<a id="b05ch10p07s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P07.S04 — Evidence & audits: data maps, DPIA-style packs, incident logs, third-party disclosures

Data governance must be enforceable. OBA requires evidence packs that make data flows auditable.

### Evidence packs (binding)
- **Data maps**: inventories of data categories, collection points, processing steps, storage locations, and retention periods.
- **DPIA-style packs**: risk assessments for sensitive processing (profiling, large-scale analytics, identity systems) including purpose
  justification, minimisation measures, and alternatives considered.
- **Third-party disclosures**: list third parties and processors, purposes, and data categories shared; prohibit silent sharing.
- **Incident logs**: breaches, unauthorised access, data loss, model drift, and misuse incidents with remediation actions.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated transparency statistics: categories of processing, third-party sharing counts, incident volumes and severities,
  and remediation completion rates (privacy-safe aggregation).

### Hard stop (binding)
- Concealment of incidents, undisclosed third-party sharing, or inability to produce evidence packs triggers conformance suspension and
  mandatory governance intervention (fail-closed against data opacity).
