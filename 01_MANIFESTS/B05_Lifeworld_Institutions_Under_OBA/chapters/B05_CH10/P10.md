# B05.CH10.P10 — Digital labour and the creator economy: preventing new toll gates

This paragraph specifies how digital labour and the creator economy must operate under OBA to prevent platforms from becoming new toll
gates on work and income. Platform-mediated work (creators, freelancers, small sellers) can expand opportunity, but under network
effects it often produces monopsony-like dependence: platforms control discovery, monetisation, and payout rails, and can extract via
take-rates, opaque ranking, and discretionary demonetisation. OBA therefore requires portability of audience and content, open
monetisation rails where feasible, transparent contract standards for revenue shares and fees, and dispute procedures with reproducible
payout calculations and auditable enforcement. Where platform dependence is structural and abusive, unbundling and public option
remedies apply.

---

<a id="b05ch10p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH10.P10.S01 — Platform dependence: risk of monopsony and take-rate extraction

Platform dependence becomes a labour-market clamp when income requires remaining inside one platform’s rules and ranking.

### Dependence mechanisms (binding)
- **Monopsony-like power**: creators/workers cannot realistically multi-home; platform is the dominant channel to reach an audience or
  customers.
- **Take-rate extraction**: rising platform margins and fee-stacks reduce net income without proportional service improvements.
- **Ranking control**: discovery and distribution are controlled by opaque algorithms; paid boosting becomes de facto required.
- **Policy volatility**: sudden changes to terms, monetisation rules, or eligibility that can collapse income without due process.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- High income share dependent on one platform; low portability success; rising effective take-rates; high demonetisation or takedown
  incident volumes; and high dependence on paid promotion to maintain baseline reach.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Where dependence is structural, stronger take-rate caps, interoperability mandates, and public option triggers become relevant.

<a id="b05ch10p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH10.P10.S02 — Portability of audience: followers/subscribers, content exports, open monetisation rails

Portability is the core anti-monopsony rail in the creator economy.

### Portability requirements (binding)
- **Audience portability**: enable export of follower/subscriber lists (where lawful and privacy-safe), including contact mechanisms
  that allow creators to notify audiences of migration.
- **Content portability**: provide standard exports of content, metadata, and performance analytics in open formats.
- **Reputation portability (where feasible)**: provide mechanisms to port ratings/reviews or verifiable reputation proofs without
  enabling fraud (e.g., signed attestations).

### Open monetisation rails (binding)
- Support interoperable monetisation: standard payment interfaces, multiple payout providers, and portability of subscriptions where
  feasible.
- Prohibit lock-in via exclusive payout rails that can be used as leverage.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Portability must be time-bounded and not paywalled; publish aggregated portability success and completion metrics.

<a id="b05ch10p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH10.P10.S03 — Contract standards: transparent revenue shares, no exclusivity without exit

Contracts in platform work must be explicit, comparable, and contestable. Hidden revenue shifts are treated as extraction (E).

### Contract standards (binding)
- **Transparent revenue shares**: disclose all take-rate components, fees, and payout rules in a standard all-in format.
- **No hidden fee-stacks**: prohibit layered charges (withdrawal fees, “service” fees, dispute fees) that effectively increase take
  rates without disclosure.
- **Change discipline**: material rule changes must have notice and transition windows; retroactive changes are non-conforming.

### Exclusivity constraints (binding)
- Exclusivity is permitted only if:
  - it is explicitly chosen, time-bounded, and compensated,
  - it includes a clear exit path with portability support,
  - and it does not block baseline participation elsewhere through penalty clauses.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated take-rate and fee distributions, and volumes of contract changes and disputes (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch10p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH10.P10.S04 — Dispute flow: reproducible payouts, audit of takedowns/demonetisation

Disputes must be resolvable through evidence, not through asymmetrical persistence. Payout and enforcement decisions are audit-critical.

### Reproducible payouts (binding)
- Provide payout calculation packs: gross revenue basis, take-rate components, refunds/chargebacks, adjustments, and net payout.
- Creators/workers must be able to recompute payouts from disclosed parameters and logged events.

### Takedowns and demonetisation (binding)
- Provide reason codes and rule references for takedowns, visibility suppression, and demonetisation; “silent punishment” is
  non-conforming.
- Provide time-bounded appeal procedures with neutral review; emergency actions must have rapid post-action review.

### Audit requirement (binding)
- Maintain enforcement logs (privacy-minimal) and publish aggregated transparency reports: action volumes by category, appeal rates,
  reversal rates, and resolution times.
- Persistent opaque enforcement triggers structural remedies: algorithm audit, unbundling, interoperability mandates, or public option
  deployment (fail-closed against discretionary labour clamp).
