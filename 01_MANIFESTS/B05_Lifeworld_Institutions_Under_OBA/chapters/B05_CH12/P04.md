# B05.CH12.P04 — Quasi-public bodies: when allowed and under which rails

This paragraph specifies when quasi-public bodies are admissible under OBA and the rails that must govern them. Quasi-public bodies
perform public tasks but operate with autonomous governance (corporate-like structures, boards, revenue models). This structure is
high-risk for toll economics: fees and cross-subsidies can become opaque, procurement can be captured, and accountability can degrade
because the body is neither fully public nor fully market-disciplined. OBA therefore permits quasi-public bodies only under strict
rails: baseline access and quality minima, transparency and audit packs, procurement anti-lock-in rules, and contestability for users.
Hidden fee-stacks, exclusivity lock-ins, and opaque cross-subsidies are prohibited. Where drift is detected, OBA applies remedies
ranging from contract rewrite to governance reset and conversion to commons governance.

---

<a id="b05ch12p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P04.S01 — Definition: bodies with public tasks but autonomous governance (toll risk)

A quasi-public body is defined by function and governance structure, not by branding.

### Definition (binding)
- Performs **public or essential tasks** (utilities, housing bodies, transport operators, care/education chains, service platforms), but
  has **autonomous governance**: independent board, internal revenue model, and operational discretion not directly controlled by
  elected bodies.
- Often sits between public budgets and user fees; may control access and pricing interfaces.

### Toll risk (binding)
- Autonomous governance + essential dependence creates a predictable toll surface:
  - fees can expand with weak comparability,
  - cross-subsidies can hide margin capture,
  - procurement can lock-in vendors and entrench intermediaries,
  - users face weak appeal rights compared to public administration.

### Identification requirement (binding)
- Jurisdictions must maintain a registry of quasi-public bodies with task scope, governance structure, funding model, and access
  interfaces.

---

<a id="b05ch12p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P04.S02 — Admissibility: only with access/quality minima, transparency, procurement rails, contestability

Quasi-public bodies are admissible only if they behave like audited commons operators, not like hidden toll corporations.

### Admissibility minima (binding)
- **Access minima**: baseline access requirements must be met; denial must be reason-coded and appealable; no exclusion via hidden fees
  or bureaucracy.
- **Quality minima**: service quality standards and maintenance-first discipline must apply; backlog thresholds and incident reporting
  required.
- **Transparency**: publish tariffs/fees in standard formats; publish decision logs and exceptions registers; provide audit packs for
  pricing and allocation.
- **Procurement rails**: open standards, anti-lock-in clauses, change-order transparency, and vendor dependency monitoring.
- **Contestability**: independent dispute/ombuds pathway for users; time-bounded resolution; access to calculations and rule references.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Quasi-public bodies must be auditable as if they were public executors: signed releases of templates/parameters, versioned rule
  changes, and public aggregated reporting.

<a id="b05ch12p04s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P04.S03 — Prohibited structures: hidden fee-stacks, exclusivity, opaque cross-subsidies

Certain structural patterns are treated as presumptively non-conforming because they embed toll economics.

### Prohibited patterns (binding)
- **Hidden fee-stacks**: layered fees, “service charges”, penalty cascades, or mandatory add-ons without cost basis and disclosure.
- **Exclusivity**: exclusive contracts that block alternative providers, prevent portability, or create captive user dependence.
- **Opaque cross-subsidies**: internal transfers and pricing structures that obscure who pays for what and allow margin capture under
  public-task branding.
- **Self-preferencing**: governance arrangements that advantage affiliated vendors or subsidiaries in procurement and service delivery.
- **Non-auditable algorithms**: automated allocation/triage decisions that cannot be reproduced or appealed.

### Presumption rule (binding)
- If a quasi-public body cannot provide cost decomposition and rule audit packs, presume non-conformance and restrict its role in
  essentials delivery until corrected (fail-closed against quasi-public opacity).

<a id="b05ch12p04s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P04.S04 — Remedies: contract rewrite, governance reset, conversion to commons, status downgrade

OBA defines a remedy ladder for quasi-public drift; behavioural promises are not sufficient when structure is wrong.

### Remedy ladder (binding)
- **Contract rewrite**: rewrite tariffs, fee schedules, and user contracts into standard formats; remove prohibited clauses; impose fee
  caps and disclosure; mandate portability.
- **Governance reset**: replace board/leadership, restructure oversight, rotate auditors, and impose tighter transparency requirements.
- **Conversion to commons**: convert the body (or its essential functions) into commons governance with maintenance-first budgeting,
  public decision logs, and direct accountability.
- **Status downgrade**: remove or restrict the body’s role in essentials delivery; reassign functions to a compliant operator or public
  option.

### Escalation rule (binding)
- Repeat non-conformance (fee-stacks, exclusivity, opacity, selective enforcement) triggers mandatory structural remedies rather than
  incremental fixes (fail-closed against toll entrenchment).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Remedies and conformance actions must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to prevent quiet drift
  and rebranding.
