# B05.CH12.P09 — Local finance and T mix (within rails)

This paragraph specifies local finance under OBA: how municipalities/regions fund execution and commons portfolios within rails that
prevent regression, arbitrariness, and postcode-lottery outcomes. Local finance is a governance surface: poorly designed levies and
opaque transfers can create hidden L (fees, friction), distort priorities, and enable political capture. OBA therefore permits local
levies only within anti-regression and essentials rails, requires intergovernmental transfers to follow transparent auditable formulas,
rings-fences maintenance and baseline access budgets to prevent silent capacity decay, and treats unexplained postcode-lottery
outcomes as legitimacy breaches that create migration friction and social instability.

---

<a id="b05ch12p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P09.S01 — Local levies: admissible within anti-regression and essentials rails

Local levies are admissible only if they do not create regressive burdens or convert essentials access into tolls.

### Admissibility conditions (binding)
- Levies must respect **anti-regression**: they may not disproportionately burden low-income households or data-poor groups; fixed fees
  that function as regressive head taxes are non-conforming.
- Levies must not create **essentials clamp**: no fees that block baseline access to housing allocation, benefits, education access, or
  core municipal services.
- Where user charges exist, they must be bounded, disclosed, and contestable with exemptions for baseline cohorts (parameters).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish levy schedules, exemptions, and incidence summaries (median/p90 burden proxies) in privacy-safe aggregate form.

<a id="b05ch12p09s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P09.S02 — Intergovernmental transfers: transparent formulas, no political arbitrariness, auditable

Transfers are necessary to equalise capacity, but they can become political tools. OBA requires formula-based transparency.

### Transfer rules (binding)
- Transfers must follow published formulas tied to measurable need and cost drivers (population, deprivation proxies, geography, service
  demand, infrastructure condition).
- Transfers may include performance conditions only if those conditions are measurable, non-gameable, and do not create selection
  incentives or punish high-need regions.

### No political arbitrariness (binding)
- Discretionary transfer deals, opaque “special grants”, and politically contingent funding without audit packs are non-conforming.
- Exceptions must be logged in an exceptions register with reason codes and sunsets.

### Auditability (binding)
- Transfer calculations must be reproducible from disclosed parameters; publish method_id and revision policy.

<a id="b05ch12p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P09.S03 — Ring-fencing: maintenance budgets and baseline access protected

Local systems fail by silently raiding maintenance and baseline services to meet short-term budget pressures.

### Ring-fencing requirements (binding)
- Ring-fence budgets for:
  - **execution capacity** (staffing, training, tooling continuity),
  - **commons maintenance** (housing/care/mobility/community assets),
  - **baseline access** (service desks, dispute handling, accessibility support).
- Ring-fenced budgets may not be diverted to discretionary projects, consultant expansion, or politically motivated showcases without
  explicit audit packs and approval.

### Backlog linkage (binding)
- Where maintenance and execution backlogs breach thresholds, ring-fenced spending must increase or trigger intergovernmental support;
  deferral is treated as negative ΔG.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated accounts: ring-fenced spend categories, backlog indicators, and reallocation events (privacy-safe aggregation).

## B05.CH12.P09.S04 — Failure mode: postcode lottery without explanation → legitimacy breach and migration friction

Local variation becomes illegitimate when outcomes diverge without transparent parameter differences or capacity explanations.

### Failure signals (binding)
- Large differences in lead times, access outcomes, enforcement posture, or service quality across municipalities without published
  parameter differences or measured capacity constraints.
- Persistent divergence in essentials indices (housing access, energy burden support, care access) that correlates with administrative
  choices rather than with transparent need/cost factors.
- Migration friction rises: households move (or cannot move) due to arbitrary local conditions, amplifying social instability.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat unexplained divergence as a legitimacy breach: trigger audit and intergovernmental intervention; require alignment to national
  minima and standard templates.
- Where local under-capacity causes divergence, deploy support mechanisms and shared service centres; where discretionary drift causes
  divergence, restrict local autonomy until conformance is restored (fail-closed against arbitrary postcode lotteries).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish comparative dashboards in aggregate with method_id, highlighting divergence drivers and remediation progress (no “dashboards
  without remedies”).
