# B05.CH12.P02 — Roles and powers model (actor versus position)

This paragraph specifies the roles and powers model for local institutions under OBA using an actor-versus-position discipline. OBA
treats governance failure as often structural: not “bad people” but concentrated block power, ambiguous mandates, and missing appeal
rails. Local execution systems must therefore be designed so that no single actor can simultaneously set rules, execute them, judge
disputes, and audit their own performance. OBA defines explicit positions (mandate-grantor, executor, steward/manager, auditor,
ombuds/dispute arbiter), requires separation of functions, constrains delegation to parameterised variation while preserving
invariants, and defines intergovernmental coupling: national minima with local modules and explicit escalation paths. The design goal is
contestability and anti-capture by construction.

---

<a id="b05ch12p02s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P02.S01 — Positions: mandate-grantor (council), executor, steward/manager, auditor, ombuds/dispute arbiter

Positions are functional roles; actors may change, but position responsibilities and constraints remain stable.

### Core positions (binding)
- **Mandate-grantor (council)**: sets local parameters within national invariants; approves budgets; sets service-level commitments and
  local commons portfolios; owns local conformance registers.
- **Executor**: applies rules and delivers services; produces decision logs, audit packs, and incident reports; implements procurement
  under rails.
- **Steward/manager**: manages commons assets and operational capacity (staffing, maintenance, tooling); responsible for backlog and
  continuity discipline.
- **Auditor**: independent oversight of execution, procurement, and incident handling; verifies conformance; publishes aggregate audit
  summaries.
- **Ombuds/dispute arbiter**: independent dispute intake, triage, and adjudication/forwarding; detects systemic patterns and triggers
  escalation.

### Position integrity rule (binding)
- Each position must have documented authority boundaries, data access rights, and decision responsibilities; ambiguity is treated as a
  capture risk.

<a id="b05ch12p02s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P02.S02 — Separation of functions: decision, execution, oversight, appeal (no concentration of block power)

Separation of functions prevents discretionary accumulation of block power.

### Separation requirements (binding)
- **Decision ≠ execution**: the body setting rules/parameters cannot be the sole body applying them without external oversight.
- **Execution ≠ audit**: executors may not audit themselves; internal QA is permitted but not sufficient.
- **Audit ≠ appeal**: auditors cannot be the final arbiter of individual disputes; dispute adjudication must be independent.
- **Procurement separation**: procurement decision authority must be separated from vendor delivery and from audit sign-off; no vendor
  self-certification.

### Conflict-of-interest controls (binding)
- Mandatory disclosures, cooling-off periods, and recusal rules for officials with vendor ties.
- Prohibit “single-firm capture” where one consultancy or vendor designs, implements, and audits the same system.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Concentration of these functions triggers redesign: structural separation, rotation, and external oversight (fail-closed against local
  block power).

<a id="b05ch12p02s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P02.S03 — Delegation: what may vary locally (parameters) and what may not (invariants)

OBA allows local variation only as parameterised choice within invariant rails.

### Parameterised local variation (allowed; binding requirement to parameterise)
Examples include:
- service-level targets within national minima (lead-time norms, coverage targets),
- local prioritisation weights based on L hotspots and R/B tail exposure,
- regional implementation details for commons portfolios (housing types, mobility solutions),
- local levy rates within anti-regression constraints.

### Invariants (non-delegable; binding)
- Kernel definitions and classification rules (P/E/C, L, R/B tails, control/block tests).
- Contestability minima: reason codes, appeal rights, time bounds, access to calculations.
- Procurement rails: open standards, transparency, anti-lock-in, and audit pack requirements.
- Privacy minimalism and purpose limitation; no secondary profiling.
- Fail-closed discipline: opacity and missing evidence default to non-conformance.

### Delegation discipline (binding)
- Every local parameter must be versioned, signed, and published with method references and change logs; “informal practice” is
  non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch12p02s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P02.S04 — Intergovernmental coupling: national minima, local modules, escalation paths

Local autonomy exists inside a coupling architecture so minimum rights and rails are uniform, while modules allow local fit.

### Coupling model (binding)
- **National minima**: baseline access rights, contestability minima, core definitions/invariants, and measurement requirements.
- **Local modules**: parameter sets and implementation choices consistent with minima, using standard templates and data dictionaries.
- **Escalation paths**: defined triggers for when national/regional support intervenes (capacity breach, capture signals, persistent
  non-conformance).

### Escalation triggers (binding)
- Persistent lead-time and backlog breaches; high drop-off rates (administrative L).
- Repeated selective enforcement or opaque exceptions.
- Procurement capture or vendor lock-in breaches.
- Failure to publish required metrics or to provide audit packs.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Coupling and escalation protocols must be documented, published in aggregate, and exercised in practice; “never-used escalation” is
  treated as governance theatre and must be tested (fail-closed against non-intervention drift).
