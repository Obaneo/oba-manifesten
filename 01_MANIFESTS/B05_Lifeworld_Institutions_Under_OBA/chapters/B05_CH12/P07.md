# B05.CH12.P07 — Audit of the auditor: local oversight architecture

This paragraph specifies the oversight architecture for local institutions under OBA, with emphasis on **audit of the auditor**.
Local governance is a capture surface; audits are the mechanism intended to detect drift, but auditors themselves can become captured,
performative, or dependent on the institutions and vendors they assess. OBA therefore requires meta-audits (audits of auditors),
rotation and peer review, public openness of audit outputs (aggregated), integrity controls for local rule artefacts (signed releases
of templates/parameters with change logs), and hard stops when selective enforcement, opaque exceptions, or capture signals appear. The
design goal is that oversight is contestable, reproducible, and resistant to “compliance theatre”.

---

<a id="b05ch12p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P07.S01 — Meta-audits: audits of auditors, rotation, peer review

Audits must themselves be auditable. OBA treats oversight as a system with failure modes.

### Meta-audit requirements (binding)
- **Audits of auditors**: periodically evaluate auditor methods, independence, sampling choices, and conflict-of-interest controls.
- **Rotation**: rotate audit teams and lead auditors on a schedule; avoid permanent auditor–institution relationships.
- **Peer review**: require peer review of major audit findings and of “clean” reports in high-risk domains (procurement, enforcement,
  quasi-public bodies).

### Independence constraints (binding)
- Auditors may not receive material compensation from vendors they assess; consulting relationships that create dependency are
  prohibited.
- Audit scopes may not be negotiated to exclude high-risk areas without documented rationale and publication.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Meta-audits must produce findings with remediation actions and deadlines; repeated meta-audit failures trigger auditor replacement.

<a id="b05ch12p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P07.S02 — Openness: audit summaries, conformance status, remediation progress

Oversight that cannot be seen cannot maintain legitimacy or deterrence. OBA requires public openness in privacy-safe form.

### Openness minima (binding)
- **Audit summaries**: publish aggregate summaries of audit findings: domains assessed, key issues, severity distribution, and repeat
  patterns.
- **Conformance status**: maintain a public conformance register for local institutions and quasi-public bodies: status (conform /
  non-conform / under remediation), scope notes, and dates.
- **Remediation progress**: publish remediation plans, milestones, and completion rates; “open findings” must have deadlines.

### Anti-theatre rule (binding)
- Dashboards without remedies are non-conforming. If findings recur without enforcement, audit is treated as captured and triggers
  governance intervention.

<a id="b05ch12p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P07.S03 — Integrity: signed releases of local templates/parameters, change logs

Rules and templates are governance code. Without integrity controls, drift can be hidden in “small edits”.

### Integrity controls (binding)
- **Signed releases**: local template sets, parameter sets, and decision-rule versions must be released as signed artefacts with
  version identifiers.
- **Change logs**: every change requires a change log entry: what changed, why, expected impacts, and rollback conditions.
- **Diff visibility**: provide machine-readable diffs for auditors; prevent “silent” updates to decision rules and notices.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Enforcement decisions must reference the versioned rule set used; missing version references are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch12p07s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P07.S04 — Hard stop: selective enforcement, opaque exceptions, or capture signals → immediate intervention

Certain patterns indicate governance failure and must trigger immediate intervention rather than gradual remediation.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- **Selective enforcement**: patterns of unequal enforcement across cohorts or regions without transparent criteria; anomalies in
  sanction rates.
- **Opaque exceptions**: missing or incomplete exceptions registers; discretionary deals without audit packs and sunsets.
- **Capture signals**: procurement anomalies (repeat sole-source awards, inflated change orders), vendor dominance without exit plans,
  revolving-door violations, and audit suppression attempts.
- **Incident concealment**: missing incident logs or repeated “no incidents” claims inconsistent with operational realities.

### Immediate interventions (binding)
- Trigger external investigation/forensics; freeze contested enforcement practices; suspend suspect procurements; mandate auditor
  replacement or rotation.
- Impose temporary constraints on local autonomy: require use of standard templates/modules and central oversight until integrity is
  restored.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If local institutions cannot maintain contestable auditability, their discretionary scope is reduced to auditable functions until
  redesign and governance reset is completed (fail-closed against local capture).
