# B05.CH12.P03 — Local commons: registration, governance, and maintenance-first

This paragraph specifies local commons under OBA: registration, governance, and maintenance-first discipline. Local commons are the
concrete capacity layer that reduces L and stabilises R/B tails: housing access, mobility options, care support, community spaces, and
digital service desks (for inclusion and dispute navigation). Without registry discipline, commons drift into informal patronage,
opaque eligibility, and under-maintenance; without anti-capture rules, vendors and local networks can turn commons into toll positions.
OBA therefore requires an explicit commons portfolio, a standard registry (commons_id) with access rules and incident logs, allocation
prioritisation based on L hotspots and R/B tails, and anti-capture governance (rotation, conflict-of-interest controls, vendor-dominance
checks, public minutes). Commons are treated as ΔG capacity and governed accordingly.

---

<a id="b05ch12p03s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P03.S01 — Commons portfolio: housing, mobility, care, community spaces, digital service desks

Local commons are defined as a portfolio of capacity assets and services that provide baseline access and reduce lifeworld clamp.

### Portfolio categories (binding)
- **Housing commons**: public/non-profit housing units, emergency housing capacity, and housing support infrastructure (link CH07).
- **Mobility commons**: local public transport modules, active mobility infrastructure, community transport options (link CH08).
- **Care commons**: local care facilities and support services, long-term care access channels, respite capacity (link CH09).
- **Community spaces**: libraries, community centres, shared work/training spaces, safe public spaces that reduce social L and increase
  participation.
- **Digital service desks**: local assistance points for identity/payment access issues, form/navigation support, dispute initiation,
  and accessibility inclusion (link CH10/CH12.P06).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Each commons asset/service must have a defined purpose, access rule set, and maintenance/incident discipline.

<a id="b05ch12p03s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P03.S02 — Registry discipline: commons_id, access rules, maintenance budget, incident logs

Commons must be registrable and auditable to prevent drift into informal control.

### Registry discipline (binding)
- Every commons asset/service must have a **commons_id** and be listed in a local commons registry.
- Registry entries must include:
  - access eligibility rules and prioritisation logic,
  - capacity descriptors (units, seats, hours, coverage),
  - maintenance budget ring-fencing and backlog status,
  - incident logs (safety incidents, downtime, access denials, major complaints),
  - governance body and responsible steward/manager.

### Integrity and versioning (binding)
- Access rules and parameters must be versioned and signed, with change logs and rationale.
- Exceptions must be logged in an exceptions register with reason codes and sunset dates.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated registry summaries: portfolio composition, capacity trends, backlog indicators, and incident volumes (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch12p03s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P03.S03 — Prioritisation: L hotspots and R/B tails as allocation criteria

Commons allocation is governed by lifeworld incidence, not by political discretion or informal networks.

### Prioritisation principles (binding)
- Prioritise households/cohorts facing:
  - high essentials burdens (L hotspots: housing cost burden, energy burden, mobility exclusion),
  - high tail exposure (R/B): risk of homelessness, health shock vulnerability, learning loss, domestic instability.
- Use transparent scoring/priority classes with reason codes; avoid hidden criteria.

### Anti-regression constraint (binding)
- Allocation rules must not systematically disadvantage data-poor or bureaucracy-poor groups; documentation requirements must be
  minimal and contestable.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated allocation outcomes and waitlist distributions (median/p90/p95), and breach events triggering capacity escalation.

<a id="b05ch12p03s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P03.S04 — Anti-capture: rotation, conflict-of-interest rules, vendor-dominance checks, public minutes

Commons portfolios are attractive capture targets: contracts, property, and service delivery create rent surfaces.

### Anti-capture minima (binding)
- **Rotation**: rotate key governance and audit roles; avoid permanent control by a small local network.
- **Conflict-of-interest rules**: mandatory disclosure of ties; recusal requirements; cooling-off periods for officials moving to vendors.
- **Vendor-dominance checks**: monitor dependency on single providers for maintenance, IT, security, or staffing; high dependency
  triggers diversification and exit plans.
- **Public minutes and decision logs**: publish governance minutes, allocation rule changes, procurement decisions, and exceptions in
  privacy-safe form.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Evidence of favouritism, opaque allocation, or vendor capture triggers governance reset: leadership rotation, procurement review, and
  where needed conversion of delivery to a compliant operator (fail-closed against local commons capture).
