# B05.CH12.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for local institutions under OBA and the hard stops that apply when local execution
drifts into capture, discretion creep, and compliance theatre. Local institutions hold practical block power; when governance fails,
households experience immediate lifeworld harm through administrative L (lead times, errors, drop-off), arbitrary exceptions,
selective enforcement, and postcode-lottery outcomes. Because these failures are often self-concealing (the institution controls the
logs, metrics, and narratives), OBA defines explicit non-conformance signals and fail-closed responses. The objective is order
compatibility: local power must remain contestable, auditable, and parameterised; when it cannot, local autonomy is temporarily
restricted and redesign becomes mandatory.

---

<a id="b05ch12p13s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P13.S01 — Local capture: networks of vendors/officials → forensics and governance reset

Capture is a structural failure mode: local networks convert public tasks into toll ecosystems through procurement, exceptions, and
selective enforcement.

### Capture indicators (binding)
- Repeated sole-source procurement awards, inflated change orders, and vendor dominance without exit plans.
- Revolving-door patterns, undisclosed relationships, and repeated recusal failures.
- Exceptions and discretionary deals concentrated among connected actors; opaque decision logs and missing audit packs.
- Audit suppression, incident concealment, and retaliation against whistleblowers.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Trigger independent forensics: procurement bid analysis, contract and change-order audits, beneficial ownership mapping, and
  communications/decision-log review under lawful authority.
- Freeze contested procurements and discretionary programmes where necessary; replace auditors and rotate leadership.
- Apply governance reset: restructure roles, tighten procurement rails, enforce signed template releases, and reassign functions to
  compliant operators where capture is embedded (fail-closed against local capture networks).

<a id="b05ch12p13s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P13.S02 — Discretion creep: ever more “tailoring” without rules → non-conform

Discretion creep is the gradual expansion of “case-by-case” tailoring until rules lose meaning and arbitrariness becomes normal.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Increasing exceptions volumes without published criteria, reason codes, and sunsets.
- Decision rules applied inconsistently across offices or cohorts; drift justified as “local practice”.
- Informal thresholds and undocumented eligibility heuristics; reliance on staff discretion instead of parameterised rules.
- Growing administrative L (lead times, drop-off) as users must negotiate outcomes rather than follow clear processes.

### Fail-closed response (binding)
- Require parameterisation and template standardisation: convert discretion into explicit rule variants with signed releases and change
  logs.
- If discretion cannot be bounded, restrict the pathway to auditable modules and require external oversight until rules are restored
  (fail-closed against arbitrariness).

<a id="b05ch12p13s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P13.S03 — Compliance theatre: dashboards without remedies → legitimacy breach

Compliance theatre is measurement used as performance rather than as a trigger for correction.

### Theatre indicators (binding)
- Dashboards published without remediation actions, deadlines, or ownership.
- Repeat audit findings and recurring incident categories without structural change.
- Targets that are met via reclassification games (moving waiting off the books) rather than real capacity improvements.
- Public communications that cite “constraints” while exceptions and procurement opacity persist.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat theatre as legitimacy breach: require action registers linked to metrics (what will change, by when, with what resources).
- Where governance refuses remedies, escalate to external intervention: auditor replacement, management changes, and procurement review
  (fail-closed against performative transparency).

<a id="b05ch12p13s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: persistent failure on contestability/auditability → temporarily restrict local autonomy and force redesign

OBA treats persistent loss of contestability and auditability as incompatibility signals that require structural intervention.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- Dispute/appeal rails ineffective in practice (long delays, no access to calculations, retaliation signals).
- Missing or non-tamper-evident logs; inability to reproduce decisions; missing exceptions registers.
- Persistent procurement capture signals and vendor lock-in without exit plans.
- Failure to publish required metrics safely and reproducibly; refusal to provide audit packs.

### Mandatory response (binding)
- Temporarily restrict local autonomy to auditable modules: enforce standard templates, central oversight of procurements, and external
  audit control until integrity returns.
- Deploy intergovernmental support mechanisms: shared service centres, standard tooling modules, and capacity surge programmes.
- Force redesign: restructure roles/separation of functions, rewrite contracts and procurement arrangements, and re-establish
  contestability rails with time bounds and ombuds escalation.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Fail-closed interventions, restricted-autonomy periods, and redesign milestones must be logged in a public conformance register
  (privacy-safe aggregation) to prevent quiet drift and to preserve legitimacy.
