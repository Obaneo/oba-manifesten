# B05.CH12.P08 — Local procurement rails: preventing vendor lock-in and corruption

This paragraph specifies local procurement under OBA as a primary anti-capture rail. Local procurement is where public money becomes
vendor dependence: software systems, maintenance contracts, staffing agencies, construction, and service outsourcing. Procurement is a
known entry point for both corruption and structural lock-in (vendors become indispensable, change orders inflate, “no alternative”
becomes the permanent justification). Under OBA, procurement must preserve contestability and auditability. OBA therefore requires open
standards and portability (with escrow/source-access triggers for critical systems), transparent procurement logs (including change
orders and vendor dependency graphs), performance-based contracting tied to ΔG and access outcomes, and forensic escalation when
corruption risks appear. Procurement that normalises lock-in or opacity is non-conforming.

---

<a id="b05ch12p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P08.S01 — Open standards: mandatory interop, portability, escrow/source-access triggers

Open standards are the default anti-lock-in mechanism; critical systems require continuity guarantees.

### Open standards (binding)
- Require documented, open interfaces and data formats for procured systems (case management, payments, scheduling, logistics, digital
  service desks).
- Prohibit proprietary formats and paywalled interoperability for systems that become execution-critical.

### Portability and exit (binding)
- Contracts must include portability obligations: standard exports, migration support, and time-bounded switching windows.
- Require exit-cost disclosure and staged migration plans for critical vendors.

### Escrow / source-access triggers (binding)
- For critical software and control systems, require escrow or source-access triggers that activate under vendor failure, repeated
  breach, insolvency, or lock-in abuse.
- Avoid “single maintainer” clauses; ensure multi-vendor support pathways.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Procurement decisions must include an interoperability audit pack: standards compliance, exit plan, and lifecycle maintenance
  commitments.

<a id="b05ch12p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P08.S02 — Transparency: tenders, award rationales, change orders (aggregated), vendor dependency graphs

Transparency is the primary deterrent against procurement capture.

### Transparency minima (binding)
- Publish tenders and award criteria; publish award rationales with scoring summaries (privacy-safe; protect legitimate trade secrets,
  but not procurement logic).
- Publish change-order volumes and categories (aggregated) and require justification packs for material change orders.
- Maintain a vendor registry with ownership look-through where feasible and conflict-of-interest disclosures.

### Vendor dependency graphs (binding)
- Maintain and publish (aggregate) vendor dependency indicators: share of spend by vendor, critical-path systems by vendor, and
  concentration metrics.
- High dependency triggers diversification and exit planning.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Sole-source awards require explicit justification and short renewal cycles; repeat sole-source patterns are presumptive capture
  signals.

<a id="b05ch12p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P08.S03 — Performance contracting: SLAs tied to ΔG/access; penalties; exit assistance

Procurement must be tied to lifeworld outcomes and infrastructure performance, not to paper deliverables.

### Performance SLAs (binding)
- Contracts must specify SLAs aligned with ΔG and access:
  - uptime and recovery times for systems,
  - lead-time reductions for execution pathways,
  - backlog reduction and maintenance response times,
  - user support response times and dispute-handling assistance.
- SLAs must include tail reporting (p90/p95) and incident classifications.

### Penalties and incentives (binding)
- Apply penalties for SLA breach and incident concealment; prohibit “bonus for compliance theatre” structures.
- Incentives may exist for measurable improvements, but must include anti-Goodhart constraints (no improvement by shifting burdens into
  tails or excluded cohorts).

### Exit assistance (binding)
- Vendors must provide exit assistance: documentation, migration tooling, data export, and handover support within time bounds.
- Failure to provide exit support triggers contract remedies and eligibility restrictions for future tenders.

## B05.CH12.P08.S04 — Corruption risk: conflict-of-interest, revolving door, procurement anomalies → forensics

OBA treats corruption risk as a structural failure mode and requires forensic escalation, not internal reassurance.

### Corruption risk indicators (binding)
- Conflicts of interest, undisclosed relationships, revolving-door movements, and repeated recusal failures.
- Procurement anomalies: repeated sole-source awards, inflated change orders, unusual price dispersion, and non-competitive bid
  patterns.
- Vendor dominance combined with weak performance and repeated contract renewals (“too embedded to replace”).

### Forensic escalation (binding)
- Trigger independent forensics: contract review, bid analysis, change-order audit, and beneficial ownership mapping.
- Freeze contested procurement actions where necessary; impose temporary procurement controls and external oversight.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven corruption, concealment, or systematic procurement capture triggers governance reset: leadership changes, vendor bans,
  contract unwinds, and tighter procurement rails (fail-closed against local procurement capture).
