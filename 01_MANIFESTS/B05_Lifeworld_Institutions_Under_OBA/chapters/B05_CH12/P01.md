# B05.CH12.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of local institutions under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of local institutions under OBA. Local institutions are where
abstract policy becomes lived reality: allocation decisions, enforcement posture, and exceptions handling are experienced directly as
L (time, friction, arbitrariness) and as R/B tail exposure (loss of housing access, loss of benefits, cascading administrative
failures). Because local actors hold practical block power (service access, permits, enforcement, procurement), local governance is a
primary capture surface: discretionary deals, vendor dependence, and selective enforcement can override formal rights. OBA therefore
treats local execution as constitutive infrastructure: power must be close to the lifeworld for responsiveness, but bound by hard rails
(templates, audit packs, contestability, and invariants) to prevent arbitrariness and capture.

---

<a id="b05ch12p01s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P01.S01 — Purpose: execution power close to the lifeworld, with hard rails against arbitrariness and capture

Under OBA, local execution exists to translate order rules into practical outcomes without becoming discretionary power.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Proximity to lifeworld**: decisions are made close enough to local conditions to be responsive (housing pressure, mobility gaps,
  care capacity, local shocks), using explicit parameters rather than ad hoc discretion.
- **Hard rails against arbitrariness**: decisions must be reproducible from published rules and documented evidence; exceptions are
  logged and sunset-bounded.
- **Anti-capture posture**: prevent networks of vendors/officials from converting local execution into a toll ecosystem; procurement and
  service delivery must remain contestable.
- **Administrative L reduction**: local execution must minimise lead times, drop-off, and paperwork burdens; bureaucracy is treated as
  an L surface to be engineered down.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- “Local tailoring” used to justify undocumented discretionary deals, selective enforcement, or vendor-controlled policy execution is
  non-conforming.

---

<a id="b05ch12p01s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P01.S02 — Scope: municipalities/regions, executing agencies, quasi-public institutions, local commons, enforcement

CH12 covers the local execution stack across public and quasi-public actors.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Municipalities and regions**: councils and administrations, service desks, permits, local taxes/fees, and programme execution.
- **Executing agencies**: benefits administration, housing allocation, care coordination, employment services, inspections, and
  enforcement agencies operating locally.
- **Quasi-public institutions**: autonomous bodies delivering public tasks (utilities, housing bodies, transport operators, education
  chains) where they interact with local execution.
- **Local commons**: local commons portfolios (housing, mobility assets, care facilities, community spaces, digital service desks) and
  their governance.
- **Enforcement and dispute**: rule enforcement posture, sanctions, inspections, and local dispute/appeal pathways.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH12 does not define national legislative content; it defines local execution rails, interfaces, and accountability structures.

<a id="b05ch12p01s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P01.S03 — Binding: Kernel Spec (definitions/invariants), Codex (templates, audit, detection), commons and procurement rails, R/B lifeworld test

Local institutions are bound by higher-level invariants and by evidence-based execution standards.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **Kernel Spec**: local policy must preserve core definitions and invariants (P/E/C, L, R/B tails, control/block tests, caps and
  anti-avoidance logic where applicable). Local interpretation may not redefine canonical terms.
- **Codex**: use standard templates, data dictionaries, audit packs, and detection protocols; local variants must be versioned and
  signed, with diffs and justification.
- **Commons rails**: local commons must be registered, maintenance-first, and governed with transparent access rules and incident logs.
- **Procurement rails**: open standards, anti-lock-in, transparency logs, and performance contracting; procurement is treated as a
  capture surface.
- **R/B lifeworld test**: evaluate local execution by its incidence on tails: whether households face cascading failures, exclusion, and
  time/administrative clamp.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Every major local programme and rule must link: decision rule → parameter set → evidence pack → appeal path → audit trail (reproducible).

<a id="b05ch12p01s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: local discretionary deals without an audit pack/contestability are non-conform

Because local discretion is a primary capture channel, OBA applies fail-closed discipline.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- Discretionary local “deals” or exceptions granted without an audit pack: no documented criteria, no evidence basis, no sunset.
- Procurement awards and change orders without transparency logs, rationale, and standards compliance.
- Enforcement actions without reason codes and appeal paths; selective enforcement patterns.
- Local parameter changes made informally without signed releases and change logs.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Treat the action as non-conforming until corrected: require audit packs, publish the rule basis, and open contestability pathways.
- Under persistent discretionary drift, restrict local autonomy temporarily to auditable modules and enforce standard templates until
  governance integrity is restored (fail-closed against local capture).
