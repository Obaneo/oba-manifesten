# B05.CH12.P05 — Execution as infrastructure (ΔG of governance)

This paragraph specifies local execution under OBA as infrastructure capacity: the **ΔG of governance**. Execution is not merely
“administration”; it is the mechanism that determines whether rights and services are reachable, whether rules are applied uniformly,
and whether households experience administrative L (lead times, paperwork, errors, drop-off). Under-capacity produces rationing by
bureaucracy, drives selective enforcement, and invites capture through discretionary workarounds. OBA therefore requires measurable
capacity metrics (lead times, backlogs, error and appeal rates, drop-off), standardisation through templates and decision rules (without
data maximalism), maintenance-first discipline for training and tooling, and incident response as an operational norm. Structural
under-capacity is treated as a governance breach that triggers fail-closed escalation, restructuring, and intergovernmental support.

---

<a id="b05ch12p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P05.S01 — Capacity metrics: lead times, backlogs, error rates, drop-off (administrative L)

Administrative L is measurable and must be governed like any other essentials burden.

### Capacity metrics (binding)
- **Lead times**: end-to-end processing times per pathway (applications, permits, housing allocation, benefits), reported as
  distributions (median/p90/p95).
- **Backlogs**: backlog size and age distributions; breach thresholds trigger escalation.
- **Error rates**: incorrect decisions, calculation errors, missing documentation churn attributable to the institution, and reversal
  rates on appeal.
- **Drop-off**: rates of abandonment or non-completion, segmented by pathway stage (a proxy for friction and hidden exclusion).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Metrics must be computed from reproducible definitions and published as time series; reclassification games (moving waiting off the
  books) are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch12p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P05.S02 — Standardisation: templates, decision rules, uniform data dictionaries (without data maximalism)

Standardisation reduces arbitrariness and makes audit possible, but it must not become data maximalism or rigidity.

### Standardisation requirements (binding)
- **Templates**: use standard templates for applications, decisions, notices, and audit packs; templates must include reason codes,
  rule references, and appeal instructions.
- **Decision rules**: define decision rules as reproducible logic with parameter inputs; discretionary “common practice” is
  non-conforming.
- **Uniform data dictionaries**: adopt a shared data dictionary for key fields used in decisions and audits (Codex-aligned), with
  versioning and change logs.

### Without data maximalism (binding)
- Collect only data necessary for decision, execution, and audit; do not expand data collection as a substitute for competence.
- Avoid surveillance joins and secondary profiling; tiered disclosure rules apply (see P10).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Local variants must be explicit parameters (not hidden forks), versioned and signed, with documented diffs from standard templates.

<a id="b05ch12p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P05.S03 — Maintenance-first: training, tooling, continuity, incident response

Execution capacity degrades without maintenance: training, tooling, and continuity plans must be funded and operated as infrastructure.

### Maintenance-first minima (binding)
- **Training**: continuous training for staff on rule changes, templates, and dispute handling; certification where relevant; prevent
  “tribal knowledge” dependence.
- **Tooling**: reliable case management systems, calculation tooling that produces reproducible outputs, and accessibility support
  channels; tool outages are treated as service outages.
- **Continuity**: redundancy plans for staffing shortages, system outages, and surges; cross-unit mutual aid protocols.
- **Incident response**: log incidents (system outages, backlog spikes, error bursts), classify severity, and run post-mortems with
  corrective actions.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated incident and continuity metrics: outage counts, recovery times, backlog spike events, and remediation completion
  rates (privacy-safe aggregation).

## B05.CH12.P05.S04 — Hard stop: structural under-capacity → escalation, restructuring, and support mechanisms

Persistent under-capacity is not a “performance issue”; it is a structural governance failure that creates hidden exclusion.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- Persistent breach of lead-time and backlog thresholds; rising error and reversal rates; rising drop-off; inability to publish metrics.
- Chronic reliance on discretionary exceptions or informal workarounds to keep the system moving.
- Persistent tooling failures and inadequate training leading to repeated errors.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- **Escalation**: trigger intergovernmental support (regional/national) and capacity surge measures; temporarily simplify pathways and
  reduce scope to what can be executed safely and auditably.
- **Restructuring**: redesign processes, reduce administrative tolls, reallocate budgets to execution capacity, and reset management
  where needed.
- **Support mechanisms**: deploy shared service centres, commons digital service desks, and standard tooling modules to restore
  conformance.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Hard stop events and restructuring plans must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to prevent
  quiet normalisation of bureaucratic collapse.
