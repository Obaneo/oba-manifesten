# B05.CH12.P06 — Contestability: objection/appeal as a design principle

This paragraph specifies contestability under OBA as a design principle: objection and appeal are not “extras” but constitutive
infrastructure against arbitrariness, error accumulation, and capture. Local institutions hold practical block power; without low-friction
dispute rails, administrative L grows through persistence costs, and selective enforcement becomes unobservable. OBA therefore requires
a dispute flow that is low-friction, time-bounded, and reproducible (users must access the calculations and rule references), an
independent ombuds function that detects systemic patterns and triggers escalation, transparency through public aggregated case
statistics and exceptions registers, and protection against retaliation for citizens and whistleblowers. Contestability is fail-closed:
if appeal is effectively unavailable, the underlying execution regime is treated as non-conforming.

---

<a id="b05ch12p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P06.S01 — Dispute flow: low-friction, time-bounded, reproducible, with access to calculations

Disputes must be resolvable through evidence and rules, not through endurance.

### Low-friction access (binding)
- Multiple intake channels (online, phone, in-person service desk); accessibility and language support mandatory.
- Minimal documentation burden: institutions must reuse existing data and provide missing documents themselves where possible.

### Time bounds (binding)
- Strict time bounds for: acknowledgement, disclosure of evidence, provisional relief (where harm is imminent), decision, and appeal
  escalation.
- Failure to meet time bounds triggers automatic escalation and interim protections for essentials access where applicable.

### Reproducibility and access to calculations (binding)
- Users must receive: reason codes, rule references, and a calculation pack (inputs, method, thresholds/parameters) sufficient to
  recompute the decision.
- Black-box decisions and “computer says no” outcomes are non-conforming.

### Remedies (binding)
- Correction/restatement of decisions, refunds/credits where applicable, contract/template rewrite, and systemic remediation where
  patterns recur.

<a id="b05ch12p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P06.S02 — Ombuds function: independent triage and escalation for systemic patterns

The ombuds function prevents dispute systems from becoming isolated case factories.

### Ombuds role (binding)
- Independent triage of complaints and appeals, with authority to request audit packs and to escalate to auditors and mandate-grantors.
- Pattern detection: identify recurring failure modes (specific pathways, vendors, offices, discriminatory patterns) and trigger
  systemic investigations.

### Escalation powers (binding)
- Ability to mandate interim measures (pause enforcement, suspend contested fees, freeze exclusions) while investigations proceed.
- Authority to recommend structural remedies and to require management response within time bounds.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Ombuds must publish aggregated pattern reports: recurring causes, resolution times, and remediation completion rates (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch12p06s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P06.S03 — Transparency: public case statistics (aggregated), decision logs, exceptions register

Transparency is the substrate of contestability: without it, local discretion becomes untrackable.

### Public case statistics (binding)
- Publish aggregate statistics: dispute volumes by pathway, common causes, median/p90 resolution times, reversal rates, and repeat-offender
  indicators (by office/region category where safe).

### Decision logs (binding)
- Maintain and publish privacy-safe summaries of decision rule changes, major policy interpretations, and enforcement posture updates,
  with versioning and method references.

### Exceptions register (binding)
- Maintain an exceptions register for departures from standard rules: reason codes, authorisation, scope, and sunset dates.
- Opaque exceptions are treated as capture signals.

<a id="b05ch12p06s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P06.S04 — Protection against retaliation: citizens/whistleblowers, due process, non-discrimination

Contestability fails if participants fear retaliation. OBA therefore defines protective rails.

### Protection minima (binding)
- **Non-retaliation**: it is prohibited to worsen service, accelerate enforcement, or deny access because a person filed a complaint or
  acted as a witness.
- **Whistleblower protection**: secure channels for staff and contractors to report wrongdoing; protection against reprisal; mandatory
  investigation and response timelines.
- **Due process**: no sanctions or exclusions without reason codes, evidence disclosure (subject to lawful limits), and appeal rights.
- **Non-discrimination**: dispute outcomes and enforcement posture must be monitored for disparate impact; patterns trigger audit and
  remediation.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven retaliation, intimidation, or systematic denial of appeal rights triggers immediate intervention: governance reset, management
  remedies, and suspension of contested enforcement practices (fail-closed against coercive execution).
