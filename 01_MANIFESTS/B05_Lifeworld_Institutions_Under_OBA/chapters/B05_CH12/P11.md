# B05.CH12.P11 — Local crisis regimes: emergency without permanent power

This paragraph specifies local crisis regimes under OBA: how municipalities and executing agencies may operate under shocks without
turning emergency into permanent discretionary power. Local crises (energy price spikes, food shortages, health surges, infrastructure
failures) can require rapid action, but emergencies are a classic capture opportunity: opaque deals, procurement shortcuts, and
exception creep become entrenched. OBA therefore requires explicit triggers with public notice, temporariness (sunsets, scope caps,
unwind plans, post-mortem audits), prioritisation rules that protect the essentials baseline and vulnerable cohorts, and strict
prohibitions against using emergency as a channel for vendor capture or hidden fee-stacks. Emergency powers are treated as a bounded
module with fail-closed discipline.

---

<a id="b05ch12p11s01"></a>
## B05.CH12.P11.S01 — Triggers: shocks (energy/food/health) with public notice

Emergency activation must be rule-based, not purely discretionary.

### Trigger classes (binding)
- **Energy shocks**: tariff spikes, outages, heating disruptions, or baseline disconnection risks (link CH08).
- **Food shocks**: shortages, price spikes in baseline basket items, distribution failures (link CH11).
- **Health shocks**: surges in demand, outbreak events, facility failures, staffing collapse (link CH09).
- **Infrastructure incidents**: critical IT outages, cyber incidents, transport corridor failure, severe weather impacts.

### Public notice requirement (binding)
- Activation requires public notice stating: trigger evidence, scope, intended instruments, duration, and appeal channels.
- Activation must reference method_id and thresholds where applicable; “emergency by narrative” is non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch12p11s02"></a>
## B05.CH12.P11.S02 — Temporariness: sunsets, scope caps, unwind plan, post-mortem audit

Temporariness is a primary rail: emergency measures must expire by default.

### Sunset discipline (binding)
- Every emergency measure must include a sunset date (or short renewal interval) and explicit criteria for extension.
- Extensions require updated evidence packs and must be logged; indefinite continuation is non-conforming.

### Scope caps (binding)
- Measures must be bounded to the minimum scope necessary: defined domains, cohorts, and geographic areas; no blanket power expansion.
- Procurement exceptions must be limited and time-bounded; normal procurement rails resume as soon as practicable.

### Unwind plan (binding)
- Define staged unwind: restore normal rules, reconcile accounts, close exceptions, and replenish commons capacity used in the crisis.

### Post-mortem audit (binding)
- Conduct post-mortem audits within a defined window: what happened, what measures worked, capture risks, distributional incidence, and
  what ΔG investments are needed to reduce repeat crises.
- Publish public summaries (privacy-safe) and remediation plans with deadlines.

<a id="b05ch12p11s03"></a>
## B05.CH12.P11.S03 — Prioritisation: essentials baseline, vulnerable cohorts, continuity of commons

Emergency regimes must protect baseline life participation and prevent tail collapse.

### Prioritisation rules (binding)
- **Essentials baseline first**: ensure continuity of housing access rails, baseline energy provision, baseline food basket access, and
  basic healthcare access.
- **Vulnerable cohorts**: prioritise households at highest tail risk (poverty, disability, chronic illness, unstable housing, children).
- **Continuity of commons**: protect commons infrastructure operations (service desks, logistics hubs, shelters, care facilities) and
  their maintenance budgets; do not “burn the commons” to fund short-term optics.

### Non-discrimination (binding)
- Emergency allocation and enforcement must follow published rules with reason codes; selective enforcement is a hard stop.

<a id="b05ch12p11s04"></a>
## B05.CH12.P11.S04 — Prohibited: emergency as an excuse for opaque deals or vendor capture

OBA treats emergency capture as a primary failure mode.

### Prohibited patterns (binding)
- Opaque procurement awards and change orders justified by urgency without later disclosure and re-tendering.
- Emergency fees, surcharges, or new intermediary layers introduced under crisis conditions.
- Data expansion and surveillance justified by crisis without necessity proofs and sunsets.
- Vendor lock-in embedded via “temporary” platforms that become permanent.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Suspend capture-inducing measures; revert to auditable baseline instruments and transparent allocation rules.
- Trigger independent forensics for suspect procurements and exceptions; unwind contracts that embed lock-in or fee-stacks.
- Repeat violations trigger governance reset and temporary restriction of local discretionary powers (fail-closed against emergency
  capture).
