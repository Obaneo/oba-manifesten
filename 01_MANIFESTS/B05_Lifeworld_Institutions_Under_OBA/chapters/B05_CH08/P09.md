# B05.CH08.P09 — Private providers within rails: market where possible, commons where necessary

This paragraph specifies how private providers may operate in energy and mobility under OBA. OBA does not require full public
operation everywhere; it requires **rail discipline**: monopoly cores are treated as commons capacity, while contestable layers may be
served by private actors under strict anti-toll, anti-lock-in, and anti-surveillance constraints. The core governance design is a
layering model: (i) commons core infrastructure (grid/rail and critical networks) with maintenance-first and open-standards rules, and
(ii) contestable services (supply, apps, retail interfaces, value-added services) that remain portable and fee-transparent. Where
private layers drift into toll ecosystems (opaque fees, exclusivity, data extraction) they are requalified as E and must be restricted,
unbundled, or converted to commons governance.

---

<a id="b05ch08p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P09.S01 — Layering model: commons core (grid/rail) + contestable services (supply/apps)

OBA separates functions by monopoly character and capture risk.

### Commons core (binding)
- Grid/rail cores and essential network infrastructure operate under commons-like governance: maintenance-first budgets, reliability
  targets, open standards, transparent decision logs, and audit-of-the-auditor.
- Core operators may not use their position to advantage affiliated retail suppliers, apps, or service layers.

### Contestable service layers (binding)
- Retail supply, customer interfaces, mobility apps, scheduling services, and non-core services may be provided competitively, but only
  if switching is real (low friction) and fees are comparable.
- Service innovation is admissible only where it does not create hidden lock-in or surveillance rent.

### Interface rule (binding)
- The interface between core and service layers must be open: standard APIs, documented protocols, non-discriminatory access, and
  auditable billing/settlement procedures.

---

<a id="b05ch08p09s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P09.S02 — Anti-toll requirements: portability, transparent fees, bounded margins, no exclusivity lock-ins

Contestable layers must not become indirect tolls on essential access.

### Portability (binding)
- Customers must be able to switch providers without punitive fees, loss of baseline protection, or artificial delays.
- Data portability and service continuity must be ensured: usage history and necessary configuration must transfer via open formats
  without proprietary gatekeeping.

### Transparent fees (binding)
- All fees must be disclosed in standard “all-in” formats: no hidden surcharges, bundled add-ons, or penalty cascades.
- Price components must map to auditable costs; non-cost-based charges are requalified as E.

### Bounded margins in essential interfaces (binding)
- Where a service layer is functionally unavoidable (dominant platform, mandatory app), margins and fees are bounded; otherwise the
  layer is treated as a monopoly derivative and subject to stricter governance.

### No exclusivity lock-ins (binding)
- Prohibit exclusivity contracts that block competitors from using infrastructure interfaces (ticketing, charging, metering, booking).
- Prohibit long-term bundling that forces purchase of non-essential services to access essentials.

<a id="b05ch08p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P09.S03 — Data and digital control: prohibited surveillance rent in utilities/mobility apps

Digital layers can become a secondary extraction surface: behavioural data is monetised and used for steering, discrimination, and
lock-in. OBA prohibits surveillance rent in essentials.

### Prohibited practices (binding)
- Monetising behavioural or location data beyond order necessity (marketing, profiling, steering, sale of datasets).
- Dark patterns in apps that steer users into higher-cost tariffs, subscriptions, or add-ons through friction and confusion.
- Risk scoring or exclusion based on unrelated behavioural proxies, especially where it affects baseline access.

### Privacy minima (binding)
- Data collection must be minimised and purpose-limited; baseline access must not require invasive tracking.
- Provide non-digital channels for essentials access (call centres, paper options, physical ticketing) and accessibility
  accommodations.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Providers must publish privacy-safe summaries of data categories collected, retention periods, third-party sharing, and dispute/appeal
  outcomes for access denials.

<a id="b05ch08p09s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P09.S04 — Enforcement: anomaly detection (fees/lock-in) → audit → contract remedies/unbundling

OBA requires an escalation ladder so rail violations trigger correction rather than permanent tolerance.

### Step 1: anomaly detection (binding)
- Monitor indicators: fee dispersion, tariff complexity, switching friction, exclusivity patterns, complaint volumes, and denial rates.
- Trigger anomaly notices with breached thresholds and requested evidence.

### Step 2: audit pack request (binding)
- Require a standard audit pack: fee decomposition, contract terms, interface access logs, data governance policies, related-party
  relationships, and complaint/dispute handling evidence.
- Failure to provide the pack triggers fail-closed restrictions: suspension of contested fees and prohibition of new lock-in contracts.

### Step 3: contract remedies (binding)
- Apply refunds/credits for overcharges; rewrite contracts into standard formats; prohibit specific clause patterns; impose fee caps and
  switching obligations.

### Step 4: unbundling / conversion (binding)
- Where a private layer has become a toll ecosystem or monopoly derivative, enforce structural remedies:
  - **Unbundling**: separate platform/app from supply; separate billing from control; forbid affiliated self-preferencing.
  - **Interface opening**: mandate open APIs and non-discriminatory access; prohibit exclusivity.
  - **Conversion to commons governance**: if contestability cannot be restored, treat the layer as commons-critical and move it under
    stricter governance or direct public operation.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Repeated fee-stack violations, repeated lock-in patterns, or proven surveillance rent triggers licence and procurement consequences
  and mandatory structural remedy selection (fail-closed against digital toll capture).
