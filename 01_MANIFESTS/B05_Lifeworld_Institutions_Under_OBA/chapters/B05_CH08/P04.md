# B05.CH08.P04 — Tariff rails: price discipline without hidden fee-stacks

This paragraph specifies tariff rails for energy and mobility under OBA. Tariffs are not merely “prices”: in essentials and
natural-monopoly domains they are a governance interface that can either reduce L or produce a toll ecosystem. Complexity, opaque
surcharges, and fixed-charge loading can turn tariffs into fee-stacks that are hard to contest and regressive in incidence. OBA
therefore requires transparent tariff build-up, explicit separation of capex and opex recovery, bounded returns in monopoly segments,
prohibition of junk fees and opaque add-ons, and a progressive tariff mix that protects baseline consumption while charging more for
luxury and high-impact usage. Where tariff contestability is lost, OBA applies hard stops: redesign and requalification of non-auditable
components as E.

---

<a id="b05ch08p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P04.S01 — Transparent tariff build-up: cost components, indexation, capex/opex separation

Tariffs must be decomposable into auditable components so households and auditors can reproduce bills and contest unjustified charges.

### Transparent build-up (binding)
- Tariffs must provide a public component table showing: energy/transport unit cost, network cost, balancing/resilience charges,
  policy levies (if any), and service components (billing, metering), with explicit definitions.
- Every customer bill must be reproducible from published parameters, measured usage, and disclosed adjustment rules.

### Indexation discipline (binding)
- Indexation is permitted only to published standard indices, with explicit caps on adjustment frequency and magnitude (parameters).
- Indexation rules must be stated in contract terms; discretionary “market adjustments” are prohibited.

### Capex/opex separation (binding)
- Operators must separate **capex recovery** (infrastructure investment) from **opex recovery** (operations/maintenance) in tariff
  reporting.
- Capex recovery must be tied to auditable investment cases and delivered performance; “capex” cannot be used as an undefined margin
  bucket.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish annual tariff method packs: component definitions, index references, capex/opex accounts, and reconciliation against realised
  costs and performance.

<a id="b05ch08p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P04.S02 — Bounded returns: regulated margins for monopoly segments; prohibited junk fees

In natural monopoly segments, excess returns are rentier extraction. OBA therefore bounds margins and prohibits fee-stacks.

### Regulated margins (binding)
- Monopoly segments (grid/rail cores, essential distribution networks) must operate under bounded return frameworks: regulated margins
  set through published, reviewable rules linked to reliability, maintenance, and resilience performance.
- Performance incentives must be symmetric: outperformance can earn bounded reward; underperformance triggers automatic penalties and
  remediation obligations.

### Prohibited junk fees (binding)
- Opaque surcharges, “administration fees”, switching fees designed as lock-in, convenience charges, and penalty cascades are
  prohibited.
- Fees whose functional effect is to exploit inattention, complexity, or inability to exit are classified as E regardless of label.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Where operator profitability depends materially on junk fees or opaque add-ons, the tariff structure is non-conforming and must be
  rewritten into a transparent component model with bounded returns.

<a id="b05ch08p04s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P04.S03 — Progressive tariff mix: baseline cheap, luxury consumption more expensive (anti-regression)

Because energy and mobility are essentials, tariff incidence must protect baseline access and avoid regressive fixed-charge loading.

### Progressive mix (binding)
- **Baseline cheap**: baseline consumption (defined under P03 parameters) must be priced at the lowest marginal rates; the baseline is
  protected from punitive fixed charges and volatility exposure.
- **Higher tiers more expensive**: higher consumption and luxury/high-impact usage faces higher marginal rates, reflecting system
  strain and externalities without punishing baseline survival.
- **Fixed-charge discipline**: fixed charges must be minimised and justified by auditable fixed costs; regressive loading is
  prohibited.

### Peak pricing discipline (binding)
- Peak pricing may be used only with baseline protection and transparent rules; households must have clear options to avoid peaks or
  receive predictable hedges where flexibility is structurally limited (e.g., heating in cold spells).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish incidence analysis (aggregate): burden distributions by income cohort, baseline coverage, and tail impacts during seasonal
  peaks (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch08p04s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P04.S04 — Hard stop: tariff complexity as a toll ecosystem → redesign and requalification

When tariffs become too complex to reproduce and compare, complexity itself becomes a toll and an extraction surface.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- Customers cannot reproduce bills from disclosed parameters; tariffs contain non-disclosed add-ons or opaque reconciliation.
- Proliferation of plan variants whose primary function is price discrimination through confusion.
- High and rising complaint/dispute volumes correlated with tariff complexity and opaque surcharges.
- Significant share of operator revenue attributable to non-comparable components (fees, penalties, “services” without cost basis).

### Fail-closed response (binding)
- Non-reproducible components are classified as E by default and suspended from billing until corrected.
- Tariff structures must be simplified into standard templates with a limited number of comparable plans and a public component table.
- Repeat violations trigger sanctions, licence conditions, and—where necessary—conversion of the segment to stricter commons governance
  or direct public operation (fail-closed against tariff toll capture).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Hard stop events, redesign mandates, and sanctions must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to
  prevent quiet continuation via rebranding.
