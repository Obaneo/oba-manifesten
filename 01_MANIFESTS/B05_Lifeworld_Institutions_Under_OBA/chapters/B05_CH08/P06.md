# B05.CH08.P06 — Decarbonisation: investment channel without asset support

This paragraph specifies decarbonisation under OBA as an admissible investment programme only insofar as it expands real capacity
(ΔG), reduces essentials burdens (L), and improves resilience—without becoming a subsidy machine for asset values or rentier cashflows.
Because decarbonisation involves large capital flows and often uses public finance and ΔM-adjacent instruments, it is a high-risk
surface for leakage into claim/asset support, vendor capture, and lock-in. OBA therefore defines allowed and forbidden ΔM channels,
requires conditionality (interoperability, repairability, lifecycle maintenance), and binds programme evaluation to lifeworld metrics
(ΔG/L/RB tails). CO₂ reduction is treated as an important consequence, but conformance is determined by whether the programme reduces L
and expands durable commons capacity without creating new toll ecosystems.

---

<a id="b05ch08p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P06.S01 — Allowed ΔM channels: commons capex (grids, storage), renovation/insulation, public transport/rail, local heat

ΔM-adjacent finance is admissible only into channels that create durable infrastructure and reduce L through real capacity.

### Allowed channels (binding)
- **Commons capex: grids and storage**: reinforcement, interconnectors, substations, resilient control systems, and storage/flexibility
  infrastructure, under tagging, leakage tests, and open-standards procurement.
- **Renovation and insulation**: building upgrades that reduce energy burden and volatility exposure, integrated with maintenance-first
  rails (linked to housing CH07.P09).
- **Public transport and rail**: investments that reduce car dependence and lower mobility L (coverage, frequency, reliability),
  including rolling stock and signalling upgrades.
- **Local heat and district systems**: heat networks and local resilience infrastructure where governance is commons-like, maintenance
  is ring-fenced, and tariffs remain contestable.

### Channel discipline (binding)
- Each programme must tag flows by purpose class and publish an audit pack linking investment → expected ΔG output → expected L/RB
  impacts → lock-in and vendor risk controls.

---

<a id="b05ch08p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P06.S02 — Forbidden channels: subsidies that only raise prices/asset values without ΔG or L reduction

OBA prohibits decarbonisation framed as transition while functioning as asset support, rentier stabilisation, or price-capitalisation.

### Forbidden channels (binding)
- **Demand-side subsidies under constrained capacity** that capitalise into higher prices (e.g., broad incentives that raise equipment
  prices or rents without expanding installation capacity or reducing operating costs).
- **Asset-value support**: programmes whose functional effect is to raise valuations of incumbent assets, concessions, or monopoly cash
  streams without measurable ΔG or reliability gains.
- **Vendor lock-in subsidies**: funding conditioned on proprietary systems, closed standards, or exclusive service arrangements.
- **Green-washing instruments**: credits and certificates that create financial upside without real infrastructure delivery or
  maintenance.

### Leakage test (binding)
- If the dominant incidence is higher asset prices, higher rents, higher monopoly margins, or higher vendor rents—without measurable ΔG
  output and L reduction—the channel is presumed leakage and is non-conforming.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Under detected leakage, suspend the programme and redirect to allowed channels with stronger conditionality and measurable output.

---

<a id="b05ch08p06s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P06.S03 — Conditionality: interoperability, repairability, lifecycle maintenance, open data for audits

Decarbonisation investments must not create new lock-in and maintenance burdens. Conditionality is therefore binding.

### Conditionality minima (binding)
- **Interoperability**: require open interfaces and standards so equipment, control systems, and charging/heat components can be
  replaced and integrated across vendors.
- **Repairability**: require parts availability, documentation, and right-to-repair provisions; prohibit sealed ecosystems that force
  expensive replacement cycles.
- **Lifecycle maintenance**: programmes must include funded maintenance plans and backlog thresholds; capex without maintenance is
  non-conforming.
- **Open data for audits**: operational and performance data required for public audit must be available in open formats, with
  privacy-minimal aggregation (no person-tracking).

### Procurement linkage (binding)
- Procurement rules in CH08.P05 apply: avoid exclusive contracts, ensure competitive entry, and bind suppliers to measurable
  performance outcomes.

---

<a id="b05ch08p06s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P06.S04 — Measurability: CO₂ reduction as a consequence, but primary is ΔG/L/RB effects (lifeworld test)

OBA treats CO₂ reduction as necessary but not sufficient. Conformance is determined by lifeworld outcomes and durable capacity.

### Primary measurables (binding)
- **ΔG output**: installed capacity, reinforced infrastructure, storage/flexibility capacity, renovated building stock, upgraded rail
  capacity—measured with cycle time and cost per unit.
- **L reduction**: energy burden and mobility burden distributions and tails; reduced volatility exposure; fewer arrears/disconnects.
- **RB resilience**: reduced shock sensitivity, faster recovery from incidents, improved reliability metrics (e.g., outages, delays).
- **Lock-in risk**: vendor concentration, proprietary dependency, and lifecycle cost trajectory.

### Secondary measurables (binding)
- **CO₂ reduction**: measured as a consequence of the above, with method transparency and avoidance of offset accounting that obscures
  real delivery.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If a decarbonisation programme worsens L (through higher tariffs, higher housing/transport costs) or creates lock-in without durable
  ΔG delivery, it is non-conforming and must be redesigned or terminated (fail-closed against green rentierisation).
