# B05.CH08 — Energy and mobility: infrastructure, tariffs, resilience, and decarbonisation under caps/ΔM rails

This chapter specifies **energy and mobility** as essentials infrastructure under OBA: systems whose reliability, affordability, and
shock resilience directly determine L and R/B tails. Energy (electricity, heat, fuels) and mobility (public transport, rail/road,
logistics) combine natural-monopoly surfaces, high capital intensity, and strong exposure to vendor capture and rentier cashflow
engineering. Under OBA, these domains must be governed as **commons capacity (ΔG)** with maintenance-first discipline, transparent
tariff structures, and contestable service layers—while strictly prohibiting ΔM leakage into asset/claim support, monopoly toll
ecosystems, or lock-in.

CH08 is designed to be **anti-washing** and **fail-closed**. Tariff complexity that functions as a fee-stack, proprietary control
systems that create vendor lock-in, “green” investment that raises asset values without ΔG or L reduction, or emergency regimes that
quietly introduce claim support are treated as non-conforming until corrected. The chapter therefore provides diagnostics, baseline
access floors, tariff rails, commons infrastructure governance and procurement rules, decarbonisation channel discipline, mobility
system redesign away from car lock-in, shock/emergency regimes with sunset discipline, integration with caps, and publication
requirements to keep the infrastructure layer contestable and non-captured.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing energy regulation**: use CH08 to define tariff rails (transparent build-up, bounded returns, progressive
  mix), prevent fee-stack surcharges, and bind monopoly segments to audit, open standards, and procurement discipline.
- If you are **designing grid and infrastructure policy**: use CH08 to treat grids/rail as commons capacity (ΔG), prioritise
  maintenance-first backlogs, set resilience and reliability thresholds, and prevent vendor lock-in in operations/IT.
- If you are **designing decarbonisation programmes**: use CH08 to route ΔM into admissible channels (commons capex, storage, heat
  networks, insulation/renovation, public transport/rail) and prohibit channels that primarily inflate prices or rentier cashflows.
- If you are **designing mobility systems**: use CH08 to define public transport as a public option, expand active mobility as a low-L
  alternative, and redesign logistics to reduce dependence and bottlenecks.
- If you are **designing emergency regimes**: use CH08 to set triggers, instruments, and strict sunset/unwind rules; prevent emergency
  measures from becoming permanent claim support or vendor capture.
- If you are **auditing lifeworld outcomes**: use the publication requirements (P11) to track energy burden, reliability (SAIDI/SAIFI),
  disconnects/arrears, mobility accessibility and travel time, maintenance backlogs, incident logs, and lock-in/fee-stack signals.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA-compatible design of energy systems, mobility networks, tariffs, infrastructure investment,
and decarbonisation programmes. In particular, the ΔM channel rails (allowed/forbidden), tariff discipline (anti-regression and
anti-fee-stack), maintenance-first commons governance, procurement rules (open standards; vendor lock-in prevention), emergency
sunsets, and caps integration are treated as **constitutive** safeguards against infrastructure rentierisation and lock-in.

If CH08 constraints are weakened—by opaque surcharges, discretionary concession renewals, proprietary control stacks, “green” subsidies
that capitalise into asset values, or emergency regimes without sunsets—the lifeworld layer will register predictable failure signals:
higher energy burdens (especially tails), increased arrears/disconnects, declining reliability, worsening mobility accessibility, and
rising lock-in. In that case, the implementation must be treated as drifting toward incompatibility and must trigger redesign, scope
restriction to auditable coverage, procurement and governance resets, and (where necessary) hard stops on prohibited channels.
