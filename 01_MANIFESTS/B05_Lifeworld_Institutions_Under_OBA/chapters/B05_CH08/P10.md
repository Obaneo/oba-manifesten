# B05.CH08.P10 — Integration with caps: preventing rentier dominance in infrastructure assets

This paragraph specifies how OBA caps (W-MAX and I-MAX) apply to infrastructure ownership and concession cashflows in energy and
mobility. Infrastructure assets (grids, rail corridors, charging networks, utility concessions) are attractive accumulation vehicles
because they can generate stable, quasi-monopoly cashflows backed by essentials demand. If caps do not apply with look-through, the
system drifts toward rentier dominance: financial engineering captures tariff streams, procurement and investment are steered toward
cashflow protection, and ΔM and emergency regimes are pressured into implicit claim support. OBA therefore includes infrastructure
ownership interests in the W base, applies look-through across funds/SPVs and concession chains to the ultimate beneficial controller
(UBC), constrains excess returns via cashflow caps, and defines remedy ladders that prioritise L reduction and contestability over
preserving rentier claims.

---

<a id="b05ch08p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P10.S01 — Infrastructure ownership in W base: look-through on funds/SPVs, concessions, long-term contracts

Infrastructure exposure often sits inside complex vehicles. OBA treats beneficial control and economic benefit as the inclusion basis.

### Inclusion rule (binding)
- Infrastructure interests included in W: direct equity, fund units, SPV shares, concession rights, and long-term contractual rights
  that confer economically significant cashflows or control (including availability payments and guaranteed-return structures).
- Where operational control is separated from ownership, both control rights and economic benefit rights are included for cap
  assessment.

### Valuation (binding)
- Use a standard valuation basis (jurisdictional parameter) with conservative defaults under illiquidity and model risk.
- Vehicle valuations must reconcile to underlying asset registers and contract cashflow rights; discretionary internal marks without
  auditability are non-conforming.

### Look-through (binding)
- Apply look-through to the ultimate beneficial controller (UBC) across funds/SPVs, nominee chains, cross-holdings, and layered
  partnerships; holdings under common control are consolidated.
- Concession rights cannot be split into multiple entities to avoid attribution; functional consolidation applies.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Maintain an auditable infrastructure interest register and UBC attribution graph for cap assessment (privacy-minimal for public
  outputs; full detail for auditors).

<a id="b05ch08p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P10.S02 — Cashflow caps: constraints on excess returns from monopoly concessions

Even where ownership remains nominally private, monopoly cashflows must be bounded. Excess returns are treated as extraction (E).

### Cashflow cap principle (binding)
- Monopoly and concession segments must operate under bounded return envelopes: returns above the admissible range are requalified as E
  and constrained through tariff, contract, or levy mechanisms.
- Return envelopes must be linked to measurable performance (reliability, backlog reduction, resilience), with symmetric downside for
  underperformance.

### Prohibited return engineering (binding)
- Indexation clauses, “availability” structures, minimum-revenue guarantees, and opaque pass-throughs that produce excess returns
  without performance accountability are non-conforming.
- Fee-stacks embedded in billing, switching friction, or platform tolls that increase effective returns are treated as E surfaces.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Operators must publish (in aggregate) return and cost decomposition tables by segment: opex, maintenance, capex recovery, and margin,
  sufficient to audit for excess-return extraction.

<a id="b05ch08p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P10.S03 — Remedies: conversion to commons, concession revision, levy, contract rewrite

When caps exceedance or excess-return extraction is detected, remedies must reduce rentier leverage and restore contestability.

### Remedy ladder (binding)
- **Conversion to commons**: convert monopoly cores or captured concession segments to commons governance (public/commons operation or
  stricter regulated entity forms) with maintenance-first budgeting and open standards.
- **Concession revision**: renegotiate or rewrite concession terms to remove excess-return clauses, exclusivity, and opaque
  pass-throughs; bind to performance metrics and disclosure.
- **Levy**: apply escalating levies on excess returns and/or exceedance positions, with proceeds ring-fenced for ΔG (maintenance,
  resilience, capacity expansion) and baseline affordability measures.
- **Contract rewrite/unbundling**: separate control systems from billing, open interfaces, end exclusivity lock-ins, and restructure
  platform layers that function as tolls.

### Allocation rule (binding)
- Remedy selection prioritises lifeworld outcomes: prefer actions that reduce L and improve reliability/resilience. Where rentier
  dominance sustains toll pricing or underinvestment, structural conversion is mandatory.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated remedy statistics: number of interventions, remedy mix, assets affected, and ΔG reinvestment volumes (privacy-safe
  aggregation).

## B05.CH08.P10.S04 — Failure mode: financial engineering around infrastructure → detection via fee-stacks and UBC graphs

A predictable failure mode is financial engineering that hides control and extracts value through layered contracts and fees.

### Detection surfaces (binding)
- **Fee-stacks**: proliferation of surcharges, platform fees, “service” add-ons, and related-party maintenance markups that raise
  effective returns without improving service.
- **Vehicle complexity**: rapid creation of SPVs, cross-jurisdiction chains, nominee owners, and fund-of-fund structures that obscure
  beneficial control.
- **Contract layering**: multiple intermediate contracts (O&M, billing, IT, procurement) under common control that shift margins into
  opaque categories.

### UBC graph detection (binding)
- Maintain a beneficial ownership/control graph linking persons/entities to infrastructure interests, concession rights, related-party
  relationships, and material contracts.
- Flag anomalies: circular ownership, repeated related-party transfers, sudden extraction spikes, and concentration in key suppliers.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Failure to disclose beneficial ownership or material contract relationships triggers conservative attribution and immediate
  restrictions (transaction blocks, higher levies, forced disclosures).
- Repeat engineering patterns trigger escalated structural remedies: unbundling, concession termination, conversion to commons, and
  restrictions on acquiring further infrastructure interests (fail-closed against infrastructure rentierisation).
