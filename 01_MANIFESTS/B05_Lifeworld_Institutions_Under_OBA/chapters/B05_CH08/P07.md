# B05.CH08.P07 — Mobility systems: from car lock-in to contestable mobility

This paragraph specifies mobility system requirements under OBA to reduce car lock-in and make mobility contestable, affordable, and
reliable. Car dependence is treated as a structural clamp mechanism: it converts mobility into a quasi-fixed cost (fuel, insurance,
maintenance, depreciation, finance), increases vulnerability to shocks, and worsens L and R/B tails—especially where land-use
patterns and weak public transport remove exit options. OBA therefore treats public transport as a **public option**, active mobility
as low-L infrastructure, and logistics as a capacity domain where efficiency and commons-like last-mile options reduce costs and
bottlenecks. A “green” transition that merely swaps drivetrains while preserving car dependence is treated as a failure mode because
it leaves L structurally high.

---

<a id="b05ch08p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P07.S01 — Public transport as public option: frequency, reliability, affordability, coverage metrics

Public transport is a governance rail: it provides the primary exit option from car lock-in and therefore directly reduces L.

### Public option requirements (binding)
- **Coverage**: minimum coverage standards by region type (parameters), ensuring access to essential nodes (work, education, health,
  commons) without requiring car ownership.
- **Frequency**: minimum service frequency norms by corridor and time band (parameters); long headways that force car ownership are
  treated as baseline failure.
- **Reliability and punctuality**: publish on-time performance, cancellations, and recovery times; persistent underperformance triggers
  maintenance-first escalation and operational redesign.
- **Affordability**: fare structures must protect baseline mobility access; avoid regressive fixed charges and punitive penalty fees.

### Contract and governance rails (binding)
- Operator contracts must be performance-based (reliability, frequency, coverage) with bounded change-order regimes and audit packs.
- Ticketing systems must be interoperable and not app-gated; non-digital access channels are mandatory.

<a id="b05ch08p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P07.S02 — Active mobility: walking/cycling infrastructure as a low-L alternative

Active mobility is treated as essential low-L infrastructure: it reduces household transport costs, improves health, and increases
resilience.

### Infrastructure minima (binding)
- **Safe networks**: continuous, protected routes where risk is material; safe crossings; lighting; and maintenance (snow/ice/defect
  handling) treated as reliability infrastructure.
- **Accessibility**: infrastructure must accommodate children, elderly, and disabled users (design standards and parameters).
- **Integration**: connect active mobility with public transport nodes (park-and-ride equivalents for bikes, secure storage, last-mile
  connectivity).

### Measurability (binding)
- Publish coverage and safety indicators: network continuity, serious incident rates, maintenance response times, and modal share
  trends (aggregated).

<a id="b05ch08p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P07.S03 — Logistics: freight efficiency, hubs, last-mile commons options

Logistics is a core mobility layer that affects the cost and reliability of essentials supply. Inefficient logistics increases L via
higher prices and bottlenecks.

### Efficiency and resilience rails (binding)
- **Freight efficiency**: support mode shifts where feasible (rail/water), optimise hub capacity, and reduce empty runs and congestion.
- **Hub infrastructure**: treat ports/terminals and intermodal hubs as commons capacity with maintenance-first and resilience
  requirements.
- **Last-mile commons options**: where appropriate, enable shared last-mile infrastructure (consolidation points, micro-hubs, common
  loading zones) to reduce duplication and platform tolls.

### Anti-toll requirement (binding)
- Prevent exclusive platform control over last-mile logistics that converts access into a toll; require interoperability and open
  access rules for shared infrastructure.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated logistics indicators: hub throughput, congestion delays, incident rates, and key cost proxies that affect
  essentials pricing (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch08p07s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P07.S04 — Failure mode: “green” transition that keeps car dependence → L stays high

OBA treats drivetrain substitution without system redesign as a predictable failure mode: it may reduce emissions but preserves the
lock-in cost structure and vulnerability profile.

### Failure signals (binding)
- EV or alternative-fuel uptake rises while baseline accessibility and public transport coverage do not improve; car ownership remains
  necessary for participation.
- Household transport burden distributions do not fall; volatility exposure persists (energy prices, repair costs, financing).
- Charging infrastructure becomes a platform toll ecosystem (exclusive networks, opaque fees, app gating) replicating lock-in.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Decarbonisation programmes in mobility are non-conforming if they do not reduce car dependence and do not produce measurable L
  reduction and resilience improvements.
- Remedies must prioritise public option capacity, active mobility build-out, and interoperable charging infrastructure within rails.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Where “green” funding predominantly supports private vehicle markets and charging platforms without reducing L or improving baseline
  access, the channel is treated as leakage and must be redirected (fail-closed against green car-lock-in).
