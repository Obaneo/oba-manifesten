# B05.CH08.P12 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for energy and mobility governance under OBA and the hard stops that apply when the
system drifts into rentierisation, lock-in, and hidden extraction. Because energy and mobility are essentials infrastructure, failure
signals appear quickly as rising L and worsening R/B tails (affordability shocks), falling reliability (outages/delays), tariff
opacity (fee-stacks), and procurement drift (vendor capture). OBA therefore defines explicit non-conformance signals and fail-closed
responses. The objective is not to preserve incumbent revenue models or “investment confidence” at any price, but to preserve **order
compatibility**: tariffs must remain contestable and non-regressive, infrastructure must be maintenance-first and resilient, and ΔM
channels must not leak into asset/claim support or lock-in.

---

<a id="b05ch08p12s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P12.S01 — Green-washing: CO₂ claims without L reduction or without maintenance → non-conform

“Green-washing” is the pattern where decarbonisation is claimed while lifeworld burdens rise or infrastructure degrades.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Programmes that report CO₂ reductions while **energy burden tails** rise, baseline access gaps widen, or arrears/disconnections
  increase.
- Capex-heavy programmes launched without funded lifecycle maintenance plans, leading to growing backlogs and reliability incidents.
- Reliance on certificates/offsets or accounting constructs that obscure real delivery and maintenance discipline.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat the programme as non-conforming until it demonstrates measurable ΔG delivery and L/RB improvements; suspend expansion and
  redirect funding toward maintenance-first and baseline-protecting channels (fail-closed against “green” rentierisation).

<a id="b05ch08p12s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P12.S02 — Vendor capture: proprietary control systems → lock-in and procurement breach

Vendor capture is a primary infrastructure failure mode: proprietary control stacks and exclusive contracts create long-term lock-in,
raise lifecycle costs, and reduce auditability.

### Capture indicators (binding)
- Procurement awards that require proprietary standards, closed interfaces, or exclusive maintenance rights.
- Critical operational data held in closed formats or withheld from auditors under “commercial confidentiality”.
- Repeated emergency justifications for sole-source renewals (“no alternative vendor”), indicating structural dependence.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat proprietary lock-in as a procurement breach: suspend the procurement stream, require open-standards redesign, and trigger
  audit-of-the-auditor on procurement decisions.
- Where lock-in is already embedded, require interface opening, escrow/continuity arrangements, and staged migration to interoperable
  systems; repeat breaches trigger governance reset (fail-closed against vendor dominance).

<a id="b05ch08p12s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P12.S03 — Tariff clamp: regressive fixed charges or opaque surcharges → R/B tails worsen

Tariff clamp occurs when tariff structures shift burdens onto low-income households, hide extraction in complexity, or expose baseline
needs to volatility.

### Clamp indicators (binding)
- Rising fixed-charge share of bills and worsening energy burden tails (p90/p95), especially for low-consumption households.
- Proliferation of opaque surcharges, “administration” fees, and plan variants that prevent comparison and contestation.
- Peak pricing or volatility exposure applied without baseline protection, correlating with arrears/disconnections.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Trigger tariff redesign: simplify into standard templates, minimise fixed charges, protect baseline consumption, and requalify
  non-auditable surcharges as E (suspend until corrected).
- Repeat clamp signals trigger tighter regulation of monopoly returns and potential conversion of tariff setting to stricter commons
  governance (fail-closed against tariff toll ecosystems).

<a id="b05ch08p12s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P12.S04 — Fail-closed: if L rises, reliability falls, or leakage signals (asset support) appear, redesign is mandatory

OBA treats worsening lifeworld outcomes as evidence of drift toward incompatibility. In energy/mobility, the primary signals are
rising L, declining reliability, and ΔM leakage into claim/asset support.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- **L rises**: sustained increase in energy/mobility burden distributions and tails; baseline access gaps widen; arrears/disconnections
  rise.
- **Reliability falls**: rising SAIDI/SAIFI (or equivalents), longer outage/delay durations, repeated major incidents, and growing
  maintenance backlogs.
- **Leakage signals**: emergency or investment programmes that stabilise asset values or concession cashflows without ΔG/reliability
  gains; hidden guarantees; opaque pass-throughs and minimum-revenue structures.
- **Opacity**: inability to publish core metrics safely and reproducibly; non-reproducible tariff calculations.

### Mandatory response (binding)
- Treat current policy mix as non-conforming; initiate redesign with explicit mechanism diagnosis (tariff incidence, monopoly returns,
  maintenance backlog, vendor dependence, lock-in).
- Suspend leakage-prone channels and redirect resources to ΔG (maintenance, resilience, grids/storage, public transport) and tariff
  redesign that protects baseline access.
- Tighten procurement rails and enforce open standards; apply structural remedies (unbundling, concession revision, conversion to
  commons) where rentier dominance is systemic.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Fail-closed events, redesign decisions, and structural remedies must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe
  aggregation) to prevent quiet drift and selective enforcement.
