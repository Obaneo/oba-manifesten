# B05.CH08.P05 — Grids and infrastructure as commons capacity (ΔG)

This paragraph specifies energy grids and core mobility infrastructure (rail/critical corridors) as **commons capacity** under OBA
(ΔG). These cores exhibit natural monopoly characteristics and therefore cannot be governed as ordinary profit surfaces without
creating toll extraction (E), underinvestment, and vendor capture. OBA treats grid/rail cores as infrastructure whose primary outputs
are reliability, resilience, and affordable access (L down). Governance is therefore maintenance-first, incident-driven, and
measurable: backlogs and reliability metrics drive escalation. Capacity expansion (reinforcement, interconnectors, charging networks,
rail upgrades) is admissible only when it is auditable, interoperable, and does not embed lock-in. Procurement is treated as a major
capture surface and is bound to open standards, lifecycle accountability, and performance-based contracting.

---

<a id="b05ch08p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P05.S01 — Maintenance-first: replacement, reliability, cyber/physical resilience, backlog thresholds

Maintenance and resilience are first claim on infrastructure budgets. Deferred maintenance is treated as hidden L via outages, delays,
and shock sensitivity.

### Maintenance-first requirements (binding)
- **Replacement discipline**: maintain asset registers with condition grades and replacement schedules; prioritise end-of-life
  components whose failure creates systemic outages.
- **Reliability discipline**: define reliability targets and monitor incidents; underperformance triggers capacity surge and
  remediation plans.
- **Cyber/physical resilience**: implement baseline security controls, redundancy, and rapid recovery protocols; resilience investment
  is treated as mandatory commons maintenance, not as discretionary “IT spend”.

### Backlog thresholds (binding)
- Maintain an auditable maintenance backlog register (type, severity, age, systemic risk).
- Jurisdictions must set backlog thresholds (parameters) that trigger escalation: workforce surge, procurement acceleration, and
  governance intervention.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated maintenance performance: backlog size/age, replacement execution rates, incident volumes, outage/delay durations,
  and resilience test results (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch08p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P05.S02 — Capacity expansion: grid reinforcement, interconnectors, charging infrastructure, rail upgrades

Capacity expansion is admissible where it increases resilience, reduces bottlenecks, and supports L reduction, without creating new
toll points or lock-in.

### Admissible expansion (binding)
- **Grid reinforcement**: distribution upgrades, congestion relief, transformer and substation expansion, and local reliability
  improvements.
- **Interconnectors and redundancy**: cross-region interconnectors and redundancy that reduce single-point failure risk and volatility
  exposure.
- **Charging infrastructure**: interoperable charging networks as mobility infrastructure, designed to avoid exclusive platforms and
  lock-in.
- **Rail upgrades**: signalling, rolling stock renewal, capacity and punctuality improvements, and accessibility upgrades.

### Expansion discipline (binding)
- Expansion projects must include a published investment case: bottleneck evidence, expected reliability/resilience gains, L incidence,
  lifecycle maintenance plan, and interoperability requirements.
- Expansion must not be used as a substitute for maintenance; projects that grow capacity while backlogs deteriorate are non-conforming
  unless justified by emergency risk.

<a id="b05ch08p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P05.S03 — Governance: clear roles, transparent decision logs, audit of the auditor

Because infrastructure operators wield block power, governance must be designed as anti-capture infrastructure.

### Governance minima (binding)
- **Clear roles**: explicit separation of policy-setting, regulation, operation, and procurement oversight; avoid ambiguous mandates
  that enable selective enforcement.
- **Decision logs**: publish major decisions (tariff assumptions, investment prioritisation, incident classification, procurement
  awards) with rationale, criteria references, and audit pack pointers (privacy-safe).
- **Uniform rule application**: exception handling must be logged and sunset-bounded; selective service degradation is prohibited.

### Audit-of-the-auditor (binding)
- Independent audits of operator models, asset-condition reporting, procurement decisions, and incident handling; rotation and peer
  review; public summary findings.
- Auditors must themselves be contestable: no permanent single-firm monopoly on assurance in critical infrastructure.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven concealment of incidents, selective enforcement, or persistent non-disclosure triggers governance reset: leadership rotation,
  tighter disclosure, and (where needed) conversion to stricter commons governance (fail-closed against operator capture).

<a id="b05ch08p05s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P05.S04 — Procurement rails: open standards, vendor lock-in prevention, performance-based contracting

Procurement is a primary channel for vendor capture. OBA therefore binds procurement to openness, interoperability, and lifecycle
accountability.

### Open standards (binding)
- Require open interfaces and documented protocols for control systems, metering, charging, and mobility ticketing where relevant.
- Data formats must be open and auditable; proprietary black-box control stacks without audit access are non-conforming.

### Vendor lock-in prevention (binding)
- Prohibit exclusive maintenance rights, closed spare-part ecosystems, and licensing terms that prevent switching or independent
  security review.
- Require escrow or continuity arrangements for critical software and operational data to prevent hostage dynamics.

### Performance-based contracting (binding)
- Contracts must specify measurable deliverables: reliability improvements, backlog reduction, incident response times, uptime
  guarantees, and security requirements.
- Change orders are bounded and transparent; repeated change-order inflation triggers review and re-tendering.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish procurement logs (aggregate): major awards, supplier concentration, change-order volumes, performance outcomes, and vendor
  lock-in risk assessments (privacy-safe aggregation).
