# B05.CH08.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of energy & mobility under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of energy and mobility under OBA. Energy and mobility are treated
as essentials infrastructure: without reliable and affordable electricity, heat, fuels, and mobility access, households face direct
L escalation and R/B tail failure. These domains also contain structural risk surfaces—natural monopolies, high capital intensity,
vendor dependence, and concession cashflows—that can easily drift into extraction (E), toll ecosystems, and asset/claim support via
misdirected ΔM channels. OBA therefore treats energy/mobility governance as constitutive: it must deliver reliability, affordability,
and resilience while expanding real commons capacity (ΔG/maintenance), and it must prohibit channels that stabilise asset values,
embed rentier cashflows, or create lock-in.

---

<a id="b05ch08p01s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P01.S01 — Purpose: essentials reliability and affordability (L down) with high resilience, without asset/claim support

Under OBA, the purpose of energy and mobility policy is to keep essentials reliable and affordable while preventing rentierisation and
claim support.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Essentials reliability**: ensure continuous access to electricity/heat and functional mobility networks, with explicit resilience
  against shocks (weather, geopolitics, supply chains, cyber/physical incidents).
- **Affordability (L down)**: reduce energy and mobility burden distributions and tails; prevent tariff structures and infrastructure
  finance from worsening R/B.
- **Resilience-first**: prioritise maintenance, redundancy, and rapid recovery over headline expansion that cannot be sustained.
- **No asset/claim support**: treat investment channels and emergency regimes as governance rails, not as mechanisms to stabilise
  private valuations or concession cashflows.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- Policies whose functional objective is to preserve incumbent revenue models, monopoly margins, or asset prices—rather than deliver
  L reduction and resilience—are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch08p01s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P01.S02 — Scope: electricity/heat/fuels, grids, storage, public transport, road/rail, logistics, tariffs and investment regimes

CH08 covers the full energy/mobility stack: physical infrastructure, tariffs, operators, investment channels, and governance.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Energy supply and delivery**: electricity generation and supply, gas/heat networks where applicable, district/local heat, fuels
  (including transition fuels), and retail supply layers.
- **Grids and system infrastructure**: transmission/distribution grids, substations, interconnectors, metering, control systems, and
  operational IT.
- **Storage and flexibility**: batteries, thermal storage, demand response, and grid-balancing mechanisms (as infrastructure services).
- **Mobility networks**: public transport (bus/tram/metro), rail, roads where relevant to access, charging infrastructure, and
  intermodal hubs.
- **Logistics**: freight corridors, ports/terminals, last-mile systems, and essential supply chains.
- **Tariffs and pricing**: tariff structures, fixed/variable charges, peak pricing, surcharges, fee decompositions, and consumer
  contracts.
- **Investment regimes**: capex/opex planning, procurement, concession models, public finance and ΔM channel use, and resilience
  programmes.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH08 does not prescribe specific technology choices except where they affect lock-in, interoperability, repairability, auditability,
  and maintenance burden.

<a id="b05ch08p01s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P01.S03 — Binding: ΔM channel rails (allowed/forbidden), commons standard (ΔG/maintenance), P/E/C, caps, audit and procurement rails

Energy/mobility governance under OBA is bound to canonical rails. Claims that ignore these rails are non-conforming.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **ΔM channel rails**: investment and emergency finance must pass allowed/forbidden channel tests; ΔM is admissible into real
  infrastructure capacity (ΔG) and maintenance/resilience, not into asset/claim support or concession cashflow stabilisation.
- **Commons standard (ΔG/maintenance)**: natural-monopoly cores (grid/rail) are treated as commons capacity with maintenance-first
  budgeting, backlog thresholds, incident logging, and governance contestability.
- **P/E/C classification**: distinguish productive infrastructure delivery and service (P) from toll extraction (E) and commons flows
  (C); tariff and fee components must be decomposable and auditable.
- **Caps (W/I)**: infrastructure ownership and concession rights are within cap logic via look-through; excess returns and financial
  engineering around monopoly assets are constrained.
- **Audit standards**: decisions must be reproducible: tariff build-ups, investment cases, procurement selections, and incident
  handling require audit packs.
- **Procurement rails**: open standards, interoperability, anti-vendor-lock-in rules, and performance-based contracting with clear
  deliverables and bounded change-order regimes.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any major tariff change, concession award, or investment programme must provide an audit pack linking: mechanism → expected incidence
  → L/ΔG/reliability outcomes → lock-in risk → rollback/sunset discipline.

<a id="b05ch08p01s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: infrastructure finance that amplifies asset inflation, rentier cashflows, or lock-in is non-conform

Because energy/mobility is capital-intensive and politically sensitive, OBA applies fail-closed discipline to prevent drift into
rentierisation and lock-in.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- **Asset/claim support**: programmes whose functional effect is to stabilise valuations (infrastructure funds, utilities equity) or to
  guarantee private cashflows without ΔG/reliability gains.
- **Rentier cashflow engineering**: concession structures that produce excess returns from monopoly segments (opaque fees, guaranteed
  margins, hidden indexation) without performance accountability.
- **Vendor lock-in**: proprietary control systems, closed data interfaces, exclusive maintenance rights, and procurement practices that
  prevent contestability and raise lifecycle costs.
- **Tariff opacity**: complex surcharges, bundled charges, or non-reproducible tariff calculations that prevent contestation.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Suspend or redesign the programme: tighten channel conditions, force open standards, rewrite concessions, or convert monopoly cores
  to commons governance where necessary.
- Where the channel is structurally prone to leakage (e.g., broad subsidies under constrained capacity), apply hard prohibition and
  redirect resources to ΔG (grids, storage, maintenance, public transport) and tariff redesign.
