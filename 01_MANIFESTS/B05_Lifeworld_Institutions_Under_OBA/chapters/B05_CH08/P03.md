# B05.CH08.P03 — Baseline access: energy and mobility floor

This paragraph specifies baseline access requirements for energy and mobility under OBA: an essentials floor that prevents households
from falling into R/B tail failure due to disconnection, unaffordable heating, or mobility exclusion. Baseline access is not treated
as charity; it is a constitutive safety rail in an essentials domain. OBA therefore requires (i) a defined energy baseline (minimum
electricity/heat necessary for health and participation), (ii) a defined mobility baseline (minimum accessibility to essential nodes),
(iii) non-discrimination and no exclusion via debt status or digital barriers, and (iv) a fast dispute flow that prevents billing and
metering errors from turning into clamp. Thresholds are parameterised by jurisdiction, but the rails—measurability, contestability,
and fail-closed discipline—are binding.

---

<a id="b05ch08p03s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P03.S01 — Energy baseline: minimum kWh/heat for health and participation (parameters)

Energy baseline access protects health, safety, and participation, especially during seasonal extremes.

### Baseline definition (parameters; binding requirement to set and publish)
- Jurisdictions must set a minimum baseline for **electricity** and **heat** sufficient for health and participation (kWh/period,
  heating degree-day adjusted where relevant) and publish it as a parameter.
- The baseline must account for household size and basic medical/assistive needs (parameter categories), using privacy-minimal
  verification.

### Delivery rule (binding)
- Baseline energy supply may not be cut for inability to pay; disconnection may apply only to consumption above the baseline and only
  with due process and dispute rights.
- Where physical disconnection is technically unavoidable, the provider must supply a safe fallback (e.g., limited-power mode) until
  dispute and restructuring are resolved.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish baseline coverage indicators: share of households receiving baseline protection, arrears/disconnection rates, and seasonal
  stress metrics (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch08p03s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P03.S02 — Mobility baseline: minimum accessibility (public transport/alternatives) per region (parameters)

Mobility baseline access ensures that living location does not become a coercive constraint on work, education, health care, and
participation.

### Baseline definition (parameters; binding requirement to set and publish)
- Jurisdictions must define a minimum accessibility standard per region type (urban/suburban/rural), such as maximum travel time to
  essential nodes (work/education/health/commons), minimum service frequency, and minimum coverage radius.
- Where fixed-route public transport is infeasible, the baseline may be met via alternative modes (demand-responsive transit,
  shared mobility with rails, community transport), but the standard must remain measurable and auditable.

### Non-lock-in objective (binding)
- Baseline mobility must reduce forced car dependence where possible; if the baseline cannot be met without car ownership, this is
  treated as a structural L risk and triggers mobility system redesign and investment prioritisation.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish accessibility indicators: coverage, frequency, punctuality, travel-time distributions (median/p90/p95), and baseline-gap
  maps at privacy-safe aggregation levels.

<a id="b05ch08p03s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P03.S03 — Non-discrimination: access without exclusion via debt status or digital barriers

Baseline access must be delivered without exclusion mechanisms that convert essentials into coercion.

### Non-discrimination minima (binding)
- Prohibit denial of baseline energy or mobility access due to debt status beyond due-process adjudication of fraud.
- Prohibit exclusion via opaque blacklists, automated denial systems without appeal, or selective enforcement against vulnerable
  cohorts.

### Digital barrier prohibition (binding)
- Essential access may not depend on proprietary apps, smartphone ownership, or invasive identity proofing.
- Providers must offer non-digital access channels (paper/phone/in-person) and ensure accessibility for disabled and elderly users.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Providers must publish aggregate denial/exclusion statistics with reason codes and appeal outcomes (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch08p03s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P03.S04 — Dispute flow: rapid correction for disconnections, faulty meters, tariff abuse

Dispute handling must be fast enough to prevent errors from becoming clamp or disconnection.

### Fast dispute procedures (binding)
- Strict time bounds for: acknowledgement, document provision, provisional relief, and final decision for billing, metering, and tariff
  disputes.
- During disputes, **baseline access is preserved**: no disconnection of baseline energy; no exclusion from baseline mobility options.

### Reproducible calculations (binding)
- Bills must be reproducible from disclosed tariff parameters, meter readings, and adjustment rules.
- Metering systems must provide audit-safe logs; “black box” estimation without disclosure is non-conforming.

### Remedies (binding)
- Immediate correction of errors, refunds/credits with statutory uplift where applicable, and tariff/contract rewrite where abuse is
  identified.
- Repeat offender patterns trigger audit escalation and, where necessary, licence and procurement consequences.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated dispute statistics: volumes, root causes (metering, tariffs, disconnections), resolution times, outcomes, and
  repeat-offender indicators (privacy-safe aggregation).
