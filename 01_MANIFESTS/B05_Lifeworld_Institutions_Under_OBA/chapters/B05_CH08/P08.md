# B05.CH08.P08 — Shock resilience and emergency regimes (energy/mobility)

This paragraph specifies shock resilience and emergency regimes for energy and mobility under OBA. Because these domains are
essentials infrastructure, shocks (price breaks, shortages, blackouts, infrastructure failures) translate rapidly into R/B tail
collapse. Emergency response is therefore treated as a governance rail: it must preserve baseline access, stabilise essentials, and
restore capacity—without becoming a backdoor for claim/asset support, rentier cashflow guarantees, or vendor capture. OBA requires
explicit triggers, a bounded toolset, strict sunset and unwind discipline, and post-mortem audits with public incident logs. Where
emergency measures drift into permanent subsidy or opaque procurement, fail-closed hard stops apply.

---

<a id="b05ch08p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH08.P08.S01 — Triggers: price breaks, supply shortages, blackouts, infrastructure incidents

Emergency regimes must be activated by explicit, measurable triggers rather than political discretion alone.

### Trigger classes (binding)
- **Price breaks**: rapid increases in wholesale/retail prices that materially raise burden tails (energy or mobility cost spikes) or
  threaten arrears/disconnections.
- **Supply shortages**: fuel shortages, generation shortfalls, grid congestion events, or critical logistics constraints.
- **Blackouts and reliability incidents**: outage events exceeding reliability thresholds (duration, affected customers), cascading
  failures, or repeated near-miss events indicating systemic fragility.
- **Infrastructure incidents**: cyber attacks, physical sabotage, major asset failures (transformers, signalling systems), or extreme
  weather damage.

### Trigger discipline (binding)
- Triggers must be parameterised (thresholds set and published) and tied to predefined response bundles.
- Trigger activation and deactivation decisions must be logged with rationale and data (audit pack pointers).

---

<a id="b05ch08p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH08.P08.S02 — Instruments: temporary price stabilisation, rationing, essentials prioritisation, emergency capacity

OBA permits a bounded emergency toolset focused on preserving baseline access and restoring capacity, not on preserving private
valuations.

### Permitted instruments (binding)
- **Temporary price stabilisation**: time-bounded measures to protect baseline consumption and prevent arrears/disconnections, using
  transparent formulas and targeted incidence controls.
- **Rationing and demand management**: rule-based rationing under shortage, prioritising baseline access and essential services;
  curtailment rules must be transparent and contestable.
- **Essentials prioritisation**: priority allocation for hospitals, care services, food supply chains, and baseline household needs;
  mobility prioritisation for essential workers and critical logistics.
- **Emergency capacity**: rapid procurement and deployment of repair crews, spare parts, temporary generation/heat solutions, backup
  transport capacity, and redundancy measures.

### Prohibited drift (binding)
- Emergency instruments may not be used to maintain profit margins or to backstop speculative positions; they must be tied to baseline
  protection and capacity restoration.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Each instrument must specify: eligible cohorts, expected incidence on L/RB tails, operational steps, and measurable exit criteria.

---

<a id="b05ch08p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH08.P08.S03 — Sunsets and unwind: temporariness required; post-mortem audit

Emergency regimes are allowed only as temporary departures from normal rails. Permanence is treated as governance failure.

### Sunset discipline (binding)
- Every emergency measure must include a fixed sunset date (or short renewal interval) and explicit criteria for extension.
- Extensions require updated evidence packs and must be logged; indefinite extensions are non-conforming.

### Unwind rules (binding)
- Define a staged unwind plan: return tariffs and market rules to standard rails, restore normal procurement, and reconcile temporary
  accounts.
- Where emergency measures created temporary exceptions (e.g., procurement speed-ups), those exceptions must be rolled back or
  converted into audited standard pathways.

### Post-mortem audit (binding)
- Conduct a post-mortem audit within a defined window: root causes, performance of instruments, distributional incidence, vendor and
  procurement integrity, and lessons for resilience investment.
- Publish a public summary with incident logs and corrective actions (privacy-safe).

<a id="b05ch08p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH08.P08.S04 — Hard stop: emergency measures may not introduce claim/asset support or vendor capture

Emergency regimes are a classic entry point for capture. OBA therefore defines explicit hard stops.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- **Claim/asset support**: emergency measures that primarily stabilise asset values, concession cashflows, or incumbent profit margins
  rather than protecting baseline access and restoring capacity.
- **Opaque emergency procurement**: repeated sole-source awards without disclosure, proprietary control systems introduced without open
  standards, or lifecycle costs hidden behind “urgent” justification.
- **Vendor capture**: exclusivity clauses, closed data interfaces, mandatory proprietary apps, or long-term lock-in commitments signed
  under emergency conditions.
- **Selective enforcement**: baseline protection applied inconsistently across cohorts or regions without transparent criteria.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Suspend the measure and revert to baseline protection tools with transparent incidence; unwind capture-inducing procurement and
  re-tender under open-standards rules.
- Trigger mandatory governance reset and external audit where capture signals are confirmed, including review of all emergency awards
  and contracts (fail-closed against emergency capture).
