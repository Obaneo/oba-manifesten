# B05.CH13.P07 — Agency constraints: norm shifts without reduction to KPI steering

This paragraph specifies agency constraints for cultural and norm instruments under OBA. OBA rejects the idea that agency, dignity, and
meaning can be reduced to a single KPI or “wellbeing score”. Attempts to steer culture through monitoring and scoring predictably
create coercion, gaming, and resistance—and they expand surveillance rent and block power. OBA therefore allows experience inputs, but
only as structured qualitative signals with procedural validation: sampling discipline, contradiction handling, transparent methods,
and contestability. Norm policy must operate by designing conditions (access, commons availability, lock-in reduction, transparency),
not by enforcing “desired feelings” or compliance with a moral doctrine. Policies that discipline status through behavioural scoring are
explicitly prohibited and non-conforming.

---

<a id="b05ch13p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P07.S01 — Principle: agency and dignity cannot be captured in a single yardstick

Agency is multi-dimensional and context-dependent; single-index governance creates perverse incentives.

### Principle claims (binding)
- Agency and dignity are not reducible to one metric without losing essential information and enabling manipulation.
- Cultural legitimacy cannot be proven by survey scores alone; it must be inferred from conditions and lifeworld outcomes (L down,
  friction down, commons continuity).

### Anti-Goodhart implication (binding)
- If one indicator becomes the target (happiness score, “trust index”), institutions will optimise the indicator rather than the
  underlying reality, often by manipulating sampling, messaging, or coercion.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Cultural governance must use triangulation and procedural validity rather than single-score optimisation.

---

<a id="b05ch13p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P07.S02 — Experience input: structured qualitative signals with procedural validation (contradiction, sampling, transparency)

Experience signals are admissible when they are treated as evidence with method discipline, not as sentiment instruments.

### Structured qualitative inputs (binding)
- Collect experience input as:
  - structured narratives (coded categories),
  - focus-group style evidence sessions with recorded procedures,
  - complaint and dispute pattern analysis (from contestability systems),
  - and independent ethnographic observation where justified.

### Procedural validation (binding)
- **Sampling**: define sampling frames; avoid “only the loudest” bias; publish inclusion criteria.
- **Contradiction handling**: explicitly record contradictory signals; do not collapse them into a single average; analyse subgroup
  differences.
- **Transparency**: publish method_id, question frames, coding schemes, and revision policy.
- **Contestability**: allow objections to method and interpretation; maintain an exceptions register for disputed interpretations.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Experience inputs must be linked to design decisions: what changed, why, and what measurable effects are expected (audit pack
  linkage).

<a id="b05ch13p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P07.S03 — Prohibited: policy that enforces status discipline via monitoring or behavioural scoring

OBA treats surveillance-based norm enforcement as incompatible with agency and plurality.

### Explicit prohibitions (binding)
- Behavioural scoring systems that rank citizens by “civic virtue”, “sustainability behaviour”, “social trustworthiness”, or similar.
- Monitoring-based access conditioning: requiring behavioural compliance to access essentials or commons services.
- Platform-style reputation systems used by public institutions to allocate rights or opportunities without due process and appeal.
- Continuous tracking for “nudging” without explicit consent and opt-out symmetry.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Any deployment of behavioural scoring or monitoring-based discipline triggers immediate non-conformance and rollback (fail-closed
  against surveillance culture).

---

<a id="b05ch13p07s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P07.S04 — Consequence: design conditions (access, lock-in reduction), not “desired feelings”

Norm shifts should be achieved indirectly by changing incentives and conditions, not by attempting to engineer inner states.

### Condition design (binding)
- Expand commons access and reduce reliance on private gatekeepers.
- Reduce lock-in in education, platforms, and labour pathways (credential inflation, audience portability, dispute rails).
- Enforce transparency and anti-manipulation in consumer and media interfaces.
- Make maintenance and contribution visible through contestable recognition systems (P10) rather than moral pressure.

### Evaluation rule (binding)
- Evaluate norm instruments by their effect on conditions and lifeworld outcomes (L, friction, R/B tails), not by whether citizens
  report “the right values”.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If a norm instrument increases monitoring, reduces plurality, or grows block power, it must be rolled back and replaced with condition
  design that preserves agency and contestability (fail-closed against coercive cultural engineering).
