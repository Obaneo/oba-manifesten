# B05.CH13.P12 — Measurement and publication requirements (cultural layer as lifeworld test)

This paragraph specifies measurement and publication requirements for the cultural layer under OBA. Cultural drift is easy to narrate
and hard to verify: elites can form around credentials and networks; access can degrade through friction and stigma; platform attention
can become a new toll economy; and “legitimacy storytelling” can replace delivery. OBA therefore requires a **public measurement layer**
that makes lock-in, cultural access, and platform status dynamics contestable as time series while enforcing privacy minimalism. The
objective is not to score individuals but to detect system-level gatekeeping, manipulation, and exclusion. If required indicators cannot
be published safely and reproducibly, governance must restrict scope to auditable modules and trigger redesign (fail-closed against
invisible cultural capture).

---

<a id="b05ch13p12s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P12.S01 — Lock-in indicators: credential barriers, gatekeeping complaints, friction-driven drop-off (aggregated)

Measurement must reveal whether status competition is recreating lock-in through access gates.

### Lock-in indicators (binding)
- **Credential barriers**: growth of “required credentials” for roles and programmes; time-to-entry and cost-to-entry proxies for
  professions and training pathways.
- **Gatekeeping complaints**: complaint and appeal volumes related to admissions, licensing, and access denials; reversal rates and
  resolution times.
- **Friction-driven drop-off**: abandonment rates in application/training/licensing pipelines; stage-by-stage drop-off distributions
  (proxy for administrative L and exclusion).
- **Network dependence proxies**: where measurable, share of opportunities filled via informal referrals versus open processes (aggregate
  survey or audit-derived measures).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish as time series by domain (education, licensing, professional bodies) and region category, with stable definitions.

<a id="b05ch13p12s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P12.S02 — Access measures: participation in cultural commons, regional coverage, affordability

Cultural commons must be reachable, not merely “available”.

### Access indicators (binding)
- **Participation**: aggregate participation counts and utilisation rates for libraries, venues, studios, sport facilities, public media
  services, and knowledge repositories.
- **Regional coverage**: coverage proxies (travel-time categories, opening hours availability) for cultural commons by region category.
- **Affordability**: baseline affordability indicators (free/low-cost tier availability, median/p90 costs for typical participation),
  including barriers for low-income cohorts.
- **Continuity**: opening hour reliability, programme continuity, staffing stability, and maintenance backlog indicators (link to P05).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated, privacy-safe; avoid collecting personal participation histories.

<a id="b05ch13p12s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P12.S03 — Platform status: take-rates, ranking appeals, deplatform incidents, portability success rates

Because status may migrate into attention platforms, platform governance must be measured as a cultural-status rail.

### Platform status indicators (binding)
- **Take-rates**: effective take-rate distributions for creator platforms and marketplaces; fee-stack depth and changes over time.
- **Ranking appeals**: appeal volumes, reversal rates, and resolution times for ranking/moderation actions; share of “silent”
  suppressions detected via audits where feasible.
- **Deplatform incidents**: lockouts, demonetisation events, and account restrictions affecting livelihoods (aggregated).
- **Portability success**: success rates and completion times for audience/content portability and migration tooling (link CH10).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated transparency-report minima and require standard definitions so platforms cannot relabel enforcement categories to
  evade visibility.

<a id="b05ch13p12s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P12.S04 — Privacy minimalism: no individual reputation scores; only aggregated system indicators

OBA explicitly forbids turning cultural measurement into surveillance-based norm discipline.

### Privacy rules (binding)
- **No individual reputation scores** published or maintained by public institutions as governance tools for access or allocation.
- **No person-tracking** of cultural participation as a baseline measurement strategy; use aggregates, sampling, and privacy-safe
  audits.
- **Tiered access**: micro-level data (if any) is restricted to regulated audit contexts with access logs and purpose limitation.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Any attempt to introduce behavioural scoring or reputation-based eligibility for essentials or commons access is non-conforming and
  triggers immediate rollback (fail-closed against surveillance culture).
