# B05.CH13.P08 — Media, platforms, and the information ecosystem (cognitive status)

This paragraph specifies media and the information ecosystem under OBA as a cultural-status arena. Under caps, status competition can
shift toward **attention**: visibility, reach, verification badges, and algorithmic prominence become scarce resources. This creates
new extraction (E) opportunities through platform tolls, ad-tech intermediation, reputational manipulation, and outrage
amplification. It also creates block power: ranking and moderation can exclude voices, collapse livelihoods, and shape public
perception—especially if enforcement is opaque or politically deployable. OBA therefore requires contestable and auditable
ranking/moderation, anti-manipulation constraints (limits on targeting, dark patterns, outrage optimisation), and fail-closed remedies
against informal censorship via reputation systems. Where contestability cannot be restored, interoperability mandates and public
options become mandatory (link to digital lifeworld CH10).

---

<a id="b05ch13p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P08.S01 — Status shifts toward “attention”: risk of new extraction via platforms and reputation systems

Attention becomes a positional good: it is scarce, amplifies opportunity, and is mediated by gatekeepers.

### Extraction mechanisms (binding)
- **Platform tolls on attention**: pay-to-reach boosting, take-rates on creator revenue, and fee-stacks in monetisation tooling.
- **Reputation systems**: verification badges, follower graphs, rating systems, and eligibility thresholds that function as gatekeeping
  assets.
- **Ad-tech intermediation**: opaque auctions and intermediaries capturing value between advertisers and publishers/creators.
- **Manipulative amplification**: engagement optimisation that rewards outrage and polarisation, turning attention into a coercive
  substrate.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- When attention is monetised through opaque tolls or reputational gatekeeping, it is treated as E and triggers platform discipline
  and measurement requirements.

<a id="b05ch13p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P08.S02 — Ranking/moderation: block power must be contestable, auditable, and not politically deployable

Ranking and moderation are governance functions; under OBA they are treated as block-power surfaces.

### Contestability minima (binding)
- Provide reason codes and rule references for removals, demonetisation, visibility suppression, and account restrictions.
- Provide time-bounded appeal procedures; emergency actions must have rapid post-action review.

### Auditability requirements (binding)
- Maintain versioned policy/rule sets; log enforcement actions (privacy-minimal) sufficient to reproduce decisions under authorised
  audit.
- Publish aggregated transparency statistics: action volumes, categories, appeal rates, reversal rates, resolution times.

### Not politically deployable (binding)
- Prohibit opaque discretionary enforcement that can be selectively applied to political opponents or disfavoured groups without audit.
- Where platforms serve essential functions (public communication, payments, identity), due-process constraints must be stronger and
  public option triggers apply (link CH10).

<a id="b05ch13p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P08.S03 — Anti-manipulation: limits on targeting, dark patterns, outrage amplification (link to digital lifeworld)

OBA treats manipulation as a cognitive toll that increases L (time theft, misinformed decisions, coercive consent).

### Anti-manipulation constraints (binding)
- **Limits on targeting**: restrict microtargeting and cross-site tracking dependence; require purpose limitation and minimisation (link
  CH10.P07).
- **Dark pattern prohibition**: ban coerced consent flows, cancellation traps, misleading “recommendations”, and deceptive ads.
- **Outrage amplification limits**: require transparency about ranking objectives; prohibit optimisation for engagement at the expense of
  user intent where it predictably increases harm and polarisation.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Platforms and media actors must publish rule summaries and transparency-report minima; refusal is treated as non-conforming for
  essential communication functions.

<a id="b05ch13p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P08.S04 — Failure mode: informal censorship via reputation/algorithms → hard stop and public option/interop remedies

Informal censorship is suppression without accountable legal process: reputation scoring, shadow bans, and algorithmic throttling.

### Failure signals (binding)
- Visibility suppression without disclosure; high rates of unexplained demonetisation; inconsistent enforcement across similar cases.
- Reputation systems that become eligibility gates without appeal; deplatforming that functions as livelihood exclusion.
- “Safety” narratives used to justify opaque suppression without necessity proofs and sunset discipline.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat opaque suppression as non-conforming: require disclosure, contestability, and authorised audits of rule application.
- If platforms refuse auditability or portability, trigger structural remedies:
  - interoperability mandates and federation where feasible,
  - separation/unbundling of monetisation and ranking control,
  - and deployment of public options for essential communication/distribution functions (fail-closed against informal censorship via
    private block power).
