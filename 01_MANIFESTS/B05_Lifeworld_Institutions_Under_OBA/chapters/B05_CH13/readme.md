# B05.CH13 — Culture, status, and norm shifts: what “success” becomes under caps and commons

This chapter specifies the **cultural layer** under OBA: how status competition, prestige, and “success” norms reconfigure when caps
(W/I) constrain monetary winner-take-all and commons expansion changes what is scarce. OBA does **not** attempt to impose a single
culture, moral doctrine, or taste regime. It treats culture as an incentives-and-institutions interface: status drive remains, and will
seek new arenas. If unmanaged, it can migrate into non-monetary capture surfaces—credentials, titles, network access, platform ranking
power—and recreate lock-in (L), extraction (E), and block power in new forms. OBA therefore aims to re-channel status toward competence,
contribution, and commons maintenance (P/ΔG), while explicitly prohibiting disciplinary or homogenising “norm policy” that relies on
monitoring, behavioural scoring, or coercive re-education.

CH13 is designed to be **anti-washing** and **fail-closed**. “Culture talk” can become a substitute for material improvement: symbolic
campaigns, moral purity politics, and PR narratives without measurable linkage to lifeworld outcomes are disqualifying for legitimacy
claims. Conversely, over-instrumenting culture (surveillance, scoring, forced conformity) is explicitly prohibited. The chapter
therefore focuses on incentive architecture, contestability, and measurable conditions (access, lock-in reduction, commons continuity),
not on policing feelings or tastes.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing caps + commons legitimacy**: use CH13 to anticipate status migration under caps and to prevent new elite
  bottlenecks (credential inflation, network clubs, platform ranking capture).
- If you are **designing cultural policy**: use CH13 to treat culture as commons infrastructure (libraries, venues, studios, sport,
  public media) with maintenance-first funding and baseline access without stigma.
- If you are **regulating media/platforms**: use CH13 to constrain attention-based extraction and informal censorship risks via
  contestable ranking/moderation, anti-manipulation constraints, and public option/interoperability remedies (link CH10).
- If you are **managing norm conflicts**: use CH13 to handle plural value conflicts via procedure (contestability, audit, traceable
  criteria) rather than moral doctrine.
- If you are **building rituals and recognition systems**: use CH13 to make maintenance and contribution visible with transparent
  award procedures and objection/appeal, avoiding elite-club dynamics and propaganda backfire.
- If you are **iterating interventions**: use CH13’s evolutionary infrastructure (pilots, version control, rollback rules, audit of the
  norm-curators) to learn without hollowing out invariants.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA-compatible design of cultural commons, recognition and prestige systems tied to contribution,
and governance of the attention economy where it affects lifeworld participation. In particular, re-channeling status competition toward
competence and commons maintenance, preventing credential and network lock-in, prohibiting surveillance-based norm discipline,
maintaining pluralism via procedural conflict handling, and enforcing anti-washing legitimacy rules are treated as **constitutive**
safeguards against non-monetary capture under caps.

If CH13 constraints are weakened—by allowing credential inflation and elite networks to become new choke points, tolerating opaque
platform ranking as a status gate, using moral purity politics to enforce conformity, or replacing material improvement with symbolism—
predictable failure signals follow: rising gatekeeping complaints and drop-off, greater dependence on platform attention, polarisation
and cynicism, and declining perceived fairness despite macro indicators. In that case, the implementation must be treated as drifting
toward incompatibility and must trigger rollback of norm instruments, redesign of access and contestability rails, and (where needed)
public option/interoperability remedies to restore plurality and reduce lock-in.
