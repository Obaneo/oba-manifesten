# B05.CH13.P09 — Norm conflicts and plurality (no uniform culture)

This paragraph specifies how norm conflicts are handled under OBA without imposing a uniform culture. OBA is not a value monolith: it
does not require citizens to share one ideology, religion, or lifestyle. It requires compatibility with invariants—caps (W/I), commons
maintenance (ΔG), and anti-extraction (E) rails—because those are the structural conditions of order compatibility. Within those
invariants, plurality is expected and legitimate. Norm conflicts (merit vs solidarity, local vs national, tradition vs innovation) are
therefore handled procedurally: through contestability, audit, and traceable criteria, not through moral purity politics. Moral purity
politics is treated as a predictable failure mode because it produces polarisation, sabotage, and selective enforcement.

---

<a id="b05ch13p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P09.S01 — OBA does not require a single value system; it requires compatibility with invariants (caps/commons/anti-E)

OBA distinguishes between invariants (order conditions) and cultural plurality (legitimate variance).

### Invariants (binding)
- **Caps (W/I)**: monetary dominance is constrained; attempts to bypass caps through new gatekeeping assets are non-conforming.
- **Commons**: baseline access and maintenance-first capacity are constitutive; proposals that degrade commons continuity violate
  compatibility.
- **Anti-E rails**: extraction mechanisms (tolls, fee-stacks, surveillance rent, coercive lock-in) are disallowed regardless of moral
  justification.

### Plurality (allowed)
- Different communities can value different lifestyles and cultural practices, provided they do not rely on coercion, exclusion, or
  extraction that violates invariants.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Disputes about “values” must be translated into invariant checks: does the practice increase lock-in, expand block power, or embed
  extraction? If not, plurality is admissible.

## B05.CH13.P09.S02 — Conflict arenas: merit vs solidarity, local vs national, tradition vs innovation

Norm conflicts are expected under caps and commons because scarcity and incentives shift.

### Typical conflict arenas (binding recognition)
- **Merit vs solidarity**: how much status and allocation should track competence/performance versus equal access and protection of
  vulnerable cohorts.
- **Local vs national**: local tailoring and identity versus uniform minima and portability; postcode-lottery risks.
- **Tradition vs innovation**: preservation of heritage and norms versus experimentation and institutional redesign.

### Risk (binding)
- These conflicts can be captured by elite gatekeepers (credentials, networks) or by platform attention dynamics, converting legitimate
  plural conflicts into polarising status wars.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Institutions must provide legitimate procedural channels for conflict resolution; otherwise conflicts shift into sabotage and
  non-compliance equilibria.

<a id="b05ch13p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P09.S03 — Procedure over morality: conflict handled via contestability, audit, and traceable criteria

OBA governs norm conflict through procedures that keep decisions contestable and non-captured.

### Procedural requirements (binding)
- **Contestability**: affected parties can object and appeal; reason codes and rule references are provided; time bounds apply.
- **Auditability**: decision criteria and evidence are logged; exceptions are registered and sunset-bounded.
- **Traceable criteria**: criteria must be stated in operational terms (access, safety, maintenance capacity, non-discrimination), not
  as moral labels.

### Translation rule (binding)
- Convert moral claims into testable claims: what is the alleged harm, what is the mechanism, how is it measured, and what is the
  remedy? Narrative without mechanism is non-evidence.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Institutions must publish aggregated conflict-resolution statistics and remedies; secrecy breeds capture and delegitimation.

## B05.CH13.P09.S04 — Failure mode: moral purity politics → polarisation and non-compliance

Moral purity politics treats disagreement as moral defect; it converts governance into identity warfare.

### Failure signals (binding)
- Policies framed as punishing “bad people” rather than fixing mechanisms (lock-in, extraction, maintenance failure).
- Expanding surveillance and behavioural scoring to enforce conformity.
- Selective enforcement justified by moral narratives; exclusion from commons access based on value compliance.
- Escalating social sanctions and deplatforming without due process.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat moralised enforcement and surveillance-based discipline as non-conforming; roll back to procedure and condition design.
- Recenter legitimacy on lifeworld outcomes (L down, friction down, commons continuity) and on auditable claims.
- Where polarisation dynamics are driven by platform ranking and attention tolls, apply CH10/CH13.P08 remedies (contestability,
  interoperability, public options) (fail-closed against purity-driven governance).
