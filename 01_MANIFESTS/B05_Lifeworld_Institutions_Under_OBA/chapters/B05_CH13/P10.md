# B05.CH13.P10 — Institutional rituals and symbolism (making maintenance and contribution visible)

This paragraph specifies institutional rituals and symbolism under OBA: how to make maintenance and contribution socially visible
without propaganda. Under caps and commons, the legitimacy of the order depends on whether people see continuity and repair in daily
life. Cultural recognition systems can help re-channel status toward P and commons maintenance (ΔG), but they are also a high-risk
surface for elite club formation and for “symbolism without delivery”. OBA therefore permits public recognition and rituals only under
transparent, contestable procedures with rotation and appeal, and only when linked to material outcomes (L reduction and ΔG
improvements). Where rituals become performative or coercive, they backfire and are treated as non-conforming.

---

<a id="b05ch13p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P10.S01 — Public recognition: prestige for maintenance work and commons-building (without propaganda)

Recognition is an incentive tool: it directs attention and imitation.

### Recognition objectives (binding)
- Attach prestige to:
  - maintenance and reliability work (repair, safety, continuity),
  - commons building and stewardship (libraries, care continuity, housing upkeep, local logistics),
  - and competence demonstrated in service of lifeworld outcomes.

### Without propaganda (binding)
- Recognition must not be partisan messaging or ideological loyalty signalling.
- Participation must remain voluntary; recognition cannot be conditioned on behavioural conformity or surveillance.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Recognition programmes must define measurable contribution classes and avoid vague virtue language that enables discretionary
  gatekeeping.

---

<a id="b05ch13p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P10.S02 — Transparent award procedures: criteria, rotation, objection/appeal (anti-elite-club)

Awards and honours are potential elite curation mechanisms; OBA binds them to procedural integrity.

### Procedure minima (binding)
- **Criteria**: publish clear criteria and evidence requirements; link to measurable outputs (ΔG, reliability, access improvements).
- **Rotation**: rotate selection committees and juries; prohibit permanent membership and closed alumni self-selection.
- **Conflict-of-interest**: disclose relationships; recusal rules; prohibit sponsor capture of selection.
- **Objection/appeal**: provide a mechanism to contest awards (fraud, nepotism, misattribution) with time-bounded review.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated award statistics: nomination volumes, selection distributions, committee composition (aggregate), and appeal
  outcomes (privacy-safe).

---

<a id="b05ch13p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P10.S03 — Local rituals: commons days, maintenance reporting, public audit sessions

Local rituals are admissible as civic infrastructure when they increase transparency and participation, not when they enforce identity.

### Admissible rituals (binding)
- **Commons days**: periodic events where commons facilities (libraries, repair workshops, community spaces) open access, recruit
  volunteers, and demonstrate services without stigma.
- **Maintenance reporting**: public reporting sessions where local stewards show backlog reduction, incident logs, and next actions in
  plain language (linked to CH12 publication discipline).
- **Public audit sessions**: scheduled public sessions where auditors present findings, remediation progress, and take objections.

### Boundary rules (binding)
- Participation is voluntary; no attendance-based scoring; no political intimidation.
- Events must publish agendas and minutes; avoid capture by vendors or local elites.

---

<a id="b05ch13p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P10.S04 — Risk: symbolism without materiality (L/ΔG) → backfire and delegitimation

Symbolism becomes washing when it is not anchored in delivery.

### Backfire mechanisms (binding)
- Rituals and awards used to distract from worsening L (housing queues, energy burden) or from maintenance failure.
- Frequent ceremonies while backlogs grow; public praise while service quality declines.
- Recognition used as a substitute for fair pay and real capacity investment in maintenance roles.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If indicators show worsening L or ΔG decay while symbolic programmes expand, symbolic programmes are suspended and resources are
  reallocated to delivery.
- Legitimacy claims based on symbolism without measurement linkage are disqualifying (link P06 anti-washing) and trigger governance
  intervention (fail-closed against performative culture).
