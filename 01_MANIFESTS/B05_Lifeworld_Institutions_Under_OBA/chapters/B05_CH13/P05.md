# B05.CH13.P05 — Culture as infrastructure (commons approach)

This paragraph specifies culture under OBA as infrastructure in a commons sense. Culture is not only “content”; it is participation,
learning, belonging, and public meaning-making. In the lifeworld, cultural access reduces L indirectly (social isolation, stress,
navigation burdens) and supports agency and resilience. Cultural systems also have capture risks: elite curation can become a new status
gate; project-subsidy regimes can reward optics over continuity; and platform distribution can impose surveillance rent. OBA therefore
treats cultural capacity as a commons portfolio (libraries, venues, studios, sport, public media, knowledge repositories) with baseline
access without stigma, boundary rules against abuse and capture, maintenance-first funding for continuity and craft, and measurability
based on minimal system signals rather than personal profiling.

---

<a id="b05ch13p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P05.S01 — Cultural commons: libraries, stages/venues, studios, sport, public media, knowledge repositories

Cultural commons are shared capacity assets and services enabling participation and creation.

### Commons portfolio (binding)
- **Libraries**: access to books, digital resources, quiet spaces, learning support, and community programmes.
- **Stages/venues**: theatres, concert halls, community venues, rehearsal spaces; access for local creators and audiences.
- **Studios and maker spaces**: music and art studios, workshops, fabrication labs, craft infrastructure.
- **Sport and physical culture**: public sport facilities, clubs and grounds, access programmes for youth and low-income cohorts.
- **Public media**: non-commercial information and cultural production capacity with independence safeguards.
- **Knowledge repositories**: open archives, museums, and public knowledge infrastructure (including digital repositories) under open
  access rules.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Each commons asset/service must have: access rules, maintenance budgets, incident logs, and governance bodies (link CH12 commons
  registry discipline).

<a id="b05ch13p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P05.S02 — Access: baseline access without stigma; boundary rules against abuse/capture

Cultural commons must be genuinely accessible; access that creates stigma recreates exclusion.

### Baseline access (binding)
- Provide baseline access pathways that do not require elite credentials, complex paperwork, or costly membership.
- Affordability rules must be anti-regressive: free/low-cost baseline tiers with transparent optional paid extras.

### Non-stigma and inclusion (binding)
- Design access so participation does not mark users as “charity cases”; universal design and mixed-cohort participation is preferred.
- Accessibility for disability and language is mandatory; provide non-digital access routes where relevant.

### Boundary rules (binding)
- Define boundary rules for safety and abuse prevention without turning spaces into surveillance regimes.
- Prohibit capture via exclusive bookings, insider allocation, or opaque curation; allocations must be logged and contestable.

<a id="b05ch13p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P05.S03 — Maintenance-first: continuity and craft over event-driven project subsidies

Cultural capacity degrades when funding is episodic and optics-driven.

### Maintenance-first discipline (binding)
- Ring-fence maintenance budgets: facility upkeep, equipment replacement, staff continuity, and safety.
- Prioritise continuity of programmes and craft development over one-off events and “launch” optics.

### Anti-theatre rule (binding)
- Project subsidies are admissible only if they do not starve baseline operations; event spend without maintenance is treated as washing
  (symbolism without capacity).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated accounts: maintenance spend, staffing continuity indicators, utilisation, and backlog measures.

<a id="b05ch13p05s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P05.S04 — Measurability: minimal signals (reach, accessibility, continuity) without personal profiling

Cultural commons require measurement for accountability, but OBA prohibits surveillance-based cultural governance.

### Minimal measurement signals (binding)
- **Reach**: participation counts in aggregate (not individual tracking), including regional coverage.
- **Accessibility**: disability access compliance, language availability, affordability tiers, and barrier reports.
- **Continuity**: opening hours reliability, programme continuity, staff retention, and maintenance backlog indicators.

### Privacy minimalism (binding)
- No individual behavioural profiles or reputation scoring; no cross-service tracking of cultural participation.
- Sampling and aggregate reporting preferred; regulated micro-access only for audits under strict purpose limitation.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Cultural programmes that require personal profiling to operate, or that use participation as a scoring input for eligibility elsewhere,
  are non-conforming (fail-closed against surveillance culture).
