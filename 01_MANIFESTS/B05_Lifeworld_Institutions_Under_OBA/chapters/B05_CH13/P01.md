# B05.CH13.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of culture/status under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of culture and status under OBA. OBA treats culture not as a moral
programme but as an incentive-and-institutions layer: what people admire, compete for, and imitate determines where energy flows. Under
caps (W/I), monetary winner-take-all is constrained, but status drive remains and will migrate to new arenas. If unmanaged, that
migration can recreate extraction (E) and block power through credentials, elite networks, platform attention, and symbolic gatekeeping.
OBA therefore aims to re-channel status competition so it strengthens productive capacity (P) and commons maintenance (ΔG), while
explicitly prohibiting norm policy that becomes disciplining, homogenising, or surveillance-based. Legitimacy is tested in the lifeworld
via L and R/B tails, not by ideological conformity.

---

<a id="b05ch13p01s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P01.S01 — Purpose: re-channel status competition so it strengthens P and commons maintenance rather than E

Status competition is a permanent human dynamic; OBA treats it as a design variable.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Strengthen P**: status should reward competence, craftsmanship, and contribution that expands or maintains productive capacity
  (reliability, quality, repair, safety).
- **Strengthen commons maintenance (ΔG)**: prestige should attach to maintaining shared infrastructure and services (housing upkeep,
  care continuity, education quality, local commons stewardship), not only to novelty or consumption.
- **Weaken E**: reduce prestige pathways that depend on extraction—scarcity display, opaque gatekeeping, platform toll control, or
  reputational manipulation.
- **Preserve plurality**: status re-channeling must not require a single value doctrine; it must remain compatible with multiple
  subcultures as long as invariants hold.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- Campaigns whose functional effect is moral policing, conformity enforcement, or surveillance-based behavioural control are
  non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch13p01s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P01.S02 — Scope: values, prestige, consumption norms, career status, media/marketing, elite institutions

CH13 covers the cultural mechanisms that shape perceived success and social ranking.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Values and prestige signals**: what is publicly rewarded and socially admired (recognition systems, awards, reputation systems).
- **Consumption norms**: luxury and display consumption, and how marketing constructs scarcity and status pressure.
- **Career status**: status hierarchies in firms, professions, and education pathways; credential structures and gatekeeping.
- **Media and marketing**: attention allocation, narratives, influencer and platform dynamics, dark patterns and manipulation.
- **Elite institutions**: universities, boards, professional guilds, cultural curators, and network access systems.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH13 does not prescribe personal taste, lifestyle, or ideology; it specifies rails that prevent status systems from becoming new toll
  gates and block-power surfaces.

<a id="b05ch13p01s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P01.S03 — Binding: caps (W/I) as incentive structure, commons as allocation base, R/B as lifeworld test

Cultural design under OBA is bound to structural incentives and lifeworld outcomes, not moral declarations.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **Caps (W/I)**: caps change the payoff landscape; they reduce monetary dominance but create incentives for status migration into
  non-monetary assets (credentials, networks). Cultural design must anticipate and detect these shifts.
- **Commons as allocation base**: as commons options expand, baseline participation becomes less dependent on private gatekeepers; this
  changes what is scarce and therefore what status can attach to.
- **R/B lifeworld test**: legitimacy of norm shifts is tested by their impact on tail exposure and stability—do they reduce friction,
  improve essentials access, and make maintenance visible?
- **Anti-E compatibility**: any status mechanism that creates toll positions, lock-in, or opaque gatekeeping is treated as E-like and
  must be neutralised.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Any “norm instrument” (recognition schemes, campaigns, institutional rituals) must include an audit pack: mechanism → expected
  behavioural shift → risks (lock-in, gatekeeping, surveillance) → safeguards → rollback criteria.

<a id="b05ch13p01s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: norm policy that becomes disciplining or homogenising (instead of incentive architecture) is non-conform

OBA treats coercive norm policy as a high-risk failure mode because it creates resistance, sabotage, and hidden exclusion.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- **Disciplining**: policies that monitor individuals, score behaviour, or attach essential access to value compliance.
- **Homogenising**: policies that enforce a single cultural template and marginalise plural norms without necessity.
- **Gatekeeping drift**: “virtue” used as a credential barrier (titles/certificates required for participation) without contestability.
- **PR substitution**: storytelling and campaigns used as substitutes for material L/ΔG improvements and measurable outcomes.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Roll back the instrument; revert to condition design (access, portability, commons options) instead of behavioural enforcement.
- Require contestability and transparency: if an instrument cannot be audited and appealed, it is non-conforming by default.
- Where norm instruments create lock-in or new elites, trigger governance reset and redesign toward plural, procedure-based
  compatibility (fail-closed against cultural coercion).
