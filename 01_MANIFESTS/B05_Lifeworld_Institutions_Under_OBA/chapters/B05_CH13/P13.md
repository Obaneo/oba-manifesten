# B05.CH13.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for the cultural/status layer under OBA and the hard stops that apply when status
migration recreates elite choke points, when moralistic backlash polarises legitimacy, when surveillance-based norm discipline is
attempted, and when norm instruments increase lock-in or block power. Under caps, status drive does not disappear; it reallocates. If
that reallocation produces new gatekeeping assets (credentials, networks, platform reputation), domination reappears in a different
currency. If cultural governance is handled as moral politics, it produces identity warfare and non-compliance. If cultural governance
becomes surveillance culture, it violates OBA constraints on agency and plurality. OBA therefore defines explicit non-conformance
signals and fail-closed responses: rollback and redesign are preferred over coercive persistence.

---

<a id="b05ch13p13s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P13.S01 — Status shift to new elites: certificates/networks as a new W → governance reset and contestability requirements

A primary failure mode is replacing monetary elites with credential and network elites.

### Failure signals (binding)
- Rapid growth of required certificates/titles for participation without functional necessity.
- Closed network control of opportunities: board seats, grants, gatekeeper committees, and “who is in the room” dominance.
- Pay-to-enter pipelines (expensive credentials, unpaid internships) functioning as access tolls.
- High gatekeeping complaint volumes, low appeal success, and rising drop-off in access pipelines.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Trigger **governance reset** of gatekeeping institutions: rotate leadership, enforce transparent criteria, and install independent
  appeal/ombuds rails.
- Require **contestability**: reason codes, auditable selection rubrics, and time-bounded appeals; prohibit silent exclusions.
- Expand commons/public options for essential training and access pathways where private gatekeeping dominates (fail-closed against new
  elite choke points).

<a id="b05ch13p13s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P13.S02 — Moralistic backlash: “caps = envy” frame vs “community = coercion” frame → mitigate via lifeworld outcomes and transparency

Backlash is a narrative failure mode: camps interpret the order through moral frames rather than through lifeworld incidence.

### Backlash frames (binding recognition)
- **“Caps = envy/punishment”**: caps framed as resentment or moral punishment rather than as anti-extraction incentive architecture.
- **“Community = coercion”**: commons framed as forced conformity, loss of agency, or collectivist control.

### Risk mechanism (binding)
- If lifeworld outcomes do not improve visibly (L down, friction down, commons reliability up), moral frames dominate and polarisation
  escalates.
- If governance uses moral language or symbolic campaigns without measurement linkage, it confirms backlash narratives.

### Mitigation rule (binding)
- Anchor legitimacy claims in **auditable lifeworld outcomes**:
  - publish essentials indices and tail distributions (not only averages),
  - publish incident logs and restatements,
  - show visible maintenance and access improvements,
  - and keep conflict handling procedural (contestability, audit) rather than moralising (link P06 and P09).
- Treat “PR substitution” as disqualifying: narratives must reference method_id and published indicators.

<a id="b05ch13p13s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P13.S03 — Surveillance culture: measuring behaviour/values → non-conform and explicitly prohibited

Surveillance-based cultural governance is incompatible with OBA’s agency and plurality constraints.

### Explicit prohibitions (binding)
- Behavioural scoring systems that rank citizens by “virtue”, “community spirit”, “sustainability behaviour”, “trustworthiness”, or
  similar constructs.
- Conditioning access to essentials or commons services on monitored behaviour, ideology, or value compliance.
- Public or quasi-public reputation systems that allocate opportunities without due process, appeal, and auditability.
- Cross-domain tracking of cultural participation or media consumption for governance purposes.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Immediate rollback of surveillance-based instruments; purge of secondary profiling data; audit of procurement and governance that
  enabled the system; sanctions where applicable (fail-closed against surveillance culture).

<a id="b05ch13p13s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: if norm instruments increase lock-in (L), reduce plurality, or grow block power, the design must be rolled back and rebuilt

OBA treats deterioration of plurality and contestability indicators as evidence of cultural capture.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- **Lock-in increases (L)**: rising credential barriers, increased drop-off in access pipelines, increased dependency on closed
  platforms or gatekeepers.
- **Plurality reduces**: narrowing of admissible cultural participation; coercive conformity pressure; exclusion of minorities without
  invariant-based justification.
- **Block power grows**: opaque ranking/moderation, deplatforming without due process, gatekeepers controlling access without appeal.
- **Measurement corruption**: dashboards without remedies; cherry-picked narrative metrics; refusal to publish required indicators.

### Mandatory response (binding)
- Roll back the norm instrument; revert to condition design (commons access, portability, anti-manipulation) rather than behavioural
  discipline.
- Redesign with:
  - transparent criteria and audit packs,
  - contestability and appeals,
  - portability/interoperability where platforms are involved,
  - and governance rotation/audit-of-the-auditor for norm curators.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Rollbacks, redesign decisions, and conformance actions must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) so
  cultural drift cannot be hidden behind narrative.
