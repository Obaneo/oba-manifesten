# B05.CH13.P06 — Narratives and legitimacy: why people experience it as “fair”

This paragraph specifies legitimacy in OBA as a lived phenomenon rather than a rhetorical achievement. People experience “order” through
the lifeworld: whether essentials burdens (L) are low, whether friction and arbitrariness are reduced, and whether commons improvements
are visible and reliable over time. Macro indicators can improve while lifeworld experience worsens; in that case, cynicism, sabotage,
and non-compliance become rational responses. OBA therefore treats legitimacy narratives as accountability infrastructure, not PR:
claims must be auditable, incident discipline must exist (including restatements), and storytelling without measurement linkage is
explicitly disqualifying. This chapter segment is anti-washing by design: legitimacy is earned through measurable delivery and
contestable truth, not through messaging.

---

<a id="b05ch13p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P06.S01 — Core: people experience order via L (essentials), friction, and visible commons improvement

Legitimacy is primarily determined by daily experience, not by ideological agreement.

### Core determinants (binding)
- **Essentials burden (L)**: housing, energy, food, care, mobility and digital baseline costs and access conditions (tails matter).
- **Friction**: lead times, paperwork, navigation burdens, arbitrary rules, and disputes that require endurance (administrative L).
- **Visible commons improvement**: maintenance-first delivery that is observable—fewer outages, shorter waits, better access, safer
  spaces, lower backlog.

### Visibility principle (binding)
- Improvements must be legible in the lifeworld: people must see reliability and continuity, not only “announced investments”.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- A system that improves macro aggregates while leaving friction and tail burdens unchanged will lose legitimacy even if it is
  technically “efficient”.

<a id="b05ch13p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P06.S02 — Risk: macro-gains while lifeworld friction persists → cynicism and sabotage risk

When lifeworld experience does not match official narratives, people rationally infer deception or capture.

### Risk mechanism (binding)
- **Narrative gap**: officials cite growth, carbon targets, or “record budgets”, while individuals face housing queues, energy bills,
  and administrative clamp.
- **Distributional invisibility**: average improvement hides p90/p95 tails; those in tails experience “nothing works”.
- **Capture suspicion**: opaque procurement, elite exemptions, and unaccountable platforms create the perception that rules are for
  some, not for all.

### Consequences (binding)
- Cynicism becomes a stable equilibrium; compliance becomes conditional; sabotage and bypass behaviour increases; trust in audits and
  institutions collapses.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- If narrative gaps persist, governance must prioritise friction and tail reduction over symbolic wins; otherwise the order is
  politically unstable regardless of formal coherence.

<a id="b05ch13p06s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P06.S03 — Public accountability: not “PR”, but auditable claims, incident logs, restatements

Accountability is operational: publish claims, expose error, and correct publicly.

### Accountability minima (binding)
- **Auditable claims**: any public claim (“prices down”, “wait times improved”, “maintenance fixed”) must link to method_id and
  published indicators.
- **Incident logs**: outages, backlog spikes, procurement breaches, and enforcement anomalies are logged and published in aggregate.
- **Restatements**: when claims were wrong due to data or method errors, publish a restatement with explanation, corrected figures, and
  implications; hiding restatements is non-conforming.

### Audit-of-the-auditor linkage (binding)
- Auditors and comms functions are subject to meta-audit: selective reporting and narrative manipulation are capture signals.

<a id="b05ch13p06s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P06.S04 — Anti-washing: storytelling without measurement linkage is explicitly disqualifying for legitimacy claims

OBA treats “story without measurement” as a governance anti-pattern.

### Disqualifying patterns (binding)
- Campaign narratives that do not reference measurable indicators or that cherry-pick averages while tails worsen.
- “Vision” documents without implementation and conformance logs; awards and ceremonies without ΔG/L improvements.
- PR certificates and third-party endorsements used as substitutes for audit packs and decomposition.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If legitimacy claims cannot be linked to published measurement and audit artefacts, treat them as non-evidence; require correction or
  withdraw the claim.
- Where washing is repeated, trigger governance intervention: comms discipline reset, auditor review, and stronger publication
  requirements (fail-closed against narrative substitution for truth).
