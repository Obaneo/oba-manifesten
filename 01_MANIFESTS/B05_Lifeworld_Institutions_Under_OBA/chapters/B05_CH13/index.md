# B05.CH13 — Culture, status, and norm shifts: what “success” becomes under caps and commons

This chapter specifies the **cultural layer** under OBA: how status competition, prestige, and “success” norms reconfigure when caps
(W/I) constrain monetary winner-take-all and commons expansion changes what is scarce. OBA does not attempt to impose a single culture
or moral doctrine. It treats culture as an incentives-and-institutions interface: status drive remains, and will seek new arenas. If
left unmanaged, it can migrate into non-monetary capture surfaces (credentials, titles, network access, platform ranking power) and
recreate lock-in and block power. OBA therefore aims to re-channel status competition toward competence, contribution, and commons
maintenance (P/ΔG), while explicitly prohibiting disciplinary or homogenising “norm policy” that uses monitoring or behavioural scoring.

CH13 binds cultural governance to caps as incentive structure, commons as allocation base, and lifeworld R/B as the test of legitimacy.
It defines structural status shifts under caps, consumption and luxury without moralising politics, career status and institutional
gatekeeping risks, cultural commons design, legitimacy narratives with anti-washing requirements, agency constraints and anti-KPI
steering, media/platform dynamics as cognitive-status arenas, plural norm conflict handling via procedure not morality, institutional
rituals that make maintenance visible without propaganda, evolutionary infrastructure for iterating norm instruments without drift,
publication requirements, and hard stops. It is designed to fail-closed: if norm instruments increase lock-in (L), reduce plurality, or
expand block power, they must be rolled back and rebuilt.
