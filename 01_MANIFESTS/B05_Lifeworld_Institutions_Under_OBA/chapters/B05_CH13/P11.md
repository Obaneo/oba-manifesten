# B05.CH13.P11 — Evolutionary infrastructure: norm shift as an iterative process

This paragraph specifies “evolutionary infrastructure” for cultural and norm instruments under OBA: norm shift is treated as an
iterative engineering process, not as a one-shot moral campaign. Because cultural instruments can easily drift into coercion,
gatekeeping, or washing, OBA requires controlled experimentation: pilots with measurable criteria and stop/rollback rules, version
control for norm instruments (transparent changes, impact analysis, deprecation), learning mechanisms that do not hollow out core
invariants (caps/commons/anti-E), and anti-capture oversight for norm curators via audit-of-the-auditor. The design goal is adaptive
learning without drift and without elite capture of “meaning-making”.

---

<a id="b05ch13p11s01"></a>
## B05.CH13.P11.S01 — Pilots: small scale, measurable criteria, clear stop/rollback rules

Norm instruments must be tested like any other high-leverage policy tool.

### Pilot discipline (binding)
- Start small: limited scope (region, sector, institution type), explicit duration, and bounded budget.
- Define measurable criteria linked to conditions and outcomes (access, lock-in reduction, participation in commons, complaint and
  appeal rates), not “value compliance”.
- Predefine stop conditions: thresholds for harm signals (increased exclusion, increased gatekeeping complaints, increased monitoring,
  increased administrative friction).

### Rollback rules (binding)
- Pilots must have rollback plans: how the instrument is withdrawn, what data is retained (privacy-minimal), and how harms are
  remediated.
- Extensions require evidence packs and must be logged; indefinite pilots are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch13p11s02"></a>
## B05.CH13.P11.S02 — Version control for norm instruments: transparent changes, impact analysis, deprecation

Norm instruments are governance code; they must have version control.

### Versioning requirements (binding)
- Assign version identifiers to norm instruments (award schemes, rituals, messaging frameworks, access rules for cultural commons).
- Maintain change logs: what changed, why, expected impacts, and rollback conditions.
- Publish method_id for measurement linkages and any qualitative input coding schemes.

### Impact analysis (binding)
- For each version change, produce an impact note:
  - expected effect on L/lock-in/friction,
  - risk of gatekeeping or surveillance expansion,
  - and expected changes in complaint/appeal patterns.

### Deprecation (binding)
- Instruments that show harm signals or capture risk must be deprecated: declared end-of-life, replaced, and archived with rationale.
- “Never retire” cultural programmes are treated as drift risk.

<a id="b05ch13p11s03"></a>
## B05.CH13.P11.S03 — Learning without drift: updates must not hollow out core invariants

Adaptive learning is permitted only if invariants remain intact.

### Drift prevention (binding)
- Updates may not:
  - weaken caps (W/I) through status workaround tolerance,
  - degrade commons baseline access or maintenance-first discipline,
  - or normalise extraction (E) via new gatekeeping, reputational tolls, or surveillance rent.
- “Cultural adaptation” cannot become an excuse to reintroduce coercion or opaque block power.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Each update must include an invariants check: explicit statement of how caps/commons/anti-E and plurality constraints are preserved.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If an update threatens invariants or increases lock-in/block power, it is rolled back and redesigned (fail-closed against drift).

<a id="b05ch13p11s04"></a>
## B05.CH13.P11.S04 — Anti-capture: those who “curate” norms must themselves be auditable (“audit of the auditor”)

Norm curators (committees, boards, media bodies, cultural institutions) can become a new elite layer.

### Anti-capture minima (binding)
- Transparent governance: published membership (aggregate), selection procedures, rotation schedules, and conflict-of-interest rules.
- Contestability: objection and appeal processes for decisions (awards, access, curation choices) with reason codes.
- Audit-of-the-auditor: independent audits of curator decisions, sampling, and bias; peer review and rotation.
- No vendor or sponsor capture: funding and partnerships must be disclosed; sponsors cannot control curation criteria.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven closed-club dynamics (self-recruiting elites), opaque exclusions, or selective enforcement triggers governance reset and
  structural remedies (opening access, rotating leadership, public option alternatives for cultural commons functions) (fail-closed
  against elite capture of norm curation).
