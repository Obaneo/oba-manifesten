# B05.CH11.P12 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for food and basic essentials under OBA and the hard stops that apply when the
system drifts into washing, concentration-driven toll economics, and crisis regimes that become permanent extraction or claim support.
Because food is an essentials domain, these failures translate directly into L escalation and R/B tail exposure. OBA therefore defines
explicit non-conformance signals and fail-closed responses. The objective is **order compatibility**: affordability and resilience must
improve through real capacity (ΔG), chain transparency, and elimination of E layers—not through PR narratives, opaque pricing, or
permanent emergency controls.

---

<a id="b05ch11p12s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P12.S01 — PR certificates: “sustainability” without price/chain audit → washing

A predictable failure mode is using sustainability branding to avoid chain scrutiny while prices and margins drift upward.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Reliance on third-party “sustainability” certificates that lack auditable chain decomposition, labour evidence packs, and pricing
  transparency.
- Marketing claims used to justify price premiums without disclosure of cost components and value capture.
- “Ethical” labels paired with opaque intermediary layers, slotting fees, and unexplained spread expansion.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat PR certificates as non-evidence unless accompanied by audit packs: chain flow mapping, margin decomposition, labour audits, and
  third-party disclosure. Without these, claims are non-conforming and excluded from public procurement and baseline-basket policy
  programmes (fail-closed against sustainability-washing).

<a id="b05ch11p12s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P12.S02 — Concentration regrowth: input/retail monopoly → remedies (unbundling, public option)

Concentration regrowth is a structural failure mode: it converts essentials supply into a toll position.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Rising concentration indicators (HHI/dependency graphs) in processing, retail, or key input markets, combined with rising margins,
  fee-stacks, or coercive contract terms.
- Entry barriers increasing via exclusivity, slotting fees, or control of logistics/IT interfaces.
- “Competition on paper” while effective take-rates/mark-ups rise and supplier dependence worsens.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Trigger structural remedies:
  - **Unbundling** dominant chains (separate processing/logistics/retail control points),
  - **Interoperability mandates** for logistics and data interfaces,
  - **Public options** for distribution or inputs where contestability cannot be restored,
  - **Procurement shifts** toward commons logistics and diversified supplier bases.
- Repeat concentration drift without remediation is treated as governance breach (fail-closed against monopoly food tolls).

<a id="b05ch11p12s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P12.S03 — Crisis excuses: emergency measures that extend fee-stacks or claim support → hard stop

Crises are a classic entry point for capture: emergency regimes can be used to entrench intermediaries, preserve margins, or support
asset/claim positions.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Emergency procurement repeatedly awarded to the same intermediaries without disclosure, open standards, or exit plans.
- Temporary measures that introduce new fees, platform tolls, or exclusive distribution rights.
- Stabilisation measures whose functional effect is preserving private margins or commodity-position rents rather than protecting
  baseline access and restoring supply.
- Emergency regimes extended without sunsets and post-mortem audits.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Suspend capture-inducing emergency measures; revert to baseline-protection tools with transparent allocation and chain decomposition.
- Unwind emergency contracts that embed exclusivity or fee-stacks; re-tender under procurement rails (open standards; contestability).
- Proven claim/asset support leakage triggers conformance suspension and redesign (fail-closed against crisis capture).

<a id="b05ch11p12s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P12.S04 — Fail-closed: lacking decomposition/auditability or rising essentials-L without explanation requires redesign

OBA treats loss of auditability and worsening lifeworld outcomes as incompatibility signals.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- **Missing decomposition**: inability to decompose prices and margins for baseline basket items; refusal to provide audit packs.
- **Opacity**: undisclosed intermediaries, undisclosed contract terms (slotting fees, rebates), or paywalled chain transparency.
- **Rising essentials-L**: sustained increase in baseline basket burden distributions and tails without an explained input/shock cause
  consistent with decomposition evidence.
- **Resilience failure**: recurring shortages and lead-time spikes without reserve/logistics improvements.

### Mandatory response (binding)
- Treat current policy regime as non-conforming; restrict scope to auditable product groups and channels (commons logistics, public
  procurement) while redesign occurs.
- Trigger audits of concentrated segments and intermediary layers; apply structural remedies (unbundling/public options) where E layers
  are embedded.
- Tighten emergency regime sunsets and procurement rails; expand ΔG capacity in logistics and reserves.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Fail-closed events, redesign decisions, and structural remedies must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe
  aggregation) to prevent quiet drift and narrative substitution for evidence.
