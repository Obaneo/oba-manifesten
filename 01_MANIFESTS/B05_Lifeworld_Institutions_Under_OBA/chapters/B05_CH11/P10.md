# B05.CH11.P10 — Import/export and geopolitics: openness within leakage rails

This paragraph specifies import/export governance for food and basic essentials under OBA: openness is admissible, but only within
rails that prevent fragility, arbitrage extraction, and rights violations. Food supply chains are geopolitically exposed; single-point
dependencies can collapse under sanctions, export bans, conflict, or transport disruption. At the same time, trade can be used to
dump products, conceal forced labour, and route extraction through opaque intermediaries. OBA therefore requires diversification and
redundancy planning, treaty/controls hooks that activate under shocks or arbitrage, explicit prohibitions and presumptions against
dumping/forced labour/opaque chains, and international cooperation mechanisms for buffer pools and information sharing under privacy
minimalism. Trade policy is treated as a resilience and anti-leakage instrument, not as an ideology.

---

<a id="b05ch11p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P10.S01 — Diversification: no single-point dependencies; redundancy planning

Resilience requires that essential supply is not hostage to a single corridor, supplier country, or transport chokepoint.

### Diversification requirements (binding)
- Identify critical import dependencies for baseline basket staples and key inputs (seeds, fertiliser, energy-coupled inputs).
- Maintain redundancy: multiple sourcing regions, substitute products where nutritionally feasible, and alternative transport routes.
- For critical nodes (ports, rail corridors, cold chain), maintain contingency routing and surge logistics capacity (link P05/P09).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish (aggregate) dependency indicators: concentration of sourcing by product group, corridor fragility metrics, and contingency
  plans (privacy-safe; commercially sensitive micro-details can be restricted to auditors).

<a id="b05ch11p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P10.S02 — Treaty/controls hooks: under shocks or arbitrage (link Book 2 and Codex templates II)

OBA requires pre-defined legal and operational hooks for rapid response under shocks and arbitrage-driven leakage.

### Hooks (binding)
- **Shock hooks**: emergency measures that activate when supply disruption triggers occur (see P09): temporary controls on exports,
  emergency procurement protocols, and reserve activation.
- **Arbitrage hooks**: controls that activate when price differentials and leakage signals indicate speculative hoarding, cross-border
  arbitrage that drains local supply, or intermediary margin capture.
- **Contract hooks**: clauses in public procurement and concessional support that allow rapid renegotiation or termination under
  non-conformance (fee-stacks, opacity, labour violations).

### Codex linkage (binding)
- Use standard legal templates and audit-pack requirements (Codex templates II) to ensure hooks are reproducible and not discretionary
  political tools.
- Hooks must be time-bounded and include post-mortem audit requirements (link P09 sunsets).

<a id="b05ch11p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P10.S03 — Prohibited routes: dumping, forced-labour imports, opaque intermediaries (presumptions)

OBA treats certain trade patterns as presumptively non-conforming due to rights and anti-toll requirements.

### Prohibited routes (binding)
- **Dumping**: imports priced below cost bands in ways that destroy local productive capacity (ΔG loss) and create future dependency.
- **Forced-labour imports**: supply chains with credible forced labour indicators or inability to provide labour evidence packs (link
  P07).
- **Opaque intermediaries**: routing through intermediaries that refuse chain attribution and decomposition, especially where price
  anomalies and margin spikes are present.

### Presumption rule (binding)
- Where evidence packs cannot be produced (labour, chain decomposition, ownership/UBC), presume non-conformance and restrict entry for
  public procurement and baseline basket programmes until proven otherwise (fail-closed against trade opacity).

<a id="b05ch11p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P10.S04 — International cooperation: commons-like buffer pools and info sharing (privacy-minimal)

Resilience can be increased through cooperative buffers rather than unilateral hoarding.

### Cooperation mechanisms (binding)
- **Buffer pools**: cooperative reserve arrangements for staples and critical inputs, with pre-defined sharing and replenishment rules.
- **Mutual aid logistics**: cross-border logistics support during shocks (cold chain, transport capacity, repair crews) under audited
  governance.
- **Information sharing**: early-warning signals for shortages, disease outbreaks, and transport disruptions; share aggregate indicators
  and methods rather than personal or commercially sensitive micro-data.

### Privacy minimalism (binding)
- Cooperation data must be aggregate and purpose-limited; no person-tracking; avoid creating commercial intelligence pipelines that can
  be exploited for arbitrage.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Cooperative regimes must have transparent governance, audit-of-the-auditor, and sunset/renewal review cycles (fail-closed against
  captured “cooperation” frameworks).
