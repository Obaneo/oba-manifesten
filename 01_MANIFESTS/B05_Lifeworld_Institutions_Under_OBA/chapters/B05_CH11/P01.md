# B05.CH11.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of food/basic essentials under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of food and basic essentials under OBA. Food is treated as a
dominant essentials domain: affordability and reliability of basic provision directly determine L and R/B tails, and supply shocks
translate immediately into hardship, unrest, and cascading failures across households and institutions. Food systems are also
high-risk extraction (E) surfaces: concentration enables extreme margins, intermediaries build fee-stacks, and contracts can impose
clamp on producers and workers. OBA therefore treats food governance as constitutive infrastructure: ensure affordable, reliable basic
provision with high shock resilience, while enforcing chain transparency, P/E/C decomposition, commons logistics and reserves, and
strict prohibition of ΔM leakage into asset/claim support.

---

<a id="b05ch11p01s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P01.S01 — Purpose: affordable, reliable basic provision with low L and high shock resilience

Under OBA, the purpose of food governance is to keep basic provision affordable and reliable while preventing shock-driven tail
failures.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Affordability (L down)**: reduce essentials-basket burden distributions and tails; prevent regressive pricing structures and hidden
  mark-ups from becoming normal.
- **Reliability**: ensure continuous availability of staple foods and basic essentials across regions and cohorts.
- **Shock resilience**: build redundancy and response capacity against energy price shocks, climate variability, transport disruption,
  disease outbreaks, and geopolitical supply interruptions.
- **Anti-toll discipline**: prevent food chains from becoming toll ecosystems through concentration, fee-stacks, and coercive contract
  structures.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- Policies whose functional objective is to preserve incumbent margins, commodity-position rents, or opaque intermediary ecosystems
  rather than affordability and resilience are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch11p01s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P01.S02 — Scope: food supply chains, retail/wholesale, input markets (fertiliser, seeds, energy), logistics, prices, emergency reserves

CH11 covers the full food essentials stack: production-to-consumption flows, input dependencies, pricing interfaces, and crisis
infrastructure.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Supply chains**: farmers/producers, processors, wholesalers, distributors, retailers, and institutional procurement (schools,
  care, hospitals).
- **Retail/wholesale interfaces**: pricing, mark-ups, promotions, packaging/shrinkflation, loyalty schemes, and consumer information.
- **Input markets**: seeds, fertiliser, pesticides, feed, energy and water dependencies, IP/licensing, and bundling/exclusivity.
- **Logistics**: transport, cold chain, storage, warehousing, hubs, last-mile distribution, and logistics IT/control systems.
- **Prices and contracts**: price setting, indexation, contract terms (offtake, slotting fees, late payment), and dispute mechanisms.
- **Emergency reserves**: strategic stockpiles, reserve logistics capacity, triggers, rationing/allocation regimes, and unwind rules.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH11 does not prescribe dietary choices or cultural preferences except insofar as they affect baseline nutrition parameters and
  measurability of the essentials basket.

<a id="b05ch11p01s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P01.S03 — Binding: P/E/C classification (tolls/fee-stacks), ΔM rails (forbidden asset/claim support), commons standard (logistics/reserves), R/B (tails)

Food governance under OBA is bound to canonical rails; claims that ignore these rails are non-conforming.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **P/E/C classification**: distinguish productive food activity (P: production, processing, logistics, retail service) from extraction
  (E: scarcity rents, fee-stacks, coercive contract tolls, opaque intermediary margins) and commons flows (C: reserves, commons
  logistics, baseline access mechanisms).
- **ΔM rails**: prohibit ΔM-adjacent finance being used as asset/claim support for commodity positions, incumbent valuations, or margin
  preservation; emergency measures must not become claim support.
- **Commons standard (logistics/reserves)**: treat key logistics and reserve capacity as commons infrastructure with maintenance-first
  budgeting, allocation rules under shocks, and audit-of-the-auditor governance.
- **R/B (tails)**: evaluate food systems by tail resilience: shortages, price spikes, and access failures are primary tail events and
  must be dampened by redundancy and baseline protections.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Major price discipline measures, reserve activations, and procurement regimes must provide audit packs linking: chain mechanism →
  expected affordability/resilience outcomes → E leakage risks → governance and sunset controls.

<a id="b05ch11p01s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: price discipline without chain transparency/audit packs is non-conform (washing risk)

Food policy is highly susceptible to washing: price controls or subsidies can preserve margins or shift extraction into hidden layers
unless chain economics are transparent.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- Price interventions implemented without chain attribution and decomposition (who captures the difference).
- Reliance on voluntary reporting, PR certificates, or aggregate “inflation narratives” without auditable margin and fee-stack
  breakdown.
- Emergency measures that lack sunset rules and post-mortem audits.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Treat interventions as non-conforming until chain transparency is provided: flow mapping, price decomposition, margin bands, and
  audit packs for major actors.
- Under opacity, apply conservative presumptions: classify undisclosed components as E and escalate to audit; restrict programmes to
  auditable product groups and regions until transparency is restored (fail-closed against price-policy washing).
