# B05.CH11.P06 — Input markets and dependencies (seeds, fertiliser, energy, water)

This paragraph specifies input markets and dependencies under OBA: seeds, fertiliser, energy, and water are upstream essentials that
determine food affordability and resilience. Input markets are high-risk extraction (E) surfaces because IP licensing, bundling, and
exclusive distribution can convert production into a toll position, increase farmer dependence, and amplify shock transmission. Energy
and water coupling further links food affordability to infrastructure volatility and governance (CH08). OBA therefore requires
identification and constraint of input market power, remedies including open commons options and licensing rails, stabilisation through
infrastructure commons, and detection protocols for price/margin anomalies and contract clamp in input supply.

---

<a id="b05ch11p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P06.S01 — Market power in inputs: IP and licensing tolls, bundling, exclusivity

Input suppliers can extract by controlling essential upstream capabilities and making exit costly.

### Market power mechanisms (binding)
- **IP/licensing tolls**: seed patents, proprietary genetics, and licensing terms that impose recurring payments and restrict saving,
  replanting, or local breeding.
- **Bundling**: tying seeds to specific fertilisers/pesticides, equipment, or data services; “full package” contracts that prevent
  modular choice.
- **Exclusivity**: exclusive dealership agreements, territorial restrictions, or single-supplier procurement that create dependency.
- **Data gatekeeping**: agritech platforms that capture farm data and charge for access or interoperability (digital toll in inputs).

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- Rising input price-to-cost divergence; high and rising licensing fee shares; reduced availability of independent alternatives; and
  contract terms that restrict switching or reverse engineering.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Where input dependency is structural and abusive, the layer is classified as E and triggers anti-bundling and public option/commons
  remedies.

<a id="b05ch11p06s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P06.S02 — Remedies: open seed commons, public options, licensing rails, anti-bundling

OBA treats input contestability as a resilience and affordability condition.

### Remedy classes (binding)
- **Open seed commons**: maintain public/open breeding programmes, seed banks, and commons licensing that allows saving and replanting
  under bounded quality and biosecurity rules.
- **Public options**: public or commons suppliers for key inputs where private concentration is persistent (seed distribution, fertiliser
  procurement pools, shared agronomic services).
- **Licensing rails**: require fair, bounded licensing terms; prohibit clauses that lock farmers into single-vendor ecosystems; mandate
  transparent fee schedules.
- **Anti-bundling**: prohibit tying of inputs to unrelated services; require modular purchasing and interoperability between equipment,
  software, and agronomic data tools.

### Allocation rule (binding)
- Remedy selection prioritises reducing L (input cost pass-through), increasing redundancy, and preventing lock-in; where concentration
  is severe, structural remedies are mandatory candidates.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated input market metrics: concentration indicators, licensing fee shares, bundling incidence, and availability of
  commons/public options (privacy-safe aggregation).

## B05.CH11.P06.S03 — Energy/water coupling: stabilisation via infra commons (link CH08)

Food affordability is coupled to energy and water infrastructure; volatility in those domains transmits directly into input costs and
logistics.

### Coupling mechanisms (binding)
- Fertiliser production is energy-intensive; processing and cold chain depend on electricity; transport depends on fuel.
- Irrigation and water access depend on water infrastructure and governance; drought and allocation rules affect yields and prices.

### Stabilisation approach (binding)
- Treat critical energy and water infrastructure affecting food as commons capacity (ΔG) with maintenance-first and resilience
  discipline (link CH08).
- Where possible, prioritise investments that reduce volatility exposure: storage, redundancy, efficiency, and local resilience
  infrastructure.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Food policy packages must reference energy/water coupling analysis in audit packs; “food affordability” programmes that ignore
  coupling are treated as incomplete and prone to washing.

## B05.CH11.P06.S04 — Detection: price/margin anomalies and contract clamp in input supply

OBA requires early warning detection so input extraction does not silently convert into higher food prices.

### Detection indicators (binding)
- **Price anomalies**: input prices rising beyond cost indices and beyond seasonal norms; persistent price-to-cost divergence.
- **Margin anomalies**: rising supplier margins correlated with concentration and exclusivity patterns.
- **Contract clamp**: increased exclusivity clauses, late delivery penalties, forced data-sharing requirements, and restrictive
  termination clauses.
- **Bundling growth**: rising share of purchases requiring bundled packages or subscriptions.

### Escalation ladder (binding)
- Anomaly → audit pack request (contracts, fee schedules, licensing terms) → requalification (E) → remedies (anti-bundling, licensing
  rails, commons/public options, procurement shifts).

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If suppliers refuse disclosure of licensing and contract terms needed for audit, apply conservative presumptions and restrict
  admissibility for public procurement or subsidy eligibility until transparency is restored (fail-closed against input toll opacity).
