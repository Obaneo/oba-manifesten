# B05.CH11.P04 — Chain attribution and P/E/C decomposition in food chains

This paragraph specifies chain attribution and P/E/C decomposition for food under OBA. Price discipline is unenforceable without
knowing where value is captured. Food chains are multi-stage, and extraction (E) can hide in spreads, rebates, slotting fees, trading
layers, and “services” that do not add productive capacity (P). OBA therefore requires flow mapping from producer to consumer,
standard decomposition of price components (inputs, processing, logistics, retail), identification of E layers (tolls/fee-stacks and
coercive contract economics), and benchmarking against cost bands and margin standards by product group. Where prices cannot be
decomposed and audited, OBA applies fail-closed presumptions: opaque components are treated as E until proven otherwise.

---

<a id="b05ch11p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P04.S01 — Flow mapping: farmer → processor → wholesale → retail → consumer (actor/position)

Food price governance begins with a chain map: who touches the product, who sets terms, and who holds block power.

### Flow mapping (binding)
- For each covered product group, map the standard flow:
  - **Producer** (farmer/fisher),
  - **Processor** (slaughter, milling, packaging, transformation),
  - **Wholesale/trading** (aggregation, distribution contracts, trading desks),
  - **Retail** (stores, online, food service),
  - **Consumer/institution** (households, schools, care, hospitals).
- Record actor roles and positions: buyer/seller, contract owner, logistics operator, brand owner, platform operator, and any mandatory
  intermediaries.

### Position and power tagging (binding)
- Tag where block power exists: single-buyer regions, exclusive distribution rights, shelf access control, and proprietary logistics/IT
  gatekeepers.
- Tag contract clamp points: exclusivity, late payments, penalties, slotting fees, forced rebates.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Maintain flow maps as auditable artefacts (versioned) with clear actor categories and link to data sources.

<a id="b05ch11p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P04.S02 — Decomposition protocol: price components (inputs, processing, logistics, retail) + identify E-layers

OBA requires a standard decomposition so price changes can be attributed to functional costs versus extraction.

### Price decomposition (binding)
Decompose the consumer unit price into standard components:
- **Inputs**: raw commodity cost, seeds/fertiliser/feed, energy and water inputs (where attributable), packaging inputs.
- **Processing**: transformation costs, labour, equipment depreciation, compliance costs (bounded), and waste handling.
- **Logistics**: transport, cold chain, storage, warehousing, shrink/waste, and last-mile delivery.
- **Retail/service**: store operations, labour, rents, and necessary overhead (bounded), plus reasonable margin.
- **Taxes/levies** (if applicable) as separate disclosed lines.

### Identify E layers (binding)
Flag and quantify where possible:
- **Fee-stacks**: brokerage fees, trading spreads, platform access fees, “marketing contributions”, slotting fees, data-service fees.
- **Coercive rebates**: retroactive charges and forced discounts that function as tolls.
- **Opacity premiums**: margins justified only by “market conditions” without input linkage.

### Evidence rule (binding)
- Any actor claiming a component must provide an audit pack: invoicing basis, contracts, and allocation method; missing evidence
  triggers conservative classification as E.

<a id="b05ch11p04s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P04.S03 — Benchmarks: cost bands per product group, margin standards, seasonality handling

Benchmarks provide the reference frame for detecting abnormal margins and fee-stack regrowth.

### Cost bands (binding)
- Define cost bands per product group: expected ranges for input costs, processing/logistics costs, and retail/service overhead under
  normal conditions.
- Bands must reflect region, seasonality, and known structural differences (cold chain, perishability).

### Margin standards (binding)
- Define admissible margin standards (bands) for each stage under normal conditions, with explicit treatment of risk and waste.
- Require disclosure of deviations beyond margin bands with justification codes and evidence.

### Seasonality handling (binding)
- Separate seasonal expected variance from abnormal spikes; use rolling windows and p90/p95 tails to avoid averaging away spikes.
- Require explicit explanation for persistent deviations beyond seasonal norms.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated benchmark tables and observed distributions (privacy-safe; commercially sensitive micro-data can be restricted to
  auditors under rules, but aggregated results must be public).

<a id="b05ch11p04s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P04.S04 — Hard stop: opaque pricing without decomposition → presumption of E and escalation to audit

Where decomposition is not possible, price discipline becomes guesswork and is susceptible to washing.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- Actors refuse to provide cost and contract evidence needed for decomposition.
- Pricing changes cannot be reproduced from disclosed inputs and benchmark methods.
- Significant share of price is attributable to undefined “services”, rebates, or spreads without audit packs.

### Fail-closed response (binding)
- Apply conservative presumption: non-decomposable components are classified as E by default and are restricted, capped, or excluded
  from admissible price adjustments until evidence is provided.
- Escalate to audit: require targeted audits of the relevant chain segments, including contract clamp review and fee-stack inventory.
- Under persistent opacity, restrict policy tools to auditable product groups and shift supply toward commons logistics/public
  procurement pathways (fail-closed against opaque margin capture).
