# B05.CH11.P09 — Emergency reserves and shock resilience (crisis commons)

This paragraph specifies emergency reserves and shock resilience for food and basic essentials under OBA. Food shocks are fast tail
events: shortages and price spikes translate immediately into L escalation and R/B collapse for vulnerable households, and can
destabilise institutions. OBA therefore requires crisis commons infrastructure: strategic reserves of staples (and linked essentials
where relevant), reserve logistics capacity, explicit triggers for activation, a bounded instrument set (temporary stabilisation,
allocation, rationing) governed by transparent boundary rules, and strict sunsets with unwind plans and post-mortem audits. Emergency
regimes must not become permanent toll structures or backdoor claim support; fail-closed discipline applies.

---

<a id="b05ch11p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P09.S01 — Strategic reserves: staple foods, medicines (link care), logistics capacity

Reserves are treated as commons infrastructure that reduces tail exposure.

### Reserve scope (binding)
- **Staple foods**: define a reserve basket of staple items sufficient to protect baseline nutrition during disruptions (parameters by
  jurisdiction and region).
- **Linked essentials**: where governance integrates, include critical medicines and assistive supplies linked to care pathways (link
  CH09) or define coordination protocols with healthcare reserves.
- **Logistics capacity**: maintain reserve distribution capacity (storage, cold chain where needed, transport contracts, last-mile
  options) so reserves are deployable, not only stored.

### Maintenance-first requirements (binding)
- Rotate inventory (FIFO/FEFO), monitor expiry, and maintain audited stock records.
- Maintain facility and equipment maintenance backlogs and SLAs; reserve infrastructure must have redundancy and backup power where
  relevant.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated reserve indicators: coverage days/weeks by category, geographic distribution at coarse granularity, rotation
  performance, and readiness tests (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch11p09s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P09.S02 — Triggers: shortages, price spikes, supply-disruption indicators

Emergency regimes must be activated by explicit triggers rather than pure discretion.

### Trigger classes (binding)
- **Shortages**: stockout incidence above threshold levels across regions or product groups; persistent unfilled orders for essential
  institutions.
- **Price spikes**: essentials-basket price indices exceeding threshold increases (parameters) and correlating with rising burden tails.
- **Supply-disruption indicators**: lead-time spikes, transport disruption indicators, disease outbreak signals, or geopolitical export
  restrictions affecting key inputs/products.

### Trigger discipline (binding)
- Triggers must be parameterised and published; activation decisions must be logged with data and rationale (audit pack pointers).
- Trigger thresholds must include tail sensitivity: early activation to protect p90/p95 burden and availability tails is permitted.

<a id="b05ch11p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P09.S03 — Instruments: temporary price stabilisation, allocation, rationing with boundary rules

OBA permits a bounded emergency toolset focused on protecting baseline access and restoring supply, not preserving private margins.

### Permitted instruments (binding)
- **Temporary price stabilisation**: time-bounded stabilisation of baseline basket items using transparent formulas and incidence
  controls; must be linked to chain decomposition to prevent margin capture.
- **Allocation**: prioritise supply to essential institutions (schools, care, hospitals) and baseline household needs; apply transparent
  allocation rules.
- **Rationing**: rule-based rationing under severe shortage, with baseline nutrition protection and clear enforcement boundaries.
- **Emergency logistics deployment**: surge last-mile capacity and distribution hubs to prevent regional deserts and waste.

### Boundary rules (binding)
- Measures must specify eligibility, quantities, duration, and enforcement steps; no discretionary ad hoc rationing without logs.
- Emergency measures must not introduce new fee-stacks or require surveillance/identity profiling beyond minimal necessity.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Each instrument must include an evidence pack linking: trigger → chosen tool → expected effect on availability and burden tails →
  anti-capture controls → exit criteria.

<a id="b05ch11p09s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P09.S04 — Sunsets: time-bounded use, unwind plan, post-mortem audits (no permanent emergency)

Emergency regimes are allowed only as temporary departures from normal rails. Permanence is treated as governance failure.

### Sunset discipline (binding)
- Every emergency measure must include a fixed sunset date (or short renewal interval) and explicit criteria for extension.
- Extensions require updated evidence packs and must be logged; indefinite continuation is non-conforming.

### Unwind plan (binding)
- Define staged unwind: taper stabilisation, restore normal procurement flows, replenish reserves, and reconcile accounts.
- Remove any emergency procurement exceptions or convert them to audited standard pathways.

### Post-mortem audit (binding)
- Conduct post-mortem audits within a defined window: root causes, performance of instruments, distributional incidence, capture and
  fee-stack risks, and lessons for resilience investments.
- Publish public summaries (privacy-safe) and remediation plans with deadlines.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Emergency measures that persist without sunsets, lack post-mortems, or become vehicles for claim support or intermediary fee-stacks
  trigger conformance suspension and mandatory redesign (fail-closed against permanent emergency governance).
