# B05.CH11.P05 — Commons logistics: distribution as infrastructure (ΔG)

This paragraph specifies distribution and logistics for food and basic essentials under OBA as **commons capacity** (ΔG). Logistics is
often the hidden determinant of both affordability and resilience: without cold chain, storage, and reliable last-mile capacity, food
waste rises, shortages cluster regionally, and shocks translate directly into L and R/B tails. Logistics is also a capture surface:
platform intermediaries can impose tolls, and proprietary IT/control systems can create vendor lock-in. OBA therefore treats key
distribution infrastructure as shared capacity with maintenance-first discipline, transparent allocation rules (especially under
shocks), audit-of-the-auditor governance, and procurement rails that enforce open standards and prevent lock-in.

---

<a id="b05ch11p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P05.S01 — Distribution hubs: cold chain, storage, last-mile as shared capacity

Commons logistics is built around shared hubs and interoperable last-mile capacity.

### Shared capacity components (binding)
- **Cold chain**: refrigerated storage and transport capacity to reduce spoilage and ensure stable supply of perishables.
- **Storage and warehousing**: scalable storage for staples and inputs; buffer capacity to smooth lead times.
- **Distribution hubs**: regional hubs that connect producers/processors to retail and institutional buyers; include cross-docking where
  appropriate.
- **Last-mile**: shared last-mile delivery options (route pooling, community pickup points, mobile markets) to address access gaps and
  reduce duplication.

### Access objective (binding)
- Hubs must support baseline basket availability across regions, including low-density or low-income areas where private margins are
  weak.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish capacity inventories in aggregate: hub locations (coarse), throughput ranges, cold-chain capacity, and last-mile coverage
  indicators (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch11p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P05.S02 — Maintenance-first: inventory management, equipment upkeep, continuity planning

Logistics resilience is maintained through discipline: equipment uptime, inventory integrity, and continuity plans.

### Maintenance-first requirements (binding)
- **Inventory management**: rotation (FIFO/FEFO), expiry monitoring, stock accuracy, and waste minimisation targets with auditable logs.
- **Equipment upkeep**: scheduled maintenance for refrigeration, vehicles, generators, and handling equipment; backlog registers with
  severity grading and SLAs.
- **Continuity planning**: redundancy for critical nodes, backup power for cold chain, spare parts availability, and rapid repair
  contracts that are contestable and audited.

### Backlog thresholds (binding)
- Define backlog thresholds (parameters) for logistics equipment and facility maintenance; breaches trigger escalation and capacity
  surge measures.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated performance: spoilage/waste rates, downtime incidents, maintenance backlog size/age, and recovery times
  (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch11p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P05.S03 — Governance: transparent allocation rules, prioritisation under shocks, audit of the auditor

Because logistics capacity controls access, allocation rules are governance-critical and must be contestable.

### Allocation governance (binding)
- **Transparent allocation rules**: define how capacity is allocated among regions and buyers, including priority for baseline basket
  and essential institutions (schools, care, hospitals).
- **Shock prioritisation**: pre-define prioritisation tiers for shortages (baseline staples first, medically necessary diets, essential
  institution supply), with measurable triggers (linked to P09 emergency regimes).
- **No selective enforcement**: allocation decisions must follow published rules; exceptions require logging and sunset discipline.

### Audit-of-the-auditor (binding)
- Independent audits of allocation decisions, inventory records, procurement, and incident handling; rotation and peer review; public
  summaries.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven favouritism, corruption, or incident concealment triggers governance reset: leadership rotation, tighter disclosure, and where
  needed conversion to stricter commons governance (fail-closed against logistics capture).

## B05.CH11.P05.S04 — Procurement rails: open standards, interoperability, anti–vendor lock-in in logistics/IT

Procurement is a primary channel for vendor capture, especially via logistics IT and routing platforms.

### Open standards (binding)
- Require open data formats and documented APIs for inventory, routing, order management, and reporting.
- Ensure interoperability between hubs, retailers, and institutional buyers to avoid platform tolls.

### Vendor lock-in prevention (binding)
- Prohibit exclusive control of routing and fulfilment data, closed interfaces, and licensing terms that prevent switching.
- Require escrow/continuity arrangements for critical software and operational data.

### Performance-based contracting (binding)
- Contracts must specify measurable deliverables: uptime, throughput, spoilage reduction, response times, and security requirements.
- Change orders are bounded and transparent; repeated change-order inflation triggers review and re-tendering.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish procurement logs (aggregate): supplier concentration, standards compliance, change-order volumes, performance outcomes, and
  exit-cost estimates (privacy-safe aggregation).
