# B05.CH11 — Food and basic essentials: supply chains, price discipline, commons logistics, and shock resilience

This chapter specifies **food and basic essentials** under OBA as a lifeworld essentials domain: affordability and reliability of
basic provision directly determine L and R/B tails, and shocks propagate rapidly through supply chains, input markets, and logistics.
Food systems are prone to concentration, intermediary fee-stacks, contract clamp, and cognitive toll at the consumer interface. Under
OBA, food governance is therefore a rail discipline problem: make chains transparent, decompose prices and margins into P/E/C, neutralise
toll layers (E), build commons logistics capacity (ΔG), and maintain shock resilience via reserves and emergency regimes with strict
sunset discipline. ΔM channels must not be used as backdoor asset/claim support for commodity positions, incumbents, or rentier
cashflows.

CH11 is designed to be **anti-washing** and **fail-closed**. “Price discipline” policies that operate without chain transparency and
audit packs are treated as washing risk: without decomposition, margins can be hidden, scarcity rents can be preserved, and emergency
measures can become permanent tolls. The chapter therefore defines diagnostics, baseline baskets and price rails, chain attribution and
decomposition protocols, commons logistics design, input market remedies, labour rails for seasonal work, anti-cognitive-toll rules at
retail interfaces, emergency reserve regimes, import/export governance within leakage rails, publication requirements, and hard stops.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing food affordability policy**: use CH11 to define the baseline basket and price rails, and to ensure that any
  intervention is backed by chain decomposition and audit packs (anti-washing).
- If you are **auditing supply chains**: use CH11’s chain attribution and decomposition protocol to identify E layers (fee-stacks,
  excessive margins, coercive contracts) and trigger remedies.
- If you are **building distribution infrastructure**: use CH11 to design commons logistics (hubs, cold chain, storage, last-mile)
  with maintenance-first and procurement rails (open standards; no vendor lock-in).
- If you are **regulating input markets**: use CH11 to detect IP/licensing tolls, bundling, and exclusivity in seeds/fertiliser, and
  to deploy open commons/public option remedies.
- If you are **governing crisis regimes**: use CH11 to define reserves, triggers, rationing/allocation boundary rules, and strict
  sunsets with post-mortem audits.
- If you are **reducing cognitive toll**: use CH11 to eliminate dark patterns (misleading discounts, shrinkflation, loyalty lock-in),
  enforce clear unit pricing and labelling, and prohibit profiling-based price discrimination.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA-compatible design of food supply chains, essential-goods retail and wholesale regimes,
distribution/logistics infrastructure, input markets, and emergency provisioning. In particular, chain transparency and P/E/C
decomposition, prohibition of fee-stacks and contract clamp, commons logistics and reserves governance, anti-washing audit packs,
anti-cognitive-toll standards, and strict sunset discipline for emergency measures are treated as **constitutive** safeguards against
essentials-L escalation and shock fragility.

If CH11 constraints are weakened—by tolerating opaque pricing, permitting concentration and intermediary fee-stacks to regrow, allowing
loyalty profiling to drive discrimination, or running emergency regimes without sunsets—the lifeworld layer will register predictable
failure signals: rising essentials-basket burdens (especially tails), increased shortage incidents, widening margin anomalies, and
higher reliance on coercive contracts. In that case, the implementation must be treated as drifting toward incompatibility and must
trigger redesign, scope restriction to auditable coverage, structural remedies (unbundling/public options), and hard stops on
non-auditable price regimes.
