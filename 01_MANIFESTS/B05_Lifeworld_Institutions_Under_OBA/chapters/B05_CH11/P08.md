# B05.CH11.P08 — Retail and consumer interface: marketing, labelling, and cognitive toll

This paragraph specifies the retail and consumer interface for food and basic essentials under OBA. Retail is a high-leverage
lifeworld surface: households make frequent decisions under time pressure, and cognitive manipulation can convert essentials purchase
into a toll through misleading discounts, shrinkflation, bundling traps, and loyalty lock-in. OBA therefore requires elimination of
dark patterns, clear unit pricing and ingredient transparency, and loyalty programme governance under data minimalism with prohibition
of profiling-based price discrimination. Remedies are procedural and enforceable: standard label formats, fines, contract rewrites, and
public warnings. The goal is to keep the food interface contestable and non-manipulative, so L is not amplified by cognitive toll.

---

<a id="b05ch11p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P08.S01 — Dark patterns: misleading discounts, shrinkflation, bundling, loyalty lock-in

Retail deception and manipulation are treated as cognitive extraction (E) in an essentials domain.

### Prohibited dark patterns (binding)
- **Misleading discounts**: false reference pricing, rotating “sales” that are effectively baseline, and promotions that obscure unit
  price increases.
- **Shrinkflation**: reducing quantity/quality while holding headline price, without clear and prominent disclosure.
- **Bundling traps**: forcing purchase of non-essential extras to reach baseline affordability (multi-buy coercion, “only in bundle”
  pricing).
- **Loyalty lock-in**: pricing that makes baseline affordability contingent on joining a programme with data extraction or exclusion
  risk.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Retailers must maintain auditable price histories for baseline basket items (unit price and package size), enabling detection of
  deceptive patterns.

<a id="b05ch11p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P08.S02 — Labelling standards: clear unit pricing, ingredient transparency (without surveillance)

Labelling is a governance rail: it determines whether consumers can compare, substitute, and detect manipulation.

### Unit pricing (binding)
- Mandatory, prominent unit pricing (per kg/litre/unit) on shelves and online, using standard placement and font minima.
- Promotions must show unit prices before and after promotion; “per serving” framings are not allowed to replace unit prices.

### Ingredient transparency (binding)
- Clear ingredient lists, allergens, and origin categories where relevant; avoid misleading “health” claims without standard criteria.
- Transparency must not require app-based scanning tied to identity; access must be available offline and without profiling.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Standard label templates must be used; deviations require justification and are subject to enforcement review.

<a id="b05ch11p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P08.S03 — Loyalty programmes: data minimalism; prohibited price discrimination via profiling

Loyalty programmes often act as surveillance and price discrimination mechanisms. Under OBA, they are tightly constrained.

### Data minimalism (binding)
- Collect only the minimum data needed to administer discounts (if allowed at all); prohibit behavioural profiling, cross-service
  enrichment, and third-party sharing.
- Provide non-digital alternatives (cardless codes, paper options) where feasible to avoid exclusion of data-poor groups.

### Prohibited practices (binding)
- **Profiling-based price discrimination**: using purchase history, location, or inferred attributes to set personalised essentials
  prices.
- **Forced consent**: requiring tracking permission as a condition for baseline prices.
- **Lock-in penalties**: making baseline affordability conditional on surrendering data or using specific payment methods.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish a loyalty data-use register: collected fields, purposes, retention, third-party sharing (should be none), and opt-out
  mechanisms.

<a id="b05ch11p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P08.S04 — Remedies: standard formats, fines, contract rewrites, public warnings

OBA requires an escalation ladder so cognitive toll patterns trigger correction rather than becoming normalised.

### Remedy ladder (binding)
- **Standard formats**: mandate label and price-display templates; require unit-price prominence; enforce shrinkflation disclosure rules.
- **Fines and restitution**: fines for repeated deception and restitution/refunds where mispricing affected consumers.
- **Contract rewrites**: rewrite loyalty terms to remove data extraction; remove bundling clauses and coercive discount structures.
- **Public warnings**: publish violations and repeat-offender indicators in aggregated public notices to restore contestability.

### Enforcement triggers (binding)
- High complaint volumes, anomaly detection in unit price changes, repeated shrinkflation incidents, and loyalty lock-in patterns
  trigger audits and remedial mandates.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Persistent deception or refusal to provide auditable price histories triggers restrictions on promotional practices and, where
  necessary, licensing/procurement consequences for essential supply programmes (fail-closed against retail manipulation).
