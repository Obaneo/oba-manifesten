# B05.CH11.P07 — Labour, seasonal work, and conditions (lifeworld within chains)

This paragraph specifies labour conditions in food and essentials supply chains under OBA, with emphasis on seasonal work and migrant
labour. Food chains frequently rely on workers with low bargaining power, limited local support, and high dependence on employers or
recruiters for housing, transport, documentation, and wages. This creates hidden L and coercion risks: workers can be trapped through
recruitment fees, debt-like payroll deductions, passport retention, unsafe housing, and retaliatory threats. Under OBA, such dynamics
are treated as extraction (E) and order-incompatible clamp. OBA therefore defines minimum rails for housing/health/safety and rights
portability, prohibits debt-bondage-like structures and payroll fee-stacks, and requires labour audits with evidence packs rather than
PR certificates.

---

<a id="b05ch11p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH11.P07.S01 — Labour migration and exploitation: risks of hidden L and coercion

Seasonal and migrant labour is a primary risk surface for coercion because dependence is structural.

### Exploitation mechanisms (binding)
- **Employer dependence**: housing and immigration status tied to the employer; threats of termination become threats of homelessness or
  deportation.
- **Recruitment chains**: intermediaries charge fees, create debt obligations, and control job access.
- **Information asymmetry**: contracts not understood (language), hidden deductions, false promises, and lack of grievance pathways.
- **Living conditions as clamp**: overcrowded housing, unsafe transport, withheld wages, and retaliatory discipline.

### Hidden L translation (binding)
- Exploitation increases lifeworld L through unsafe living conditions, health deterioration, time poverty, and loss of autonomy; it
  also distorts food prices by embedding coercion as a cost model.

### Diagnostic indicators (binding)
- High prevalence of deductions, long working hours without rest, high injury rates, high worker turnover, and patterns of withheld
  documents or wages.

<a id="b05ch11p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH11.P07.S02 — Minimum rails: housing/health/safety, portability of rights, due process

OBA treats basic labour protection in essentials chains as constitutive. Minimum rails apply regardless of worker nationality.

### Minimum rails (binding)
- **Housing**: safe, inspected housing meeting baseline standards (link to housing rails CH07 where relevant); bounded charges and
  transparent deductions.
- **Health and safety**: enforce safety standards, protective equipment, training, and access to healthcare; injury reporting and
  incident logging required.
- **Pay and time**: transparent pay statements, time recording, bounded overtime, and predictable rest; no wage theft through opaque
  deductions.
- **Portability of rights**: ability to change employers without losing legal status or housing abruptly; access to independent advice
  and support channels.
- **Due process**: reason codes for termination and disciplinary actions, objection/appeal paths, and protection against retaliation
  for complaints.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Employers and labour intermediaries must provide standard evidence packs: contracts (language-accessible), pay statements, housing
  agreements, and safety incident logs.

---

<a id="b05ch11p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH11.P07.S03 — Prohibited: recruitment fees, debt-bondage-like structures, payroll fee-stacks

OBA prohibits structural coercion mechanisms that transform work into debt-like bondage.

### Prohibited practices (binding)
- **Recruitment fees** paid by workers, directly or indirectly; any recruitment cost must be borne by the employer or chain principals.
- **Debt-bondage-like structures**: loans tied to employment, excessive advances with punitive repayment terms, or contractual penalties
  that trap workers.
- **Passport/ID retention** or threats to report workers to authorities as discipline tools.
- **Payroll fee-stacks**: layered deductions (housing, transport, equipment, “admin fees”) that are opaque, inflated, or mandatory and
  reduce net pay below agreed terms.
- **Retaliation**: eviction threats, blacklisting, and intimidation for exercising rights or filing complaints.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Detection triggers immediate non-conformance: employer loses eligibility for public procurement and certification claims until
  remediation is verified; repeat offenders trigger bans and chain-level sanctions.

<a id="b05ch11p07s04"></a>
## B05.CH11.P07.S04 — Audits: supply-chain labour audits with evidence packs (no PR certificates)

OBA rejects PR-style labour certification that lacks evidence. Audits must be reproducible and adversarially robust.

### Audit requirements (binding)
- **Evidence packs**: contracts, pay records, deduction schedules, housing inspections, safety logs, incident reports, and grievance
  handling records.
- **Worker interviews**: conducted with privacy and safety protections (no employer presence), language access, and retaliation
  safeguards.
- **Chain responsibility**: audits apply to chain principals (processors/retailers) as well as to farms and labour providers; look-through
  across subcontractors and recruiters.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated audit outcomes: non-conformance categories, remediation completion rates, repeat-offender indicators, and sanction
  actions (privacy-safe aggregation).

### Hard stop (binding)
- Substitution of “PR certificates” for evidence packs is non-conforming. Where audits cannot access evidence or workers safely, the
  chain segment is treated as high-risk and subject to procurement exclusion until auditable compliance exists (fail-closed against
  labour-washing).
