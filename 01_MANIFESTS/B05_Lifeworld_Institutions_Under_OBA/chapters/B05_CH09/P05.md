# B05.CH09.P05 — Staffing as infrastructure (maintenance-first for capacity)

This paragraph specifies staffing under OBA as **infrastructure capacity**, governed with maintenance-first logic. In healthcare and
education, staffing is the dominant constraint on access, quality, continuity, and wait times; it is also the primary point where
systems silently ration via burnout, churn, and reduced contact time. OBA therefore treats staffing as a governed capacity asset:
define minima (ratios, skill mix, supervision), maintain early-warning proxies for burn-out risk, invest in the training pipeline
(intake and placement capacity), and design retention conditions (rosters, workload, autonomy) as quality prerequisites. Persistent
understaffing and backlog growth are treated as structural capacity breaches that trigger fail-closed escalation: status downgrade,
scope restriction to auditable coverage, and an emergency capacity plan.

---

<a id="b05ch09p05s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P05.S01 — Staffing minima: ratios, skill mix, supervisor coverage, burn-out risk proxies

Staffing minima are not “nice-to-have”; they are safety and learning integrity constraints.

### Staffing minima (binding)
- **Ratios**: define minimum staff-to-user ratios by pathway (clinical units, classrooms, long-term care, special needs), including
  coverage by time band (nights/weekends) where relevant (parameters required).
- **Skill mix**: define required skill composition (licensed professionals, assistants, specialists) to prevent unsafe substitution and
  hollowing out.
- **Supervisor coverage**: minimum supervisor-to-frontline ratios and protected supervision time; supervision is treated as safety and
  learning quality infrastructure.

### Burn-out risk proxies (binding)
- Track early-warning indicators: overtime rates, sick leave, turnover, vacancy duration, reliance on temporary agencies, average
  caseload/class size, and documentation time burdens.
- Rising tails in these proxies trigger escalation before visible collapse (fail-closed against burnout rationing).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregate staffing and burn-out proxy distributions (median/p90/p95) by institution type and region (privacy-safe
  aggregation).

<a id="b05ch09p05s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P05.S02 — Training pipeline: intake, placement capacity, upskilling, career paths (link to Work CH02)

Capacity cannot be maintained without a governed pipeline. Training is treated as ΔG for essentials services.

### Pipeline components (binding)
- **Intake capacity**: size education/training intakes to projected demand; avoid boom/bust cycles that produce chronic shortages.
- **Placement capacity**: ensure sufficient supervised placements/apprenticeships; placement bottlenecks are treated as hard capacity
  constraints and must be expanded.
- **Upskilling**: provide continuous training and cross-skilling pathways to adjust skill mix without lowering professional standards.
- **Career paths**: maintain progression routes that support retention and quality (specialisation, leadership without leaving frontline,
  protected development time).

### Work linkage (binding)
- Work conditions and career architecture must be consistent with Work CH02 rails (contract discipline, time/roster governance, and
  anti-extraction in labour intermediation), since staffing pipelines fail if employment terms create churn.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish pipeline metrics: intake, completion, placement slots, vacancy coverage, and regional distribution (aggregated).

<a id="b05ch09p05s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P05.S03 — Retention: rosters, workload, autonomy, professional standards as quality conditions

Retention is a system output. Under OBA, retention conditions are treated as quality conditions, not as HR preferences.

### Retention conditions (binding)
- **Rosters and workload**: schedule stability, bounded overtime, protected rest, and workload limits; avoid chronic understaffing
  compensated by heroics.
- **Administrative load**: treat paperwork and reporting burdens as explicit targets for reduction (see anti-toll P07); admin that
  displaces service time is capacity loss.
- **Autonomy and standards**: preserve professional autonomy within standards; prohibit coercive productivity regimes that degrade
  dignity and safety/learning.
- **Continuity incentives**: staffing models must reward continuity of relationships (care continuity, teaching continuity) rather than
  pure throughput.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- High churn, chronic agency dependence, or sustained overtime indicates systemic breach; it triggers capacity interventions rather
  than blaming individual staff.

<a id="b05ch09p05s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P05.S04 — Fail-closed: persistent understaffing/backlog = capacity breach → status downgrade and emergency plan

Under OBA, persistent understaffing is treated as a structural capacity breach with mandatory escalation.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- Staffing minima breached persistently; vacancy and turnover remain high; burnout proxies worsen; wait-time tails and backlogs grow.
- Quality incidents rise (safety events, learning disruption) correlated with staffing collapse.
- “Temporary” staffing measures become permanent without pipeline expansion.

### Mandatory responses (binding)
- **Status downgrade**: restrict service scope to what can be delivered safely and auditably; suspend non-essential programmes until
  capacity is restored (fail-closed against unsafe coverage claims).
- **Emergency capacity plan**: deploy surge staffing, regional mutual aid, temporary facilities, and accelerated placement capacity
  expansion under audited standards.
- **Structural remediation**: redesign rosters, reduce admin toll, adjust funding incentives, and expand training pipeline capacity.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Capacity breach events, downgrades, and emergency plans must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to
  prevent quiet normalisation of unsafe understaffing.
