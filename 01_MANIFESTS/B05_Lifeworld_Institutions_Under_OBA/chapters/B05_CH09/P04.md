# B05.CH09.P04 — Quality logic: outcomes without reduction to a single KPI

This paragraph specifies how quality must be defined and governed in healthcare and education under OBA without collapsing into a
single KPI. In essentials domains, simplistic metric regimes predictably produce Goodhart drift: providers optimise what is measured,
shift harms into tails, and expand administrative burdens that reduce actual care/learning capacity. OBA therefore defines
multi-dimensional quality (safety, effectiveness, continuity, dignity, accessibility), requires triangulation across outcomes, process
indicators, and experience signals, and mandates anti-Goodhart constraints: it is forbidden to improve one metric by worsening wait
times, tails, or agency. To keep quality claims contestable, OBA requires transparency for every published quality statistic: method_id,
definitions, uncertainty bands, and explicit revision policies.

---

<a id="b05ch09p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P04.S01 — Quality dimensions: safety, effectiveness, continuity, dignity, accessibility

Quality is defined as a vector, not a scalar. Institutions must be assessed on multiple dimensions simultaneously.

### Core quality dimensions (binding)
- **Safety**: preventable harm, adverse events, safeguarding failures, and risk control effectiveness.
- **Effectiveness**: outcome improvement relative to baseline and need (clinical outcomes; learning outcomes), adjusted for severity and
  context where necessary.
- **Continuity**: coherence of care/learning pathways over time; handover integrity; avoidance of fragmentation and repeated
  re-documentation.
- **Dignity**: respectful treatment, informed consent/agency, non-coercive interactions, and protection against humiliation and
  intimidation.
- **Accessibility**: reachability and usability of services (geographic, temporal, disability access), and removal of administrative
  barriers.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- A regime that improves one dimension while degrading another (especially accessibility and continuity) is treated as non-conforming
  drift unless explicitly justified and remediated.

<a id="b05ch09p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P04.S02 — Triangulation: mix of outcomes, process indicators, and experience signals (procedurally testable)

OBA requires triangulation so quality is robust to gaming and measurement noise.

### Triangulation components (binding)
- **Outcome indicators**: core outcome proxies relevant to the domain (health stabilisation, preventable admissions, functional status;
  learning progression, completion, foundational skill acquisition), with risk adjustment where applicable.
- **Process indicators**: pathway integrity and capacity signals (wait times, continuity measures, staffing ratios, supervision
  coverage, maintenance backlog, downtime incidents).
- **Experience signals**: structured user experience reporting (patients/parents/students) focused on procedural dimensions (respect,
  clarity, responsiveness), designed to be testable and not purely narrative.

### Procedural testability (binding)
- Each indicator must have a method definition that specifies inputs, computation, sampling, and limits; indicators must be auditable
  and reproducible.
- Distributions and tails must be reported where relevant (median/p90/p95), not just averages.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Public reporting must present a balanced bundle of indicators; “single-score” rankings are non-conforming unless explicitly shown as
  a derived view with full component disclosure.

<a id="b05ch09p04s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P04.S03 — Anti-Goodhart: forbidden to optimise one metric at the expense of tails/wait times/agency

OBA treats Goodhart drift as a primary failure mode. Optimisation must be constrained by hard rules.

### Forbidden optimisation patterns (binding)
- Improving reported outcomes by excluding hard cases (selection/cherry-picking), reclassifying pathways, or shifting delays into
  pre-referral bureaucracy.
- Reducing costs by cutting maintenance or staffing, causing backlog growth, downtime, and safety/learning harms.
- Improving throughput by degrading dignity and agency (coercive triage, rushed interactions, punitive enforcement for non-compliance).
- Hitting a target mean by allowing tails to worsen (p90/p95 wait times, adverse events, dropout/relapse tails).

### Anti-Goodhart constraints (binding)
- Any performance regime must include tail protections: maximum wait times, minimum staffing/supervision minima, and explicit
  safeguards for vulnerable cohorts.
- If an improvement in one KPI correlates with worsening tails or agency signals, the KPI is treated as unreliable and triggers
  indicator revision and governance review (fail-closed against metric gaming).

## B05.CH09.P04.S04 — Transparency: method_id, definitions, uncertainty bands, and revision policy for all public quality claims

Quality claims must be contestable. OBA therefore requires explicit method disclosure and revision discipline.

### Transparency minima (binding)
- **method_id**: every published indicator must reference a stable method identifier linked to a public method note.
- **Definitions**: define numerator/denominator, inclusion/exclusion, cohort segmentation, and event definitions.
- **Uncertainty bands**: publish uncertainty or confidence bounds where sampling or model-based estimation is used; avoid false
  precision.
- **Revision policy**: define how indicators are revised when errors are found or definitions change; publish restatements with diffs.

### Audit pack requirement (binding)
- For sampled institutions and contested claims, provide audit packs: raw aggregation logic, sampling frames, risk-adjustment methods,
  and data quality checks (privacy-minimal; authorised auditor access).

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If an indicator cannot be reproduced or its method is not disclosed, it is non-conforming and must not be used for funding,
  enforcement, or public ranking until corrected (fail-closed against opaque quality governance).
