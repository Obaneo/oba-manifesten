# B05.CH09.P06 — Maintenance logic: buildings, equipment, IT, and processes

This paragraph specifies maintenance logic for healthcare and education under OBA: buildings, equipment, IT systems, and operational
processes are treated as essential infrastructure, governed with maintenance-first discipline. In these domains, “maintenance cuts”
are a common form of washing: apparent short-term savings produce negative ΔG over time via growing backlogs, downtime, safety risks,
and reduced service capacity. Maintenance failure also amplifies L by increasing waiting, travel, administrative friction, and
avoidable harm. OBA therefore requires auditable maintenance backlogs with thresholds and SLAs, incident logging as early warning,
continuity and supply resilience planning, and process maintenance that reduces administrative load without degrading care/learning.
Where maintenance is cut to meet targets, OBA applies hard stops.

---

<a id="b05ch09p06s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P06.S01 — Maintenance backlog: registration, thresholds, SLAs, incident logs (early warning for negative ΔG)

Maintenance must be measurable, prioritised, and enforced. Backlog growth is treated as a leading indicator of capacity collapse.

### Backlog registration (binding)
- Maintain a standardised maintenance backlog register for:
  - **Buildings** (safety, accessibility, HVAC, moisture control),
  - **Equipment** (clinical devices, lab equipment, classroom/assistive devices),
  - **IT systems** (EHR/LMS uptime, security patches, integration interfaces).
- Each backlog item must have: severity grade, safety/continuity impact, date opened, target SLA, and responsible owner.

### Thresholds and SLAs (binding)
- Define repair SLAs by severity (emergency/urgent/routine) and publish them as parameters.
- Define backlog thresholds (size/age/severity) that trigger automatic escalation: capacity surge, procurement acceleration, temporary
  service re-routing, and governance review.

### Incident logs (binding)
- Log incidents and near-misses tied to maintenance failures (equipment downtime, safety incidents, IT outages, classroom closures).
- Persistent incidents trigger root-cause remediation rather than repeated temporary fixes.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated maintenance performance: backlog size/age distributions (median/p90/p95), SLA compliance, downtime incidents, and
  repeat-fault rates (privacy-safe aggregation).

## B05.CH09.P06.S02 — Continuity: redundancy, crisis plans, supply resilience (medication, assistive devices, teaching materials)

Continuity is a quality dimension and a lifeworld safeguard. Essentials systems must plan for shocks and shortages.

### Continuity minima (binding)
- **Redundancy**: define critical single points of failure (key equipment, IT systems, transport links) and maintain redundancy or
  fallback pathways.
- **Crisis plans**: documented plans for incidents (outbreaks, cyber attacks, building failures, staff shortages) with triggers,
  roles, and service prioritisation.
- **Supply resilience**: maintain resilience plans and buffer strategies for critical supplies:
  - healthcare: medications, consumables, assistive devices, oxygen and related essentials,
  - education: teaching materials, assistive tech, meals provision where applicable.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated resilience indicators: stockout events, downtime durations, recovery times, and crisis activation frequencies
  (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch09p06s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P06.S03 — Process maintenance: lean without care/learning degradation; admin reduction as an explicit goal

Process design is infrastructure. Poor processes create bureaucratic tolls (E) and reduce real service capacity.

### Lean-with-guardrails (binding)
- Process improvement is admissible only if it does not degrade safety, dignity, learning quality, or continuity.
- Changes must be tested with tail protections: do not shift burdens into complex cases, vulnerable cohorts, or after-hours access.

### Admin reduction as explicit goal (binding)
- Treat administrative time burden as a measurable target for reduction:
  - reduce duplicate documentation,
  - standardise forms and data structures,
  - eliminate reporting that does not feed back into safety/quality or governance.
- Admin tasks that primarily exist to satisfy intermediaries or billing layers are requalified as E and must be redesigned (link to P07).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish (in aggregate) admin-time burden proxies and process-cycle times for major pathways (triage, referrals, admissions), including
  p90/p95 tails.

<a id="b05ch09p06s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P06.S04 — Hard stop: cutting maintenance to hit short-term targets (washing)

OBA treats maintenance cuts as a primary washing failure mode: they create apparent efficiency while embedding future harm.

### Hard stop triggers (binding)
- Maintenance budgets reduced while backlogs, downtime, or safety incidents rise.
- Deferred maintenance reclassified or hidden; backlog registers incomplete or manipulated.
- IT security patching deferred leading to incidents; equipment kept in service beyond safe lifecycle.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Trigger governance intervention: ring-fence maintenance budgets, mandate backlog reduction plans, and restrict scope to what can be
  delivered safely until maintenance returns to thresholds.
- Where maintenance neglect is persistent, apply status downgrade and management remedies (replacement, external operator assignment,
  procurement reset).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Maintenance hard-stop events and remediation plans must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe aggregation) to
  prevent quiet normalisation of decay.
