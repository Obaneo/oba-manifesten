# B05.CH09.P09 — Data, interoperability, and digital rails (without surveillance rent)

This paragraph specifies data and digital rails for healthcare and education under OBA. Digital systems (EHR/LMS, scheduling, billing,
triage/placement tools) can either increase continuity and reduce administrative L, or they can become toll infrastructure through
vendor lock-in, paywalled interoperability, and surveillance rent. Because these systems govern access pathways and record life
history, they are also high-stakes surfaces for exclusion and coercion. OBA therefore requires interoperability as a procurement
invariant (open standards and portability), privacy minimalism (purpose limitation and join safety), auditability for algorithms used
in triage or education decisions, and strict incident discipline for leaks, drift, and downtime. Black-box decisioning and
non-portable proprietary stacks are treated as non-conforming until corrected.

---

<a id="b05ch09p09s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P09.S01 — Interoperability: open standards, portability, vendor lock-in prohibited (procurement rails)

Interoperability is treated as a commons condition: without it, continuity breaks and vendors acquire block power.

### Open standards (binding)
- Require open, documented interfaces and data formats for core records, scheduling, messaging, billing, and reporting.
- Prohibit proprietary formats or APIs that prevent independent integration and audit.

### Portability (binding)
- Users and institutions must be able to export and transfer records in standard formats without paywalls, punitive fees, or degraded
  fidelity.
- Switching providers (institutions or vendors) must not require re-documentation of entire histories where data exists; continuity is
  a quality dimension.

### Vendor lock-in prohibition (binding)
- Prohibit exclusive maintenance rights, closed integration ecosystems, and long-term contracts that prevent contestability.
- Require escrow/continuity arrangements for critical software and operational data to prevent hostage dynamics.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Procurement awards must include an interoperability audit pack: standards compliance, migration plan, exit costs, and lifecycle
  maintenance obligations.

<a id="b05ch09p09s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P09.S02 — Privacy minimalism: minimum data, purpose limitation, tiered access, join safety

Because these domains handle sensitive personal data, OBA treats privacy as a constitutive rail, not an optional ethics layer.

### Minimum-data principle (binding)
- Collect only data necessary for care/learning delivery, safety, and audit; prohibit collection “just in case” or for commercial
  optimisation.
- Where possible, store derived indicators rather than raw behavioural traces.

### Purpose limitation (binding)
- Data may be used only for defined purposes: delivery, safety, continuity, and governance audit. Marketing, profiling, and behavioural
  steering are prohibited.
- Secondary use requires explicit legal/rail justification and must remain privacy-minimal.

### Tiered access (binding)
- Access to sensitive data is role-based and least-privilege; audit access is logged and reviewable.
- Users (patients/parents/students) have rights to view and correct records, and to see who accessed them (within lawful bounds).

### Join safety (binding)
- Prohibit joins that create surveillance profiles across domains (health ↔ education ↔ welfare ↔ policing) unless explicitly required
  for narrowly defined safety cases and governed under strict audit and minimisation rules.
- Public reporting must be aggregate and suppression-protected (see P12 measurement discipline).

<a id="b05ch09p09s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P09.S03 — Algorithms in triage/education: auditability, bias tests, explainability; black-box = non-conform

Algorithms used for triage, prioritisation, placement, or educational decisions wield block power. Under OBA, they are audit-critical.

### Auditability requirements (binding)
- Maintain model documentation: purpose, inputs, training data provenance (where applicable), update schedule, and performance metrics.
- Decisions must be reproducible: given the recorded inputs and model version, the output must be recomputable.

### Bias and fairness tests (binding)
- Require periodic disparate-impact testing across relevant cohorts; publish aggregate results and remediation actions.
- Prohibit proxies that encode discrimination or socioeconomic exclusion where not directly justified by outcome necessity.

### Explainability and reason codes (binding)
- For any adverse decision (denial, deferral, lower priority, placement rejection), provide human-readable reason codes and the rule or
  model factors involved, with a path to contestation.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Black-box models without audit access, reproducibility, and contestability are non-conforming and must not be used in baseline access
  pathways (fail-closed against algorithmic gatekeeping).

<a id="b05ch09p09s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P09.S04 — Incident discipline: data leaks, model drift, downtime → mandatory public incident reports (aggregated)

Digital failure is service failure in essentials domains. Incidents must be logged and made contestable.

### Incident logging (binding)
- Log and classify incidents: data leaks, unauthorised access, downtime, integrity errors, interoperability failures, and model drift.
- Maintain time-bounded response SLAs: containment, notification, remediation, and service restoration.

### Public incident reporting (binding)
- Publish aggregated incident statistics: incident volumes by class, severity distribution, downtime durations, recovery times, and
  corrective actions taken (privacy-safe).
- Significant incidents require a public post-mortem summary and a remediation plan with deadlines.

### Conformance and hard stop (binding)
- Persistent downtime, repeated leaks, concealment of incidents, or unresolved model drift triggers procurement and governance
  intervention: contract remedies, vendor replacement, and system redesign.
- Evidence of surveillance rent (data monetisation, profiling, third-party resale) triggers immediate conformance suspension for the
  digital system (fail-closed against digital capture).
