# B05.CH09.P11 — Lifeworld impact: how healthcare/education lands directly in R/B and agency

This paragraph specifies how healthcare and education translate into lifeworld outcomes under OBA: directly into L, R/B tails, and
agency. These domains are not “social policy add-ons”; they are core determinants of household stability. When access is delayed,
opaque, or expensive, households pay in three ways: direct costs, indirect time costs (waiting/travel), and administrative friction.
That composite burden is L. Health shocks and learning loss are primary tail drivers that can collapse R/B and lock households into
long-term disadvantage. OBA therefore treats baseline access and continuity as stabilisers that dampen tails, and treats education as
exit power into the labour market—provided it does not impose debt clamp (link to CH06). The paragraph also defines a key failure
pattern: “free but unreachable” systems where nominal coverage exists but effective L rises through gatekeeping and wait-time
normalisation.

---

<a id="b05ch09p11s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P11.S01 — L channel: direct costs + indirect time costs + administrative friction (hidden L)

Healthcare/education burden is not just prices. Under OBA, L includes cost, time, and friction.

### L components (binding)
- **Direct costs**: user charges, co-payments where applicable, transport costs to access services, mandatory materials, and any fees
  required to complete the pathway.
- **Indirect time costs**: waiting time (lead-times), time spent travelling, time off work/care duties, and repeated appointment or
  meeting cycles induced by poor continuity.
- **Administrative friction**: documentation burdens, repeated data entry, referral loops, eligibility proofs, and disputes required to
  correct errors.

### Measurement implication (binding)
- Systems must measure and publish proxies for time and friction burdens (wait distributions, admin-time indicators, repeat-visit
  cycles) so hidden L is contestable.

<a id="b05ch09p11s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P11.S02 — R/B tails: health shocks and learning loss as primary tail drivers; buffers and access dampen this

Health and learning shocks are classic tail events: they can permanently change income, costs, and participation capacity.

### Tail mechanisms (binding)
- **Health shocks**: untreated or delayed care increases severity, causes work absence, raises ongoing costs, and increases dependency.
- **Learning loss**: early deficits compound, reduce labour market options, and increase lifetime instability.
- **Care burdens**: long-term care failures shift unpaid labour to families, reducing income and increasing stress and time poverty.

### Buffer role of access (binding)
- Baseline access and early intervention reduce tail severity and prevent shock amplification.
- Continuity and dignity reduce drop-out and disengagement, improving long-run stability.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- Rising tail indicators (avoidable harm, long-term sickness absence, drop-out spikes, regional outcome divergence) trigger capacity
  and access escalation rather than moralising about “individual responsibility”.

<a id="b05ch09p11s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P11.S03 — Mobility: education as exit power (labour market), provided no debt clamp (link to CH06)

Education is treated as a primary exit rail: it increases agency by widening feasible work and life trajectories.

### Exit power (binding)
- Vocational and adult education pathways must be accessible without coercive debt or lock-in contracts.
- Placement and completion systems must be designed to minimise clamp (bureaucratic gates, exclusion via fees, opaque selection).

### Debt clamp prohibition (binding)
- Where education finance involves debt, it must comply with CH06 rails: transparent all-in costs, bounded terms, restructuring and
  dispute flows, and prohibition of distress extraction.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Education systems must publish accessibility and completion distributions by cohort and region, and track whether finance mechanisms
  increase or reduce clamp signatures.

<a id="b05ch09p11s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P11.S04 — Failure mode: “free but unreachable” (wait times/gatekeepers) → effective L rises

A key failure pattern is nominal universality combined with practical inaccessibility.

### Failure signals (binding)
- Coverage exists on paper, but wait times exceed rails; gatekeeping and referral loops ration access; admin burdens select for
  persistence and navigation skill.
- Denial/deferral churn rises without transparent reason codes; second-opinion and dispute paths are slow or ineffective.
- Users shift to private alternatives or debt-financed substitutes, increasing effective L and reinforcing inequality.

### Hard stop implication (binding)
- If effective L rises due to waiting and gatekeeping, the system is non-conforming even if direct fees are low. It must trigger
  automatic escalation: capacity surge, pathway simplification, and governance intervention (fail-closed against unreachable
  essentials).
