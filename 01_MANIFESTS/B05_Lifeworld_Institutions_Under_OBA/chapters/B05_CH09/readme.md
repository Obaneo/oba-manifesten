# B05.CH09 — Healthcare and education: access, quality, staffing, maintenance logic, and anti-toll design

This chapter specifies **healthcare and education** as essentials institutions under OBA: domains where access failures, scarcity, and
bureaucratic tolls translate directly into L escalation, R/B tail events, and agency loss. Under OBA, these systems must operate as
commons-like capacity environments (ΔG/maintenance) with baseline access guarantees, wait-time rails, and quality logic that is
multi-dimensional and anti-Goodhart. Because both domains are vulnerable to fee-stacks (billing/claims industries, management layers,
compliance consulting) and digital capture (proprietary EHR/LMS stacks, data lock-in, paywalls for interoperability, opaque
algorithms), OBA treats interoperability, procurement rails, auditability, and privacy minimalism as constitutive safeguards.

CH09 is designed to be **anti-washing** and **fail-closed**. Opaque triage, non-auditable waitlist management, normalised long waits,
fee-stacks hidden in “administration”, and black-box decisioning are treated as **non-conforming** until corrected. The chapter
therefore combines diagnostics, baseline packages and escalation mechanisms, quality triangulation, staffing and training pipelines,
maintenance discipline (buildings/equipment/IT/processes), anti-toll design, funding architectures that avoid selection and
cherry-picking, digital rails without surveillance rent, local governance with audit-of-the-auditor, and publication requirements to
keep these essentials domains contestable and non-captured.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing healthcare or education policy**: use CH09 to define baseline access packages, enforce wait-time rails with
  automatic escalation, and prevent “free but unreachable” systems where effective L rises through delays and bureaucracy.
- If you are **designing quality regimes**: use CH09 to avoid single-KPI optimisation; apply triangulated quality logic (outcomes,
  process indicators, and experience signals) with method transparency and revision policies.
- If you are **designing workforce capacity**: use CH09 to treat staffing as infrastructure: minima, skill mix, supervisor coverage,
  burn-out proxies, training pipeline capacity, and retention as quality conditions (linked to Work CH02).
- If you are **reducing administrative extraction**: use CH09’s anti-toll rules to identify billing/claims complexity, management
  layers, and paperwork burdens as E; redesign and standardise rather than expanding compliance industries.
- If you are **building digital systems**: use CH09 to enforce open standards and portability, prohibit vendor lock-in and surveillance
  rent, and require auditability for algorithms used in triage or education pathways.
- If you are **auditing lifeworld impact**: use P11 and P12 to track hidden L (time costs, admin friction), R/B tail exposure from
  health shocks and learning loss, denial patterns, wait times, staffing and maintenance backlogs, downtime, and fee-share signals.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA-compatible design of healthcare systems, childcare/education/vocational pathways, and the
institutions and supply chains that deliver them. In particular, baseline access and wait-time rails, anti-discrimination in triage
and allocation, staffing and training capacity, maintenance-first discipline, anti-toll elimination of fee-stacks and administrative
extraction, open-standards interoperability, auditability of decision rules (including algorithms), and publication requirements are
treated as **constitutive** safeguards against clamp and capture.

If CH09 constraints are weakened—by tolerated long waits without escalation, selection/cherry-picking incentives, growth of billing and
consulting layers, opaque triage rules, vendor-locked digital stacks, or maintenance and staffing neglect—the lifeworld layer will
register predictable failure signals: worsening wait-time tails, rising denial/deferral, increased professional burn-out, declining
continuity and dignity, and rising hidden L through administrative friction. In that case, the implementation must be treated as
drifting toward incompatibility and must trigger redesign, scope restriction to auditable coverage, governance and procurement resets,
and (where necessary) hard stops on non-auditable decision systems.
