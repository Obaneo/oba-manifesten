# B05.CH09.P07 — Anti-toll design: eliminating fee-stacks and bureaucratic extraction

This paragraph specifies anti-toll design for healthcare and education under OBA: how to eliminate fee-stacks and bureaucratic
extraction (E) that grow around essentials institutions. In these domains, complexity itself often becomes the business model:
billing/claims industries, management layers, and consulting ecosystems expand, while frontline capacity and continuity decline.
Bureaucratic tolls also shift labour onto professionals and users, increasing hidden L through time costs and reducing effective
service capacity. OBA therefore requires billing and funding simplification, prohibits mandatory intermediary layers that add no
measurable access or outcome value, treats paperwork burdens as requalifiable E when they function as tolls, and defines enforcement
mechanisms to detect and remediate extraction patterns.

---

<a id="b05ch09p07s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P07.S01 — Billing/claims simplification: standardisation, bundling where appropriate, transparent tariffs

Where billing exists, it must be simple, comparable, and auditable. Complexity is treated as an extraction surface.

### Simplification requirements (binding)
- **Standardisation**: use standard claim formats, code sets, and minimal required fields; avoid local variants that force consulting
  industries.
- **Bundling where appropriate**: for common pathways, bundle payments to reduce per-item billing incentives and documentation load,
  provided bundling does not create selection incentives (risk adjustment must apply).
- **Transparent tariffs**: publish tariff build-ups and allowed cost components; prevent hidden surcharges and “administration” fees.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Billing systems must be able to generate reproducible calculation packs for any charge: tariff parameters, inputs, adjustments, and
  rule references.

### Prohibited drift (binding)
- Payment models that maximise documentation complexity as a revenue strategy are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch09p07s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P07.S02 — Prohibited layers: mandatory intermediaries that add nothing to outcomes or access

Mandatory intermediaries are treated as toll positions unless they demonstrably improve access or outcomes.

### Prohibited intermediary patterns (binding)
- **Brokerage layers**: agents or platforms required to access services, where their primary output is navigation friction and fee
  extraction.
- **Management proliferation**: layers whose primary output is reporting volume, compliance paperwork, and internal steering rather
  than measurable access/quality improvements.
- **Claims/billing “middlemen”** that exist primarily because of avoidable complexity and denial cycles.
- **Compliance consulting dependencies** where institutions must purchase consulting services to interpret proprietary rules or
  constantly shifting formats.

### Burden-of-proof rule (binding)
- Intermediaries bear the burden of proof: they must show measurable impact on access (wait times, coverage) or quality outcomes. If
  not, their revenues are requalified as E and the layer must be removed or made optional.

<a id="b05ch09p07s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P07.S03 — Paperwork as E: admin burden that is mainly offloaded onto professionals → redesign/requalification

Administrative load is a capacity destroyer. Under OBA, paperwork is treated as a possible extraction channel.

### Paperwork-as-E criteria (binding)
- Admin tasks are treated as E when they:
  - exist primarily to satisfy intermediaries (billing/claims, compliance vendors) rather than safety or learning integrity,
  - are duplicated across systems due to lack of interoperability,
  - shift burden onto frontline professionals, reducing direct care/teaching time,
  - or function as gatekeeping to ration access (bureaucratic clamp).

### Redesign requirements (binding)
- Measure and publish admin-time burden proxies (documentation time share, form counts, repeated data entry events).
- Standardise and automate data capture only where it reduces total burden and does not create surveillance rent.
- Eliminate redundant reporting; adopt “collect once, use for multiple governance purposes” with strict purpose limitation.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- Where paperwork burdens are persistent and not tied to measurable safety/quality gains, the associated spend is requalified as E and
  triggers mandatory simplification and procurement intervention.

<a id="b05ch09p07s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P07.S04 — Enforcement: anomaly detection (fee shares, consulting growth, denial patterns) → audit → remedies

OBA requires an escalation ladder so anti-toll signals lead to structural correction.

### Step 1: anomaly detection (binding)
Monitor and flag:
- **Fee shares**: administrative spend share, billing/claims spend, and management overhead as share of total funding.
- **Consulting growth**: consulting and vendor-services spend growth rates, especially where correlated with unchanged or worse access.
- **Denial patterns**: denial/deferral rates and “missing documentation” churn that suggests bureaucratic rationing.
- **Time burden signals**: staff-reported admin load and reduced contact time proxies.

### Step 2: audit pack request (binding)
Require a standard audit pack: billing rules, tariff build-ups, denial reason-code distributions, vendor contracts, consulting scopes,
and workflow maps showing admin burden drivers.

### Step 3: remedies (binding)
- **Simplification mandates**: reduce code sets, bundle payments, standardise forms, remove duplicative reporting.
- **Fee caps / spend caps**: cap administrative overhead categories; require justification for exceptions with sunsets.
- **Contract remedies**: terminate or rewrite vendor and consulting contracts that embed lock-in or toll economics.
- **Structural remedies**: remove mandatory intermediaries; reassign functions to commons/public operators where necessary.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- Persistent fee-stack growth, persistent denial churn, or refusal to disclose contracts and workflows triggers conformance suspension
  for the affected pathway and mandatory governance intervention (fail-closed against bureaucratic toll capture).
