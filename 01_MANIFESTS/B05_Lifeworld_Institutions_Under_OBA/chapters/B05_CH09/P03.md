# B05.CH09.P03 — Baseline access: rights, coverage, and wait-time rails

This paragraph specifies baseline access requirements for healthcare and education under OBA: rights, coverage, and wait-time rails
that prevent “free but unreachable” systems. Baseline access is a constitutive safeguard in an essentials domain: if households face
unbounded waits, opaque denials, or administrative clamp, effective L rises through time costs and foregone outcomes, and R/B tails
worsen through untreated health shocks and learning loss. OBA therefore requires (i) a defined baseline package per domain (minimum
services tied to health/learning outcomes), (ii) wait-time rails with maximum lead-times per pathway and automatic escalation on
breach, (iii) non-discrimination through auditable triage/allocation rules and prohibition of exclusion via price or bureaucracy, and
(iv) fast dispute flows with reproducible decision rules and a second-opinion rail. Thresholds are parameterised, but the rails
(measurability, contestability, and fail-closed discipline) are binding.

---

<a id="b05ch09p03s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P03.S01 — Baseline package: define minimum services per domain (parameters) tied to health/learning outcomes

Baseline access requires an explicit package: what is guaranteed, for whom, and on what outcome rationale.

### Baseline definition (parameters; binding requirement to set and publish)
- Jurisdictions must define a minimum baseline package for:
  - **Healthcare**: primary care access, urgent care, acute pathways, mental health access, essential medications, and long-term care
    support (domain breakdown as parameters).
  - **Education**: childcare/early years access, compulsory schooling coverage, special needs support, vocational pathways, and adult
    re-skilling access (domain breakdown as parameters).
- The baseline package must be linked to explicit outcome rationales (health maintenance, preventable harm reduction, learning
  foundations, participation capability).

### Coverage rule (binding)
- Baseline services must be accessible without exclusion via inability to pay, debt status, or compulsory add-ons.
- Where co-payments exist by jurisdictional choice, they must be bounded and must not apply to baseline-protected pathways (anti-clamp
  incidence).

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish baseline package definitions, eligibility rules, and coverage indicators as stable references (versioned parameters).

<a id="b05ch09p03s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P03.S02 — Wait-time rails: maximum lead-times per pathway; breach → automatic escalation and remediation

Wait times are treated as a primary L channel and a governance output. OBA forbids normalisation of chronic waiting.

### Wait-time rails (binding)
- For each major pathway (domain and urgency class), set maximum lead-times (parameters required) and publish them.
- Wait times must be measured and published as distributions (median/p90/p95), not just averages.

### Automatic escalation on breach (binding)
- Breach of maximum lead-times triggers mandatory escalation:
  - capacity surge actions (staffing, extended hours, temporary clinics/classes, regional support),
  - pathway redesign (triage simplification, admin reduction),
  - procurement and funding adjustments (shift resources to bottleneck nodes),
  - and where needed, temporary cross-provider purchasing under strict anti-toll and audit rules.

### Remediation discipline (binding)
- Escalation plans must be time-bounded, measurable, and publicly tracked; repeated breach without improvement triggers governance
  intervention (fail-closed against normalised waiting clamp).

<a id="b05ch09p03s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P03.S03 — Non-discrimination: auditable triage/allocation; prohibited exclusion patterns via price or bureaucracy

Allocation and triage are high-risk surfaces for hidden selection and exclusion. Under OBA, they must be contestable.

### Auditable triage/allocation (binding)
- Criteria must be explicit, written, and publicly accessible: priority classes, weighting, eligibility, and documentation
  requirements.
- Each decision must provide a reason code and rule reference; “silent denial” and indefinite limbo are non-conforming.
- Triage tools (including algorithms) must be reproducible and auditable; black-box scoring is non-conform.

### Prohibited exclusion patterns (binding)
- Exclusion via price (mandatory add-ons, paid fast tracks for baseline pathways, coercive insurance tiers).
- Exclusion via bureaucracy (documentation loops, repeated re-submission, discretionary “missing info” churn) when it functions as
  rationing rather than as safety/eligibility verification.
- Proxy discrimination via administrative burdens that disproportionately exclude vulnerable cohorts.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Require disparate-impact audits with published aggregate results and remediation plans; denial/deferral patterns trigger audits and
  pathway redesign.

<a id="b05ch09p03s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P03.S04 — Dispute flow: rapid objection procedure with reproducible decision rules and a second-opinion rail

Disputes must be resolvable quickly enough to prevent bureaucratic persistence from becoming de facto power.

### Rapid objection procedure (binding)
- Strict time bounds for: acknowledgement, evidence provision, provisional relief (where needed), and final decision.
- During disputes, baseline access must be preserved where delay would cause harm (health deterioration, learning loss).

### Reproducible decision rules (binding)
- Decision rules for denial/priority must be reproducible from disclosed inputs; applicants/users must be able to understand and
  contest the basis for decisions.

### Second-opinion rail (binding)
- Provide an independent second-opinion pathway for contested triage/placement decisions, with authority to override where rule
  application was incorrect or unjustified.
- Second-opinion decisions must be logged and sampled for audit to detect systematic bias or gatekeeping drift.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated dispute statistics: volumes, causes (triage, denial, waitlist), resolution times, outcomes, and repeat-offender
  indicators by institution/region (privacy-safe aggregation).
