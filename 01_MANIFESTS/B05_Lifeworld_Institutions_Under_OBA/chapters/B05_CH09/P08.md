# B05.CH09.P08 — Funding architecture: incentives without selection, cherry-picking, or perverse substitution

This paragraph specifies funding architecture for healthcare and education under OBA. Funding is not a neutral accounting device: it
creates incentives that can either expand essentials capacity and quality or generate selection, cherry-picking, and bureaucratic
tolls. OBA therefore binds funding to three primary goals—access, quality, and maintenance-first capacity (ΔG)—and explicitly rejects
volume maximisation regimes that drive Goodhart drift and administrative overhead. Because both domains are susceptible to selection
(avoiding complex patients/students) and perverse substitution (offloading responsibility to families, private markets, or debt
channels), OBA requires risk adjustment, ring-fenced maintenance and capacity budgets, and strict discipline on any ΔM-adjacent role:
public finance may invest in capacity and infrastructure only through allowed channels with traceability and leakage tests.

---

<a id="b05ch09p08s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P08.S01 — Funding goals: access + quality + maintenance, not volume maximisation

Funding regimes must align incentives with the essentials mission rather than with throughput or billing output.

### Funding goals (binding)
- **Access**: reduce wait times and baseline gaps; expand capacity where bottlenecks persist.
- **Quality**: improve safety, effectiveness, continuity, dignity, and accessibility under triangulated measurement (link to P04).
- **Maintenance-first capacity (ΔG)**: protect staffing pipelines, facility upkeep, equipment and IT maintenance, and resilience
  capacity.

### Prohibited goal drift (binding)
- Regimes that primarily reward volume (visits, procedures, test counts, instructional seat-time) without outcome and tail safeguards
  are non-conforming.
- Funding schemes that increase billing complexity and admin burden to “prove activity” are treated as toll-creating.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Funding models must publish a clear incentive map: what is rewarded, what is penalised, and what tail protections constrain gaming.

---

<a id="b05ch09p08s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P08.S02 — Risk adjustment: avoid selection on “easy” patients/students

Selection is a predictable failure mode when funding does not reflect need. OBA requires risk adjustment to prevent exclusion.

### Risk adjustment minima (binding)
- Funding must adjust for relevant need and complexity factors (jurisdictional parameters), so providers are not penalised for serving
  higher-need cohorts.
- Risk adjustment methods must be auditable: method_id, definitions, and revision policy must be published (link to P04 transparency
  discipline).

### Prohibited selection patterns (binding)
- Avoiding complex cases through denial, referral dumping, delayed intake, or “paperwork filters”.
- Incentives that reward institutions for serving low-need cohorts while offloading high-need cohorts to safety nets.

### Enforcement posture (binding)
- Detect selection via denial/deferral patterns, case-mix drift, and disparity signals; confirmed selection triggers funding model
  revision and governance intervention.

<a id="b05ch09p08s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P08.S03 — Budget ring-fencing: maintenance and capacity protected against skimming

Maintenance and capacity are often silently raided to meet short-term targets. OBA prevents this through ring-fencing.

### Ring-fencing requirements (binding)
- Protect budgets for:
  - **Staffing and training pipeline** (minima, placements, upskilling),
  - **Maintenance** (buildings, equipment, IT security and uptime),
  - **Resilience** (redundancy, crisis plans, critical supplies).
- Ring-fenced budgets may not be diverted to discretionary spending, consultant expansion, marketing, or surplus extraction.

### Backlog linkage (binding)
- Where maintenance or staffing backlogs breach thresholds, ring-fenced spending must automatically increase or trigger capacity surge
  measures; “deferral” is treated as negative ΔG (link to P06).

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Publish aggregated accounts showing ring-fenced spend, backlog indicators, and reallocation events (privacy-safe aggregation).

<a id="b05ch09p08s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P08.S04 — ΔM role: capacity/infra investment only via allowed channels with traceability and leakage tests

Public finance and ΔM-adjacent instruments may be used only to expand real capacity and resilience—not to subsidise intermediaries or
create claim support.

### Allowed ΔM roles (binding)
- **Capacity expansion**: facilities, equipment, training capacity, and essential IT upgrades under open standards and procurement
  rails.
- **Maintenance and resilience**: backlog reduction, redundancy investments, and supply resilience where they reduce L and tail risk.
- **Digital interoperability**: migration away from vendor lock-in, open interfaces, and audit-capable systems.

### Forbidden roles (binding)
- Subsidies that primarily raise private margins or valuations without measurable capacity/maintenance gains.
- Funding that locks institutions into proprietary platforms, long-term consulting dependencies, or opaque algorithmic triage systems.
- ΔM use to backstop rentier cashflows (PPP/concession guarantees) without performance accountability.

### Traceability and leakage tests (binding)
- Every ΔM-linked programme must tag flows, provide an audit pack, and run leakage tests (intermediary capture, vendor lock-in,
  bureaucracy growth). Under leakage, conservatism applies: tighten conditions, cap amounts, or suspend (fail-closed).
