# B05.CH09.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of healthcare & education under OBA

This paragraph specifies the purpose, scope, and normative status of healthcare and education under OBA. These domains are treated as
essentials institutions: access failures and quality collapse translate directly into L escalation (direct costs, time costs,
bureaucratic friction), R/B tail events (health shocks, learning loss), and agency loss. They are also high-risk surfaces for
extraction (E) via fee-stacks (billing/claims industries, management layers, consulting) and for capture via opaque triage/allocation,
vendor lock-in, and underinvestment masked as “efficiency”. OBA therefore treats healthcare/education governance as constitutive: it
must deliver baseline access and high quality with maintenance-first capacity (ΔG), minimal clamp/lock-in, and explicit anti-toll
design.

---

<a id="b05ch09p01s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P01.S01 — Purpose: baseline access and high quality as essentials, with L reduction and minimal clamp/lock-in

Under OBA, the purpose of healthcare and education policy is to guarantee baseline access and high quality while reducing lifeworld
burdens (L) and preventing clamp dynamics.

### Purpose claims (binding)
- **Baseline access**: ensure access to essential health and learning services without exclusion via price, debt status, or hidden
  bureaucratic gates.
- **High quality as an essentials condition**: treat safety, effectiveness, continuity, dignity, and accessibility as constitutive
  outputs, not as optional “service levels”.
- **L reduction**: reduce direct cost exposure, indirect time costs (waiting), and administrative friction; prevent “free but
  unreachable” systems where effective L rises through delays and gatekeeping.
- **Minimal clamp/lock-in**: ensure portability and second-opinion/alternative pathway options; prevent captive networks and opaque
  referral/placement systems from becoming coercive constraints.

### Prohibited purpose drift (binding)
- Programmes whose functional objective is to preserve intermediaries, billing complexity, or vendor platforms—rather than access and
  quality—are non-conforming.

<a id="b05ch09p01s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P01.S02 — Scope: primary/acute/long-term care; childcare/education/vocational education; institutions and chains

CH09 covers the full delivery stack for healthcare and education, including the institutions, chains, and intermediaries that can
create tolls or lock-in.

### Included scope (binding)
- **Healthcare**: primary care, acute/hospital care, mental health, long-term care, rehabilitation, pharmaceuticals and assistive
  devices (continuity and supply resilience), and care coordination pathways.
- **Education**: childcare/early years, primary and secondary education, special needs support, vocational education and training,
  adult re-skilling pathways, and access-to-education infrastructure.
- **Institutions and chains**: public providers, non-profit providers, private providers where applicable, insurer/commissioner
  networks, platform intermediaries, staffing agencies, and subcontracting chains.
- **Allocation and triage**: referral systems, waitlists, placement mechanisms, admissions, and prioritisation logic.
- **Digital infrastructure**: EHR/LMS systems, scheduling, billing/claims systems, and algorithmic decision support used in triage or
  placement.

### Explicit exclusions (non-binding guidance)
- CH09 does not prescribe specific clinical curricula or pedagogical philosophies except where they affect auditability, accessibility,
  and measurable quality and continuity outcomes.

<a id="b05ch09p01s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P01.S03 — Binding: commons standard (access/maintenance/ΔG), R/B standard (tails), P/E/C (anti-toll), procurement rails

Healthcare and education governance under OBA is bound to canonical rails; claims that ignore these rails are non-conforming.

### Binding rails (binding)
- **Commons standard (access/maintenance/ΔG)**: treat core capacity (staffing, facilities, training pipelines, essential IT) as commons
  infrastructure with maintenance-first budgeting, backlog thresholds, and incident discipline.
- **R/B standard (tails)**: treat health shocks and learning loss as primary tail drivers; systems must dampen tails through baseline
  access, early intervention, and continuity.
- **P/E/C (anti-toll)**: classify revenue and activity by function: productive care/learning capacity (P), extraction via intermediaries
  and bureaucracy (E), and commons flows (C). Fee-stacks and mandatory intermediaries that add no outcome value are requalified as E.
- **Procurement rails**: open standards, interoperability, vendor lock-in prevention, performance-based contracting, transparent
  change-order regimes, and audit-of-the-auditor on major procurements.

### Conformance requirement (binding)
- Major policy changes, procurement awards, and triage/allocation rule updates must provide audit packs linking: mechanism → expected
  access/quality outcomes → L/RB incidence → lock-in risk controls → rollback/sunset discipline.

<a id="b05ch09p01s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: opaque triage, fee-stacks, or wait-time normalisation without audit is non-conform

Because healthcare and education are high-stakes essentials, OBA applies fail-closed discipline when contestability is lost.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- **Opaque triage/allocation**: decision rules cannot be reproduced or explained; black-box scoring without audit access; denial without
  reason codes and appeal.
- **Fee-stacks**: growth of billing/claims complexity, management layers, or consulting spend that is not tied to measurable access or
  quality improvements.
- **Wait-time normalisation**: chronic lead-time breaches treated as “structural reality” without automatic escalation, capacity
  remediation, and published evidence.
- **Vendor lock-in**: proprietary EHR/LMS systems that prevent interoperability, portability, and independent audits.

### Fail-closed responses (binding)
- Suspend or restrict the affected pathway/system until decision rules are made auditable, fee components are decomposed and capped,
  and wait-time breaches trigger remediation.
- Where the channel is structurally prone to toll extraction (billing layers) or lock-in (proprietary digital stacks), enforce
  redesign, unbundling, or conversion to stricter commons governance (fail-closed against essentials capture).
