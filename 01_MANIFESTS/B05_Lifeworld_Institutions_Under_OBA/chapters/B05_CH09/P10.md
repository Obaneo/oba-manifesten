# B05.CH09.P10 — Local institutions and governance: contestability and “audit of the auditor”

This paragraph specifies local institutional governance for healthcare and education under OBA. Because these domains are delivered
through local institutions (clinics, hospitals, schools, training centres) with high discretion, governance is a primary anti-capture
surface. Without contestability, wait-time normalisation, selective allocation, vendor lock-in, and fee-stack growth can become the
true operating model while the system remains formally “universal”. OBA therefore defines a role model (mandator, operator,
independent auditor, user councils), requires transparency through decision logs and exceptions registers, imposes anti-capture rules
(revolving-door constraints, vendor dominance checks, rotation), and specifies escalation paths when structural breaches occur. The
design is fail-closed: persistent opacity or uncorrected breaches trigger governance intervention and management remedies.

---

<a id="b05ch09p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P10.S01 — Role model: mandator, operator, independent auditor, user councils (patients/parents/students)

OBA separates roles so that power is contestable and accountability is auditable.

### Role model (binding)
- **Mandator**: sets baseline packages, wait-time rails, quality bundles, staffing/maintenance minima, and funding rules; owns the
  public conformance registers.
- **Operator**: delivers services (healthcare/education), maintains staffing and maintenance discipline, and produces audit packs and
  incident logs.
- **Independent auditor**: audits operator performance, decision rules, procurement, and incident handling; reports publicly in
  aggregate; must itself be contestable.
- **User councils**: patients/parents/students (and frontline professional representation where applicable) provide structured
  experience signals, identify clamp and gatekeeping patterns, and trigger dispute/escalation pathways.

### Separation rule (binding)
- Operators may not audit themselves. Vendors providing core systems may not be the exclusive source of their own assurance.

<a id="b05ch09p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P10.S02 — Transparency: decision logs, waitlist management, procurement logs, exceptions register

Transparency is treated as a rail: without it, governance becomes discretionary and captured.

### Decision logs (binding)
- Log major decisions: triage/allocation rule changes, waitlist prioritisation changes, service scope changes, and pathway redesigns,
  with rationale and rule references.
- Provide public summaries in privacy-safe form and link to method/definition-set references.

### Waitlist management transparency (binding)
- Publish wait-time distributions (median/p90/p95) by pathway and cohort; publish breach events and remediation actions.
- Require auditable waitlist algorithms/rules; no opaque prioritisation.

### Procurement logs (binding)
- Publish procurement awards, supplier ownership look-through summaries, change-order volumes, and performance outcomes for major
  contracts (EHR/LMS, facilities, staffing agencies, suppliers), in aggregate.

### Exceptions register (binding)
- Maintain an exceptions register for rule departures (triage exceptions, funding exceptions, emergency procurement), with reason codes,
  authorisation, and sunset dates.

<a id="b05ch09p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P10.S03 — Anti-capture: revolving-door rules, vendor dominance checks, rotation, independent reviews

OBA treats capture as a predictable failure mode and designs governance to detect and disrupt it.

### Revolving-door constraints (binding)
- Cooling-off periods for senior officials and procurement decision-makers moving to vendors/consultancies; mandatory disclosure of
  prior vendor ties.
- Prohibit vendor-funded advisory roles that steer policy or procurement without disclosure.

### Vendor dominance checks (binding)
- Monitor concentration and dependency in critical systems (EHR/LMS, staffing agencies, billing platforms); high dependency triggers
  diversification and interoperability plans.
- Prohibit proprietary lock-in clauses and exclusive maintenance rights that prevent switching.

### Rotation and independent review (binding)
- Rotate auditors and require peer review for major findings; avoid permanent single-auditor monopolies.
- Require periodic independent reviews of governance bodies themselves (“audit of the auditor”), including procurement integrity and
  waitlist management practices.

### Hard stop (binding)
- Proven concealment, systematic favouritism, or vendor capture triggers governance reset: contract suspensions, leadership rotation,
  and tightened disclosure/procurement rails (fail-closed against institutional capture).

<a id="b05ch09p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P10.S04 — Escalation: structural breaches (wait time/quality) → intervention, restructuring, management remedies

OBA requires escalation ladders so breaches lead to correction rather than normalisation.

### Structural breach triggers (binding)
- Persistent wait-time rail breaches without improvement; chronic backlog growth; repeated denial/deferral churn.
- Quality collapse signals: safety incident spikes, continuity breakdown, dignity/accessibility failures, or staffing/maintenance minima
  breaches.
- Persistent opacity: missing audit packs, undisclosed exceptions, or non-reproducible decision rules.

### Intervention ladder (binding)
- **Targeted remediation**: capacity surge plans, staffing pipeline support, admin simplification, procurement resets, and pathway
  redesign with measurable milestones.
- **Restructuring**: replace management layers, change operator mandates, unbundle captured vendor stacks, and restructure funding
  incentives that drive selection.
- **Management remedies**: leadership replacement, temporary external operator assignment, or transfer of functions to a compliant
  provider network.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If breaches persist across defined review cycles, the institution’s status must be downgraded and scope restricted to what can be
  delivered safely and auditable; continued operation without remediation is non-conforming (fail-closed against chronic breach).
