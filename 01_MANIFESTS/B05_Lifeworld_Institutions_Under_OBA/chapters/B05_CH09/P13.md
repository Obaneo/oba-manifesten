# B05.CH09.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes for healthcare and education under OBA and the hard stops that apply when the
system drifts into selection, gatekeeping, digital capture, and bureaucratic toll ecosystems. Because these are essentials
institutions, failure often appears as “paper universality” combined with practical exclusion: long waits, opaque triage, denial
churn, and administrative burdens that ration access by persistence and navigation skill. Extraction (E) grows through fee-stacks
(billing/claims, management layers, consulting) and through proprietary digital systems that lock institutions into paywalled
interoperability and data extraction. OBA therefore defines explicit non-conformance signals and fail-closed responses. The objective
is to preserve **order compatibility**: baseline access must be reachable, decision rules must be auditable, staffing and maintenance
capacity must be sustainable, and anti-toll constraints must hold.

---

<a id="b05ch09p13s01"></a>
## B05.CH09.P13.S01 — Selection and exclusion: cherry-picking, denial patterns, bureaucratic gates → non-conform

Selection is a predictable failure mode when incentives and gatekeeping allow institutions to avoid complex cases.

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- **Cherry-picking**: case-mix drift toward easier patients/students; dumping of complex cases into safety nets without capacity.
- **Denial/deferral patterns**: elevated denial/deferral rates with opaque or unstable reason codes; “incomplete documentation” churn
  used as rationing.
- **Bureaucratic gates**: repeated referral loops, administrative hurdles, network restrictions, or digital barriers that correlate
  with socioeconomic status rather than with need.
- **Paid fast tracks** for baseline pathways that create a two-tier system by stealth.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat selection patterns as non-conform: trigger audit of denial and triage rules, funding model revision (risk adjustment),
  capacity reallocation, and governance intervention. Persistent selection triggers status downgrade and structural remedies.

<a id="b05ch09p13s02"></a>
## B05.CH09.P13.S02 — Wait-time normalisation: “structurally too long” without escalation → governance breach

Normalising chronic waiting is treated as a governance breach because it converts time into an implicit price (hidden L).

### Non-conformance signals (binding)
- Wait-time rails breached repeatedly without automatic escalation plans and measurable remediation.
- “Structural shortage” narratives used to justify permanent lead-times without capacity pipeline expansion or pathway simplification.
- Breach management focused on reclassification (moving waits off the books) rather than on reducing tails.

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat the pathway as in breach: require capacity surge and pipeline expansion, admin reduction, and reallocation of funding to
  bottlenecks.
- Persistent breach triggers governance reset and scope restriction to auditable safe delivery until rails are met (fail-closed against
  unreachable essentials).

<a id="b05ch09p13s03"></a>
## B05.CH09.P13.S03 — Digital capture: proprietary EHR/LMS lock-in or data extraction → procurement breach and hard stop

Digital capture creates long-term dependence and enables surveillance rent. In essentials institutions, this is treated as a hard stop.

### Capture indicators (binding)
- Proprietary EHR/LMS systems that block portability and interoperability or impose paywalls for integration and export.
- Long-term exclusive contracts with closed interfaces, exclusive maintenance rights, or “no alternative vendor” renewal patterns.
- Data extraction or monetisation beyond order necessity; third-party sharing for marketing/profiling.
- Algorithmic triage/placement systems that cannot be audited or contested (black-box decisioning).

### Hard stop response (binding)
- Treat capture as a procurement breach: suspend further procurement in the affected stack, require open-standards migration plans, and
  impose contract remedies (interface opening, escrow/continuity provisions, staged replacement).
- Proven surveillance rent or black-box gatekeeping triggers immediate conformance suspension of the digital system and mandatory
  redesign (fail-closed against digital toll capture).

---

<a id="b05ch09p13s04"></a>
## B05.CH09.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: if decision rules are unauditable, capacity collapses structurally, or bureaucratic toll grows, redesign is mandatory

OBA treats worsening lifeworld outcomes and loss of contestability as evidence of drift toward incompatibility.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
- **Unauditable decision rules**: triage, allocation, denial, or algorithmic systems cannot be reproduced; no reason codes; no appeal.
- **Structural capacity collapse**: persistent understaffing and maintenance backlog growth; rising downtime; sustained wait-time tails.
- **Bureaucratic toll growth**: rising admin-time burden, consulting share, and billing/claims complexity without access/quality gains.
- **Opacity**: refusal or inability to publish core access/quality/capacity indicators safely and reproducibly.

### Mandatory response (binding)
- Treat current governance and funding regime as non-conforming; initiate redesign with explicit mechanism diagnosis (selection,
  gatekeeping, staffing pipeline, maintenance decay, digital lock-in).
- Restrict scope to auditable safe delivery; suspend non-essential programmes until minima are restored.
- Apply structural remedies: simplify billing/claims, remove intermediaries, reset procurement under open standards, and restructure
  management where toll ecosystems are embedded.

### Publication requirement (binding)
- Fail-closed events, redesign decisions, and structural remedies must be logged in a public conformance register (privacy-safe
  aggregation) to prevent quiet drift and selective enforcement.
