# B05.CH05.P02 — Legitimacy foundation: why T exists (and what it must not become)

This paragraph defines the legitimacy foundation of T (taxes, contributions, premiums, and quasi-taxes) under OBA. In OBA, T is not
just a financing mechanism; it is the explicit **social exchange** that funds commons capacity and security while constraining
extraction. Legitimacy depends on whether people can see—and verify—that contributions translate into access, maintenance, continuity,
and tail stability. When T becomes opaque, regressive, or mediated by private toll gates, it is experienced as arbitrary extraction,
even if it is legally “tax.”

CH05 therefore treats legitimacy as an auditable relation: contribution ↔ outcomes. If that relation cannot be made testable, the
system drifts toward resentment, non-compliance, and sabotage dynamics (CH18), regardless of macro claims.

---

## B05.CH05.P02.S01 — T as exchange for commons: contribution ↔ access/maintenance/continuity (ΔG)

T is legitimate when it is the visible exchange for durable commons and security. The core is not “redistribution rhetoric,” but
**maintained capacity**: people pay into a system that reliably provides essential access and prevents tail collapse.

### Exchange structure (functional)
- **Contribution side (T)**: what cohorts and positions pay, when they pay it, and with what predictability/friction.
- **Delivery side (commons/security)**: what services and protections are provided, at what quality, with what continuity.
- **Maintenance side (ΔG analogue)**: whether the system is improving/maintaining capacity (backlogs down, uptime up, lead times down).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Every major T stream MUST have an explicit destination mapping: which commons/security obligations it funds and which maintenance
  budgets are ring-fenced.
- If a T stream cannot be linked to measurable access/maintenance outputs (or is persistently diverted), legitimacy claims are washing
  and must be restated.

---

## B05.CH05.P02.S02 — T must not become extraction: no private toll via mandatory intermediation

A central illegitimacy driver is when public obligations are financed through structures that create private toll positions.

### Prohibited extraction patterns (binding)
- **Mandatory intermediation**: citizens must pay private intermediaries to access legally mandated rights (brokers, “service fees,”
  paid claims processors, paid compliance gateways).
- **Fee-stacks around payment**: charges that are not functional cost (P) but are layered rent (E), especially in essentials.
- **Complexity rents**: a compliance industry grows because the system is deliberately or negligently complex.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a payment path requires paid intermediaries, the arrangement must be redesigned to a direct/public/commons rail or the
  intermediation must be strictly bounded, exitable, and auditable.
- Under opacity (fees not disclosed; contracts not inspectable), conservative presumptions apply: treat as tolling and requalify as E.

---

## B05.CH05.P02.S03 — Transparency requirement: destination, costs, and effects on R/B must be testable

Legitimacy requires testability. “Trust us” fiscal narratives are disallowed in the lifeworld layer.

### Transparency surface (binding)
- **Destination**: where money goes (commons domains, security instruments, maintenance budgets), including earmarks and reallocations.
- **Costs/overhead**: administrative cost share, compliance burden proxies, vendor dependency indicators.
- **Effects**: measurable impacts on R/B tails, essentials affordability (L), access/lead times, and distributional incidence.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Publish public tables for T composition, destination, overhead, and cohort incidence (join-safe), with method_id and revision policy.
- Any fiscal claim based on a model MUST have an audit pack: definitions, assumptions, data sources, uncertainty, and restatement
  procedure.

---

## B05.CH05.P02.S04 — Social consequence: opaque T fuels resentment and sabotage risk (link CH18)

Opaque or seemingly arbitrary T is not a mere communications problem; it is a structural instability driver. When people cannot see
the exchange, they infer extraction, corruption, or targeted discipline.

### Consequence mechanisms
- **Resentment**: people experience high burdens without visible commons improvement; they interpret the system as unfair or captured.
- **Non-compliance**: tax avoidance, informal work, resistance to enforcement; legitimacy collapse becomes behavioural reality.
- **Political sabotage**: carve-outs, selective enforcement, and budget raids become politically rewarded strategies in adversarial
  environments.

### Conformance implications (binding)
- If transparency and destination mapping cannot be sustained, the system must treat this as a legitimacy breach and trigger
  correction: publish restatements, simplify instruments, reduce friction, and tighten anti-capture enforcement.
- If cohort-level burdens rise while commons delivery is stagnant or declining (ΔG negative), scaling of additional T is blocked until
  lifeworld outcomes improve.
