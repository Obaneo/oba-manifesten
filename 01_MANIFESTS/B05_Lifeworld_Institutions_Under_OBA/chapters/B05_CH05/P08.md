# B05.CH05.P08 — Charges versus taxes: avoiding toll dynamics in essentials

This paragraph specifies how OBA distinguishes (and governs) **charges** versus **taxes** in essentials domains, and why the label is
secondary to function. In practice, many “charges” behave as quasi-taxes: they are unavoidable, regressive, and attached to monopoly
or rights gating. They are also a primary pathway for toll regrowth: instead of paying a visible tax, households face layered “service
fees,” connection fees, admin fees, broker fees, and penalty cascades.

OBA’s lifeworld constraint is simple: in essentials, the primary objective is **L reduction and volatility reduction** without creating
private toll positions. Charges are permitted only if they preserve baseline access, remain transparent and contestable, and do not
spawn fee-stacks or lock-in intermediaries.

---

## B05.CH05.P08.S01 — Essentials rails: charges must not undermine baseline access (L reduction is primary)

Essentials domains (housing-related charges, energy baselines, core digital access, care access rails) have high clamp risk. Pricing
and charging must therefore be governed as a lifeworld constraint, not as “cost recovery realism.”

### Essentials rail (binding)
- Charges must preserve **baseline access**: inability to pay must not convert into exclusion or cascading penalties in essential
  participation.
- Charges must be incidence-checked: fixed charges that hit tails are presumptively regressive and require redesign or offsets.
- L reduction is primary: where charging structures increase L volatility or arrears cascades, they are non-conforming.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any essential charge must define a baseline package (or baseline allowance) that is protected against exclusion, with clear
  non-disconnection/non-eviction rails where applicable.
- If baseline access cannot be preserved under a given charging regime, the regime must be replaced by tax financing or commons
  funding (or a redesigned tariff structure).

---

## B05.CH05.P08.S02 — Prohibited fee-stacks: mandatory intermediaries stacking “service fees” around public rights

Fee-stacks are extraction layers that attach themselves to mandatory payments and public rights. In essentials, this is treated as
E regrowth and must be neutralised.

### Prohibited patterns (binding)
- Mandatory intermediaries (brokers, paid agents, “platform services”) required to access rights or pay charges.
- Layered administrative fees that do not correspond to functional cost components (junk fees).
- Penalty cascades that convert small arrears into compounding fee/interest stacks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Payment and access rails must be direct, low-friction, and exitable; if intermediaries exist, they must be optional and bounded.
- Under opacity (fees not fully disclosed; no audit trail), presume fee-stack extraction and requalify as non-conforming until fixed.

---

## B05.CH05.P08.S03 — Pricing governance: open calculation rules, indexation, objection/appeal, audit of pricing decisions

Charges are legitimate only if their calculation is open, reproducible, and contestable. Closed pricing turns into discretionary toll.

### Required pricing governance (minimum; binding)
- **Open calculation rules**: publish cost components, allowed margins, allocation of fixed costs, and cross-subsidy logic (if any).
- **Indexation rules**: explicit indexation formulae and update cadence; no opaque “adjustments.”
- **Objection/appeal**: fast dispute mechanisms for mispricing, wrongful penalties, and eligibility errors; provisional relief where
  necessary.
- **Audit of pricing decisions**: independent review, publishable summaries, and restatement procedures.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a charge cannot be explained and reproduced from published rules (given the relevant inputs), it is non-conforming.
- Pricing governance must include a prohibition on “complexity as revenue”: complexity that increases hidden L triggers redesign.

---

## B05.CH05.P08.S04 — Consequence of bad design: hidden L rises, R/B falls, and resistance materialises as non-compliance

Bad charge design is not a marginal technical issue; it drives systemic instability.

### Causal consequences
- **Hidden L rises**: households pay through time (admin burden), uncertainty (volatile bills), and penalties (arrears cascades).
- **R/B tails worsen**: volatility and tail events increase, pushing people toward clamp contracts (debt, predatory plans).
- **Legitimacy fracture**: people experience “paying for rights,” interpret the system as captured, and shift into avoidance.
- **Non-compliance**: informal work, arrears strategies, fraud narratives, political backlash—resistance becomes rational.

### Conformance implication (binding)
- If charges in essentials increase arrears rates, disconnection threats, or administrative friction, the system must be redesigned:
  restructure tariffs, eliminate fee-stacks, shift funding to commons/tax financing, and publish restatements. Scaling is blocked until
  lifeworld metrics improve.
