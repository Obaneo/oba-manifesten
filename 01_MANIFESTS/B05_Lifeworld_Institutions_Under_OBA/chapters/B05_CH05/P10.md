# B05.CH05.P10 — Anti-avoidance in the T domain: linkage to the shift taxonomy

This paragraph specifies anti-avoidance in the T domain by linking fiscal enforcement directly to the broader OBA **shift taxonomy**:
legal, technical, financial, and international steering that attempts to bypass bases, incidence, and caps compatibility through form,
timing, and opacity. Under OBA, anti-avoidance is not an add-on list of “schemes.” It is a semantic integrity function: it prevents
actors from manufacturing alternate ontologies where identical economic reality is labelled into lighter bases or routed through
fee-stack ecosystems.

CH05 requires systematic detection and requalification: substance-over-form, look-through to ultimate control, automatic flags for
high-risk patterns, and a fail-closed regime under obstruction. The aim is not maximal enforcement; it is stable conformance under
adversarial adaptation.

---

## B05.CH05.P10.S01 — Relabelling: labour→consultancy→royalty; costs→perks; profit→transfer pricing (detection and requalification)

Relabelling is the primary avoidance mechanism because it exploits institutional boundaries: different labels trigger different rates,
bases, disclosure rules, and enforcement intensity.

### Canonical relabelling routes (non-exhaustive)
- **Labour → consultancy/contracting**: converting employment income into “services” to reduce contributions, shift obligations, or
  move into regimes with weaker auditability.
- **Labour → royalty/licensing**: converting compensation into IP rent streams through contrived assignments and licensing contracts.
- **Costs → perks/in-kind**: shifting consumption into “business costs” or fringe benefits, lowering I-base and incidence visibility.
- **Profit → transfer pricing/base erosion**: manipulating intra-group pricing, debt placement, and IP charges to relocate profit.

### Detection signals (binding)
- Abrupt changes in form without operational change (same work, same control, new invoice structure).
- Persistent profit-location mismatch (high-margin entities with low substance; high IP charges without development capacity).
- Fee-stack patterns: recurring “service fees” to controlled parties, especially where they track revenue not cost.

### Requalification rule (binding)
- Apply functional classification: if the economic reality is labour under control, treat as labour for I-base and contributions.
- Disallow “label supremacy”: contracts are evidence, not determinants; look to control/benefit/block tests and cashflow reality.

## B05.CH05.P10.S02 — Legal form vs economic reality: look-through and ultimate control are decisive

OBA treats legal form as a manipulable surface. Anti-avoidance therefore relies on look-through and ultimate control.

### Look-through principles (binding)
- **Ultimate control governs**: beneficial ownership and economic exposure determine base inclusion, not nominee titles.
- **Substance-over-form**: arrangements whose primary function is base shifting or incidence masking are disregarded.
- **Integrated group view**: controlled networks are assessed as a unit where fragmentation is used to arbitrage thresholds or rates.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Enforcement must have authority to recharacterise arrangements and to aggregate entities/positions under control.
- Where measurement depends on valuations or private reporting, disclosure duties and audit packs are mandatory; missing packs trigger
  conservative presumptions.

## B05.CH05.P10.S03 — International routes: treaty shopping, IP-box, re-domiciliation; automatic flags and presumptions

International arbitrage is a systematic risk: actors exploit mismatched rules, treaties, and enforcement asymmetries.

### Canonical routes (non-exhaustive)
- **Treaty shopping**: routing flows through jurisdictions to obtain reduced withholding or double non-taxation.
- **IP-box and licensing hubs**: relocating profits via royalties and IP ownership in low-tax regimes with minimal substance.
- **Re-domiciliation and control-centre relocation**: moving “headquarters” or management location on paper while operations remain.
- **Hybrid mismatch and conduit entities**: entities designed to be treated differently across jurisdictions.

### Automatic flags (binding)
- High royalty/service flows to low-substance jurisdictions.
- Repeated restructuring near tax/cap events (vesting, asset sales, large distributions).
- Disproportionate debt/interest flows within groups that track profit rather than financing need.

### Presumptions and remedies (binding)
- Denial-of-benefits style presumptions where substance is absent.
- Withholding hooks, reporting duties, and look-through allocation to ultimate control.
- Escalation to temporary controls where leakage threatens order stability (subject to sunset/rollback discipline).

## B05.CH05.P10.S04 — Fail-closed: under obstruction/non-disclosure, apply conservative presumptions and provisional assessment

Non-disclosure is a strategy, not a neutral absence. OBA therefore fails closed under obstruction.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If disclosure is missing, obstructed, or unverifiable, apply conservative presumptions: classify into the heaviest relevant base,
  use protective valuations, and apply provisional assessments.
- Appeals remain available, but obstruction does not suspend enforcement; resolution may require escrow/lockbox until verified.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The presumption ladder and provisional assessment procedures must be published (Codex linkage) and applied uniformly to prevent
  selective enforcement.
- If enforcement capacity cannot apply these rules consistently, the system must reduce scope to auditable coverage rather than
  tolerating drift through unenforced complexity.
