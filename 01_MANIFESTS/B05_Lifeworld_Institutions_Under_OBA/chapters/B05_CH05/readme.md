# B05.CH05 — Taxes, contributions, and public finance: T-architecture and legitimacy without extraction

This chapter specifies **T-architecture** as a **lifeworld-critical interface**: the set of taxes, contributions, premiums, regulated
charges, and quasi-taxes that fund commons capacity and security without recreating extraction. In OBA, T is not just “how the state
raises money.” It is the formal exchange that must remain **legible, auditable, and contestable**, because opaque or regressive
financing becomes a direct driver of resentment, non-compliance, and political sabotage.

CH05 is intentionally **anti-toll** and **anti-drift**. It treats fiscal complexity, mandatory intermediation, carve-outs, and
administrative friction as re-entry points for E. The chapter therefore binds T design to incidence-first logic, anti-regression
rails, integration with caps (W/I), ΔM channel discipline, and evidence/publication requirements that make claims checkable.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing fiscal policy**: use CH05 as the rails for what is admissible as T, how to prevent toll dynamics, and how to
  keep incidence compatible with R/B tail stability.
- If you are **writing laws/mandates**: import the no-arbitrage rules (caps ↔ T), the transparency surface (destination/overhead), and
  the anti-avoidance presumptions under opacity.
- If you are **building systems and data**: implement the publication tables (composition, destination, incidence, R/B effects) with
  definition-set pinning and method_id discipline; avoid data maximalism.
- If you are **auditing implementations**: treat carve-outs, complexity inflation, regressive drift, and non-reproducible models as
  hard-stop triggers requiring redesign/restatement.

## Status

This chapter is **normative** for any OBA implementation that claims legitimacy and stability. T-architecture is treated as
constitutive for lifeworld compatibility: if financing relies on lock-in, regressive fee-stacks, discretionary deals, or opaque models
that cannot be audited and contested, the order will drift toward incompatibility.

Hard-stop logic applies: if T increases administrative L, worsens R/B tails, or reconstitutes E through complexity and carve-outs,
redesign is mandatory and compatibility claims must be suspended until restated and corrected.
