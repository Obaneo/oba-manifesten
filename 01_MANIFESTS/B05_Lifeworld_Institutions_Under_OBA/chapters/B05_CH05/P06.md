# B05.CH05.P06 — Progressivity and distribution: explicit distributive goals tied to R/B

This paragraph specifies how progressivity and distribution goals are defined under OBA. “Progressive” is not a moral label; it is a
lifeworld property: a T system is progressive if it reduces tail harm, stabilises residual space, and does not shift burdens onto
those with low exit power. Distribution design is therefore bound to **R/B metrics**, not to symbolic rate structures or political
identity.

CH05 requires explicit target metrics, an instrument mix that combines direct levies with structural L reduction, and an anti-Goodhart
discipline that prevents optimisation on medians while tails worsen. The chapter also treats perception as causally relevant: if
distribution gains are not experienced as lower burdens and higher stability, legitimacy collapses into “accounting trick” framing.

---

## B05.CH05.P06.S01 — Target metrics: share_below_alpha, tail volatility, median R/B, essentials affordability

Distribution goals must be measurable and must reflect both tails and central tendency.

### Target metrics (minimum; binding)
- **share_below_alpha**: proportion structurally below the minimum residual space threshold (α), by cohort/region (join-safe).
- **Tail volatility**: volatility of R/B and frequency of tail events (arrears cascades, disconnections, emergency debt uptake).
- **Median R/B**: median residual space relative to gross (or relevant baseline) as a stability indicator (secondary to tails).
- **Essentials affordability (L)**: burden shares for essentials baskets and their volatility (housing/energy/food/digital, as defined).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Targets must be stated as distributions (not only averages) and must include cohort visibility; aggregate improvements can mask
  structural harm.
- If a T change improves median while tails worsen (or share_below_alpha rises), it is treated as non-conforming unless corrected.

---

## B05.CH05.P06.S02 — Instrument mix: progressive direct levies + L reduction via commons + targeted transfers

Progressivity is not achieved by one lever. The order uses a mix to avoid regressive drift and to reduce burdens structurally.

### Canonical instrument mix
- **Progressive direct levies**: income/profit/wealth/claims bases aligned with caps; reduce concentration and fund commons/insurance.
- **L reduction via commons** (preferred structural route): essentials price discipline, public options, maintenance-first investment to
  lower recurring burdens and volatility.
- **Targeted transfers**: used when L reduction cannot fully or quickly neutralise tail harm; must be measured for uptake and drop-off.
- **Execution design**: low-friction delivery; high administrative burden converts transfers into hidden L and collapses progressivity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any reliance on transfers must include anti-toll execution: minimal proof, fast delivery, contestable decisions.
- If indirect levies exist, compensating instruments must be explicit and demonstrable (not rhetorical).

---

## B05.CH05.P06.S03 — Avoid “median-only” optimisation: tails are primary (anti-Goodhart)

Median optimisation is a predictable political temptation: it yields good headlines while leaving tail groups exposed. OBA treats this as
a governance failure mode.

### Goodhart risk in distribution
- Policies optimise for a single summary statistic (median net income, average tax rate) while tails face higher volatility and clamp.
- Apparent “progress” is achieved by shifting burdens into less visible channels (fees, waiting times, compliance costs).

### Anti-Goodhart discipline (binding)
- Tails are primary: any proposal must show effects on share_below_alpha and tail volatility.
- Use multi-metric evaluation: combine R/B tails, arrears proxies, essentials affordability, and admin friction measures.
- Require restatement and rollback paths: if tail metrics deteriorate after deployment, redesign is mandatory.

---

## B05.CH05.P06.S04 — Political consequence: distribution gains without visible L decline feel like an accounting trick (legitimacy risk)

Legitimacy depends on experienced reality, not fiscal narratives. If people pay more (or feel they pay more) while essentials remain
expensive, unstable, or gated, the system is framed as extraction.

### Mechanism
- **Visible costs vs invisible benefits**: taxes are salient; commons improvements are slow or hidden; resentment rises.
- **Friction and stigma**: transfers that require humiliating procedures feel like coercion, not support.
- **Fee-stack substitution**: taxes fall but regulated charges and private fees rise; the lifeworld sees no relief.

### Conformance implications (binding)
- Distribution policy must be paired with visible L reduction (essentials) and published destination maps; otherwise legitimacy erodes.
- If cohort-level burdens rise without measurable improvements in access/maintenance and tail stability, scaling is blocked and the
  financing mix must be redesigned.
