# B05.CH05.P12 — Measurement cadence and public accountability (T as a testable contract)

This paragraph specifies the measurement cadence and accountability surface required for T under OBA. T is treated as a **testable
contract**: contributions are legitimate only if the system can show, with reproducible methods and contestable evidence, who pays,
where funds go, what overhead is incurred, and what lifeworld effects result (R/B tails, L burdens, ΔG/commons delivery).

Accountability is not satisfied by “a dashboard.” OBA requires public tables that can be independently checked, a reproducibility
discipline (method_id, definition-set pinning, uncertainty), and a dispute/contestability mechanism that can trigger audits and
restatements. Where this cannot be executed, legitimacy claims fail.

---

## B05.CH05.P12.S01 — Public tables: T composition, destination, overhead, cohort incidence, R/B effects (aggregated)

The minimum accountability surface is a set of public tables that expose fiscal reality without violating privacy.

### Required public tables (minimum; binding)
- **T composition**: breakdown of levy streams by type (direct, social contributions, indirect, quasi-taxes), with time series.
- **Destination**: allocation to commons domains, security instruments, maintenance budgets, and transfers (including earmarks and
  reallocations).
- **Overhead**: execution costs (collection, adjudication, audit, IT/tooling, appeals) as absolute amounts and as % of relevant T.
- **Cohort incidence**: who bears the burden by cohort/region/position classes (join-safe), including pass-through assumptions where
  relevant.
- **R/B effects**: aggregated impacts on share_below_alpha, tail volatility proxies, median R/B, and essentials burden indicators.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Tables must be published on a fixed cadence and include revision histories; “one-off reports” are insufficient.
- Aggregation must not be used as opacity: cohort breakdowns are mandatory within join-safety constraints.

---

## B05.CH05.P12.S02 — Reproducibility: method_id, definition-set ID, uncertainty bands, revision policy

Public tables without reproducibility are narrative artefacts. OBA requires that fiscal claims can be re-executed.

### Reproducibility requirements (binding)
- **method_id**: every published series must reference the computation method version.
- **definition-set ID**: bases and classifications must pin the canonical definitionset/version (no drifting semantics).
- **Uncertainty bands**: where incidence or pass-through is modelled, publish uncertainty ranges and sensitivity parameters.
- **Revision policy**: publish how errors are corrected (restatement rules), including retroactive revisions and audit triggers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a claim cannot be reproduced from the published method and inputs (within permitted disclosure tiers), it must not be used for
  legitimacy assertions.
- Silent edits are prohibited: revisions require explicit restatement logs.

---

## B05.CH05.P12.S03 — Contestability: citizens/organisations can challenge incidence claims via dispute flow

Accountability requires not only publication, but **challengeability**: the ability to contest claims and trigger corrective action.

### Contestability mechanism (binding)
- **Dispute flow**: a formal process to challenge incidence assumptions, base definitions, and destination claims, with timelines.
- **Access to evidence**: challengers can request audit packs or the minimal evidence needed to evaluate claims.
- **Escalation**: material disputes can trigger audits, schema reviews, requalification decisions, and policy restatements.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Contestability cannot be symbolic; disputes must have the power to change methods, correct tables, and force remedies.
- If contestability is blocked by opacity or institutional veto layers, the fiscal regime is treated as captured and must escalate.

---

## B05.CH05.P12.S04 — Hard stop: “policy based on a model without an audit pack” → no legitimacy claim

OBA forbids model-based legitimacy without auditable evidence. Where policy depends on models (incidence, behavioural response,
macroeffects), those models must be inspectable and contestable.

### Hard stop rule (binding)
- Any policy justified primarily by a model must ship with an **audit pack**: data sources, assumptions, structure, validation checks,
  sensitivity analysis, and known failure modes.
- If an audit pack cannot be provided (vendor black box, proprietary restrictions, missing data lineage), the policy cannot be used as
  a legitimacy claim and must be treated as non-conforming until corrected or replaced.

### Conformance implications
- No audit pack → no legitimacy claim → scaling blocked.
- Repeated reliance on non-auditable modelling triggers governance escalation (audit-of-auditor, procurement remedy, or scope rollback).
