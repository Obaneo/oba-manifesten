# B05.CH05.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of T-architecture under OBA

This paragraph defines the purpose, scope, and normative status of **T-architecture** under OBA. T-architecture is the fiscal layer
that funds commons capacity and security while maintaining legitimacy. In OBA, T is not a secondary “policy area”; it is an order
interface because it shapes lifeworld burdens directly (NET, timing, predictability, administrative friction) and because it is a
primary surface for extraction regrowth (mandatory intermediation, fee-stacks, carve-outs, complexity industries).

CH05 therefore treats T as a **testable contract**: who pays (incidence), what it funds (destination), how it is executed (friction),
and what it achieves (ΔG/commons, R/B tails) must be measurable and contestable. Any financing regime that cannot be audited and that
produces toll dynamics is treated as drifting toward incompatibility.

---

## B05.CH05.P01.S01 — Purpose: public finance that safeguards commons capacity and R/B stability without toll mechanisms

The purpose of T-architecture is to finance the order’s functional obligations without recreating E.

### Purpose components
- **Safeguard commons capacity**: stable funding for access, maintenance, and continuity (ΔG) across essential domains.
- **Stabilise R/B tails**: ensure that the fiscal burden does not structurally worsen tail outcomes and that security systems remain
  financeable under shocks.
- **Preserve legitimacy**: make the exchange “contribution ↔ commons/security outcomes” explicit, visible, and checkable.
- **Prevent toll regrowth**: block the conversion of public finance into private extraction via mandatory intermediaries, junk fees,
  and complexity rents.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any T instrument must be evaluated by incidence and by its effect on L and R/B tails, not by legal form or headline rates.
- Financing mechanisms that require private gatekeeping or that systematically increase hidden L (administrative friction) are treated
  as non-conforming unless redesigned.

## B05.CH05.P01.S02 — Scope: taxes, premiums, contributions, regulated charges, hypothecation, execution costs, fiscal institutions

CH05 covers the full fiscal surface that households and firms experience as “what is taken” or “what must be paid.”

### Included within scope (binding)
- **Taxes**: direct and indirect levies, including profit, income, wealth/claims where chosen, and consumption/excise within rails.
- **Premiums and contributions**: social insurance contributions and other mandatory risk-pooling payments.
- **Regulated charges**: quasi-taxes and mandatory fees that function as public levies (including utility-like mandatory charges where
  applicable).
- **Hypothecation/ring-fencing**: earmarked funds, trust techniques, and budget protection mechanisms.
- **Execution costs**: administrative overhead, compliance burdens, and enforcement costs as part of the true incidence.
- **Fiscal institutions**: collection agencies, audit bodies, rules for discretion/appeal, and intergovernmental transfer formulas.

### Scope boundary
- Sector-specific pricing design and detailed commons operations live in their relevant chapters; CH05 specifies how financing must
  avoid becoming a toll system and how it must remain testable and compatible with the kernel rails.

## B05.CH05.P01.S03 — Binding: Definition Set II (T in B,T,N,L,R), caps (W/I), P/E/C requalification, ΔM rails, evidence standards

T-architecture is not free-form. It is bound by canonical definitions and invariants elsewhere in OBA.

### Binding references (non-exhaustive)
- **Definition Set II**: T is defined as part of the lifeworld accounting (B,T,N,L,R); classification and measurement must reference
  canonical definition-set IDs and versions.
- **Caps (W/I)**: no-arbitrage between fiscal bases and caps; tax design cannot create bypass routes around wealth/income constraints.
- **P/E/C requalification**: fiscal structures that function as extraction (fee-stacks, mandatory intermediation rents) must be
  requalified and neutralised, regardless of label.
- **ΔM rails**: fiscal policy must not rely on monetary channels that become asset/claim support; when ΔM is used (crisis), leakage
  tests are mandatory.
- **Evidence standards**: incidence claims, distribution claims, and “this will pay for itself” claims require audit packs and
  reproducible methods.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any new fiscal instrument must declare: base definition (I/W/T), incidence model (who bears), destination, execution friction, and
  compatibility checks (caps, P/E/C, ΔM).
- If definitions or methods are not pinned (no definition-set ID / version), the instrument is non-conforming until corrected.

## B05.CH05.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: financing that becomes dependent on lock-in, regressive fee-stacks, or discretionary deals is non-conform

The fiscal layer must fail-closed against the most common drift patterns.

### Non-conformance triggers (binding)
- **Lock-in dependence**: financing depends on mandatory intermediaries, long-term vendor lock-in, or employer-tied access that
  restricts exit.
- **Regressive fee-stacks**: the effective burden shifts onto low R/B cohorts via fixed charges, junk fees, or complexity that
  requires paid help.
- **Discretionary deals**: carve-outs, bespoke exemptions, or negotiated settlements for powerful positions that bypass published
  rules.
- **Administrative toll**: execution friction rises (lead times, proof pressure, filing complexity) and becomes a gate.

### Fail-closed rule
- If a financing regime exhibits these patterns and cannot be corrected via redesign/requalification, it must be treated as
  non-conforming and must not be used as a basis for legitimacy claims. Scaling is blocked until restated and repaired.
