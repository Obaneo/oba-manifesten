# B05.CH05.P03 — Taxonomy of levies: canonical classification of T components

This paragraph defines a canonical taxonomy for T components. Under OBA, labels are unreliable: “fee,” “premium,” “charge,” “service
cost,” and “contribution” are routinely used to disguise incidence, to create private toll gates, or to shift burdens regressively.
CH05 therefore treats T classification as an incidence-and-function problem: if a payment is mandatory (de facto) and finances public
obligations or regulated access, it is treated as T and must obey the T rails (anti-regression, transparency, no toll intermediaries,
auditability).

The taxonomy is not merely descriptive. It is a control mechanism: it prevents semantic arbitrage and ensures that all levy-like flows
are captured in the lifeworld accounting (B,T,N,L,R) with consistent bases and no-arbitrage against caps (W/I).

---

## B05.CH05.P03.S01 — Direct levies: income/wages, profit, wealth/claims, gifts/inheritance (where chosen)

Direct levies are assessed on income flows and asset/claim positions (or transfers of those positions). Under OBA, the primary design
constraint is that bases must align with caps definitions and look-through rules.

### Direct levy classes (canonical)
- **Income/wages**: labour income and labour-like remuneration, including reclassified forms under substance-over-form.
- **Profit**: corporate and business profits, adjusted for transfer pricing and artificial base erosion (link anti-avoidance).
- **Wealth/claims**: net worth and/or claim-based positions, including look-through to beneficial control and economic exposure.
- **Gifts/inheritance** (where chosen): transfers of wealth/claims across persons or entities, subject to the order’s legitimacy design.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Bases MUST be defined in alignment with I-base and W-base rules (caps compatibility); deferrals, perks, and relabelling are included
  under control/benefit tests where applicable.
- Under opacity or obstruction, conservative presumptions apply: count in the heaviest relevant base (fail-closed against arbitrage).

## B05.CH05.P03.S02 — Social contributions: premiums for risk pooling (insurance as P, not E)

Social contributions are mandatory payments earmarked for risk pooling (health, disability, unemployment, old age). Functionally, they
are T components with a specific destination and therefore must be governed as public finance, even if administered via mixed systems.

### Canonical features
- **Earmarked purpose**: funds a defined coverage package with published entitlements and service minima.
- **Predictable incidence**: who pays is explicit; regressivity and burdens on tails are tested.
- **Coverage linkage**: contributions map to coverage and continuity outputs, not to private profit streams.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Insurance must remain P by function: denial games, opaque exclusions, and private toll intermediaries are prohibited (link CH04.P05).
- If a “premium” is mandatory and effectively functions as a tax, it is treated as T and must obey transparency and anti-toll rules.

## B05.CH05.P03.S03 — Indirect levies: consumption/excise (only within anti-regression rails)

Indirect levies are levied on spending or consumption proxies. Under OBA, they are permitted only if designed to avoid structural tail
harm, because incidence is frequently regressive.

### Canonical indirect levy classes
- **Consumption taxes**: broad-based levies on goods and services.
- **Excise taxes**: targeted levies on specific goods/services (often framed as externality pricing).

### Anti-regression constraints (binding)
- Indirect levies MUST not structurally worsen R/B tails; if used, they require compensating mechanisms (L reduction via commons,
  targeted transfers, or differential rates tied to baseline essentials).
- Essentials rails apply: indirect levies must not undermine baseline access to essentials; if they do, they are non-conforming unless
  fully neutralised by explicit offsets.

## B05.CH05.P03.S04 — Quasi-taxes: regulated charges and mandatory contributions (criteria for when this is T)

Quasi-taxes are payments not legally labelled “tax,” but mandatory in practice due to regulation, monopoly, or rights gating. They are
high-risk for toll dynamics and must be forced into the T taxonomy to prevent semantic evasion.

### Canonical quasi-tax examples (non-exhaustive)
- Regulated utility charges that fund public obligations or universal service.
- Mandatory service fees required to access rights or comply with law.
- Sector levies collected via intermediaries (platform levies, licensing fees, compulsory dues) when participation is de facto required.

### Criteria: when a charge is treated as T (binding)
A payment is treated as T if one or more holds:
- **Mandatory by design or by constraint**: payment is required to access an essential service/right or to comply with law/regulation.
- **Incidence resembles a levy**: the payer cannot realistically avoid it without losing basic participation.
- **Destination funds public/regulated obligations**: the payment finances commons, security, or legally mandated service continuity.
- **Pricing is regulated or monopoly-bound**: the payment occurs in a non-contestable market or under statutory conditions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Quasi-taxes must publish the same transparency surface as formal taxes: destination, overhead, incidence, and lifeworld effects.
- Mandatory charges must not be routed through private fee-stacks; if intermediation exists, it must be bounded, exitable, and audited.
- Under opacity, presume tolling and requalify/replace the charge mechanism.
