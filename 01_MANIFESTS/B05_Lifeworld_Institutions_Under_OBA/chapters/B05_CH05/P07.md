# B05.CH05.P07 — Hypothecation and ring-fencing: earmarking as a trust technique

This paragraph specifies hypothecation (earmarking) and ring-fencing as trust and anti-capture techniques in T-architecture. Under
OBA, earmarking is not “budget decoration.” It is a governance tool to make the exchange relation (T ↔ commons/security outcomes)
verifiable and to prevent political raiding, vendor capture, and maintenance neglect.

However, hypothecation can also become washing: labels substitute for outcomes, and funds are “earmarked” while delivery is absent or
non-measurable. CH05 therefore binds earmarking to measurable ΔG-style outputs, audit packs, and transparent overhead disclosure.

---

## B05.CH05.P07.S01 — Ring-fencing maintenance: maintenance-first budgets cannot be drained

Maintenance is the first casualty of political cycles and fiscal stress. OBA treats maintenance capacity as constitutive: if maintenance
decays, commons provision becomes unstable and lifeworld burdens rise.

### Ring-fencing logic (binding)
- **Protected maintenance budgets**: lifecycle upkeep and backlog reduction funds for essentials commons and critical infrastructure
  cannot be repurposed as discretionary slack.
- **Backlog discipline**: ring-fencing must be linked to measurable backlog indicators, SLAs, incident rates, and continuity metrics.
- **Governance protection**: ring-fencing rules include constraints on emergency reallocation, with explicit procedures and public logs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If ring-fenced maintenance budgets are repeatedly drained, or if backlogs grow structurally, the system is in breach and must trigger
  escalation (governance intervention, scope reduction, or reallocation toward capacity rebuilding).

---

## B05.CH05.P07.S02 — Commons funds: link C flows and cap remedies to concrete ΔG projects with audit packs

Earmarked funds are most legitimate when they are linked to concrete deliverables and measurable improvements in capacity and lifeworld
burdens.

### Funding linkage (binding)
- **Commons funds**: dedicated pools that finance access, maintenance, and expansion of commons provision.
- **Cap remedy linkage**: when cap enforcement yields levies/clawbacks/conversions, flows into commons funds must be traceable and
  tied to public projects (not absorbed into opaque general budgets).
- **Project audit packs**: each significant project must have an audit pack: scope, cost, timelines, procurement rails, expected ΔG
  outputs, and measurement plan.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Earmarked flows MUST have traceable allocation to projects/services with published identifiers and outcome dashboards.
- Projects that cannot produce an audit pack (or that are vendor-black-box dependent) are not admissible recipients of earmarked
  commons funds.

---

## B05.CH05.P07.S03 — Transparent overhead: publish execution costs explicitly as a percentage of T

Administrative cost is part of incidence and a primary drift surface: overhead expansion can become an internal extraction mechanism
(“bureaucratic E”) and can undermine trust.

### Overhead transparency (binding)
- Publish **execution costs** as a percentage of relevant T streams and as absolute amounts.
- Separate overhead into functional categories (collection, adjudication, audit, IT/tooling, appeals).
- Publish vendor dependency indicators where procurement costs and lock-in risk are material.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If overhead grows without corresponding improvements in accuracy, lead times, contestability, and tail stability, it is treated as
  drift and requires redesign/simplification.
- If overhead depends on non-exitable proprietary tooling, governance is captured; procurement rails must be enforced or the system is
  non-conforming.

---

## B05.CH05.P07.S04 — Hard stop: hypothecation without measurable output (ΔG/RB) is washing and undermines legitimacy

Earmarking is only legitimate if it produces measurable output. Otherwise, it is an optics layer that accelerates cynicism.

### Washing mechanisms
- “Earmarked” funds are reallocated informally or diluted across general spending without traceability.
- Outputs are reported as narratives or counts, not as capacity/maintenance improvements or lifeworld metrics.
- Dashboards exist without audit packs, restatements, or contestability.

### Hard stop rule (binding)
- Hypothecation without measurable output is **non-conforming**: if earmarked funds cannot be linked to ΔG-style capacity improvements
  and/or R/B tail stabilisation using published methods, the earmark must be treated as washing.
- Under such failure, the system must restate: publish allocation trace, correct outputs, redesign earmarking rules, or remove the
  earmark claim (and suspend legitimacy arguments that relied on it).
