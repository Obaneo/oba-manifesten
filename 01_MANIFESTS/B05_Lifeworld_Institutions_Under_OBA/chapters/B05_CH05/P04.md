# B05.CH05.P04 — Design principles: T rails in a caps/commons order

This paragraph defines the canonical design principles (“rails”) for T under OBA. T-architecture must remain compatible with caps,
commons provisioning, and lifeworld stability. The key failure mode is fiscal formalism: designing by legal labels, headline rates, or
institutional convenience while ignoring true incidence, hidden L, and extraction re-entry points.

OBA therefore constrains T with four rails: incidence-first, anti-regression (tail-first), E-elimination priority, and administrative
minimalism. These rails are normative: a T system that violates them cannot claim OBA compatibility, even if it is revenue-sufficient.

---

## B05.CH05.P04.S01 — Incidence-first: design around who actually pays, not the legal label

Legal incidence (who is “liable”) diverges from economic incidence (who bears the burden). Under OBA, only economic incidence is
relevant for lifeworld effects.

### Incidence-first rule (binding)
- Every levy must specify **who bears** the burden across cohorts and positions (households, workers, firms, landlords, platforms),
  including pass-through mechanisms.
- Design must account for **market power** and **contract clamp**: when exit is costly, burdens are shifted onto those least able to
  avoid them.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any T proposal must include an incidence statement with method_id and contestability (auditable assumptions and restatement path).
- If incidence cannot be established under privacy and data constraints, conservative presumptions apply: treat as likely regressive
  or clamp-amplifying and redesign.

---

## B05.CH05.P04.S02 — Anti-regression: T must not structurally lower R/B tails; compensate via L reduction or transfers

OBA is tail-first. A T system that worsens tails breaks lifeworld compatibility even if it improves averages.

### Anti-regression rule (binding)
- T must not structurally increase share_below_alpha, tail volatility, or arrears cascades.
- Indirect burdens that hit low R/B cohorts (fixed charges, regressive consumption levies, compliance costs) require explicit offsets.

### Permitted compensation routes
- **L reduction**: commons-based essentials price discipline, baseline access, removal of fee-stacks (preferred because it lowers
  burdens structurally).
- **Transfers**: targeted or universal transfers that demonstrably neutralise tail harm (must be measured, not asserted).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Compensation must be demonstrable in public tables (incidence + R/B + L outcomes). “We will compensate later” is non-evidence.
- If compensation cannot be executed reliably (capacity constraints), the levy is non-conforming or must be scaled back (fail-closed).

---

## B05.CH05.P04.S03 — E-elimination priority: the T system must not strengthen fee-stacks or rent-seeking

A central drift risk is that T becomes a growth substrate for E: complexity industries, mandatory intermediaries, and carve-out markets.
CH05 treats these as extraction regrowth and therefore as incompatibility.

### E-elimination principles (binding)
- **No mandatory intermediation**: payments should not require private brokers, paid compliance agents, or proprietary vendor rails.
- **No fee-stacks**: junk fees, “service fees,” and layered charges around mandatory payments are prohibited or requalified as E.
- **No carve-out markets**: exemptions, loopholes, and bespoke deals create an optimisation industry and undermine legitimacy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any mechanism that predictably creates a paid optimisation ecosystem (complexity, exceptions, discretionary relief) must be redesigned
  toward standard templates and rule-bound procedures.
- Under opacity or non-disclosure, apply conservative requalification and enforcement (substance-over-form).

---

## B05.CH05.P04.S04 — Administrative minimalism: execution friction is hidden L and counts in the lifeworld test

Execution is part of incidence. Filing time, proof pressure, delays, and appeal burdens are lifeworld burdens (hidden L) and cannot be
ignored.

### Administrative minimalism rule (binding)
- Design for **low friction**: simplicity, pre-fill, standard forms, short lead times, and clear appeal rails.
- Treat administrative overhead and compliance burden as first-class metrics, not as “implementation detail.”
- Avoid data maximalism: simplification must not be achieved by building surveillance infrastructures.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Publish friction proxies (lead time, error/appeal rates, drop-off) for major T processes; deterioration triggers redesign.
- If collection depends on complex procedures that require paid help, the system has created a bureaucratic toll and is non-conforming.
- Black-box computation for assessments is prohibited: decisions must be reproducible and contestable.
