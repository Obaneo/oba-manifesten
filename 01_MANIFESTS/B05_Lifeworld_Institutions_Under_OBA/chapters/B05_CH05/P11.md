# B05.CH05.P11 — Public debt, deficits, and ΔM: financing without relapse into asset/claim-support

This paragraph specifies how public debt, deficits, and monetary space (ΔM) may be used within OBA without relapsing into prohibited
asset/claim-support dynamics. OBA does not treat deficits as intrinsically illegitimate; it treats *channels and effects* as decisive.
Debt and ΔM are acceptable only when they (i) build or maintain real commons capacity (ΔG analogue) or (ii) stabilise R/B tails under
shocks, and when they do not leak into asset price support, rentier cashflows, or claim stack inflation.

The core governance problem is that crisis finance is politically easy to declare and hard to unwind. CH05 therefore imposes deficit
discipline, strict channel discipline with leakage tests, bounded debt-as-instrument rules, and crisis regime constraints (sunsets,
unwind plans, post-mortems).

---

## B05.CH05.P11.S01 — Deficit discipline: legitimate deficits via demonstrable ΔG build-up or R/B stabilisation

Deficits are legitimate only if they can be justified as financing (a) measurable capacity build/maintenance or (b) measurable tail
stabilisation. “Stimulus” rhetoric is not a sufficient criterion.

### Legitimate deficit purposes (binding)
- **ΔG build-up / maintenance**: funding projects that expand or restore commons infrastructure and service capacity (backlogs down,
  reliability up, access improved).
- **R/B stabilisation**: temporary support that prevents tail collapse (arrears cascades, disconnections, forced deleveraging) under
  shock conditions, with demonstrable effect.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any deficit programme must have an audit pack: purpose, channel, project/service identifiers, expected outputs, method_id, and
  restatement policy.
- If deficits persist without demonstrable ΔG outputs or tail stabilisation, the regime is treated as drift and must be redesigned or
  unwound.

---

## B05.CH05.P11.S02 — ΔM channel discipline: monetary space only via allowed channels; leakage tests mandatory

ΔM is a powerful tool and a high-risk drift surface because it can easily become indirect asset/claim support. OBA therefore constrains
ΔM by channel admissibility and leakage testing.

### Channel discipline (binding)
- **Allowed channels**: commons capex, maintenance programmes, essentials stabilisation mechanisms that reduce L, and transition
  instruments that build productive capacity.
- **Forbidden channels**: policies that primarily support asset prices, sustain claim stacks, socialise losses for rentier positions,
  or fund excess returns in monopoly concessions.

### Leakage tests (binding)
- For any ΔM deployment, define and test leakage routes: where new money can be captured into claims (asset prices, rents, fees,
  spreads, intermediary margins).
- Tag funds and require traceability through the delivery chain; enforce sanctions and redesign if leakage indicators trigger.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If leakage cannot be measured (no data, no auditability), ΔM deployment must be scaled back to auditable channels (fail-closed).

## B05.CH05.P11.S03 — Debt as instrument: allowed if productive (commons/infra) and bounded; prohibited as claim stack

Public debt is an instrument. It is not automatically “bad,” but it becomes incompatible when it functions as claim support or
permanent rent streams.

### Admissible debt uses (binding)
- **Productive/commons investment**: infrastructure, maintenance backlogs, capacity expansion, and resilience projects with measurable
  outputs.
- **Smoothing under shock**: bridging finance for stabilisation programmes with explicit unwind paths.

### Prohibited debt patterns (binding)
- **Claim stack replication**: debt issuance primarily used to preserve asset valuations, to fund rentier cashflows, or to roll over
  extraction without structural change.
- **Permanent emergency normalisation**: debt used as a standing substitute for fiscal design, without output linkage.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Debt programmes must be bounded (size, duration, eligible uses) and must include performance metrics tied to ΔG and/or R/B tails.
- If debt service becomes a structural driver of regressive incidence or crowds out maintenance budgets, redesign is mandatory.

---

## B05.CH05.P11.S04 — Crisis regime: temporary scale-up with sunsets, unwind plan, and post-mortem audit

Crisis regimes are where rules are most likely to be suspended and capture most likely to occur. OBA allows crisis scale-up only under
strict temporariness and verification constraints.

### Crisis regime constraints (binding)
- **Triggers**: explicit measurable criteria (price breaks, shortages, systemic stress indicators) with public notice.
- **Sunsets**: automatic expiry dates; extensions require explicit re-authorisation with evidence.
- **Unwind plan**: pre-specified path to reduce programmes and retire extraordinary instruments; no “we will see later.”
- **Post-mortem audit**: mandatory evaluation, restatement of claims, leakage review, and publication of incident logs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If crisis measures cannot be unwound, or if they generate sustained leakage into claims/fees, they are treated as drift toward
  incompatibility and must trigger rollback, governance intervention, or scope restriction.
