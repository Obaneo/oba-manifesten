# B05.CH05.P05 — Bases and integration with caps (no-arbitrage between W/I and T)

This paragraph specifies how T bases integrate with caps so that there is no-arbitrage between wealth (W), income (I), and fiscal
burden (T). In a caps/commons order, actors will search for steering routes: shift income into wealth, wealth into untaxed forms,
obligations into fees, or caps compliance into tax loopholes. If T bases are inconsistent with I-MAX and W-MAX, the system creates
semantic and technical arbitrage and undermines both enforcement and legitimacy.

CH05 therefore requires base alignment (I-base and W-base), explicit no-arbitrage rules across bases, and a conservative fail-closed
principle under opacity. The aim is not maximal taxation; it is *compatibility*: fiscal definitions cannot become a parallel ontology
that weakens the order’s core constraints.

---

## B05.CH05.P05.S01 — I-base alignment: income definitions consistent with I-MAX (deferral/perks/relabelling under control)

The I-base for taxation and contributions must track the same economic reality as I-MAX. Otherwise, high-incidence flows are relabelled
to escape both caps and fiscal incidence.

### I-base alignment requirements (binding)
- **Substance-over-form**: legal labels (salary, fee, royalty, dividend, “expense reimbursement”) do not govern; control/benefit tests
  and economic function govern.
- **Deferrals and timing**: deferral mechanisms (bonus deferral, vesting, carried interest realisation scheduling) must be addressed by
  look-back or accrual rules consistent with cap event logic.
- **Perks and in-kind**: in-kind benefits that function as consumption or compensation are included where they are a bypass route,
  using standard valuation rules.
- **Relabelling controls**: labour → consultancy → licensing; compensation → cost; profit distribution → loan; must be detected and
  requalified where economic reality indicates compensation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- I-base definitions MUST reference the canonical cap-aligned definition set/version; local redefinitions are disallowed.
- Where valuation is uncertain, conservative valuation rules apply to prevent systematic undercounting.

---

## B05.CH05.P05.S02 — W-base alignment: wealth base consistent with W-MAX (look-through, synthetics, valuation)

The W-base must capture economic exposure and control, not merely formal ownership. Otherwise, W shifts into shells, funds, nominees,
or synthetics.

### W-base alignment requirements (binding)
- **Look-through**: beneficial control and economic exposure dominate; layered vehicles do not shield W incidence.
- **Synthetics and derivatives**: total-return swaps, options, contracts-for-difference, and similar instruments are treated as wealth
  exposure under look-through rules.
- **Valuation discipline**: private assets, illiquid holdings, and controlled valuations require standard methods, conservative bands,
  and disclosure obligations.
- **Aggregation and concentration**: portfolios and controlled groups must be aggregated consistent with cap logic; fragmentation is
  treated as an evasion pattern.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- W-base must be compatible with W-MAX measurement primitives (including valuation and exposure rules); if not, the system creates a
  parallel, weaker base.
- Under non-disclosure, apply presumptive valuations and escalation (escrow/lockbox where needed).

---

## B05.CH05.P05.S03 — No-arbitrage rules: prevent steering via T to bypass caps, or vice versa

No-arbitrage means that actors cannot improve their position by shifting the same underlying economic reality across bases (I, W, T)
through relabelling, timing, or contractual form.

### Canonical arbitrage routes (non-exhaustive)
- **I → W**: deferring compensation into equity/claims to reduce I incidence while increasing W in hidden forms.
- **W → I**: extracting wealth as “loans,” “fees,” or controlled distributions to exploit rate differences.
- **T → private charges**: converting public levies into regulated charges with private fee-stacks, or vice versa.
- **Caps compliance via tax**: creating “tax paid” carve-outs that effectively legalise cap breaches through payments without
  deconcentration or neutralisation of claim power.

### No-arbitrage controls (binding)
- Harmonise bases and timing: the same event cannot be treated as “out of base” in one system while “in base” in another.
- Apply anti-avoidance requalification across domains: if a structure is non-substantive for caps, it is non-substantive for T.
- Restrict carve-outs: “special regimes” must be template-based, time-bounded, and evidentially justified; otherwise treated as E.

---

## B05.CH05.P05.S04 — Conservatism: under opacity, count it in the heaviest relevant base (fail-closed)

Opacity is not neutral. It is an advantage to the actor controlling disclosure. OBA therefore imposes a conservative presumption rule
to prevent non-disclosure from becoming a strategy.

### Conservative presumption rule (binding)
- If classification between bases is uncertain due to missing disclosure or obstructed audit, the amount is counted in the **heaviest
  relevant base** (the base that best prevents arbitrage and tail harm).
- Provisional assessments are allowed, subject to dispute-flow and later restatement, but non-disclosure does not suspend enforcement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The presumption hierarchy and “heaviest base” mapping must be published as part of the Codex standards for caps and T.
- Appeals are permitted, but where flight risk or structural opacity exists, escrow/lockbox mechanisms apply until resolution.
