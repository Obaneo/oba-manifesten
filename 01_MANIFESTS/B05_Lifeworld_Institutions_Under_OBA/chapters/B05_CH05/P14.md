# B05.CH05.P14 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes of T-architecture under OBA and defines hard-stop conditions. Fiscal systems are
highly adaptive: once a levy exists, actors build avoidance, optimisation, lobbying, and intermediation around it. The state also
adapts: it accumulates procedures, exceptions, and tooling dependencies. These dynamics predictably regenerate E and undermine
lifeworld stability unless constrained.

CH05 treats failure modes as systemic, not incidental. The response is not moral condemnation; it is redesign discipline: simplify,
requalify, eliminate toll layers, and restore incidence and transparency. Where failure modes cannot be corrected, OBA requires
fail-closed: suspend legitimacy claims and adjust architecture until lifeworld tests pass.

---

## B05.CH05.P14.S01 — Complexity inflation: an industry of exceptions/optimisation (E) grows back → redesign required

Complexity inflation occurs when the tax system becomes a fabric of special cases, exemptions, and interpretive ambiguity. This
inevitably creates an optimisation industry and undermines both fairness and capacity.

### Mechanism
- **Exception proliferation**: new carve-outs are politically cheaper than redesign; each exception adds new edge cases.
- **Interpretive ambiguity**: rules become negotiable; enforcement becomes selective by capacity and power.
- **E regrowth**: paid advisors, brokers, compliance vendors, and structuring specialists become necessary to navigate the system.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Complexity inflation is treated as an E regrowth indicator and triggers redesign: simplify bases, standardise templates, remove
  special regimes, and reduce discretionary interpretation surfaces.
- If the system cannot be simplified without revenue collapse, the base design is wrong and must be re-architected rather than patched.

---

## B05.CH05.P14.S02 — Regressive financing: indirect burdens hit tails → non-conform unless fully compensated and demonstrable

Regressive drift is a structural failure mode: burdens migrate toward low-capacity cohorts through fixed charges, quasi-taxes, and
indirect levies, even while headline progressivity remains.

### Mechanism
- **Indirect incidence**: consumption levies and fixed charges fall harder on low R/B cohorts.
- **Hidden L**: compliance costs, time burdens, and penalty cascades function as regressive taxation.
- **Offset fiction**: compensation is promised but not delivered, or delivered with high friction and drop-off.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If tails worsen (share_below_alpha rises, arrears/disconnects increase, volatility rises), the financing mix is non-conforming unless
  full compensation is demonstrable in public tables and executed with low friction.
- “Compensate later” is not admissible; compensation must be immediate, measurable, and contestable.

---

## B05.CH05.P14.S03 — Capture and carve-outs: exceptions for powerful positions → compatibility breach and audit trigger

Capture manifests fiscally as carve-outs: exemptions, special regimes, discretionary settlements, and preferential definitions for
powerful positions or sectors.

### Mechanism
- **Lobby carve-outs**: regimes tailored to a narrow set of high-capacity actors.
- **Settlement discretion**: negotiated outcomes replace rules; equal treatment collapses.
- **Opacity shields**: disclosure exceptions and proprietary structures block auditability.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- Any repeated exception pattern benefiting concentrated positions triggers an audit-of-the-auditor and a compatibility breach review.
- Carve-outs must be time-bounded, template-based, and evidentially justified; otherwise they are treated as capture and must be removed.

---

## B05.CH05.P14.S04 — Fail-closed: if T increases lifeworld friction (administrative L) or worsens R/B tails, the architecture must be adjusted

The lifeworld test is decisive. A fiscally “successful” system that increases hidden L or worsens tails is incompatible.

### Hard-stop triggers (binding)
- **Administrative L rises structurally**: longer cycle times, increased proof pressure, higher error/appeal backlog, higher drop-off,
  greater dependence on paid help.
- **R/B tails worsen**: higher share_below_alpha, increased tail volatility, more arrears cascades, more essential disconnections.
- **Non-auditable governance**: model-based policy without audit packs, black-box tooling, or suppressed revision logs.

### Fail-closed action
- Suspend legitimacy claims that rely on the failing T architecture.
- Adjust the system: simplify instruments, reduce exceptions, shift incidence away from tails, eliminate fee-stacks, and re-balance
  toward structural L reduction via commons.
- If correction cannot be executed due to capacity loss, reduce scope to what is auditable (fail-closed against drift-through-overreach).
