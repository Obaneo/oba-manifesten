# B05.CH04.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies the primary failure modes of the security and buffers layer and defines hard-stop rules. CH04 fails in
predictable ways: political narratives shift the system from tail stabilisation to discipline; administration turns into a gatekeeping
machine; private industries reconstitute extraction around “help”; and the system continues to scale even while tails worsen.

Under OBA, these are not unfortunate side effects—they are incompatibilities. Security exists to reduce L volatility and stabilise R/B
tails while preserving autonomy and exit power. When security becomes a clamp or a toll ecosystem, it undermines legitimacy and
recreates the very extraction dynamics OBA is designed to neutralise.

---

## B05.CH04.P13.S01 — Moral hazard framing: using “abuse of security” rhetoric to block L reduction (detection)

“Moral hazard” narratives are a recurrent sabotage vector: they shift attention from structural clamp and L drivers to policing the
behaviour of recipients. The result is policy that deters uptake, delays access, and normalises hardship as “discipline.”

### Failure mechanism
- **Recipient scapegoating**: abuse rhetoric substitutes for analysis of essentials L, fee-stacks, and lock-in.
- **Deterrence by friction**: proof pressure, waiting times, and humiliation are justified as “fraud prevention.”
- **Structural avoidance**: price rails, commons expansion, and fee-stack neutralisation are blocked as “too generous.”

### Detection signals (binding)
- Rising **drop-off** and non-uptake alongside increased compliance burdens.
- Eligibility churn and re-assessment cycles justified by “integrity” with weak evidence.
- Public discourse and policy texts that emphasise deterrence more than tail metrics (share_below_alpha, arrears, volatility).

### Mitigation (binding)
- Require incidence-first and tail-first reporting: show whether L is declining and tails are stabilising; rhetoric without metrics is
  non-evidence.
- Fraud controls must be targeted and auditable; generalised deterrence is treated as bureaucratic tolling.

---

## B05.CH04.P13.S02 — Bureaucratic clamp: security becomes dependent on humiliation/control → legitimacy breach

Bureaucratic clamp occurs when security access becomes conditional on submission: invasive proof cycles, behavioural monitoring, and
paternalistic control regimes. This converts rights into hidden L and drives households toward private clamp alternatives (debt,
predatory insurance, abusive employers).

### Failure mechanism
- **Humiliation conditionality**: stigmatizing interviews, punitive discretion, “deservingness” rituals.
- **Control creep**: mandatory budgeting programs, spending monitoring, behavioural scoring.
- **Delay-as-gate**: long lead times, repeated reassessments, opaque denials; appeals become impractical.

### Consequence
- Legitimacy breach: the system is experienced as coercive and selectively punitive, increasing resentment and non-compliance risk.

### Hard rule (binding)
- Security that requires humiliation, control, or surveillance to function is **non-conforming** and must be redesigned toward
  low-friction, rule-bound access with contestability.

---

## B05.CH04.P13.S03 — Private capture: insurance or debt industry builds fee-stacks around buffers → non-conform

Private capture is the reconstitution of extraction around security. Industries whose business models depend on fees, spreads, and
denial games will attempt to position themselves as necessary intermediaries.

### Capture mechanisms
- **Insurance tolling**: opaque exclusions, network gating, denial strategies, paid priority, broker layers.
- **Debt/collections ecosystems**: “arrears management” and “restructuring services” that add fees, penalties, and lock-in.
- **Consultancy and tooling capture**: vendor platforms that become non-exitable infrastructure, enforcing black-box rules.

### Detection signals (binding)
- Rising fee shares in security delivery chains; growth of intermediaries around eligibility and claims.
- Increased denial rates, longer appeals, or divergence between formal coverage and effective access.
- Procurement dependency graphs showing vendor lock-in; inability to audit decision logic.

### Hard rule (binding)
- If buffers route through private fee-stacks or lock-in intermediaries, the security layer is **non-conforming** and must be
  neutralised (contract rewrite/unbundling, conversion to commons/public rails, fee caps, restructuring).

---

## B05.CH04.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: if tails worsen or administrative toll rises, redesign is mandatory

CH04 must be executed as a test layer. If outcomes contradict the purpose, scaling is prohibited.

### Fail-closed triggers (binding)
Redesign (or rollback/scope reduction) is mandatory if, without an external shock explanation:
- **share_below_alpha** fails to decline structurally, or tail volatility increases.
- **Arrears and cascades** (disconnections/evictions) rise or become more persistent.
- **Administrative friction** worsens: lead times, drop-off, appeal backlog, re-assessment churn increase.
- **Selective execution** is detected (region/cohort divergences) without transparent correction.

### Required response (binding)
- Publish restatements and corrective action plans with timelines and measurable targets.
- Reduce scope to what can be executed under capacity and privacy constraints.
- Requalify and eliminate newly emerged toll layers (fees, intermediaries, clamp contracts) rather than “optimising” them.
