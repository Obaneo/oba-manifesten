# B05.CH04.P10 — Financing architecture (link to T and ΔM)

This paragraph specifies how security and buffers are financed under OBA. Financing is not neutral: it can either reinforce the order
(by funding commons capacity and tail stabilisation transparently) or it can reintroduce extraction (via toll-like contributions,
opaque intermediation, and ΔM leakage into claims and asset prices). CH04 therefore treats financing architecture as a binding
interface with the tax/contribution layer (T) and the monetary/channel discipline layer (ΔM).

The core requirements are:
- contributions must function as legitimacy and exchange for commons stability—not as hidden tolls;
- ΔM usage (when required under shocks) must be restricted to allowed channels with leakage tests;
- buffers and maintenance budgets must be ring-fenced against political raiding and vendor capture; and
- no-arbitrage: security cannot become a bypass route around caps or a subsidy to E positions.

---

## B05.CH04.P10.S01 — T architecture: contributions as legitimacy, not as toll; transparent destination

Security financing must be visible and trustable. Opaque financing breeds resentment and creates space for carve-outs.

### T principles for security financing (binding)
- **Incidence-first**: design contributions based on who actually bears the burden, not on legal labels.
- **Transparent destination**: publish where contributions go (income floor, insurance pools, fallback commons, admin overhead) and the
  measured lifeworld effects.
- **No mandatory intermediation toll**: avoid structures where access to publicly mandated security requires paid intermediaries
  (brokerage, “service fees,” paid claims processors).
- **Overhead disclosure**: administrative costs are published explicitly; if overhead grows, it is treated as hidden L and a drift
  signal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any contribution earmarked for security must have a published destination table and method_id for incidence and effect analysis.
- Financing that is structurally regressive (worsens tails) is non-conforming unless fully compensated in demonstrable ways (L
  reduction or transfers).

## B05.CH04.P10.S02 — ΔM channel role: shock absorption via allowed channels; leakage tests mandatory

Under systemic shocks, T alone may not adjust quickly enough; ΔM may be required for temporary shock absorption. This is a high-risk
zone for asset/claim support leakage.

### Allowed ΔM usage (binding)
- Temporary stabilisation of essentials provision (baseline access and continuity).
- Temporary income support to stabilise R/B tails.
- Temporary capex/opex for commons logistics and fallback capacity needed to maintain continuity.

### Leakage discipline (binding)
- **Tagging and traceability**: funds must be tagged to allowed purposes and auditable flows.
- **Leakage tests**: test whether funds are inflating claims (fees, rents, interest spreads) or asset prices rather than reducing L or
  building capacity.
- **Sunset and unwind**: ΔM usage must terminate and unwind under a published plan (link CH04.P06).

### Hard rule
- Under opacity or refusal to disclose downstream flows, apply conservative presumptions: restrict instruments, route through direct
  provision, or halt deployment pending audit.

## B05.CH04.P10.S03 — Ring-fencing: buffers and maintenance budgets are protected from reallocation toward E

Buffers and fallback commons only work if their capacity is maintained across political cycles. Budget raiding converts security into a
façade.

### Ring-fencing mechanisms (binding)
- **Protected budgets**: maintenance and readiness funds for fallback commons and key buffer instruments cannot be diverted to other
  purposes without explicit procedures and public justification.
- **Maintenance-first discipline**: staffing/training and backlog reduction funds are treated as infrastructure (ΔG analogue), not as
  discretionary overhead.
- **Anti-vendor capture**: procurement and tooling contracts must preserve exitability; otherwise ring-fenced budgets become a vendor
  annuity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If ring-fenced funds are repeatedly reallocated, or if maintenance backlogs grow structurally, the system is in breach and must
  escalate (capacity breach protocols, governance reset where required).

---

## B05.CH04.P10.S04 — No-arbitrage: security must not be used to evade caps or subsidise E

Security instruments can be exploited as bypass routes: turning benefits into hidden compensation, shifting returns into protected
channels, or using public buffers to preserve private rent streams.

### No-arbitrage rules (binding)
- **No cap bypass**: security may not function as disguised high-income/high-wealth compensation (perks, deferrals, tailored
  exemptions).
- **No E subsidy**: public funds must not preserve fee-stacks, penalty interest streams, or rentier margins; where claims are
  unsustainable, restructuring is preferred (link CH06).
- **Eligibility integrity**: prevent steering through legal form (relabel income, shift assets, create synthetic exposure) to extract
  security benefits unfairly.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any security program must be tested for arbitrage routes (I↔W↔T↔benefits) and must publish anti-avoidance rules or conservative
  presumptions under opacity.
- If a security instrument demonstrably strengthens E positions (e.g., raises rents/fees, expands collections ecosystems), it must be
  redesigned or replaced with direct L reduction/commons capacity.
