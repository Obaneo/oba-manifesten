# B05.CH04.P08 — Prevention: early-warning in the lifeworld

This paragraph specifies prevention as a lifeworld early-warning layer: mechanisms that detect deterioration before it becomes a tail
collapse. Under OBA, prevention is not “nudging individuals” to behave differently; it is **system maintenance**. The goal is to
identify rising clamp pressure (arrears, waiting times, essentials L spikes, incident growth) and to intervene early with
standardised, auditable instruments that reduce L and stabilise R/B tails.

Prevention is also a capture surface: “help” easily becomes a private intermediation market (fees, counselling products, collections
ecosystems) or a discretionary control regime. CH04 therefore binds prevention to minimal indicators, traceable instruments, and a
public correction loop (CSA) that forces restatements when prevention fails or becomes washing.

---

## B05.CH04.P08.S01 — Indicators: arrears, waiting-time spikes, essentials-index breaches, incident rates (aggregated)

Early warning relies on a small set of high-signal indicators that correlate strongly with tail collapse.

### Minimum indicator set (binding)
- **Arrears indicators**: arrears rates and growth in essentials domains (rent/mortgage, energy, key bills), including duration bands.
- **Waiting-time spikes**: increases in decision/appeal lead times for security instruments and in essential service pathways (care,
  housing, legal aid), indicating capacity stress.
- **Essentials-index breaches**: breaches of affordability bands for essential baskets (L spikes), including volatility measures.
- **Incident rates**: disconnections, evictions, service interruptions, denial spikes, and other adverse events (aggregated).

### Publication and method discipline (binding)
- Indicators must be published as join-safe aggregates with method_id and revision policy.
- Thresholds for “spike/breach” must be pre-specified; ex post threshold changes are treated as drift unless restated transparently.

---

## B05.CH04.P08.S02 — Prevention instruments: mediation, restructuring, temporary subsidies with traceability

When indicators breach thresholds, prevention instruments must activate through standard templates rather than ad-hoc discretion.

### Instrument families (binding set)
- **Mediation**: standardised dispute resolution for rent/utility/benefit conflicts, with time limits and enforceable outcomes.
- **Restructuring**: early debt/arrears restructuring templates (fee waivers, rate reduction, payment plans) to stop cascades (link CH06).
- **Temporary subsidies**: time-bounded support targeted to specific L spikes (e.g., energy shock), with explicit sunset and unwind.
- **Provisional relief**: immediate temporary relief while disputes/appeals are processed, to prevent irreversible harm (eviction,
  disconnection).

### Traceability requirements (binding)
- Each intervention must carry traceability: what indicator triggered it, which rule/template was used, what duration/sunset applies,
  and what outcome metrics will be reported.
- Temporary instruments must include an unwind plan; otherwise they become permanent support channels that invite capture.

---

## B05.CH04.P08.S03 — Anti-avoidance: prevent prevention from routing through private fee-stacks (non-conform)

Prevention frequently degenerates into an industry: debt advisors charging fees, collections firms expanding “services,” landlords or
utilities externalising costs through intermediaries. Under OBA, this is E regrowth.

### Prohibited prevention patterns (binding)
- Mandatory use of private intermediaries to access public prevention tools.
- Fee-stacked “support” products (administration fees, counselling fees, brokerage) attached to restructuring or mediation.
- Prevention that preserves claim stacks (penalty interest, junk fees) while merely smoothing payment timing.

### Anti-avoidance requirements (binding)
- Where private actors are involved, fees must be disclosed, capped, and justified as functional cost (P), otherwise requalified as E.
- Under opacity, conservative presumptions apply: restrict instruments to public/commons rails or require contract rewrite.

## B05.CH04.P08.S04 — Public correction loop: a CSA-like feedback loop on failing buffers (link CH01)

Prevention must be self-correcting. If early-warning indicators worsen despite interventions, the system must restate claims, redesign
templates, and—if necessary—scale back to what can be executed under capacity and privacy constraints.

### CSA-like loop (binding)
- **Periodic + event-driven review**: scheduled reviews plus automatic review when breach thresholds are hit.
- **Contestability**: citizens/organisations can challenge prevention claims via dispute flow; challenges can trigger audits and schema
  revisions.
- **Restatements**: when indicators were mismeasured or interventions mis-specified, outputs and claims must be restated publicly.
- **Escalation**: repeated breach patterns trigger higher-level interventions (fallback commons expansion, policy redesign, enforcement
  against fee-stack abuse).

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If prevention operates as reporting without redesign (“dashboard-only”), it is non-conforming.
- If prevention cannot be executed reliably (capacity/measurement failure), the system must fail-closed: narrow scope, strengthen
  buffers, and rebuild instruments before claiming effectiveness.
