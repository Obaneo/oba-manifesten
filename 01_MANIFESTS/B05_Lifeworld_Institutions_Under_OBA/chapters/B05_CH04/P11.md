# B05.CH04.P11 — Public versus local execution (institutions and governance)

This paragraph specifies the governance and execution model for security and buffers across levels of government. Under OBA,
security rights must be **uniform in substance** (central minima) while implementation may vary **parametrically** at local level to
fit context and capacity—provided local variation remains within rails and is auditable.

Security is a high-risk domain for selective execution and capture: local discretion can become patronage, and national systems can
become bureaucratic clamp. CH04 therefore requires explicit separation: what is fixed (rights, minima, definitions, evidence rules),
what is local (parameters, operational choices), and how contestability, auditability, and anti-capture are enforced. Execution
capacity itself is treated as infrastructure (ΔG of governance): if the system cannot execute quickly and fairly, rights are illusory.

---

## B05.CH04.P11.S01 — Central minima, local parameters: uniform rights with local implementation within rails

Uniform rights prevent postcode lotteries; local parameters allow adaptation without semantic drift.

### Central minima (binding, non-negotiable)
- **Rights package**: minimum income floor logic, baseline insurance coverage, fallback commons availability, emergency access rails.
- **Definition binding**: Definition Set II (B/T/N/L/R), method discipline, publication minima.
- **Procedural minima**: maximum decision lead times, appeal rights, provisional relief rules, non-discrimination constraints.
- **Forbidden patterns**: no surveillance-based eligibility, no humiliation conditionality, no fee-stack intermediation.

### Local parameter space (allowed within rails)
- Operational thresholds within published ranges (e.g., local capacity triggers, service-level parameters).
- Local service delivery design (one-stop interfaces, staffing models, logistics), provided outputs remain comparable and join-safe.
- Local prioritisation under scarcity within explicit boundary rules and transparent logs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Local variation MUST be published as parameter sets (versioned) and must be compatible with central minima.
- If local parameters produce systematic cohort/region harm (tails worsen, access declines), variation must be corrected or overridden.

## B05.CH04.P11.S02 — Contestability: objection/appeal, audit of the guardian, public performance registers

Security systems govern through decisions; therefore decisions must be contestable and performance must be visible.

### Contestability rails (binding)
- **Objection/appeal**: time-bounded disputes; access to calculations and criteria; provisional relief in high-harm cases.
- **Audit of the guardian**: meta-audits of auditors and executing bodies; rotation and independence rules where appropriate.
- **Public performance registers**: publish lead times, error/appeal rates, reversal rates, uptake/drop-off, and backlog indicators
  by region (join-safe), including remediation status.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If appeal pathways are slow, opaque, or retaliatory, the system becomes a clamp and is non-conforming.
- If performance registers cannot be produced reliably, scope must be reduced and execution capacity rebuilt (fail-closed).

---

## B05.CH04.P11.S03 — Anti-capture: no discretionary exception deals; everything via templates and audit packs

Discretion is the primary capture vector in security. Exceptions become permanent carve-outs; intermediaries emerge; rules are bent for
powerful positions.

### Anti-capture requirements (binding)
- **No bespoke deals**: security access and remedies must not be negotiated case-by-case through political influence or vendor
  relationships.
- **Templates**: standardised decision templates for eligibility, restructuring, and emergency measures.
- **Audit packs**: decisions must carry evidence packs (inputs, rule path, method_id, logs) sufficient for review and restatement.
- **Exceptions register**: any exception must be logged, justified, time-bounded, and published in aggregate with sunset/rollback
  discipline.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If exceptions proliferate or cluster around the same actors/regions, this is treated as capture evidence and triggers escalation
  (forensic audits, governance reset, restriction of discretion).

## B05.CH04.P11.S04 — Continuity: execution capacity as infrastructure (ΔG of governance)

Security without execution capacity is a façade. Capacity is maintained through staffing, tooling, standardisation, and incident
discipline.

### Capacity-as-infrastructure components (binding)
- **Lead-time norms**: maximum decision and payment timelines; breach triggers automatic escalation and provisional relief.
- **Backlog management**: backlog measurement, thresholds, and reduction plans; persistent backlog is a capacity breach.
- **Tooling and interoperability**: systems must be exitable, auditable, and not vendor-locked; black-box computation is prohibited.
- **Incident discipline**: publish aggregated incident logs (delays, failures, privacy issues) and post-mortem remediation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If capacity collapses (persistent delays, high error rates), the system must scale back to what is executable and rebuild—rather than
  pretending rights exist.
- If continuity depends on vendor-controlled platforms that cannot be audited or exited, the governance layer is captured and must be
  corrected.
