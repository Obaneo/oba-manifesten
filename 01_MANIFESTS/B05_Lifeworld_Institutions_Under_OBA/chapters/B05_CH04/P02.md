# B05.CH04.P02 — Risk taxonomy (what must be covered)

This paragraph defines the canonical risk taxonomy for the security/buffer layer. Under OBA, “security” is only meaningful if it
covers the dominant shock classes that push households into tail collapse: arrears, forced lock-in, debt spirals, and loss of
participation capacity. Risk coverage is therefore specified as an order requirement: the system must identify shock domains,
predefine coverage logic, and publish measurable trigger/response rules.

The taxonomy is not a list of policy programs. It is a risk map used to (1) define minimum coverage domains, (2) bind instruments to
observable lifeworld outcomes (B/T/N/L/R and tails), and (3) prevent the common failure mode of selective coverage—where politically
salient risks are covered while silent risks (time, care, administrative clamp) are externalised.

---

## B05.CH04.P02.S01 — Life risks: illness, disability, old age, care dependency, death

Life risks are high-severity, often low-frequency events with large tail impacts. Their defining feature is that they can destroy
income capacity and create persistent needs (care, accessibility, medical cost exposure).

### Risk class description
- **Illness and injury**: acute events and chronic conditions affecting work capacity and generating direct and indirect costs.
- **Disability**: long-run capability constraints requiring support, accommodations, and stable access pathways.
- **Old age**: predictable lifecycle transition with risks of income decline and increased care dependency.
- **Care dependency**: need for ongoing support services (home care, long-term care, assistive devices, respite).
- **Death**: survivor risks (income loss, housing insecurity, funeral costs), and administrative/legal burdens.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The security layer MUST prevent life risks from translating into permanent clamp via debt, insurer denial games, or bureaucratic
  gatekeeping.
- Coverage must include continuity rails (access, lead times, appeals) and must publish waiting-time and denial indicators.

## B05.CH04.P02.S02 — Labour risks: job loss, income volatility, sector transition, skills obsolescence

Labour risks are the dominant pathway by which macro change becomes micro instability. Under OBA, labour transition is expected as E
contracts and P/C expands; security must therefore stabilise transition without disciplining people into bad jobs.

### Risk class description
- **Job loss**: sudden income loss, often coupled with timing risk (bills due immediately; benefits delayed).
- **Income volatility**: variable hours, gig work, seasonal work, bonus dependence; volatility is itself a tail risk.
- **Sector transition**: displacement, mismatching, relocation needs, and temporary wage dips during reskilling.
- **Skills obsolescence**: declining demand for certain tasks; requires retraining and mobility support.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Instruments must stabilise R/B during transitions (fast access, minimal proof burden, time-bounded decisions).
- “Activation” regimes that rely on humiliation, surveillance, or forced acceptance of clamp contracts are non-conforming.

## B05.CH04.P02.S03 — Essentials risks: housing-cost shock, energy prices, mobility constraints, digital exclusion

Essentials risks are concentrated in L domains. They generate tail collapse even when income is stable, because the burden side spikes
or access is gated.

### Risk class description
- **Housing-cost shock**: rent spikes, interest resets, eviction threats, sudden repair costs, deposit loss, moving friction.
- **Energy price shock**: tariff spikes, disconnection threats, advance-payment clamp, heating costs in cold periods.
- **Mobility constraints**: car dependence under cost shocks, public transport failures, regional access deserts, disability mobility.
- **Digital exclusion**: identity lockouts, payment rail denial, device/software lock-in, required digital access for public rights.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Security mechanisms must prioritise L stabilisation in essentials: prevent arrears cascades, disconnections, eviction spirals, and
  identity/payment lockouts.
- Any “support” that operates by subsidising asset prices or provider margins (rather than reducing L or expanding commons capacity)
  is presumed to leak and must be tested under ΔM rails.

## B05.CH04.P02.S04 — System shocks: inflation, supply disruptions, financial stress, geopolitical blockades

System shocks are macro events that propagate into the lifeworld through price breaks, shortages, and institutional strain. They
require predefined emergency regimes to avoid ad-hoc discretionary capture.

### Risk class description
- **Inflation and price breaks**: rapid cost increases in essentials; erosion of real R; risk of wage/price spirals affecting access.
- **Supply disruptions**: shortages of energy, food, medicines, materials; logistics failures; rationing needs.
- **Financial stress**: credit tightening, bank failures, payment system instability; increased default and arrears risk.
- **Geopolitical blockades**: trade disruptions, sanctions, cyber/energy attacks, cross-border constraints impacting essentials.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- System shocks must trigger **temporary, controlled** regimes (CH04.P06): measurable triggers, public notice, sunsets, unwind plans,
  and post-mortem audits.
- Emergency responses must not become permanent, and must not route through asset/claim support or vendor capture (ΔM hard rails).
