# B05.CH04.P03 — Income floor as a lifeworld invariant (minimum R)

This paragraph specifies the **income floor** as a lifeworld invariant: a minimum level of **residual space (R)** that prevents tail
collapse and preserves exit power. Under OBA, the floor is not primarily a redistribution symbol; it is a stability rail. If a
material share of households repeatedly falls below α (share_below_alpha), the order becomes permanently exposed to arrears cascades,
predatory credit, forced lock-in, and legitimacy breakdown.

The income floor must therefore be designed as an anti-clamp instrument: it stabilises R/B tails without opening new extraction
channels and without converting access into a bureaucratic toll market. The floor is implemented through an instrument mix—cash,
in-kind commons, and essentials price discipline—chosen to reduce L volatility and to make the floor robust under shocks.

---

## B05.CH04.P03.S01 — Target: structural decline in share_below_alpha; tail-risk as the primary metric

The floor is evaluated on tail metrics, not on averages or on headline “poverty lines” as rhetoric.

### Primary target (binding)
- **Structural decline in share_below_alpha**: persistent reduction in the proportion of households below the minimum R threshold α.
- **Tail-risk reduction**: lower volatility of R/B, fewer abrupt collapses, faster recovery after shocks.
- **Distributional visibility**: improvements must be visible across cohorts/regions (join-safe), not only at national averages.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any floor design MUST publish the operational definition of α (reference to canonical definition set) and must report
  share_below_alpha and tail behaviour over time.
- If median outcomes improve while tails remain unstable, the floor is failing its primary purpose and must be redesigned.

---

## B05.CH04.P03.S02 — Instrument mix: cash, in-kind commons, essentials price discipline (L reduction)

A robust floor is rarely cash-only. Under OBA, the floor is implemented via a mix that stabilises both sides of the lifeworld balance:
increase effective resources and reduce burdens (L), especially in essentials.

### Instrument classes (binding set)
- **Cash transfers**: fast, predictable, and low-friction support for immediate liquidity needs.
- **In-kind commons**: baseline access to essentials (energy baseline, care continuity, food support, shelter), reducing L directly.
- **Essentials price discipline**: mechanisms that reduce volatility and clamp in housing/energy/food/digital (L reduction rather than
  compensating ever-higher prices).

### Design rule
- Prefer instruments that reduce L and volatility structurally; avoid instruments that merely chase rising prices and thus subsidise
  provider margins or asset values.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any cash instrument must be evaluated alongside L: if prices/fees rise and absorb transfers, the system is leaking into extraction
  and must be corrected (price rails/commons expansion).
- Any in-kind commons must meet access/maintenance/contestability minima; “voucher” regimes that become broker fee-stacks are
  non-conforming.

---

## B05.CH04.P03.S03 — Conditionality: limited, transparent, non-disciplining; no bureaucratic toll

Conditionality is a high-risk clamp surface. Under OBA, conditions are permissible only when narrowly justified by integrity needs and
when they do not function as humiliation, behavioural steering, or hidden rationing.

### Conditionality rails (binding)
- **Limited**: only conditions necessary to prevent clear abuse; no broad lifestyle policing.
- **Transparent**: published criteria, machine-readable where possible, with appeal rights and decision logs.
- **Non-disciplining**: conditions must not force acceptance of clamp jobs, invasive monitoring, or punitive proof cycles.
- **Low-friction**: minimal proof burden; pre-fill; time-bounded decisions; provisional support during disputes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If conditionality increases drop-off, delays, or stigma-driven non-uptake, it is functioning as bureaucratic tolling (paperwork-as-E)
  and must be redesigned.
- Surveillance-linked access (behavioural tracking, scoring) is categorically non-conforming.

---

## B05.CH04.P03.S04 — Relation to work: the floor stabilises transition and strengthens exit power (link CH02)

The floor is coupled to work, but not as discipline. Its function is to make labour markets more contestable by reducing desperation.

### Work linkage (binding)
- **Transition stabiliser**: enables reskilling, sector shifts, and job changes without catastrophic dips in R/B.
- **Exit power**: reduces acceptance of exploitative contracts (schedule capture, non-competes, fee-stacks) by providing a credible
  fallback.
- **Anti-precariat rail**: dampens volatility in variable-hour work and prevents small shocks from turning into debt spirals.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If the floor is designed primarily to push people into any job regardless of clamp dynamics, it is non-conforming (it increases E’s
  leverage).
- The floor must be assessed by its effect on mobility/exit indicators (job switching without arrears spikes, reduced acceptance of
  predatory credit), not only by employment rates.
