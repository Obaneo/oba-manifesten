# B05.CH04.P12 — Measurement and publication requirements (security as lifeworld test)

This paragraph specifies the measurement and publication surface required to execute CH04 as a lifeworld test. Security and buffers
cannot be validated by budgets, entitlement texts, or program counts. They are validated by whether **R/B tails stabilise**, whether
arrears and cascades decline, whether shock regimes behave as designed (temporary, auditable, non-leaking), and whether fallback
commons are reachable in practice.

The publication surface must be sufficient to detect washing, selective execution, and capture—while remaining strictly privacy-minimal.
All outputs must be linked to pinned definition sets (B/T/N/L/R) and must publish method_id, revision policy, and (where relevant)
uncertainty bands.

---

## B05.CH04.P12.S01 — Core statistics: share_below_alpha, tail volatility, arrears rates, uptake, drop-off

Core statistics must show whether the security layer is achieving its primary function: reducing tail collapse and preventing cascades.

### Required core statistics (minimum; binding)

**Tail metrics**
- **share_below_alpha** (by cohort/region within join-safety constraints).
- **Tail volatility** of R/B (volatility measures and tail-event frequency).
- **Time-to-recovery** proxies after shocks (aggregated; program-linked where feasible).

**Cascade metrics**
- **Arrears rates** and arrears duration bands in key essentials domains (housing, energy, critical bills).
- **Disconnections/evictions** (where measurable and publishable as aggregates) and prevention outcomes.

**Access metrics**
- **Uptake**: eligible → served rates for key instruments (income floor components, insurance pathways, buffer schemes).
- **Drop-off**: started but not completed, eligible but not served, and appeal/abandonment rates (with reason taxonomy aggregated).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Core statistics MUST be published with distributional visibility; “overall totals” are insufficient evidence.
- If tails do not improve (or worsen) while program outputs increase, the system is washing or mis-specified and must be redesigned.

## B05.CH04.P12.S02 — Shock dashboards: trigger events, instrument deployment, outcomes, sunsets, incident logs

Shock regimes must be auditable as controlled, temporary interventions rather than political discretion.

### Required shock dashboard elements (minimum; binding)
- **Trigger events**: which indicators breached which thresholds, with method_id and definition-set reference.
- **Instrument deployment**: what instruments were activated, where, for whom, at what scale, and under which templates.
- **Outcomes**: effects on essentials indices (L), arrears, share_below_alpha, and service continuity; include timing and lags.
- **Sunsets and unwind**: planned end dates, renewal decisions (if any) with re-trigger evidence, and unwind progress status.
- **Incident logs**: procurement incidents, delivery failures, fraud anomalies, privacy events, and corrective actions (aggregated).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any emergency regime without a public dashboard (trigger → action → outcome → sunset) is non-conforming.
- Renewal without re-trigger evidence is treated as permanent emergency drift and must trigger escalation.

## B05.CH04.P12.S03 — Fallback commons metrics: coverage, waiting times, service levels, backlog

Fallback commons must be measured as infrastructure capacity, not as charity distribution.

### Required fallback metrics (minimum; binding)
- **Coverage**: geographic/service coverage for fallback domains (shelter, energy baseline, food support, care continuity, legal aid).
- **Waiting times**: lead times to access (median + tails) and breach rates against published maxima.
- **Service levels**: minimum service package adherence and continuity indicators (uptime/availability, throughput capacity).
- **Backlog**: backlog size/age distributions; maintenance readiness indicators; staffing vacancy/turnover proxies.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- “Fallback exists” claims require measurable access and lead-time evidence. If access is delayed beyond crisis timescales, fallback is
  effectively absent and the system fails the lifeworld test.
- Persistent backlog growth is a capacity breach and triggers remediation and potential scope reduction elsewhere.

---

## B05.CH04.P12.S04 — Privacy minimalism: aggregates, suppression, no conditional personal tracking systems

Security measurement is prone to surveillance drift (“to prevent abuse, track everything”). OBA treats this as non-conforming.

### Privacy rails (binding)
- **Aggregates only** for public outputs; apply small-n suppression and join-safety controls.
- **No conditional personal tracking systems**: access may not require continuous behavioural monitoring, spending surveillance, or
  scoring regimes.
- **Purpose limitation**: data collected for security execution cannot become general social control infrastructure.
- **Retention caps** and access logs for sensitive records; publish retention and incident policies.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If privacy minimalism cannot be maintained (due to tooling design, vendor constraints, or policy drift), the measurement system must
  fail-closed: no compatibility claim and no scaling until redesigned.
