# B05.CH04.P04 — Household-level buffers (without moral hazard and without clamp dynamics)

This paragraph specifies household-level buffers as a second line of defence behind the income floor. The floor prevents structural
tail collapse; buffers prevent **small, frequent shocks** from cascading into arrears, predatory credit, and lock-in. Under OBA,
buffers must be designed to **increase resilience without creating clamp**: no humiliation-based conditionality, no surveillance
budgeting regimes, and no fee-stack ecosystems around “help.”

The design constraint is dual:
1) buffers must be effective in stabilising R/B tails and reducing arrears/default incidence; and  
2) buffers must not become an E-surface (penalty fees, optimisation intermediaries, control contracts) or a behavioural control tool.

---

## B05.CH04.P04.S01 — Liquidity buffer: minimum reserve/buffer rules, automatic build-up where possible

Liquidity buffers handle predictable micro-shocks (repairs, temporary income gaps, deposits, travel for care). Without liquidity, the
system routes households into high-cost credit and fee cascades.

### Buffer logic
- **Minimum reserve target**: a baseline liquid reserve level sufficient to cover typical shocks (parameterisable).
- **Automatic build-up**: where feasible, buffers are built via default mechanisms (payroll split, automatic rounding, matched savings)
  to avoid reliance on high agency/administrative capacity.
- **Access discipline**: withdrawals must be possible without punitive friction; buffers are not meaningful if they are locked behind
  discretionary gates.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any buffer scheme MUST avoid turning reserves into a private product stack (account fees, mandatory advisory services, penalties).
- If automatic mechanisms are used, they must be opt-out/contestable and must not create new lock-in relationships with providers.

## B05.CH04.P04.S02 — Debt and arrears buffer: early intervention without penalty interest/fee-stacks

Arrears are a primary tail accelerator: once arrears exist, fee cascades and penalty interest turn temporary gaps into durable clamp.
Buffers must therefore include early intervention rails that stop escalation.

### Early-intervention components (binding)
- **Fast triage**: detect arrears early (aggregated signals where possible) and offer standardised remedies before penalties accrue.
- **Penalty containment**: cap or suspend penalty interest and junk fees during verified hardship or system-error periods.
- **Restructuring pathways**: standard templates for payment plans, rate reductions, fee waivers, and (where necessary) principal
  adjustments (link CH06 debt/credit logic).
- **No collections extraction**: bailiff/collections ecosystems that add fees are treated as E and must be neutralised or replaced.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- “Early intervention” that routes through private intermediaries charging fees is non-conforming.
- If arrears support requires long proof cycles or humiliating interviews, it is bureaucratic clamp and must be redesigned.

## B05.CH04.P04.S03 — Time buffer: leave/scheduling rights as shock absorbers (link CH03)

Many shocks are time shocks: a sick child, care emergencies, administrative appointments, sudden travel needs. Without time buffers,
households lose income and spiral into arrears.

### Time buffer design (binding)
- **Leave rights**: portable entitlements for sickness/care emergencies and recovery time, not employer-granted privileges.
- **Scheduling protections**: predictability and anti-cancellation compensation; limits on on-call coercion (link CH03.P03).
- **Administrative time**: time rights must cover mandatory procedures (medical, care, legal), or else access becomes a hidden L gate.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Time buffers must be portable and non-retaliatory; otherwise they become a clamp instrument (employer/insurer control).
- If time buffers exist only for high-status contracts while precarious workers lack them, the system produces selective execution and
  must be corrected.

---

## B05.CH04.P04.S04 — Privacy/agency: no mandatory micromanagement budgeting as a condition

Buffer policy often drifts into paternalism: mandatory budgeting programs, spending controls, intrusive monitoring. Under OBA, this is
treated as a clamp surface and a surveillance rent risk.

### Agency and privacy rails (binding)
- **No mandatory micromanagement**: budgeting coaching may exist as an opt-in support, but cannot be a condition for baseline buffers.
- **No behavioural monitoring**: spending tracking and “compliance scoring” are prohibited as access conditions.
- **Minimal proof burden**: eligibility should rely on simple, auditable criteria and standard procedures, not intimate life audits.

### Conformance rule (binding)
- If a buffer regime depends on control, humiliation, or invasive data linkage to prevent “abuse,” it is non-conforming and must be
  redesigned toward structural remedies (price rails, fee caps, simpler eligibility, and fraud controls that do not profile everyone).
