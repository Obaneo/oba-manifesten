# B05.CH04.P07 — Fallback commons: “last resort” infrastructure

This paragraph specifies fallback commons as the **last-resort infrastructure** that prevents catastrophic lifeworld collapse when
the income floor, household buffers, and standard service systems fail or are temporarily overwhelmed. Under OBA, fallback commons are
not optional “safety nets” that can be politically hollowed out without consequence; they are constitutive for stability. Without a
credible last resort, households accept clamp contracts (predatory debt, exploitative work, abusive intermediaries) because the
alternative is existential.

Fallback commons must be designed as commons infrastructure: access within explicit rules, maintenance-first capacity (ΔG analogue),
contestability, and anti-capture protections. They must also be **anti-stigma** by design; stigma is not merely an ethical issue—it is
a functional failure mode that reduces uptake and converts rights into hidden L.

---

## B05.CH04.P07.S01 — Domains: shelter/housing, energy baseline, food support, care continuity, legal assistance

Fallback commons cover domains where failure becomes immediately existential or rapidly cascading.

### Minimum domain set (binding)
- **Shelter/housing**: emergency accommodation, eviction prevention pathways, temporary housing throughput, basic habitability.
- **Energy baseline**: protection against disconnection, minimum heat/power provision for health and participation.
- **Food support**: baseline essentials access and emergency distribution capacity (including logistics).
- **Care continuity**: emergency continuity for childcare/care/health pathways to prevent household collapse (link CH03/CH09).
- **Legal assistance**: access to rights enforcement in crises (appeals, eviction defence, debt/collections disputes), because lack of
  legal capacity is a clamp amplifier.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Each domain must have a defined service package, capacity minima, and escalation triggers when demand exceeds capacity.
- “Fallback” must be operational, not symbolic: if the last resort cannot be accessed in practice, it does not exist.

---

## B05.CH04.P07.S02 — Access: fast eligibility with minimal proof burden; non-discrimination

Fallback is time-critical. Access must therefore be fast, low-friction, and non-discriminatory.

### Access rails (binding)
- **Fast eligibility**: time-bounded decision norms; provisional access during disputes where basic needs are at risk.
- **Minimal proof burden**: accept reasonable evidence; avoid repeated re-verification; do not require complex documentation in crisis.
- **Non-discrimination**: explicit exclusion rules are prohibited unless justified by safety and published; discrimination audits are
  mandatory (aggregated).
- **Contestability**: denial decisions must be appealable with clear escalation paths.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If access requires long waiting times or humiliating proof cycles, fallback becomes a bureaucratic clamp and is non-conforming.
- If eligibility is selectively enforced (region/class differences without transparency), legitimacy breach is triggered.

---

## B05.CH04.P07.S03 — Continuity: maintenance-first, stock/logistics, crisis staffing

Fallback commons must endure shocks; therefore they must be maintained as infrastructure, not improvised in crisis.

### Continuity requirements (binding)
- **Maintenance-first**: ring-fenced budgets for upkeep, readiness, and capacity planning (not drained in “normal times”).
- **Stock/logistics**: reserves, distribution plans, redundancy, and procurement rails that avoid vendor lock-in.
- **Crisis staffing**: surge capacity plans, trained reserve staff pools, and cross-agency coordination protocols.
- **Incident discipline**: public incident logs (aggregated), post-mortem audits, and restatements where failures occur.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Persistent backlog and readiness decay constitute a capacity breach; it must trigger escalation and remediation.
- Emergency procurement must remain exitable and auditable; “crisis vendor capture” is treated as a hard stop.

---

## B05.CH04.P07.S04 — Anti-stigma: design that normalises access rather than disciplines

Stigma converts formal rights into hidden L: people delay access, ration themselves, or avoid the system altogether, increasing tail
collapse probability. OBA treats anti-stigma as functional design, not moral messaging.

### Anti-stigma design principles (binding)
- **Normalised access**: services are designed as baseline rights with clear rules, not as discretionary charity.
- **Dignified interfaces**: minimal humiliation; respectful procedures; privacy in service delivery.
- **No behavioural conditions**: access cannot be contingent on compliance theatre (classes, monitoring, “deservingness” rituals) beyond
  narrow integrity checks.
- **Communication discipline**: public narratives must not frame fallback users as abusers; “moral hazard” rhetoric is treated as a
  sabotage vector unless evidenced.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If uptake is low due to stigma signals (survey-based, experience evidence), the system must redesign interfaces and procedures.
- If stigma is used as policy (deterrence), the regime is non-conforming because it externalises shocks and increases clamp pressure.
