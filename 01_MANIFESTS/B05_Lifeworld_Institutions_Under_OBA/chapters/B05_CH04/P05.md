# B05.CH04.P05 — Insurance: public/commons rails versus private toll

This paragraph specifies insurance as a security instrument and defines the boundary between legitimate risk pooling (P) and
extractive insurance tolling (E). Under OBA, insurance is permitted and often necessary because it smooths tail events across time and
populations. However, insurance is also a prime clamp surface: premium discrimination, network gating, claims denial games,
administrative friction, and fee-stacked intermediation can convert a protective instrument into an extraction and control regime.

CH04 therefore treats baseline insurance coverage as order infrastructure that must be delivered via public/commons rails or via
private providers strictly constrained by anti-toll rules. The primary design goals are: universal baseline coverage (minimum set),
prohibition of lock-in and hidden L, and enforceable contestability through transparent terms, fast objection, and audit of denials.

---

## B05.CH04.P05.S01 — Functional definition: insurance as risk pooling (P) vs premium/claims manipulation (E)

Insurance is classified by function, not label. The key question is whether the arrangement pools risk to deliver predictable coverage
or whether it profits primarily from information asymmetry, denial, and clamp.

### P (risk pooling) characteristics
- Premiums and contributions fund a defined coverage package with predictable access.
- Claims are paid under transparent rules; denials are exceptional, justified, and appealable.
- Administration is bounded and purpose-limited; complexity is not a profit engine.

### E (toll/clamp) characteristics
- Profit derived from denial patterns, opaque exclusions, network gating, or “utilisation management” that functions as rationing.
- Fee-stacks around access (brokers, billing/claims layers, paid priority).
- Administrative friction used as a gate (paperwork loops, repeated proof, delays), shifting costs onto households and providers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Insurance schemes MUST publish a functional decomposition: contribution → coverage → claims decision rules → appeal rails.
- Under opacity or obstruction (terms not disclosed; denial reasons not reproducible), schemes are presumed to contain E components and
  must be requalified or restricted (fail-closed against hidden tolling).

## B05.CH04.P05.S02 — Baseline coverage: minimum set (health, disability, unemployment, old age)

Baseline coverage defines the minimum protection required to prevent predictable tail collapses.

### Minimum baseline set (binding)
- **Health**: essential health coverage with timely access; rationing must be explicit and contestable, not hidden in denials.
- **Disability**: income replacement/support and accommodations pathways, with continuity and non-discrimination rails.
- **Unemployment/transition**: stabilisers for job loss and sector transition; designed to preserve exit power, not discipline.
- **Old age**: predictable income security in retirement, with protection against poverty tails and high-cost care cascades.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Baseline coverage must be accessible without private gatekeeping, paid priority, or employer dependence.
- Coverage must include lead-time norms and escalation when capacity is breached; “covered but unreachable” is effective non-coverage.

## B05.CH04.P05.S03 — Private add-ons: allowed within rails; prohibited lock-in and hidden L via clamp contracts

Private add-ons may exist, but only if they do not undermine baseline access or reintroduce clamp via contracts.

### Permitted add-ons (within rails)
- Additional comfort, expanded provider choice, or supplementary services, provided baseline access remains intact and non-stigmatised.

### Prohibited clamp patterns (binding)
- Employer-tied add-ons that create exit friction (lose coverage when switching jobs).
- Network designs that indirectly degrade baseline access (two-tier waiting times by default).
- Bundled products with cancellation penalties, opaque exclusions, or data extraction as a condition.
- “Discount” schemes that shift cost onto households via complexity (hidden L) or that require constant re-qualification.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Add-ons must be strictly optional and must not be used to gate baseline services or to create coercive dependency.
- Contracts must prohibit lock-in clauses and must disclose all-in costs; otherwise they are treated as toll constructs.

## B05.CH04.P05.S04 — Contestability: transparent policy terms, fast objection flow, audit of claim denials

Insurance is only compatible if decisions are contestable and denial patterns are auditable. Without contestability, insurers become
de facto governors of access.

### Contestability architecture (binding)
- **Transparent terms**: coverage, exclusions, decision rules, and appeal paths must be readable and machine-addressable where feasible.
- **Fast objection flow**: time-bounded disputes with provisional relief for essential services during appeal.
- **Audit of denials**: publish aggregated denial rates, denial reasons taxonomy, reversal rates on appeal, and regional/cohort
  distribution (join-safe).
- **Anti-retaliation**: no punitive treatment for claimants who appeal; no “chilling” through complexity.

### Hard stop
- Systematic denial patterns, opaque algorithms, or unappealable decisions constitute a governance failure mode; the scheme must be
  restructured, converted to public/commons rails, or suspended pending remediation.
