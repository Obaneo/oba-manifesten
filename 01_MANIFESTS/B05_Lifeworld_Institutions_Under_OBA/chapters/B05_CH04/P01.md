# B05.CH04.P01 — Purpose, scope, and normative status of security and buffers

This paragraph defines the purpose and scope of the security/buffer layer in the lifeworld book. Under OBA, security is not a
secondary “social policy” add-on; it is **order infrastructure**. The macro order can only remain legitimate and stable if households
retain **residual space (R)** and if the **tails of the R/B distribution** do not repeatedly collapse under predictable shocks.
Without buffers, small disruptions (illness, job loss, price spikes, admin errors) cascade into arrears, debt spirals, lock-in, and
loss of exit power—creating fertile ground for resentment, sabotage, and extraction regrowth.

CH04 is therefore normative: it specifies the minimum rails that make security compatible with OBA. Security mechanisms must stabilise
tails **without** becoming new toll gates (fee-stacks), clamp devices (conditionality-by-humiliation, employer/insurer lock-in), or
surveillance channels. When those rails cannot be met, the system must fail-closed: no “compatibility” claim, and no scaling without
redesign.

---

## B05.CH04.P01.S01 — Purpose: stabilise R/B tails without opening new extraction channels

The primary purpose is tail stabilisation: reduce the frequency and depth of lifeworld collapses.

### Tail-stabilisation objective
- **R/B tails are the target**: not only median outcomes, but the probability of falling below α (share_below_alpha), volatility, and
  recovery time after shocks.
- **Exit power preservation**: buffers must increase households’ ability to refuse clamp contracts (predatory credit, exploitative
  work terms, insurer gating) rather than making them more dependent.
- **Anti-extraction**: security must not become a revenue surface for intermediaries (junk fees, broker layers, “access services”),
  nor a channel for stealth asset/claim support.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any security instrument MUST state the tail variable it targets (arrears, default, eviction risk, time-to-recovery) and how it is
  measured.
- Any security design that structurally grows fee-stacks or lock-in (private optimisation ecosystems around eligibility/access) is
  treated as E regrowth and is non-conforming unless neutralised.

---

## B05.CH04.P01.S02 — Scope: income floor, buffers, insurance, shock regimes, fallback commons, emergency procedures

CH04 covers the full buffer stack: baseline guarantees, household buffers, risk pooling, and crisis protocols.

### In scope
- **Income floor**: minimum R guarantees (cash and/or in-kind commons), with anti-stigma and low-friction access.
- **Household buffers**: liquidity/reserve mechanisms, arrears prevention, and time buffers (leave/scheduling protections; link CH03).
- **Insurance**: public/commons risk pooling vs private toll; contestability and denial discipline.
- **Shock regimes**: temporary emergency instruments triggered by measurable conditions, with sunsets and unwind plans.
- **Fallback commons**: last-resort infrastructure (shelter, food, energy baseline, care continuity, legal assistance).
- **Emergency procedures**: crisis triage, fast eligibility, provisional relief, and post-mortem audit.

### Out of scope (but referenced)
- Canonical definitions and calculation rules (Kernel Spec / Book 1) and transition sequencing (Book 2), except where needed for
  binding and enforcement.

## B05.CH04.P01.S03 — Binding: Definition Set II (B,T,N,L,R), ΔM channel rails, commons standard, evidence standards

Security claims must be executed under canonical definitions and channel discipline.

### Binding interfaces (non-negotiable)
- **Definition Set II**: all lifeworld effects are expressed via B/T/N/L/R and tail metrics; “benefit” claims must map to these.
- **ΔM channel discipline**: financing and emergency measures must respect allowed/forbidden channels; no backdoor asset/claim support.
- **Commons standard**: fallback provisions and in-kind baselines must meet access/maintenance/contestability minima (ΔG-like discipline).
- **Evidence standards**: method_id, definition-set references, uncertainty where relevant, and restatement policy for published claims.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a security instrument cannot be expressed as a change in B/T/N/L/R (and tail behaviour) under a pinned definition set, it cannot
  be treated as evidenced.
- If financing routes cannot pass leakage tests (ΔM→claims/fees), the instrument is presumed to leak and must be redesigned.

---

## B05.CH04.P01.S04 — Fail-closed: security that becomes dependent on lock-in, surveillance, or discretionary deals is non-conform

Security is a clamp surface unless guarded. CH04 therefore imposes a fail-closed rule: security mechanisms must not depend on coercion
or discretion.

### Non-conforming dependencies (hard rails)
- **Lock-in**: employer-tied benefits, insurer network gates, debt-linked eligibility, punitive exit penalties.
- **Surveillance**: continuous monitoring as a condition for access; behavioural scoring; invasive data linkage beyond necessity.
- **Discretionary deals**: bespoke exceptions, political patronage, opaque carve-outs, selective enforcement without publication.

### Fail-closed rule (binding)
- If security requires any of the above to function, it must be treated as **non-conforming** and must not be scaled.
- When capacity or privacy constraints prevent compliant execution, scope must be reduced to what is auditable and rights-preserving,
  and the design must be rebuilt before claiming “OBA-compatible security.”
