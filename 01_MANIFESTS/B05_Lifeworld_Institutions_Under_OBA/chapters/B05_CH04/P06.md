# B05.CH04.P06 — Shock regimes and emergency protocols (temporary, controlled)

This paragraph specifies shock regimes as **temporary, rule-bound emergency protocols** for macro events that would otherwise push
the lifeworld into rapid tail collapse: price breaks, shortages, and system stress. Under OBA, emergency regimes are high-risk
governance zones: they concentrate discretion and therefore invite capture, vendor lock-in, and semantic drift (“temporary” becomes
permanent; “support” becomes asset/claim support). CH04 therefore treats shock regimes as strictly controlled instruments with
measurable triggers, public notice, sunset discipline, mandatory unwind plans, and post-mortem audits.

The goal is not to eliminate shocks—shocks are real—but to prevent shocks from being used as a pretext for permanent clamp, opaque
deal-making, or ΔM leakage into claims and asset prices.

---

## B05.CH04.P06.S01 — Trigger criteria: measurable (price breaks, shortage indicators, system stress) with public notice

Emergency powers must only activate under defined, measurable conditions; otherwise “emergency” becomes a discretionary label.

### Trigger classes (indicative; parameterisable)
- **Price breaks**: rapid, large increases in essentials indices (energy, food, housing cost components) beyond pre-defined bands.
- **Shortage indicators**: inventory/availability metrics, supply lead-time spikes, distribution uptime failures.
- **System stress**: arrears spikes, disconnections/evictions surges, capacity collapse in care/health pathways, payment rail incidents.

### Activation discipline (binding)
- **Public notice**: activation must be announced with trigger values, affected domains, expected duration, and instruments used.
- **Evidence pack**: reference method_id, definition-set versions, and uncertainty bands for trigger measurements.
- **Scope definition**: define which cohorts/domains are affected and why; avoid blanket measures without evidence.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If triggers cannot be measured reliably without violating privacy rails, the regime cannot be activated at scale and must be
  redesigned or narrowed (fail-closed against unverifiable emergency claims).
- “Political urgency” alone is insufficient; the trigger must map to observable lifeworld harm risk.

---

## B05.CH04.P06.S02 — Temporariness: sunsets, size caps, mandatory unwind plan (no permanent emergency)

Emergency regimes are permitted only if they self-terminate and do not create permanent institutions of exception.

### Temporariness rails (binding)
- **Sunset clauses**: every emergency measure has a fixed end date; renewals require re-triggering under published criteria.
- **Size caps**: bounded fiscal/operational scope to prevent open-ended expansion and to reduce capture incentives.
- **Mandatory unwind plan**: pre-defined transition back to normal rails, including phased withdrawal and stabilisation checks.
- **Rollback discipline**: if measures create clamp, price distortion, or leakage, they must be rolled back immediately.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any regime without a credible unwind plan is non-conforming (it is a permanent emergency by design).
- Extensions must be treated as new decisions requiring evidence, not as automatic continuations.

---

## B05.CH04.P06.S03 — Instruments: essentials price stabilisation, temporary income support, rationing with boundary rules

Emergency instruments must prioritise lifeworld stability while preserving fairness and contestability.

### Instrument families (binding set)
- **Essentials price stabilisation**: temporary controls/compensation mechanisms that prevent sudden L spikes, with transparent cost
  build-ups and anti-fee-stack enforcement.
- **Temporary income support**: fast, low-friction payments or entitlements to stabilise R/B tails during the shock period.
- **Rationing with boundary rules**: where shortages exist, allocation must be rule-based, non-discriminatory, and auditable—rationing
  by price alone is a clamp mechanism in essentials.
- **Continuity measures**: maintain critical services (care, energy baseline, food distribution) via fallback commons capacity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Instruments must not create new intermediaries that skim fees; emergency procurement must include anti-vendor-capture rails.
- Allocation decisions must be contestable; “black-box triage” is treated as governance failure.

---

## B05.CH04.P06.S04 — Hard stop: emergency regime must not become an asset/claim-support channel (ΔM rails)

Emergency regimes are the easiest route for ΔM leakage: “support” programs inflate asset prices, protect claim stacks, or entrench
rentier cashflows.

### Forbidden channels (binding hard stop)
- **Asset price support**: policies designed to stabilise or raise housing/financial asset prices rather than reduce burdens or expand
  capacity.
- **Claim-stack protection**: using public funds to maintain private claims (interest streams, fee revenue) without restructuring.
- **Concession entrenchment**: emergency contracts that lock in excess returns or create long-term monopolies.

### Leakage control requirements (binding)
- Emergency spending must be traceable to allowed channels (essentials provision, commons capacity, direct tail stabilisation) with
  leakage tests.
- Under opacity or resistance to disclosure, conservative presumptions apply: restrict instruments, impose escrow/lockbox, or halt the
  regime pending audit.

### Hard stop rule
- If evidence shows that emergency measures are functioning as asset/claim support (ΔM leakage), the regime must be stopped, unwound,
  and redesigned, and compatibility claims must be suspended until restated and corrected.
