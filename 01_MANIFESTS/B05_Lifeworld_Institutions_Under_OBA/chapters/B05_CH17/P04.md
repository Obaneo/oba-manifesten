# B05.CH17.P04 — R/B effect and lifeworld metrics (per sector)

This paragraph specifies how each sector case must bind to **lifeworld outcomes**: the effect on **R/B** via changes in L components,
tail stabilization (shock dampening), time friction reduction, and procedurally fixed experience inputs. Sector cases are not complete
until they can state **who** benefits, **which L component** falls, **which tails** are stabilized, and **how time burden changes**—with
method binding and uncertainty discipline.

---

<a id="b05ch17p04s01"></a>
## B05.CH17.P04.S01 — B,T,N,L,R: which L component falls and for whom (cohorts)

Each sector case must state how it changes the B→T→N→L→R chain for specific cohorts, by reducing one or more L components and/or time
frictions.

### Required fields (template)
- `cohort_definitions[]` (aggregated; privacy-safe):
  - cohort_id, brief description, eligibility markers (non-identifying)
- `BTNLR_mapping` per cohort:
  - `B_effect` (usually none; but declare if sector affects income flows)
  - `T_effect` (timing/admin friction effects)
  - `N_effect` (if taxes/transfers change via sector intervention; otherwise declare none)
  - `L_component_effects[]`:
    - which L component(s) change: {housing, energy, healthcare, digital, mobility, insurance, debt_service, other}
    - pre/post share bands or delta bands
  - `R_effect` and `RB_effect` (pre/post bands; derived or stated with method binding)
- `method_binding`:
  - `definitionset_id`, `method_id`, benchmark version refs, uncertainty rules

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- The case **MUST** specify the primary L component affected and the cohorts affected; “costs go down” is non-conform.
- RB effects **MUST** be derived from declared N and L effects or explicitly stated as method-defined outputs.
- Cohort claims **MUST** be aggregated and privacy-safe.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-RB-01 (Component specificity):** at least one L component delta is specified per target cohort.
- **SC-RB-02 (Derivability):** RB effects are derivable or method-defined with explicit references.
- **SC-RB-03 (Provenance):** `definitionset_id` and `method_id` exist for the metrics used.

<a id="b05ch17p04s02"></a>
## B05.CH17.P04.S02 — Tails: which shocks are dampened (arrears, lockouts, wait times, cost spikes)

Sector cases must state which **tail risks** they reduce. “Average improvement” is insufficient: the lifeworld often fails in the tails
(arrears cascades, lockouts, long waits, sudden cost spikes).

### Required fields (template)
- `tail_events[]` (sector-specific; minimum set includes):
  - `arrears_event` (late payment, escalation, threat timeline)
  - `lockout_event` (service disconnection, eviction, account lockout)
  - `wait_time_event` (queue/wait time exceedance)
  - `cost_spike_event` (sudden tariff/price/fee increase; maintenance spike)
- For each tail event:
  - `pre_incidence_band` and `post_incidence_band` (aggregated)
  - `severity_band` (harm proxy)
  - `time_to_recovery_band`
  - `trigger_mechanism` (bottleneck vs E source vs admin failure)
  - `rail_link` (which intervention prevents the tail)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Tail events **MUST** be explicit and measured via bands/proxies; “fewer problems” is non-conform.
- The case **MUST** identify the mechanism that turns shocks into tails (E stack, lock-in, wait-time gate, enforcement timeline).
- If tails do not improve beyond uncertainty, scaling must be blocked (link P10 fail-closed).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-TAIL-01 (Event coverage):** at least two tail event types are present per sector case (more recommended).
- **SC-TAIL-02 (Mechanism):** each tail event includes a trigger mechanism and a rail link.
- **SC-TAIL-03 (Scaling gate):** non-improvement triggers redesign before scale (declared rule).

## B05.CH17.P04.S03 — Time friction: schedules, wait times, administrative burden as hidden L

Time friction is treated as hidden L. Sector cases must therefore measure time burdens that households and firms pay in addition to money:
schedules, wait times, paperwork, calls, disputes, and compliance loops.

### Required time-friction fields (template)
- `time_friction_surfaces[]` with, per surface:
  - `surface_type` ∈ {wait_time, schedule_mismatch, admin_burden, dispute_duration, travel_time, onboarding_friction}
  - `pre_band` and `post_band`
  - `measurement_mode` ∈ {observed_log, survey_aggregated, modeled_proxy}
  - `uncertainty_band`
  - `who_is_affected` (cohort refs; aggregated)
  - `evidence_class` (where applicable)
- `admin_steps_proxy` (optional but recommended):
  - step count bands for key processes (application, switching, dispute)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Time friction **MUST** be included; omission yields a non-lifeworld sector case.
- Measurement mode **MUST** be declared; time claims without provenance are non-conform.
- Time friction deltas **MUST** be linked to a concrete rail (proc simplification, capacity, interoperability, etc.).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-TIME-01 (Presence):** at least one time-fraction surface is reported pre/post.
- **SC-TIME-02 (Provenance):** measurement_mode is declared for each time metric.
- **SC-TIME-03 (Rail linkage):** each time friction delta references the relevant intervention rail.

## B05.CH17.P04.S04 — Experience inputs: procedurally fixed signals (challenge rights, sampling, transparency)

Experience is allowed only as **procedural input**, not as a KPI target. The template therefore defines fixed, auditable experience
signals that can trigger investigation or redesign without becoming gameable or punitive.

### Allowed experience inputs (minimum set)
- `challenge_rights_usage` (rate of objections/appeals; aggregated)
- `complaint_sampling` (structured sampling of complaints; method-defined)
- `service_incident_reporting` (incident rates; aggregated)
- `transparency_checks`:
  - publication completeness, log accessibility, decision reason availability

### Procedural safeguards (binding)
- Experience inputs **MUST NOT** be used as eligibility scoring or behavioural scoring.
- Inputs **MUST** be sampling-based or aggregated to avoid surveillance drift.
- Inputs **MUST** route to audit/inspection and contestability, not to punishment-by-rating.

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Experience inputs **MUST** be procedurally fixed (defined methods, sampling rules, publication rules).
- Experience inputs **MUST** be transparent and contestable; otherwise they become a new gatekeeping surface.
- If experience signals indicate persistent harm, redesign must be triggered (fail-closed against denial).

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-EXP-01 (Fixed procedure):** experience inputs specify method_id/benchmark versions and sampling rules.
- **SC-EXP-02 (Non-scoring):** explicit prohibition of behavioural scoring is stated and auditable.
- **SC-EXP-03 (Trigger):** signals route into audit/inspection and redesign triggers with time bounds.

---

### Output format (lifeworld metrics annex)

At minimum, each sector case **MUST** emit a lifeworld metrics annex (table(s) or structured lists) with:

1) **Cohort × L component table**
- `cohort_id` | `L_component` | `pre_share_band` | `post_share_band` | `delta` | `method_ref` | `uncertainty_band`

2) **Tail events table**
- `tail_event_type` | `pre_incidence_band` | `post_incidence_band` | `severity_band` | `time_to_recovery_band` | `rail_link`

3) **Time friction table**
- `surface_type` | `pre_band` | `post_band` | `measurement_mode` | `uncertainty_band` | `rail_link`

4) **Experience inputs table**
- `signal` | `aggregation_mode` | `sampling_rule_ref` | `publication_ref` | `trigger_action`

If these cannot be produced, the sector case is NON-CONFORM under CH17 fail-closed rules.
