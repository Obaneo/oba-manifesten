# B05.CH17.P10 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph specifies **failure modes** and **hard stops** for sector cases as templates. The main risks are: (i) template-washing
(stories branded as “cases” without bottleneck/E/ΔM content), (ii) data maximalism (template used to justify surveillance), (iii) vendor
capture (templates become consultancy products), and (iv) outcome failure (no demonstrable L reduction or tail stabilization). CH17 is
FAIL-CLOSED: when a hard stop triggers, scaling is blocked and redesign is mandatory.

---

<a id="b05ch17p10s01"></a>
## B05.CH17.P10.S01 — Template-washing: a “case” without bottleneck/E/ΔM analysis → disqualification

Template-washing occurs when an artefact uses sector-case language but omits the constitutive field groups that make it reusable and
auditable.

### Template-washing indicators (minimum set)
- missing bottleneck scan (no scarcity/capacity/time constraint proxies)
- missing E source map (no fee stacks/lock-in mechanisms with evidence packs)
- missing ΔM channel annex (no allowed/forbidden channels, no leakage tests, no sunset rules)
- commons option described as aspiration without governance/maintenance/procurement rails
- lifeworld metrics absent (no cohort L component deltas; no tails/time friction proxies)

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- A sector case **MUST** instantiate all binding field groups (P02–P07). Omission triggers disqualification.
- Narrative plausibility **MUST NOT** substitute for template fields; a “nice sector story” is non-conform.
- Disqualified cases **MUST** be rewritten by filling missing field groups; polishing is insufficient.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-FM-TW-01 (Field gate):** missing any of {bottlenecks, E sources, commons option, caps compatibility, R/B metrics, ΔM channel,
  governance, data/evidence} → DISQUALIFY.
- **SC-FM-TW-02 (Evidence gate):** E sources and ΔM leakage claims must cite auditpacks; missing → DISQUALIFY.

<a id="b05ch17p10s02"></a>
## B05.CH17.P10.S02 — Data maximalism: template used to justify surveillance → NON-CONFORM

A common failure mode is to use “measurement binding” as a pretext for building surveillance regimes. CH17 forbids this.

### Data maximalism indicators (minimum set)
- publishing or requiring household/person-level microdata
- join-unsafe identifiers enabling re-identification
- behavioural scoring or eligibility scoring from experience signals
- expanding data collection beyond necessity without logged justification
- “always-on monitoring” as default rather than sampling/aggregation

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Microdata publication **MUST NOT** occur. If required, the sector case is NON-CONFORM.
- Join safety **MUST** be enforced by design (aggregation, suppression, allowed joins only).
- Experience signals **MUST** remain procedural inputs, not KPIs or scoring mechanisms (P04.S04).
- Any data expansion **MUST** pass necessity and proportionality checks and be logged.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-FM-DM-01 (Microdata ban):** any microdata requirement/publishing → NON-CONFORM.
- **SC-FM-DM-02 (Join safety):** join rules exist and prevent re-identification; absence → NON-CONFORM.
- **SC-FM-DM-03 (No scoring):** explicit prohibition of behavioural scoring exists and is auditable.

<a id="b05ch17p10s03"></a>
## B05.CH17.P10.S03 — Vendor capture: sector cases become consultancy products → procurement breach

Templates can be captured by vendors: consultancy firms turn cases into proprietary frameworks, lock clients into tooling, and extract fees
from what should be commons governance knowledge.

### Vendor capture indicators (minimum set)
- proprietary, non-auditable “methodologies” replacing `method_id`/benchmarks
- procurement processes designed around a single vendor’s tooling
- change-order inflation and opaque pricing
- decision logs withheld as “commercially sensitive”
- knowledge monopolies: training/certification as gatekeeping tolls

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Procurement **MUST** follow open criteria, conflict controls, and auditability; single-vendor dependence is non-conform unless strictly
  temporary and sunset.
- Methods and benchmarks **MUST** remain public/versioned under Codex; proprietary replacements are non-conform.
- Decision logs **MUST** be accessible at least in aggregated form; secrecy is a breach unless narrowly justified.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-FM-VC-01 (Open procurement):** procurement logs and criteria exist; single-vendor tailoring → breach flag.
- **SC-FM-VC-02 (Method openness):** method_id and benchmarks are versioned and non-proprietary.
- **SC-FM-VC-03 (Change-order visibility):** change orders and contract revisions are logged and auditable.

<a id="b05ch17p10s04"></a>
## B05.CH17.P10.S04 — Fail-closed: if the sector case does not deliver demonstrable L reduction or tail stabilization, scaling is blocked and redesign is mandatory

The hard stop is outcome-based. Sector cases are not complete when the design looks coherent; they are complete when they deliver
measurable lifeworld improvements beyond uncertainty.

### Hard-stop conditions (minimum)
Scaling is **BLOCKED** if, post pilot:
- `L_component_deltas` are not demonstrably improved beyond uncertainty bands for target cohorts, or
- `tail_event_incidence` does not decline (arrears, lockouts, wait times, cost spikes), or
- `time_friction_proxies` do not improve (admin time, wait time, schedule mismatch), or
- `leakage_tests` fail (ΔM leaks into claims/fees), or
- privacy rails are violated (data maximalism), or
- selective execution variance exceeds tolerance without remediation.

### Mandatory redesign outputs (required)
When blocked:
- `attribution`:
  - which bottleneck/E surface/leakage caused failure (link to P02/P05)
- `rail_change_plan`:
  - what rules/parameters/contracts change (versioned)
- `retest_plan`:
  - rerun pilot metrics and leakage tests under new version
- `deprecation_or_rollback`:
  - stop issuance of non-conform contracts; rollback temporary instruments if needed

### Normative requirements (RFC 2119)
- Scaling **MUST** be blocked under hard-stop conditions; narrative justification is not permitted.
- Redesign **MUST** be traceable (what changed, why, version id, expected delta).
- Programs must be sunset/rolled back if they fail; persistence without outcomes is capture risk.

### Conformance tests (minimum)
- **SC-FM-FC-01 (Outcome gate):** absence of demonstrable L/tail/time improvements → scaling blocked.
- **SC-FM-FC-02 (Leakage gate):** leakage test failure → scaling blocked.
- **SC-FM-FC-03 (Redesign trace):** blocked cases produce an attribution + rail change plan + retest plan.

---

### Output format (hard-stop register)

At minimum, each sector case **MUST** emit a hard-stop register (table or structured list) with:

- `failure_mode` | `trigger_indicator` | `threshold_or_condition` | `detected` | `required_action` | `status`

Where required_action ∈ {disqualify_case, block_scaling, launch_audit, rewrite_contracts, reclassify_channel, rollback_program,
redesign_template_version, publish_precedent_update}.

If hard-stop logic is absent or discretionary, the sector template is not enforceable and is NON-CONFORM.
