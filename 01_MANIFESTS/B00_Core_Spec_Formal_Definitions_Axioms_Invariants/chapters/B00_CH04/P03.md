# B00.CH04.P03 — Strict classification rules for T (what counts as T)

This paragraph defines strict, binding rules for classifying **T (transfer-out / levies)**. T is a narrow class:
it exists to isolate **mandatory contributions to the public order** (and tightly defined quasi-public regimes)
from both **L (essentials burdens)** and from discretionary spending.

If T is allowed to drift, implementations can “pass” an R/B target by misclassifying burdens:
- shifting essentials burdens into T to make L look smaller,
- shifting extractive tolls into “mandatory” labels,
- or classifying ordinary consumption as “levy”.

Normative posture is conservative: where ambiguity would enlarge discretion, classification MUST fail closed until
the mandatory basis is demonstrated.

---

## B00.CH04.P03.S01 — Mandatory contributions to the public order (tax, social security contributions)

A payment is **T** if it is a **mandatory contribution to the public order**, imposed by law and enforceable by
sanction such that non-payment is not a real option for compliant participation.

Canonical T includes (non-exhaustive):
- income taxes and payroll taxes;
- mandatory social security contributions (employee/employer where borne by the unit under the boundary definition);
- compulsory statutory insurance contributions where participation is mandatory by law;
- legally imposed levies that fund general public functions (including earmarked levies where mandatory).

Classification requires:
- a clear legal basis (statute/regulation) and the liable party,
- periodization consistent with the B/T/N identity,
- boundary clarity (who bears the contribution under consolidation rules).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any T item MUST cite its mandatory basis (legal/regulatory source or binding obligation type) in the audit trail.
- If a payment is optional, choice-dependent, or avoidable through credible exit without illegality, it is not T.

---

## B00.CH04.P03.S02 — Quasi-taxes and mandatory contributions (semi-public, regulated)

Some payments are not “tax” in name but are **functionally mandatory** due to regulated or quasi-public regimes.

A payment may be classified as **T** as a quasi-tax only if ALL are satisfied:

1) **Mandatory participation:** the regime is mandatory for the relevant population/unit (not elective).
2) **Regulatory enforceability:** non-payment triggers enforceable sanctions analogous to public enforcement.
3) **Non-avoidability:** credible exit is not available without leaving the jurisdiction/regime in a way that is
   not plausibly available (or would constitute non-participation in the public order).
4) **Public-order linkage:** the contribution funds a public/regulated order function (not a private toll gate).

Examples (non-exhaustive, jurisdiction-dependent):
- mandatory regulated contributions administered by semi-public bodies,
- compulsory regulated fees that function as statutory contributions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Quasi-tax classification MUST be documented with the regime’s mandatory features and enforcement properties.
- If a payment is to a private provider under competitive choice (even if common), it is not T unless the regime is
  mandatory and choice is not credible in practice under declared rules.

---

## B00.CH04.P03.S03 — Not T: voluntary consumption or contractually avoidable expenditures

The following are **not T** (they belong in L or discretionary spending, depending on avoidability rules):

- ordinary consumption purchases (goods/services) even if “necessary” in a colloquial sense;
- contract-based expenditures where the unit has credible choice/exit (subscriptions, upgrades, elective insurance);
- private fees that are avoidable via provider switching or contract termination without punitive lock-in;
- toll-like charges imposed by private gatekeepers (platform fees, service fees, rent premiums) unless these are
  classified as L under essentials/lock-in rules.

If an item is not legally/regulatorily mandatory, it is presumptively not T.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST NOT include voluntary purchases in T to improve R/B optics.
- Any disputed item must be resolved by demonstrating mandatory basis; otherwise it remains non-T and evaluation
  fails closed on any claim that depends on reclassifying it as T.

---

## B00.CH04.P03.S04 — Anti-avoidance: relabelling L or E as “voluntary” is not allowed

Anti-avoidance binds both directions:
- You must not inflate T (by calling consumption “mandatory”),
- and you must not deflate T by shifting truly mandatory levies into “voluntary” categories.

A core failure mode is to relabel **L** or **E-driven tolls** as “voluntary choices” (“you could move”, “you could
switch providers”, “you accepted the contract”) when credible exit does not exist due to essentials constraints,
lock-in, or structural dependence.

Canonical anti-avoidance rule:
- **Plausible avoidability** must be demonstrated with auditable evidence of credible alternatives and exit
  feasibility under the unit’s constraints.
- Where the “voluntary” claim rests on unrealistic assumptions (costless switching, unlimited housing mobility,
  perfect information), it is invalid.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- If an implementation treats a burden as voluntary (thus excluding it from L), it MUST document credible exit and
  alternatives; otherwise the burden remains in L and cannot be washed out.
- If relabelling changes R/B materially without changing the underlying constraint structure, the evaluation is
  non-conform and must be corrected or fail closed.
