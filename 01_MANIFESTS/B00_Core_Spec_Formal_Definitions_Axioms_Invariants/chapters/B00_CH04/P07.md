# B00.CH04.P07 — Attribution of B (gross) at micro level

This paragraph defines canonical rules for attributing **B (gross receipts)** at the **micro level** (household,
person, or other evaluated unit). The purpose is to ensure that B is:
- correctly bounded (what accrues as receipts to the unit),
- complete (all relevant inflow components included under declared rules),
- comparable (consistent valuation and time indexing),
- and non-gameable (no double counting, no opportunistic inclusion/exclusion).

Micro B must support micro→macro reconciliation and must not be inflated by internal circulation, timing artifacts,
or inconsistent treatment of in-kind receipts.

---

## B00.CH04.P07.S01 — Gross income components: wages, profit distributions, interest/rent, transfers-in

Micro-level B includes gross receipts accruing to the unit from the following canonical inflow classes:

- **Wages and salaries:** gross pay before T; includes bonuses and mandatory supplements; includes employer-paid
  cash components that accrue as receipts to the unit under boundary rules.
- **Profit distributions / self-employment receipts:** distributions or attributed operating surplus accruing to the
  unit; treatment must specify whether retained earnings inside an entity are within the evaluated boundary.
- **Interest and rent received:** financial income and property income accruing to the unit (gross of any mandatory
  levies classified as T).
- **Transfers-in:** benefits, grants, remittances, and other transfers that accrue as receipts to the unit, subject
  to declared boundary and consolidation rules.

Boundary rule:
- include receipts that the unit can deploy (or that reduce required outflows by being provided as receipts in kind),
- exclude pass-through amounts that never accrue to the unit as deployable receipts under the defined boundary.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST specify the evaluated boundary (household/person/entity) and how income inside entities is
  treated (look-through vs entity boundary).
- Any omission or inclusion that materially changes B must be documented; silent conventions are non-auditable and
  non-conform for OBA claims.

## B00.CH04.P07.S02 — In-kind and quasi-cash: canonical rules for valuation and inclusion

Some receipts accrue as **in-kind** or **quasi-cash** and must be treated consistently to prevent hidden B
inflation/deflation.

Canonical inclusion rule:
- include in-kind/quasi-cash receipts when they are a **substitute for cash receipts** or directly provide baseline
  function that would otherwise require cash outflow (especially within essentials regimes).

Examples (non-exhaustive):
- employer-provided benefits with direct consumption value (housing, meals) where they substitute essential outflows,
- vouchers or credits usable for essentials (food, energy, connectivity) where they materially reduce required L,
- quasi-cash instruments (prepaid cards) that function as deployable purchasing power.

Valuation rule (canonical):
- valuation method MUST be declared and reproducible (market equivalent, regulated tariff equivalent, cost-of-service
  equivalent), and MUST avoid strategic overvaluation.
- where valuation uncertainty is material, use bands and conservative escalation; do not claim precision without basis.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any inclusion of in-kind/quasi-cash receipts MUST state valuation method and whether it reduces L (essentials) or
  increases discretionary capacity; double counting (counting as B while also not reducing L) is forbidden.
- If valuation cannot be audited, the component must be flagged uncertain/non-certifying and handled under
  fail-closed discipline for dependent claims.

## B00.CH04.P07.S03 — Seasonal and volatility corrections: smoothing rules (canonical)

Micro receipts can be volatile (seasonal work, gig income, bonuses, irregular distributions). Because R/B is an
order indicator tied to buffer and resilience, B attribution may require canonical **smoothing** to reflect
typical capacity rather than one-off spikes.

Canonical smoothing rules (to be parameterized but explicit):

- **Window definition:** smoothing window length and resolution must be declared (e.g., rolling 12 months) and
  applied consistently.
- **Separation of spikes:** non-recurrent spikes (one-time payouts) must be identified and handled under declared
  treatment (either smoothed, separately reported, or treated as buffer contributions with explicit labeling).
- **Volatility reporting:** smoothing must not hide volatility; volatility metrics must be reported alongside
  smoothed B for cohort-level interpretation.

Smoothing is not permitted to improve optics by design. If smoothing choices materially change share-below-α outcomes,
they must be disclosed and sensitivity-tested.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any smoothing MUST be declared (method, window, spike treatment) and applied consistently across cohorts.
- If smoothing is used, the implementation MUST report both smoothed and raw distributional signals (or equivalent
  volatility indicators); otherwise tail risk can be masked and the evaluation is non-conform.

## B00.CH04.P07.S04 — Avoid double counting: consolidation across households/entities

Double counting is a primary failure mode in gross receipts attribution. B must represent receipts **accruing to the
evaluated boundary**, not internal circulation.

Canonical consolidation rules:

- **Within-household consolidation:** transfers between members of the same household boundary are not new B; they
  are internal reallocation.
- **Entity look-through discipline:** where a household controls an entity that pays wages/dividends/rents to the
  household, the evaluation must specify whether the entity is inside or outside the boundary. Mixing conventions
  (counting both entity gross and household receipts) is forbidden.
- **Pass-through exclusion:** amounts collected and remitted on behalf of others (escrow, withholding that never
  accrues to the unit) are not B unless they accrue as receipts under boundary rules.

Micro→macro reconciliation requires that consolidation rules align with the macro accounting frame used.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any evaluation MUST declare consolidation boundaries and ensure that internal transfers are not counted as B.
- If consolidation cannot be performed due to missing identifiers or opaque structures, the affected B estimate must
  be flagged as uncertain/non-auditable; dependent OBA claims must fail closed or be limited to auditable subsets.
