# B00.CH04.P09 — Relation to P/E/C: how E materialises as L and R pressure

This paragraph specifies how **P/E/C composition** maps into **L (essentials/claim layers)** and therefore into
**R (remaining space)** and **R/B**. Definition set II is life-world outcome accounting; P/E/C is the upstream
mechanism accounting. The relationship is structural:

- **E** (extraction) tends to materialise as **L** when embedded in essentials and unavoidable access channels,
  compressing R and increasing tail failure.
- **C** (commons contribution) tends to reduce **L** by creating public option/commons capacity and credible exit,
  structurally disciplining tolls.
- **P** (production & maintenance) stabilises R by reducing volatility and lifecycle burdens via maintained capacity.

Therefore, R/B evaluation without P/E/C diagnostics is incomplete: it can detect pressure but not reliably explain
cause or prescribe structural interventions.

---

## B00.CH04.P09.S01 — E→L transmission: toll/lock-in raises essentials and claim layers

**E transmits into L** when toll mechanisms become unavoidable burdens in the essentials basket.

Canonical transmission mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):

- **Toll pricing in essentials:** price > functional costs sustained by power (housing scarcity rents, utility
  monopoly rents, health monopoly pricing).
- **Lock-in amplification:** switching costs and exit friction make tolls unavoidable (exclusive contracts, regional
  captivity, proprietary ecosystems for connectivity/health access).
- **Claim stacks:** layered fees/interest/commissions embedded in essentials (platform fees on housing access,
  payment/processing fees, refinancing spreads, penalty layers).
- **Information asymmetry:** hidden fees, opaque tariffs, cancellation traps that force payment for baseline access.

At the life-world level, it does not matter whether the toll is “legal”, “market rate”, or “agreed”. If the unit
cannot plausibly avoid it, the burden is L and compresses R.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any R/B evaluation in a domain where E is plausible MUST treat E→L as the default hypothesis and require evidence
  to classify the burden as purely function-driven.
- Implementations MUST record lock-in and fee-stack proxies in L measurement where they materially affect burdens;
  otherwise classification is non-auditable and fails closed.

---

## B00.CH04.P09.S02 — C→L reduction: commons/public option lowers structural burdens

**C reduces L structurally** by changing the constraint structure that enables toll extraction.

Canonical C→L reduction channels include (non-exhaustive):

- **Public option capacity:** credible baseline provision that disciplines private tolls (housing, energy, health,
  mobility, connectivity).
- **Open access and non-discrimination regimes:** prevent exclusion-based price discrimination and capture.
- **Interoperability/open standards:** reduce ecosystem captivity and switching costs, compressing toll margins.
- **Maintenance duties and repairability:** reduce lifecycle cost burdens, preventing degradation-as-rent.
- **Anti-capture governance:** prevents commons infrastructures from becoming new toll gates.

C is not defined by who owns the asset; it is defined by order-properties (access regime, enforceability, auditability).
If “public” provision operates as a toll gate without credible exit, it does not reduce L in the OBA sense.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any claim that C reduces L MUST specify the access regime and enforcement mechanism and show traceability to
  measurable burden reduction (L down) or volatility reduction.
- If C is claimed but can be appropriated or converted into a toll gate, L reduction credit fails closed.

## B00.CH04.P09.S03 — P→R stabilisation: maintenance/capacity reduces shock sensitivity

**P stabilises R** by sustaining and improving real functional capacity, which reduces both:
- baseline burdens (through efficiency, durability, maintained service levels), and
- volatility-driven cliff risk (through resilience and reduced exposure to failure).

Canonical P→R stabilisation mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):

- **Maintenance reduces lifecycle costs:** repairability, durability, and upkeep prevent costly failures and forced
  replacement.
- **Capacity reduces scarcity pressure:** expanded supply or improved throughput reduces price pressure in essentials.
- **Resilience reduces shock pass-through:** infrastructure reliability reduces sudden spikes (energy, mobility,
  connectivity).
- **Contestability improvements:** interoperability and non-exclusivity reduce dependence and compress toll margins.

P is not “GDP growth”. It is productive and maintenance capacity that measurably changes burdens and volatility.
If “growth” increases throughput while claim layers rise proportionally, R remains constrained and the mechanism is
not P-dominant.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any policy/program claiming to improve R/B via production MUST specify the P mechanism (what capacity is built or
  maintained) and show how it reduces L and/or volatility.
- If measured R/B improvement is not accompanied by plausible burden/volatility mechanism, the claim is non-auditable.

## B00.CH04.P09.S04 — Diagnostics: decomposition of L into E-driven vs function-driven components

To prevent semantic laundering (“high costs are inevitable”), OBA requires diagnostic decomposition of **L** into:

- **Lᶠ**: function-driven essentials burden (bounded by functional cost + required service level), and
- **Lᴱ**: E-driven burden (toll component embedded in essentials via power/lock-in/claim stacking).

Canonical diagnostic procedure:

1) Identify essentials categories in L (housing, energy, health, etc.).
2) For each category, apply P/E/C classification tools to the underlying flows:
   - estimate functional cost basis and bounded margin → identify function-driven component,
   - identify toll/lock-in/fee-stack residual → classify as E-driven.
3) Attribute L components to Lᶠ vs Lᴱ with declared uncertainty bands.
4) Use Lᴱ as the primary target for structural interventions (public option, anti-lock-in, cap linkage), and Lᶠ as
   the baseline that must be supported by productive capacity and maintenance.

Where splitting cannot be audited due to missing inputs, the disputed component must be treated conservatively as
E-driven or flagged unknown/non-certifying.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-grade R/B evaluation MUST support (at least in essentials domains) decomposition of L into E-driven vs
  function-driven components, or explicitly disclose inability and fail closed on causal claims.
- Reporting a single “cost of living” index without decomposability is non-conform for OBA claims where extraction
  diagnosis is required.
