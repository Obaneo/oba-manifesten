# B00.CH04.P14 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph defines canonical **failure modes** and **hard stops** for Definition set II (B,T,N,L,R, R/B, α).
These are not recommendations; they are **non-conformance conditions**. If a hard stop is triggered, the affected
outputs cannot be used to support an OBA conformance/compatibility claim until remediation is completed and logged.

The core threat model is adversarial: R/B can be made to “pass” through boundary manipulation, relabeling, or
statistical masking even while life-world burdens worsen.

---

## B00.CH04.P14.S01 — R/B-washing: inflating B, hiding L, relabelling T (detection rules)

**R/B-washing** is any practice that improves reported R/B without improving the underlying constraint structure.

Canonical R/B-washing vectors:

### (a) Inflating B
- double counting receipts via internal circulation,
- inconsistent inclusion of transfers-in or in-kind across periods/cohorts,
- timing tricks (counting spikes in B without corresponding burdens window),
- boundary drift (moving burdens outside the unit while keeping receipts inside).

### (b) Hiding or deflating L
- narrowing essentials baselines without canonical justification,
- classifying unavoidable burdens as “voluntary choices” despite non-credible exit,
- excluding lock-in burdens, compulsory add-ons, shadow fees,
- using inappropriate regional indices that understate real essentials burdens.

### (c) Relabelling T
- shifting L burdens into “mandatory fees” without legal/regulatory basis,
- shifting mandatory levies out of T into “voluntary” or “personal responsibility” categories,
- mixing employer/employee burdens inconsistently across cohorts or time.

Detection rules (minimum):
- reconciliation checks (micro→macro residuals),
- stability checks on essentials baselines and indices,
- cohort comparability checks (consistent B/T/L definitions),
- anomaly detection on sudden R/B shifts without plausible price/regime change,
- audit sampling of classifications in essentials-heavy cohorts.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If R/B improvement is primarily explainable by definition drift, boundary drift, or relabeling rather than by
  measurable reductions in L or real increases in receipts capacity, the evaluation is non-conform and must be
  suspended pending correction/restatement.

---

## B00.CH04.P14.S02 — Goodhart: optimising on median R/B with tail sacrifice (forbidden)

Goodhart failure occurs when the metric becomes the target: systems optimize for **median** R/B while allowing tails
to deteriorate, or by pushing burdens onto vulnerable cohorts.

Forbidden optimization patterns include (non-exhaustive):
- increasing median R/B by shifting L inflation to the bottom deciles,
- stabilizing macro averages via transfers while structural L rises in essentials for vulnerable cohorts,
- improving median R/B through cohort composition changes (who is counted) while within-cohort R/B declines,
- tuning α or cohort definitions to reduce measured failure rather than reducing real burdens.

Canonical rule: **tail adequacy binds**. If share below α, p10/p25, or R<0 persistence worsens, the system fails
the order floor in substance regardless of median movement.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- Any evaluation that shows median R/B improvement alongside tail deterioration (share below α rising, p10/p25
  falling, or R<0 persistence worsening) is non-conform for any claim of improved order adequacy, unless the change
  is fully explained by corrected measurement and restated accordingly.

---

## B00.CH04.P14.S03 — Black-box burden indices are non-conform (not reproducible)

Any burden index used to compute L or to claim improvements in R/B must be reproducible from declared inputs.
Black-box indices are non-conform because they enable semantic laundering.

Non-conform patterns include:
- proprietary “cost of living” scores without published basket, parameters, and indices,
- ML/AI burden estimators without inspectable inputs, feature definitions, and derivation rules,
- indices that cannot be reconciled to observed essentials prices and contract terms,
- “expert judgement” burdens without audit trails or sampling verification.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If L is computed from a black-box index (or any non-reproducible procedure) and is used for OBA-critical claims,
  the claim is non-conform and must be rejected or limited to the auditable subset.

---

## B00.CH04.P14.S04 — Fail-closed: when definition conflicts or data leaks occur, conformity is suspended until remediation

Definition set II relies on strict classification and reconciliation. When the system detects:
- definition conflicts (inconsistent B/T/L rules across components),
- data leakage (double counting, boundary mismatch, untracked imputations),
- or missingness that could hide L inflation or B inflation,

the system MUST fail closed.

Fail-closed means:
- affected outputs are marked non-certifying (unknown/non-conform),
- any dependent OBA claim is suspended for the affected scope/period,
- remediation is required: correction, restatement, and publication of changelog and impact analysis.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If definition conflicts or data leaks materially affect B/T/L/R computations and are not remediated with a logged
  restatement, OBA conformity is suspended for the affected components until remediation is completed and published.
