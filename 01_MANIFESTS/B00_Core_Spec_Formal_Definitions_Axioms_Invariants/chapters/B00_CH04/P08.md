# B00.CH04.P08 — R/B as order indicator: interpretation rules and edge cases

This paragraph defines canonical interpretation rules and edge-case handling for **R/B** as an order indicator.
R/B is a structural ratio; it must be interpreted with:
- sign (negative remainder is qualitatively different),
- proximity to zero (fragility regimes),
- composition diagnostics (why remainder is low),
- and measurement plausibility checks (when observed values are likely artifacts).

Edge rules are binding because they prevent “passing by narrative”: a system cannot claim order adequacy when tails
have negative remainder, when buffers are structurally absent, or when measurement choices hide claim layers.

---

## B00.CH04.P08.S01 — R/B < 0: structural unsustainability (definition and escalation)

**R/B < 0** implies **R < 0** (since B is non-negative under canonical domain constraints). This means that
mandatory transfers and essential burdens exceed gross receipts:

- **R = B − T − L < 0**

Interpretation: the evaluated unit is in **structural infeasibility** for the period regime. It can only persist
by:
- drawing down prior buffers,
- accruing debt/arrears,
- deferring essentials (foregoing care, energy, food, housing quality),
- or receiving unmeasured transfers/in-kind support.

Canonical escalation posture:
- R/B < 0 is not “low wellbeing”; it is an order failure signal requiring diagnosis and escalation.
- If R/B < 0 persists across periods or concentrates in cohorts, the system fails the order floor in substance even
  if aggregates look stable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-grade evaluation MUST report share with R/B < 0 (or R < 0) by cohort and must treat persistence as a hard
  constraint in policy evaluation.
- Implementations MUST not “fix” negative R by redefining L downward or inflating B via inconsistent inclusions;
  such moves trigger failure modes (CH04.P14).

---

## B00.CH04.P08.S02 — R/B just above 0: fragility; minimum buffer requirement (additional criteria)

**R/B just above 0** indicates **fragility**: the unit is barely above infeasibility and has little or no capacity
to form or replenish buffer. Small shocks (price spikes, illness, job loss) can push R negative.

Canonical fragility handling:
- define a fragility band ε above zero (parameterized by volatility regime and time resolution),
- require additional criteria beyond “R ≥ 0”, such as:
  - buffer replenishment rate (is R sufficient to rebuild B-buffer after shocks?),
  - volatility-adjusted breach probability (probability R falls below 0 or below α under shock),
  - persistence (how often the unit sits in the near-zero band).

In OBA terms, “barely positive” remainder is not success if the system structurally keeps cohorts near the cliff.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Evaluations MUST not treat R/B > 0 as sufficient. They must report proximity-to-zero mass and apply fragility
  diagnostics where relevant.
- If α is used as an order floor, share below α remains decisive even when R/B is slightly above zero.

---

## B00.CH04.P08.S03 — High B with low R/B: claim-stack diagnosis (E/L dominance)

A common edge pattern is **high gross receipts (B)** with **low remainder ratio (R/B)**. This signals that high
throughput is being captured by **claim layers**.

Canonical diagnosis:
- if B is high but R/B is low, then **T + L** is capturing most of B.
- the primary suspects are:
  - **L inflation by essentials cost regime** (housing/energy/health burdens),
  - **E-driven claim stacks** embedded in essentials (rent extraction, fee stacks, refinancing spreads),
  - **high mandatory transfers** (T) under the regime.

Interpretation rule:
- high B is not evidence of order adequacy; what matters is the retained remainder and buffer capacity.
- if the low R/B is driven by L (especially essentials), the correct next step is a P/E/C decomposition of L into
  function-driven vs E-driven components (see CH04.P09 diagnostics).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any evaluation observing high B with low R/B MUST perform claim-stack diagnostics (decompose T and L; identify
  essentials drivers; map to P/E/C where available).
- Reporting “income growth” as success while R/B remains low is non-conform for OBA-grade claims.

## B00.CH04.P08.S04 — Low B with high R/B: detection of measurement errors or exceptional regimes

The inverse edge pattern is **low B** with **high R/B**. This can be real (exceptional low-burden regimes), but
often indicates measurement boundary errors.

Canonical plausibility checks include:

- **B undercounting:** missing informal income, missing transfers-in, missing in-kind, incorrect consolidation.
- **L undercounting:** essentials basket too narrow, upgrades mis-handled in reverse (baseline excluded), missing
  lock-in burdens or compulsory fees.
- **Timing mismatch:** B and L measured over different windows/resolutions; smoothing applied asymmetrically.
- **Boundary mismatch:** household vs individual boundary inconsistency; entity receipts treated inconsistently.

Exceptional real regimes may include:
- strong commons/public option access that structurally reduces L,
- living arrangements with genuine low essentials burden (e.g., provided housing) with correctly valued in-kind,
- temporary regimes (short periods) that are not stable across longer windows.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any evaluation with low B and high R/B MUST run plausibility checks for undercounting and boundary errors before
  using the result for conformance claims.
- If the pattern is driven by missing data (B or L), results must be flagged uncertain/non-certifying and handled
  under fail-closed discipline.
