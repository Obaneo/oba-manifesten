# B00.CH04.P05 — Distribution requirement: R/B is a profile, not an average

This paragraph establishes the binding distribution requirement for Definition set II:
**R/B must be evaluated as a cohort profile, not as a macro average.**

Averages can “pass” while order fails in the tails. OBA-grade evaluation therefore requires:
- explicit cohort definitions,
- tail-aware statistics,
- micro→macro reconciliation,
- and anti-masking rules (including Simpson’s paradox controls).

Normative posture is conservative: if cohort-level measurement is missing in ways that could hide burden
concentration or extraction, conformance claims must fail closed or be explicitly limited to the measured subset.

---

## B00.CH04.P05.S01 — Cohorts: income deciles, household type, region, sector, age, vulnerability class

Cohorts are canonical index sets for distributional evaluation. Implementations MUST support cohort stratification
sufficient to detect tail failure and structural burden concentration.

Canonical cohort classes include (non-exhaustive):

- **Income position:** deciles/quantiles (or comparable rank metrics) defined on a declared base (gross, net, or
  equivalized—must be explicit).
- **Household type:** composition (adults/children), single-parent, multi-generational, disability/care load.
- **Region:** cost regime and housing/energy price regimes; urban/rural; local market constraints.
- **Sector exposure:** essentials exposure (housing tenure type, energy regime, health coverage regime), platform
  dependence, and other structural dependence markers.
- **Age / life stage:** young adult, mid-career, elderly; retirement boundary effects.
- **Vulnerability class:** volatility exposure, health vulnerability, debt fragility, migration/legal status where
  relevant and permissible under governance rules.

Cohorts must be defined so they are stable and auditable. Cohort definitions may be extended, but must not be chosen
opportunistically to hide tail failure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any R/B evaluation claiming OBA conformance MUST state cohort definitions and the base used for ranking/segmentation.
- If cohort definitions change, results must be restated or explicitly marked as non-comparable across versions.

---

## B00.CH04.P05.S02 — Core statistics: median R/B, p10/p25, share below α, tail risk

R/B reporting MUST include tail-aware statistics, not only central tendency.

Canonical minimum statistics include:

- **Median R/B** (robust central indicator),
- **p10 and p25** of R/B (lower-tail health),
- **Share below α** (fraction of units failing the order floor),
- **Tail risk indicators** (severity and persistence), such as:
  - depth below α (mean shortfall),
  - persistence across periods (duration below α),
  - volatility-adjusted risk (e.g., probability of breach under shock scenarios).

Where R can be negative, reporting MUST explicitly include:
- **share with R < 0**, and
- distribution of negative remainder depth.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Reporting only averages (mean R/B, mean R) is non-conform for OBA-grade claims.
- Any claim of “order adequacy” MUST reference share below α and lower-tail indicators, not only median performance.

---

## B00.CH04.P05.S03 — Aggregation rules: micro→macro consistency; no masking via Simpson’s paradox

Aggregates must reconcile **micro→macro** without semantic drift and must not mask distributional failure.

Canonical requirements:

- **Reconciliation:** sums/aggregates of B/T/L/R across micro units must reconcile to declared macro totals within
  stated measurement error and coverage bounds.
- **Stable weighting:** cohort profiles must declare weights (population, household, equivalized weights) and keep
  them consistent.
- **Anti-masking:** evaluation must check for cases where subgroup deterioration is hidden by composition changes
  (Simpson’s paradox). This requires reporting within-cohort changes alongside aggregate changes.

Examples of masking failures:
- median improves because higher-income share grows while lower-tail worsens,
- aggregate R/B improves via transfers while essentials L rises structurally,
- cohort boundary redefinitions “move” struggling units out of the measured cohort.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-grade evaluation MUST include micro→macro reconciliation checks and document any reconciliation gap.
- If Simpson’s-paradox masking is plausible and not tested, conformance claims must fail closed or be explicitly
  limited and caveated.

---

## B00.CH04.P05.S04 — Minimum publication requirement: R/B profiles + methodology + uncertainty

OBA-grade R/B evaluation is contestable only if profile outputs and methods are published.

Minimum publication requirements (at least):

- cohort definitions and weights,
- period/resolution and smoothing rules (if used),
- definitions and parameterization of B, T, L, α,
- reported profile statistics (median, p10/p25, share below α, tail risk),
- uncertainty reporting:
  - coverage rates,
  - missingness patterns,
  - bands/sensitivity where classification depends on assumptions (especially L basket and regional indices),
- version identifiers (definition-set ID, method version, changelog).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- R/B outputs used for OBA claims MUST publish profile results and methodology; private or black-box profiles are
  non-conform.
- Reporting point estimates without uncertainty where uncertainty is material is non-conform; ambiguous inputs
  require bands and conservative escalation.
