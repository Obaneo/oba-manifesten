# B00.CH04.P10 — Relation to caps: W-MAX/I-MAX and the R/B floor

This paragraph specifies how **caps** (**W-MAX**, **I-MAX**) relate to Definition set II and the order-floor
condition **R/B ≥ α**.

Caps are not evaluated in isolation. Their OBA-grade purpose is to constrain the **storage of extraction** and
thereby prevent structural claim accumulation that raises **L** and compresses **R** over time. If caps do not
protect or improve cohort-level R/B profiles (especially tails), they are cosmetic under OBA.

---

## B00.CH04.P10.S01 — Caps as prevention of claim accumulation that structurally raises L

Claim accumulation stored as wealth and high claim-income is a structural driver of persistent burdens in essentials.
The canonical mechanism is:

- **E-driven surplus → W/I storage → control/power → sustained tolls → L inflation → R compression**

Caps intervene by limiting:
- **W storage** (W-MAX): limits durable claim positions and control-equivalent asset accumulation.
- **I storage rate** (I-MAX): limits claim-flow extraction that feeds W and reinforces power.

By constraining storage, caps aim to:
- weaken toll power in essentials (reducing E→L transmission),
- reduce the ability to bid up scarce essentials (housing, land, monopoly inputs),
- prevent compounding claim stacks that keep cohorts below α.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any cap implementation claiming to support the R/B floor MUST specify the channel by which it prevents L inflation
  (which claim stacks are constrained; which essentials markets are affected).
- If the cap base excludes dominant storage channels (or can be bypassed without capture), its R/B relevance cannot
  be asserted and claims must fail closed or be explicitly limited.

---

## B00.CH04.P10.S02 — Interaction with I-MAX: wage/income compression versus R/B target

I-MAX interacts with R/B because it constrains upper-tail income flows. A poorly designed I-MAX can compress wages
or legitimate productive income in ways that **reduce B** for cohorts near the floor, harming R/B.

Canonical rule: caps must not be implemented as blunt compression that shifts burden downward.

Key distinctions:
- **Claim-income dominance:** when I at the top is primarily claim-based (rents, monopoly profits, fee stacks),
  I-MAX supports R/B by reducing extraction power.
- **Productive income:** when income reflects contestable productive contribution (bounded P), excessive compression
  can reduce incentives/capacity and indirectly raise L via reduced supply/maintenance.

Therefore, I-MAX must be evaluated against:
- cohort-level B impacts (especially near α),
- L impacts (does the cap reduce toll power or does it merely redistribute nominal flows),
- and P/E/C composition (is the constrained I predominantly E-driven?).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX proposal MUST include cohort-level impact analysis on R/B (median + tails) and must demonstrate that
  it does not depress floor cohorts’ B or increase their L.
- If I-MAX materially reduces R/B near the floor, the proposal is non-conform unless compensatory C/L-reduction
  mechanisms are explicit and auditable.

---

## B00.CH04.P10.S03 — Reallocation path: cap skimming → C → L down → R/B up (conditions)

Cap enforcement can generate **skim** (excess above cap). Under OBA, skim improves R/B only if it follows a
traceable structural reallocation path:

- **cap skimming → C allocation → L down (structurally) → R/B up**

The path is valid only under conditions that prevent re-appropriation or cosmetic recycling.

Canonical conditions (all required for C credit on skim):

1) **Traceability:** skim amount is traceably linked from cap calculation → collection → allocation.
2) **Commons destination:** allocation funds defined commons/public option capacity or access regimes that reduce
   essentials burdens or volatility.
3) **Enforceability:** governance prevents conversion into new toll gates; anti-capture safeguards are binding.
4) **Measurable effect:** evaluation shows L reduction (or stabilisation) and R/B improvement with uncertainty bands.
5) **Non-substitution:** allocation is additional (not replacing existing C while leaving L unchanged).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- A cap proposal claiming R/B improvement MUST specify the reallocation mechanism and publish the audit trail.
- If skim is used for general spending without a demonstrable L-reduction mechanism, it cannot be credited as C for
  R/B-floor claims (it may still be fiscal policy, but not OBA-grade structural remediation).

---

## B00.CH04.P10.S04 — Forbidden compensation: circumventing caps via price increases in essentials (L inflation)

A primary avoidance pattern is **forbidden compensation**: actors circumvent caps by shifting extraction into
essentials pricing, thereby inflating **L** and negating R/B gains.

Examples (non-exhaustive):
- raising rents, fees, or essential tariffs to compensate for capped wealth/income storage,
- introducing new mandatory add-ons in essentials (service fees, “premium baseline” tiers),
- tightening lock-in and exit friction to preserve toll power,
- shifting extraction into hidden-price channels that raise effective essentials burdens.

Canonical rule: any cap regime must be paired with anti-avoidance controls that prevent L inflation as a bypass
channel. If caps raise L, they fail their order purpose.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any cap implementation MUST include anti-avoidance tests that monitor essentials L inflation and fee-stack growth.
- If evidence shows caps induce compensatory L increases (net R/B harm), the cap implementation is non-conform unless
  corrected; continued use requires rollback/sunset or redesigned enforcement.
