# B00.CH04.P01 — Purpose and scope of Definition set II

This paragraph establishes the **purpose**, **scope**, and **normative status** of Definition set II:
the canonical remainder-space decomposition **B → T → N → L → R** and the order-floor condition **R/B ≥ α**.

The purpose is not descriptive accounting. It is **order evaluation** at life-world resolution: a formal interface
that makes household (and cohort) feasibility, buffer capacity, and shock resilience **measurable**, **auditable**,
and **reconcilable** from micro to macro. Definition set II therefore binds:
- normative claims (“people have sufficient remainder/buffer”),
- data objects (income, levies, essentials burdens),
- and evaluation procedures (profiles, thresholds, reconciliation tests).

Normative status: any document, dataset, implementation, or policy instrument claiming OBA conformance/compatibility
that uses life-world adequacy, burdens, or affordability MUST use this canonical decomposition (or an explicitly
versioned extension that does not redefine the base quantities).

---

## B00.CH04.P01.S01 — R/B as primary life-world order indicator (buffer, autonomy, shock resilience)

**R/B** is the primary life-world order indicator because it measures whether agents retain **remaining space**
relative to their gross receipts and can form a **buffer** that sustains autonomy under shocks.

Canonical interpretation:

- **R (remaining space)** is what remains after mandatory transfers and essential burdens: it is the feasible space
  for buffer formation, discretionary choice, time autonomy, and error tolerance.
- **B (gross receipts)** anchors scale: R is meaningful only relative to the size of the receipts base and the
  volatility regime.
- **R/B** is therefore a normalized indicator of:
  - **buffer capacity** (ability to absorb shocks without cascading failure),
  - **autonomy** (freedom from immediate claim-layer coercion),
  - **shock resilience** (reduced probability of arrears, forced debt, forced relocation, or health/education
    degradation in response to shocks).

R/B is not a welfare index and not a “happiness KPI”. It is a structural adequacy condition: if R/B persistently
fails (especially in tails), the order is unstable even if macro aggregates appear healthy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-grade adequacy claim MUST use R/B (and cohort profiles) as the primary life-world indicator, not macro
  averages or nominal income levels alone.
- Any implementation that reports R/B MUST specify the measurement resolution and smoothing rules (where relevant),
  because volatility changes the meaning of “buffer”.

---

## B00.CH04.P01.S02 — GROSS→NET→ESSENTIALS→REMAINING SPACE as canonical decomposition (micro and macro)

The canonical decomposition is:

- **B** (gross receipts)  
- **T** (mandatory transfer-out)  
- **N = B − T** (net after transfers)  
- **L** (essentials / claim layers not plausibly avoidable)  
- **R = N − L** (remaining space)

This decomposition is canonical at **micro level** (household/entity) and must reconcile to **macro level**
(aggregates by cohort/sector/jurisdiction) without semantic drift.

The decomposition is designed to prevent “passing by interpretation”:
- B inflation (relabeling, double counting, volatile spikes) must be controlled,
- L inflation (reclassifying optional spend as essential) is forbidden,
- T must be strict (mandatory only),
- and micro→macro reconciliation must preserve distributional reality (no masking via aggregation).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-critical evaluation MUST use the canonical identity chain; alternative decompositions are non-conform
  unless explicitly mapped to B/T/N/L/R without changing meaning.
- Implementations MUST preserve stock/flow and time-resolution discipline consistent with the canon; ambiguous
  periodization is non-auditable and fails closed.

---

## B00.CH04.P01.S03 — Scope of application: household, organisation, sector, jurisdiction

Definition set II applies wherever life-world adequacy and claim-layer pressure can be evaluated:

- **Household / person-level:** primary locus for life-world adequacy (R/B, α floor, tail risks).
- **Organisation / entity-level:** where entities function as “life-world containers” (e.g., institutions that bear
  essential burdens or provide essential services) and where internal claim structures affect downstream households.
- **Sector-level:** essentials sectors (housing, energy, health, basic connectivity, mobility, basic food) and any
  domain where L can structurally rise through extraction.
- **Jurisdiction-level:** legal and price regimes define T rules, essentials baskets, and enforcement of access;
  cross-jurisdiction comparability requires explicit indexation and regime notes.

The scope is intentionally broad to prevent extraction from migrating into “out of scope” domains while R/B claims
are made at the top.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any domain materially affecting essentials burdens or net-of-transfer feasibility is within scope and cannot be
  excluded by convention for OBA claims.
- If certain scopes cannot be measured (data gaps), the evaluation MUST disclose coverage limits and cannot claim
  conformance on the unmeasured component.

## B00.CH04.P01.S04 — Relation to P/E/C, caps and ΔM (linkages and dependencies)

Definition set II is downstream of P/E/C and upstream of caps and ΔM in the evaluation stack.

### Link to P/E/C
- **E → L pressure:** extraction in essentials translates into claim layers (rent, tolls, fee stacks, refinancing
  spreads), shrinking R and pushing cohorts below α.
- **P → R stabilisation:** productive and maintenance capacity reduces volatility and lowers lifecycle burdens.
- **C → structural L reduction:** public option/commons access reduces dependence and disciplines toll power.

Therefore, R/B profiles must be interpreted alongside P/E/C composition, especially within essentials.

### Link to caps (W-MAX/I-MAX)
Caps constrain the storage of extracted surplus that would otherwise reinforce power and keep L structurally high.
Caps are evaluated partly by whether they enable **L down → R/B up** pathways (including via C allocations under
traceable conditions).

### Link to ΔM
ΔM channel rules must be evaluated by demonstrable effects on R/B profiles:
- permitted channels are those that improve or stabilise R/B (typically by expanding C capacity or lowering
  essentials burdens),
- forbidden channels include asset support that raises L (e.g., housing rent pressure) and worsens R/B.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any cap or ΔM proposal claiming OBA compatibility MUST specify the causal chain to R/B outcomes (ΔM → C/essentials
  → L → R/B, or caps → C → L down → R/B up) with evidence standards.
- Any evaluation that claims success by raising median R/B while tails deteriorate is non-conform (distributional
  requirement binds).
