# B00.CH04.P11 — Relation to ΔM: channel rules and macro stability via R/B

This paragraph specifies how **ΔM (monetary allocation / monetary space)** must be evaluated through the lens of
Definition set II: the canonical remainder-space decomposition **B → T → N → L → R** and cohort-level **R/B**
profiles.

Within OBA, ΔM is not “stimulus” by default. It is an allocation interface that must satisfy **channel discipline**:
ΔM is conforming only insofar as it demonstrably improves or stabilises life-world order outcomes (R/B profiles),
primarily by reducing essentials burdens (L) or stabilising volatility, rather than inflating asset prices or
reinforcing claim stacks.

---

## B00.CH04.P11.S01 — ΔM allocation must demonstrably improve (or stabilise) R/B profiles

A ΔM allocation is OBA-grade only if it has a demonstrable effect on **cohort R/B profiles** (median and tails),
with declared uncertainty and a plausible causal mechanism.

Canonical admissible improvement mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):
- lowering L structurally via essentials capacity expansion (housing supply, energy reliability, baseline health access),
- stabilising volatility regimes (reducing shock pass-through into essentials prices and household burdens),
- funding C objects (public option / commons access) that discipline toll power,
- targeted relief that is explicitly temporary and does not inflate L through pricing feedbacks.

ΔM evaluations must be cohort-aware: “macro stabilization” that leaves tail cohorts below α is not sufficient under
the distribution requirement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any ΔM program claiming OBA compatibility MUST report pre/post cohort R/B profiles (including share below α and
  tail risk) and must specify the mechanism by which ΔM affects B/T/L/R.
- If a ΔM allocation cannot be linked to measurable R/B outcomes (or is unmeasured), OBA claims must fail closed.

---

## B00.CH04.P11.S02 — Forbidden channel: asset support that drives up L (rent/housing) and harms R/B

A canonical forbidden ΔM channel is **asset support** that inflates essential asset prices and thereby increases **L**.
The archetype is housing: ΔM that bids up land/housing prices transmits into rent and mortgage burdens, raising L
and compressing R.

Forbidden channel pattern:
- ΔM increases purchasing power or liquidity into an asset-constrained essentials market (housing/land),
- supply is inelastic or gated (planning barriers, monopoly land positions),
- prices rise, and essentials burdens (L) rise faster than receipts (B) for floor cohorts.

This is non-conform even if it “supports balance sheets” or “stabilizes markets” because it worsens the life-world
order indicator.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any ΔM program must explicitly test for L inflation in essentials (especially housing) as a first-order constraint.
- If the dominant effect of ΔM is higher essentials asset prices or higher rent burdens (L up), the channel is
  forbidden for OBA purposes and must be redesigned or rejected.

---

## B00.CH04.P11.S03 — Shock regime: temporary ΔM interventions with sunset and audit requirement

ΔM may be used in shock regimes (crisis stabilization) under strict temporal and audit discipline.

Canonical shock-regime constraints:

- **Temporality:** interventions must be explicitly time-bounded (sunset) and not become permanent channels by drift.
- **Targeting discipline:** shock ΔM must prioritize preventing R/B collapse in tails (avoid mass R<0), not preserving
  asset valuations at the expense of essentials burdens.
- **Exit strategy:** criteria for scaling down and rollback must be declared ex ante.
- **Auditability:** shock regimes must produce an audit trail and publish post hoc evaluation of R/B impact and
  L inflation risk.

Shock ΔM is permitted only as a controlled exception; permanent reliance on shock logic is a failure mode.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any shock-regime ΔM MUST include sunset/rollback criteria and a post-implementation audit of R/B profiles and L.
- If an intervention remains active without renewed evidence/ratification, it becomes non-conform by default.

---

## B00.CH04.P11.S04 — Traceability: causal chain ΔM → C/essentials → L → R/B (evidence standard)

OBA requires traceability: ΔM must be linked through a causal chain to life-world outcomes.

Canonical trace chain (typical):
- **ΔM → (C objects and/or essentials capacity) → L down or stabilised → R/B up or stabilised**

Evidence standard requirements:

- **Channel specification:** identify the receiving channel (public option, infrastructure, targeted relief, regulated
  essentials capacity) and its governance regime.
- **Intermediate metrics:** measure capacity outputs and/or price/burden effects (ΔG proxies; essentials price indices;
  service-level reliability).
- **Outcome metrics:** report cohort-level R/B profile changes (median + tails; share below α; tail risk).
- **Counterfactual discipline:** where feasible, show that observed changes are not dominated by composition changes
  or measurement artifacts (Simpson’s paradox controls; boundary consistency).

Where traceability cannot be established due to missing data or opaque channels, the intervention cannot claim OBA
compatibility on that component.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any ΔM program claiming OBA compatibility MUST publish the trace chain, intermediate metrics, and R/B outcomes with
  uncertainty/coverage.
- Black-box “macro stabilization” claims without traceability to L and cohort R/B are non-conform by default.
