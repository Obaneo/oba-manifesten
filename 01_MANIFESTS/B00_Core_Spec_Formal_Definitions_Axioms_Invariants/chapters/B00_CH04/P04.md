# B00.CH04.P04 — Strict classification rules for L (burdens/essentials/claim layers)

This paragraph defines strict, binding rules for classifying **L (essentials / claim layers)**. L represents
burdens that are **not plausibly avoidable** for the evaluated unit without unacceptable loss of basic function,
legality, or survival-level participation in society, under the declared jurisdiction/sector regime.

L is where extraction becomes life-world pressure: E-driven tolls embedded in essentials manifest as unavoidable
claims. If L is allowed to drift, implementations can “pass” R/B targets by:
- deflating L (excluding unavoidable burdens as “choices”), or
- inflating L (including discretionary upgrades to justify low R as “high cost of living”).

Therefore, L classification is parameterized, auditable, and conservative.

---

## B00.CH04.P04.S01 — Essentials: housing, energy, health care, mobility, basic connectivity, basic food (canonical set + parameters)

The canonical essentials set for L includes baseline burdens for:

- **Housing** (shelter baseline)
- **Energy** (baseline heating/cooking/electricity access)
- **Health care** (baseline access and necessary care)
- **Mobility** (baseline participation mobility, parameterized by context)
- **Basic connectivity** (communications/digital baseline)
- **Basic food** (nutritional baseline)

Essentials are not “average spend”; they are **baseline bundles** defined by parameters that must be explicit and
auditable. Parameterization MUST include (at minimum, where relevant):

- baseline service level (minimum adequate standard),
- household composition adjustments (size, age structure, vulnerability),
- regional price regime / indices,
- climate and geography modifiers (where essential to the baseline),
- admissible substitution set (what counts as a plausible alternative baseline).

Essentials definitions must be stable within a release and versioned across releases.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any L computation MUST publish the essentials parameter regime (baseline definitions, indices, admissible substitutes).
- If a component is included in L without an explicit baseline parameter definition, the inclusion is non-auditable
  and must fail closed for OBA conformance claims.

## B00.CH04.P04.S02 — Contractual lock: necessary burdens due to lock-in/exit friction count as L

Some burdens are not “essential goods” in themselves but become **essential claim layers** because credible exit is
blocked by lock-in or exit friction.

A burden MUST be counted in L if:
- avoiding it requires exit actions that are not realistically feasible for the unit (cost, timing, legal constraints),
- switching involves punitive penalties, loss of basic function, or inaccessible alternatives,
- the burden is tied to an essential (e.g., forced service bundles tied to housing or work access).

Canonical lock-in categories (non-exhaustive):
- long-duration contracts with penalties that are functionally unavoidable (housing, utilities, telecom),
- required complements tied to essential access (bundled insurance, mandatory add-ons),
- proprietary ecosystems where leaving implies losing basic functionality (portability denial),
- geographic lock where essential alternatives do not exist within feasible relocation bounds.

Key rule: “contract signed” does not imply “avoidable”. Avoidability is functional, not formal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any lock-in-based L inclusion MUST document the exit friction mechanism (penalties, switching costs, portability constraints).
- Conversely, excluding a burden from L on “choice” grounds requires auditable evidence of credible alternatives and exit.

---

## B00.CH04.P04.S03 — Financial claim layers: interest/fees/insurance compulsion within essentials count as L

Financial layers embedded in essential access are part of L when they are functionally unavoidable for the unit.

This includes (non-exhaustive):
- **interest spreads** and refinancing costs required to maintain housing access (mortgage/tenant financing),
- **mandatory fees** and servicing layers tied to essential contracts (utilities, telecom, health coverage),
- **compulsory insurance** required as a condition of essential access (housing, mobility, health regimes),
- **penalty layers** that become structurally recurring due to volatility and low buffer (late fees, overdraft spirals)
  when the structure is unavoidable rather than purely behavioral.

Classification discipline:
- If a financial layer is required to maintain essential access and exit is not plausible, it is L regardless of
  whether it is “priced separately”.
- Where the layer includes an extractive component (E), it remains part of L at the life-world level; decomposition
  into E vs functional finance occurs in the P/E/C layer, not by excluding it from L.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- L computation MUST include essential-linked financial layers where they are compulsory or structurally unavoidable.
- Excluding such layers requires demonstrating credible non-financial access paths or credible exit; otherwise
  exclusion is non-conform.

## B00.CH04.P04.S04 — Forbidden inflation of L: luxury/optional upgrades may not be classified as essentials

L must not be inflated by including discretionary consumption or luxury upgrades. Inflation of L destroys the meaning
of R/B and enables “passing” by narrative.

Forbidden L inclusions include (non-exhaustive):
- housing upgrades beyond the baseline adequate standard (premium location, excess space, luxury amenities),
- premium energy packages or convenience tariffs not required for baseline access,
- elective health services not required for baseline care (cosmetic/non-essential),
- discretionary mobility upgrades (premium vehicles, excess travel) beyond baseline participation needs,
- premium connectivity and entertainment bundles beyond basic connectivity.

Rule: if a downgrade to the baseline is **plausibly available** without unacceptable loss of basic function, the
upgrade portion is not L.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST separate baseline essentials from upgrades; upgrades MUST NOT be counted in L.
- If the baseline cannot be defined or measured, the L estimate must be flagged as non-auditable and cannot certify
  an OBA conformance claim (fail-closed on the affected component).
