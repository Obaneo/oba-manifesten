# B00.CH04.P06 — Definition of α (order threshold) and parameter regime

This paragraph defines **α** as the canonical **order threshold** used with **R/B**:
the floor condition **R/B ≥ α**. α is not a rhetorical target; it is a binding parameter that encodes a minimum
life-world requirement for **buffer**, **agency**, and **shock resilience**.

α is a parameter of the definition set, not an implementation convenience. Any OBA-grade evaluation MUST declare
the α regime it uses (value, indexing, cohort variation rules, and change protocol) and must treat α changes as a
versioned normative event.

---

## B00.CH04.P06.S01 — α as canonical parameter: meaning (minimum buffer/agency)

**α** represents the minimum admissible ratio of remaining space to gross receipts required for:
- **buffer formation** (ability to absorb shocks without cascading failure),
- **agency** (non-trivial discretionary space; avoidance of coercive claim dominance),
- **shock resilience** (reduced probability of arrears, forced debt spirals, forced relocation, or foregone care).

Interpretation constraints:

- α is not “minimum consumption”. It is a minimum **post-essentials remainder share**.
- α is not a one-period slogan. The meaning depends on periodization and volatility regime; evaluation MUST state
  the measurement window and smoothing (if applied).
- α must be evaluated alongside tail risk: meeting α at the median while failing in tails is not sufficient under
  the distribution requirement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any R/B evaluation MUST declare the α value(s), time resolution, and smoothing assumptions used.
- If α is used implicitly or shifted silently between evaluations, results are non-comparable and non-conform.

---

## B00.CH04.P06.S02 — α per cohort: allowed variation and prohibited discrimination patterns

α may vary by cohort only under explicitly defined, auditable rules that preserve the meaning of the order floor.

Allowed cohort variation may exist to reflect:
- structural volatility differences (income volatility, seasonal regimes),
- unavoidable essentials regimes (climate/geography baseline modifiers),
- life-stage constraints (e.g., elderly care burdens) where justified and not used to reduce the floor for vulnerable
  groups.

Prohibited patterns:
- α must not be lowered for cohorts precisely because they are burdened or weak (that would encode injustice as a
  parameter).
- α must not embed discriminatory treatment or “poverty normalization” (lowering the floor to make failure disappear).
- α variation must not be used to mask extraction or burden concentration (Goodhart/target gaming).

Canonical rule: variation may adjust for legitimate structural parameters, but must not reduce the minimum buffer
meaning for vulnerable cohorts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any cohort-specific α must publish its justification, parameter basis, and impact on share-below-α outcomes.
- If α variation reduces measured failure by redefining adequacy downward for vulnerable groups, it is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH04.P06.S03 — Procedure for α changes: evidence, impact analysis, ratification, sunset/rollback

α changes are normative events with distributional consequences. Therefore changes MUST follow a canonical procedure.

Minimum change procedure:

1) **Evidence basis:** empirical evidence and rationale for change (volatility regimes, essentials baselines,
   macro stability, life-world feasibility).
2) **Impact analysis:** cohort-level impact on R/B profiles (median, tails, share below α), and sensitivity to L and B
   measurement assumptions.
3) **Ratification:** explicit approval under the OBA governance interface (defined authority, documented decision).
4) **Sunset/rollback clause:** changes must include a review window and rollback trigger conditions if adverse effects
   are observed (tail deterioration, L inflation incentives, gaming behavior).

Versioning requirement:
- α changes must be versioned and logged; evaluations must reference the α regime version used.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any evaluation that changes α without publishing evidence + impact analysis + ratification record is non-conform.
- α changes must not be retroactively applied without restatement and explicit comparability notes.

---

## B00.CH04.P06.S04 — Non-negotiables: no α reduction via semantic redefinition of L or B

The order floor cannot be weakened by semantic tricks. The following are non-negotiable constraints:

- **No L deflation**: you may not “raise” R/B by excluding essentials burdens from L via relabeling as voluntary or
  by narrowing the essentials basket without canonical justification.
- **No B inflation**: you may not “raise” R/B by inflating B through double counting, non-accruing gross flows, or
  inconsistent inclusion of transfers.
- **No α laundering**: lowering α to make failure disappear without structural improvement is non-conform.

Canonical invariance rule:
- If the underlying constraint structure (essentials costs, lock-in, toll mechanisms) is unchanged, then any apparent
  improvement in R/B produced solely by redefining B/L/α is invalid for OBA-grade claims.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any material change to L definitions, B boundary rules, or α must be treated as a definition-set version change and
  must trigger restatements or non-comparability flags.
- If an implementation achieves R/B ≥ α primarily via semantic redefinition rather than structural change, it is
  non-conform and triggers failure modes under CH04.P14.
