# B00.CH02.P04 Flow and contract objects (transaction model)

This paragraph defines the canonical transaction model used by the Core Spec: **Flow objects** and **Contract objects**. OBA-critical evaluation (P/E/C classification, B/T/N/L/R decomposition, caps (W/I) measurement, ΔM traceability, and anti-omzeiling detection) depends on being able to attribute economic activity at the level of concrete transfers and the contractual rules that govern them.

The Core Spec therefore treats transactions as two coupled layers:

- **Flow layer**: what moved, between whom, when, and under which label.
- **Contract layer**: the rule system that determined the price, obligations, lock-in, and allocation of rights/powers relevant for control/benefit/block and for P/E/C splitting.

This object model is intentionally minimal: it does not prescribe accounting systems or payment technologies. It specifies the **minimum metadata** required for semantic closure and auditability.

Scope of this paragraph:

- Defines the minimum required fields for Flow objects and Contract objects.
- Defines canonical linking between flows and contracts (including bundles and composite agreements).
- Specifies chain attribution mechanics for supply chains and platform stacks.
- Specifies rules to prevent double counting and to reconcile gross and net flows under internal recharging.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any OBA-critical classification or metric MUST be derivable from Flow objects linked to Contract objects with the required metadata.
- Where Flow or Contract metadata is missing, implementations MUST apply conservative defaults and MUST fail closed for any dependent conformance claim.
- Implementations MUST ensure that aggregation over flows does not double count the same underlying economic transfer.

## B00.CH02.P04.S01 Flow object: {payer, payee, amount, timestamp, contract, label}

A **Flow object** represents a single economic transfer (or an atomic component of a transfer) observed at a particular time resolution.

Canonical Flow object (minimum fields):

- **payer**: Actor/Entity identifier of the party that pays (or transfers value).
- **payee**: Actor/Entity identifier of the receiving party.
- **amount**: numeric value + currency/unit (per canonical unit conventions).
- **timestamp**: time index or interval consistent with the canonical time model.
- **contract**: reference to a Contract object (or contract set) that governs the flow.
- **label**: canonical classification tag(s) for bookkeeping context (e.g., wage, rent, interest, platform fee, tax, subsidy, refund). Labels are not determinative; they are inputs for audit and default attribution.

Optional but commonly required fields (allowed):

- **instrument**: cash, in-kind, tokenized claim, credit, offset, etc.
- **jurisdiction/source**: where relevant for regime application.
- **channel/account**: payment rail or ledger reference (for traceability).
- **counterflow link**: references to related flows (refunds, reversals, internal pass-through).

Atomicity and decomposition.

- A recorded payment MAY be decomposed into multiple Flow objects when required for P/E/C splitting, for T vs non-T classification, or for gross/net reconciliation.
- Decomposition MUST preserve conservation: the sum of component flows MUST equal the original observed amount (within declared rounding rules).

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Every Flow object used for an OBA-critical result MUST have payer, payee, amount (with unit), timestamp, and a resolvable contract reference (or an explicit “no contract” declaration where applicable).
- Flow labels MUST NOT be treated as sufficient evidence of classification; classification MUST be justified via contract terms and structural conditions where required.
- Where a flow is bundled (single invoice covering multiple components), the implementation MUST either (i) decompose it into auditable component flows, or (ii) treat the entire flow conservatively under the relevant rules (typically toward E / toward inclusion in cap base / toward non-conform for dependent claims).

## B00.CH02.P04.S02 Contract object: price rule, indexation, lock-in, exit, penalties

A **Contract object** represents the binding rule system that governs one or more flows. The contract may be explicit (signed agreement) or implicit (regulated tariff schedule, terms of service, platform policy, statutory rule). In all cases, it must be representable as an auditable object with the required attributes.

Canonical Contract object (minimum attributes):

- **price rule**: how payment amounts are determined (unit price, subscription, two-part tariff, auction rule, dynamic pricing, take-rate formula, interest rate formula).
- **indexation**: how prices/fees adjust over time (CPI link, floating rate, discretionary adjustment, algorithmic adjustment).
- **lock-in**: features that create switching costs or entrenchment (minimum term, auto-renewal, data portability limits, exclusivity, bundling constraints).
- **exit**: termination rights and processes (notice periods, portability requirements, forced continuity, offboarding constraints).
- **penalties**: early termination fees, clawbacks, liquidation damages, non-compete enforcement, punitive pricing tiers.

Additional attributes (often necessary for OBA-critical evaluation):

- **allocation of rights**: who controls key parameters (unilateral change clauses, governance rights, arbitration/forum constraints).
- **service levels/maintenance obligations**: relevant for commons status and for P vs E evaluation.
- **auditability**: availability of terms, versioning of ToS, change logs, and dispute mechanism.

Contract versioning.

- Contracts MUST be versioned when terms change over time (e.g., ToS updates, tariff schedule updates). Flows MUST reference the applicable contract version at the time the flow was incurred.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- For any OBA-critical evaluation that depends on lock-in, take-rate, indexation, or exit friction, the Contract object MUST include auditable representations of those terms (text, structured fields, or both).
- If the contract terms are unobservable or black-box (e.g., uninspectable ranking rules that determine fees), then any claim that relies on those terms MUST fail closed unless a canonical evidence substitute exists.
- Unilateral-change clauses and opaque discretionary pricing SHOULD be treated as elevated E-risk and MUST be reflected in classification and audit flags where relevant.

## B00.CH02.P04.S03 Supply chains and platform stacks (chain attribution)

Many economically significant outcomes arise not from a single flow but from **chains**: supply chains (multi-stage production and distribution) and platform stacks (multiple intermediated layers: app store → payment rail → ad exchange → cloud/API layer → identity layer).

Chain attribution requirement.

- Implementations MUST support attributing value, extraction, and commons contributions along chains by linking Flow objects through Contract objects and by mapping intermediaries’ positions/roles.

Canonical chain model (minimum):

- A chain is an ordered (or partially ordered) set of linked flows where:
  - the output of one stage enables the next stage, and/or
  - multiple intermediaries impose fees/tolls on the same functional interaction.
- Each stage MUST be representable by (payer, payee, amount, contract, timestamp) with consistent identity resolution.

Chain attribution aims:

- **Decomposition for P/E/C**: separate functional production/maintenance components from toll/lock-in components across layers.
- **Cascade detection**: identify fee-stacks where multiple layers extract from the same base transaction.
- **Traceability**: establish lineage from an end-user payment back through intermediaries to ultimate beneficiaries (Actors via UBC where required).

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Where a domain is structurally intermediated (platforms, finance stacks, regulated utilities with subcontracting), OBA-critical reporting MUST provide chain-aware attribution or explicitly declare the claim out of scope.
- Any P/E/C splitting that depends on chain structure MUST be reproducible from the chain graph and the linked Flow/Contract objects.
- Chain links MUST NOT be asserted by narrative; they MUST be supported by references (invoice IDs, settlement references, contract routing) or by declared inference rules with conservative error bounds.

## B00.CH02.P04.S04 Avoiding double counting: gross/net and internal recharging

Accurate aggregation requires explicit rules to avoid counting the same economic transfer multiple times when it passes through intermediaries or when organizations use internal recharging (cross-charges, transfer pricing, cost allocations).

Canonical principle.

- **Gross flows** are recorded at the point they occur (payer→payee).
- **Net measurement** is derived by defined transformations that eliminate pass-through and internal duplication while preserving economically meaningful margins and claim layers.

Internal recharging.

- Internal charges between consolidated entities (within a UBC group or within a defined consolidation boundary) MUST be flagged as **internal** and MUST NOT inflate external receipts or burdens in macro aggregation.
- Where internal recharging masks extraction (e.g., management-fee loops), the internal tag does not immunize it: it becomes an anti-omzeiling signal and may require reclassification.

Pass-through and agency.

- Flows received “on behalf of” another party (agent collection, escrow, marketplace collection) MUST be represented as either:
  - (i) two flows (end-user→agent; agent→principal) with explicit pass-through flags, or
  - (ii) a single principal flow plus an explicit fee flow, provided auditability is preserved.

Refunds, reversals, offsets.

- Refunds and reversals MUST be linked to original flows to prevent both omission and duplication.
- Offsets/netting arrangements (e.g., clearing) MUST preserve reconstructability of underlying gross flows for audit, at least at the level required for OBA-critical metrics.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations MUST define and apply consistent gross/net rules and MUST publish them as part of the method registry when making OBA claims.
- Internal flows within a consolidation boundary MUST be excluded from external aggregates unless the evaluation explicitly concerns internal allocation mechanics (in which case they must be clearly labeled as internal).
- Any aggregation that cannot demonstrate “no double count” via flow lineage and consolidation rules MUST be treated as non-reproducible and MUST fail closed for conformance claims that depend on it.
