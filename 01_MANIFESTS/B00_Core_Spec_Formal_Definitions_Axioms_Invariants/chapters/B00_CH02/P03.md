# B00.CH02.P03 Actor, position and role model (economic entities)

This paragraph defines the canonical object model for economic entities in OBA evaluation. OBA claims about P/E/C, B/T/N/L/R, caps (W-MAX/I-MAX), ΔM channels, and commons governance require unambiguous answers to “who is involved, in what capacity, with what powers, and with what economic exposure.” The actor/position/role model is the minimum structure needed to attribute flows and stocks consistently and to prevent evasion via legal form, nominee structures, or organizational complexity.

The Core Spec therefore distinguishes:

- **Actors** (natural persons) as the ultimate locus of human agency and ultimate consolidation.
- **Entities** (legal persons/vehicles) as containers and instruments through which positions are held and contracts executed.
- **Positions** as canonical economic relationships (owner/steward/operator/platform/financier) that define default attribution expectations.
- **Roles** as attribute-level powers and duties (control/benefit/block/duty) that determine look-through consolidation and override purely formal labels.

Scope of this paragraph:

- Governs the canonical definitions of Actor, Entity, Position, and Role used across the Core Spec.
- Defines a minimal set of positions and role attributes required for consistent attribution.
- Establishes graph-based look-through and consolidation requirements for OBA-critical evaluation.
- Applies to wealth (W), income/flows (I), P/E/C classification at flow level, ΔM beneficiaries behind intermediaries, and commons governance/control.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any OBA-critical attribution MUST identify the relevant Actors and Entities and MUST state the Positions and Role attributes that justify attribution.
- Legal form MUST NOT override economic reality: consolidation and evaluation MUST follow role attributes (control/benefit/block) under look-through rules.
- Where the relevant actor/entity/role structure is not observable or not disclosed, evaluation MUST be conservative and MUST fail closed (toward consolidation and toward non-conformance for dependent claims).

## B00.CH02.P03.S01 Actor (natural person) vs entity (legal person/vehicle)

**Actor.** An Actor is a natural person. Actors are the canonical endpoints for ultimate beneficial control (UBC) and for any evaluation that depends on human agency, distributive outcomes, or power concentration. In OBA, “who” ultimately means “which Actor(s)”, even if all activity is mediated through entities.

**Entity.** An Entity is a legal person or vehicle capable of holding assets, entering contracts, and operating accounts (e.g., corporation, partnership, trust, foundation, SPV, fund, DAO-like wrapper where applicable). Entities are not the ultimate moral or distributive unit; they are attribution containers that may or may not be consolidated to Actors depending on role attributes.

**Why the distinction matters.** Many forms of avoidance are simply actor–entity separation: benefits accrue to Actors while formal ownership/control appears elsewhere. OBA therefore treats the actor/entity distinction as fundamental and makes “ultimate attribution” an explicit requirement.

Minimum attributes.

- Actor MUST have an identifier and jurisdiction/residency metadata when relevant for regime application.
- Entity MUST have an identifier, legal form, jurisdiction of registration, and relationship links (ownership, governance, contractual control) sufficient to evaluate control/benefit/block.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any OBA-critical statement about W, I, P/E/C, ΔM allocation, or commons governance MUST be attributable to one or more Actors, either directly or via look-through consolidation.
- Entity-only reporting without actor-level ultimate attribution is insufficient for OBA-critical claims unless the claim is explicitly scoped to entity-level accounting and does not assert distributive or power-related properties.
- If an entity structure prevents identification of the relevant Actors (opacity, nominees, untraceable custody), evaluation MUST default toward consolidation assumptions and MUST fail closed for any claim relying on precise attribution.

## B00.CH02.P03.S02 Positions: owner, steward, operator, platform, financier

A **Position** is a canonical economic relationship between an Actor/Entity and an asset, system, contract, or flow domain. Positions are not “job titles” or legal labels; they are evaluation categories that set default expectations for what kinds of power and benefit typically attach.

Canonical positions (minimum set):

1) **Owner**  
   Holds residual claim on upside and typically some governance rights over the asset/entity/system. Owners are the default locus for benefit attribution of residual cashflows and value appreciation, subject to contractual overrides and role attributes.

2) **Steward**  
   Holds a mandate to manage an asset/system in the interest of a defined beneficiary set under explicit duties (duty of care, duty of loyalty, maintenance obligations). Stewardship is defined by bounded discretion + duties + contestability, not by ownership.

3) **Operator**  
   Executes operations that produce or maintain functional output. Operators typically control processes and delivery, but do not necessarily own the asset or capture residual upside. Operator position is central for P (production/maintenance) attribution and for responsibility assignment.

4) **Platform**  
   Controls an access layer, matching layer, ranking/curation layer, API layer, or rule-setting interface that intermediates interactions between others. Platform position is characterized by choke-point potential (block power) and take-rate extraction risk.

5) **Financier**  
   Provides capital, liquidity, insurance, guarantees, or balance-sheet capacity in exchange for contractual claims (interest, fees, covenants, collateral rights). Financier position is central for evaluating claim stacks, leverage, and E-drivers via lock-in or collateral dominance.

Position usage.

- A single Actor/Entity may hold multiple positions simultaneously (e.g., owner-operator; platform-financier).
- Positions are not mutually exclusive and are resolved by role attributes (control/benefit/block/duty) where conflicts arise.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any OBA-critical flow or asset under evaluation SHOULD be tagged with the relevant position(s) of the involved parties when position materially affects classification, attribution, or consolidation.
- Implementations MUST NOT rely on local/novel position labels to evade the canonical set; additional labels MAY be used, but MUST map to the canonical positions and MUST NOT change their meaning.
- Where position assignment is ambiguous, the implementation MUST document the assignment rule and MUST apply it consistently; otherwise evaluation MUST fail closed for dependent claims.

## B00.CH02.P03.S03 Roles as attributes (control, benefit, block, duty)

A **Role** in OBA is an attribute-level characterization of power and responsibility that attaches to an Actor/Entity in relation to an asset, entity, system, or contract. Roles are not categorical identities; they are measurable (or at least auditable) properties used for consolidation, anti-avoidance, and classification.

Minimum role attributes (canonical):

- **control**: ability to make or direct decisions (de jure or de facto).
- **benefit**: right to receive economic upside (cashflows, appreciation, risk transfer).
- **block**: ability to veto, gate, exclude, or impose exit friction (negative control).
- **duty**: enforceable obligations (maintenance duty, fiduciary duty, service levels, disclosure duties).

Role attributes are evaluated by tests (see control/benefit/block tests and commons governance minima) and are the primary determinants of:

- **Ultimate attribution** (who is responsible / who benefits),
- **Consolidation** (what is counted together for caps and evaluation),
- **Classification risk** (e.g., block power as an E-driver in platform contexts).

Role precedence.

- Role attributes override position labels and legal form.
- Where control and benefit diverge (e.g., dual-class shares; trustees; protectors), the evaluation MUST use the canonical conflict rules (defined elsewhere) and MUST document the resolution.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- OBA-critical evaluations MUST be based on role attributes where role attributes are relevant to the claim (especially for W/I caps, ΔM beneficiaries, platform power, and commons governance).
- Implementations MUST provide an auditable rationale for assigned role attributes (documents, observable proxies, or declared assumptions with conservative defaults).
- If role attributes cannot be determined due to opacity, evaluation MUST default toward the interpretation that prevents privilege expansion (conservative) and MUST fail closed for claims that require precise role resolution.

## B00.CH02.P03.S04 Consolidation and look-through object relations (graph)

OBA treats the economic system as a **graph** of objects and relations. Consolidation and look-through are graph evaluation problems: assets, flows, and powers propagate through chains of entities, contracts, and control rights. “Substance over form” is operationalized as graph traversal under canonical rules.

Canonical object graph (minimum):

- Nodes: Actors, Entities, Assets/Positions, Contracts, Flows, Commons objects, ΔM Channels.
- Edges (relations): ownership, governance/control, beneficiary rights, contractual claims, blocking rights, agency/stewardship mandates, platform gating relations, financing/collateral relations.

Look-through principle.

- If an Actor has sufficient control/benefit/block over an Entity or structure, the relevant stocks/flows/rights of that structure are attributable (consolidated) to the Actor for OBA-critical evaluation.
- Look-through applies to: holdings, trusts/foundations, funds, nominees/custody, SPVs, layered platforms, and shadow structures where function is equivalent.

Consolidation domains.

- **Wealth domain (W-base)**: consolidate assets, claims, equivalents, and control premia as specified by W rules.
- **Income/flow domain (I-base)**: consolidate flows and deferred equivalents as specified by I rules.
- **ΔM domain**: consolidate ultimate beneficiaries behind intermediaries and program structures.
- **Commons domain**: consolidate governance control, procurement influence, and vendor lock-in routes relevant to capture risk.

Cycles and shared control.

- The graph may contain cycles (cross-holdings, circular contracts). Implementations MUST define a deterministic resolution method (e.g., cycle breaking rules, fixed-point evaluation, or conservative over-approximation).
- Shared control arrangements require explicit handling (acting-in-concert, joint ventures, syndicates). Where attribution is unclear, conservative consolidation applies.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations that evaluate caps, P/E/C chain attribution, ΔM beneficiaries, or commons capture MUST represent relevant relations as an auditable graph (explicit nodes/edges), even if stored in non-graph data structures.
- Look-through MUST be applied where role attributes indicate ultimate control/benefit/block; legal form alone MUST NOT block consolidation.
- Where the graph is incomplete, unobservable, or contains opaque substructures, evaluation MUST conservatively over-approximate consolidation and MUST fail closed for any conformance claim that depends on precise under-approximation.
