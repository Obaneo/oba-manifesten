# B00.CH02.P07 Cap objects: W-MAX and I-MAX (limits and bases)

This paragraph defines the **cap objects** that implement bounded accumulation in OBA: **W-MAX** (cap on wealth/claims/rights as stock) and **I-MAX** (cap on income/flows as flow). Caps are not “tax policy” in this kernel sense; they are **order invariants** intended to constrain the accumulation of structural power and claim stacks that drive extraction (E), inflate burdens (L), and degrade remainder-space (R/B).

Caps are evaluated **under consolidation** (UBC look-through) and **under anti-omzeiling** (substance over form). The cap objects therefore require (i) a canonical definition of the **base** (what counts), (ii) canonical treatment of equivalents and substitutes, and (iii) canonical valuation and timing conventions sufficient to prevent window dressing and deferral arbitrage.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any implementation claiming W-MAX and/or I-MAX MUST define and publish the cap **bases**, valuation conventions, and enforcement mechanics in a way that is auditable and reproducible.
- Caps MUST be applied under UBC consolidation (control/benefit/block look-through) and MUST include economic equivalents; otherwise the cap is non-conform (cap-washing).
- Where classification or valuation is uncertain, the system MUST fail closed: count the component into the cap base until it is canonically excluded by evidence.

## B00.CH02.P07.S01 W-base: wealth/claims/rights as stock (incl. equivalents)

**W-base** is the canonical measurement base for **W(t)**: the consolidated stock of **net economic claim position** on assets and future flows, including control-relevant rights that function as wealth equivalents.

Canonical scope of W-base (non-exhaustive categories):

1) **Financial assets**: cash and deposits, bonds/credit instruments, equities, fund interests, derivatives with net positive economic exposure.
2) **Real assets**: real estate, land rights, productive assets held as stores of value, commodity holdings where economically significant.
3) **Intangibles and rights**: IP positions, concessions, exclusive licenses, transferable quotas, spectrum rights, platform gatekeeping rights where they are alienable or monetizable.
4) **Economic equivalents** (anti-omzeiling): options, forwards, swaps/total return exposures, synthetic positions, structured notes, and any arrangement that replicates the economic exposure of ownership.
5) **Control premium / blocking rights attribute**: where control or block rights have independent economic value (e.g., ability to steer distributions, block sales, enforce lock-in), they MUST be treated as cap-relevant attributes for consolidation and valuation purposes.

Netting and liabilities:

- W is generally **net** of recognized passiva, but liabilities that are primarily used to manufacture cap avoidance (e.g., circular leverage, intra-group “debt” without economic substance) MUST be requalified under anti-omzeiling and MUST NOT reduce W-base.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- W-base MUST include indirect holdings under look-through (vehicles, trusts, nominees) according to UBC.
- Economic equivalents MUST be included using functional equivalence; excluding them makes W-MAX non-conform.
- Any carve-outs (e.g., primary residence, work tools) are parameters and MUST be explicit, bounded, and versioned; silent carve-outs are forbidden.

## B00.CH02.P07.S02 I-base: income/flows as flow (incl. deferral and substitutes)

**I-base** is the canonical measurement base for **I(t)**: the consolidated set of **economic inflows** accruing to an actor/household/UBC unit over a defined period, regardless of legal label, payout channel, or timing tricks.

Canonical scope of I-base (non-exhaustive categories):

1) **Labour income**: wages, salaries, bonuses, overtime, commissions, severance, deferred compensation (to earn-period), equity compensation at fair value (vesting/earn attribution per chosen convention).
2) **Capital income**: dividends, interest, rents, royalties, distributions, realized capital gains as defined by the canonical realization regime; where mark-to-market is adopted, unrealized gains may be included by rule.
3) **Entrepreneurial/managerial income**: profit shares, carried interest (as canonically classified), management fees, consulting/service fees that function as income extraction.
4) **Quasi-income and substitutes**: perks, expense accounts, in-kind benefits, below-market loans, forgiveness, and any non-cash advantage that substitutes for pay.
5) **Deferral and timing arbitrage**: arrangements designed to shift I into W or delay recognition MUST be re-attributed to the earn-period by default (look-back), unless canonically exempted.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- I-base MUST be consolidated across entities and contracts controlled/benefited by the same UBC unit (substance over form).
- Deferral, in-kind, and quasi-income components MUST be included; excluding them constitutes I-MAX-washing.
- Where the realization regime is ambiguous, the implementation MUST declare one canonically (cash/accrual/mark-to-market options as defined) and apply consistently.

## B00.CH02.P07.S03 Cap functions: limit, skimming/reallocation, exceptions (canonical)

Caps are represented as **cap functions** applied to the relevant base:

- **W-MAX** applies to W-base (stock).
- **I-MAX** applies to I-base (flow).

Canonical cap function types (implementation must choose and declare):

1) **Hard limit (absolute cap)**  
   Values above the threshold are not permitted to remain under private control in the same form; a remedial action is mandatory (conversion/transfer/lockbox).

2) **Soft cap with skimming (threshold + reallocation)**  
   Values above the threshold are permitted only subject to an automatic **skimming/reallocation rule** that redirects the excess (in whole or part) to a permitted destination, typically C under traceability constraints.

3) **Hybrid regimes**  
   A hard limit for certain base components (e.g., control-premium rights) plus a skimming regime for others, if explicitly specified and consistent with invariants.

Remedies (canonical menu; implementation selects permitted subset):

- **Afroming / skimming**: automatic extraction of the excess above the cap threshold.
- **Conversion**: mandatory conversion of excess claims into non-control-bearing forms or time-locked instruments consistent with the cap’s purpose.
- **Lockbox**: excess is sequestered pending allocation, audit, or scheduled conversion.
- **Reallocation to commons (C)**: only allowed where traceability, governance constraints, and non-capture rules are satisfied.

Exceptions and carve-outs:

- Exceptions are **parameters**, not reinterpretations. They MUST be narrowly bounded, explicitly declared, versioned, and justified under the canonical change discipline. Ad-hoc discretionary exceptions are forbidden.

Fail-closed enforcement:

- If base membership or valuation is disputed and cannot be resolved within the evidence window, enforcement MUST proceed conservatively: treat as included and apply the cap pending resolution.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- The cap function MUST be stated as a formal rule (thresholds, triggers, timing, remedies, and evidence windows).
- Any reallocation claimed as C MUST satisfy the traceability and anti-capture constraints; otherwise it is not C.
- Discretionary, case-by-case deals that change the cap’s practical effect are non-conform unless codified as parameters via canonical procedure.

## B00.CH02.P07.S04 Measurement and valuation conventions (market, book, model; conservatism)

Caps require valuation and measurement rules that are robust against manipulation, illiquidity, and window dressing. This section sets the canonical hierarchy and conservatism principle.

Valuation hierarchy (canonical):

1) **Market**: observable market prices in sufficiently liquid markets, adjusted for control premiums/discounts where canonically specified.
2) **Book**: audited book values where market values are unavailable, subject to conservatism adjustments.
3) **Model**: standardized valuation models with disclosed assumptions, inputs, and uncertainty bounds, audited for reproducibility.

Illiquidity and uncertainty:

- Illiquid assets and bespoke rights MUST apply **haircuts** or conservative bounds, with independent attestation where required.
- Model-based valuations MUST publish an assumptions register and sensitivity ranges sufficient for third-party replication.

Timing and window-dressing controls:

- Implementations MUST declare whether valuation uses point-in-time snapshots, period averages, or smoothing rules.
- Where snapshot gaming is plausible, smoothing/averaging and event-driven revaluations SHOULD be used, and large transactions SHOULD trigger immediate reassessment.

Unit of account and indexing:

- The unit (currency/real terms) and indexing rules (inflation adjustments, deflators) MUST be specified and consistent across W and I bases.

Conservatism rule (binding):

- When multiple reasonable valuations exist, the valuation that is **least favourable to privilege** MUST be used for cap enforcement unless a canonically permitted alternative is proven by auditable evidence.
- Black-box valuations, unverifiable models, or missing inputs MUST result in non-conformance for cap-grade evaluation and MUST be treated as included at conservative bounds.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- The valuation method used for each base component MUST be disclosed (market/book/model) with sufficient metadata for replication.
- If model valuation is used, the model and inputs MUST be auditable; proprietary opacity is not sufficient for conformance.
- Anti-window-dressing measures (smoothing rules, event triggers, audit trails) MUST be defined and applied consistently.
