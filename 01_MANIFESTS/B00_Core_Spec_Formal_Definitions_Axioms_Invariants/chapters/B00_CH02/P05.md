# B00.CH02.P05 Core variables B,T,N,L,R (remainder-space algebra)

This paragraph defines the **canonical remainder-space algebra** used throughout OBA: **B, T, N, L, R**. These variables provide a minimal, testable decomposition of receipts and claims that supports: (i) distributional evaluation (cohort profiles, tails, under-α shares), (ii) diagnosis of extraction pressure (E→L), and (iii) policy/evaluation linkage (caps, commons, ΔM channel discipline).

The algebra is “remainder-space” because it is constructed to make residual degrees of freedom explicit: once B and T are fixed, N is determined; once N and L are fixed, R is determined. This structure prevents “washing” via selective reporting (e.g., inflating B, hiding L, or redefining T).

Scope of this paragraph:

- Defines B, T, N, L, R as canonical quantities with required measurement conventions.
- Defines admissible classification boundaries for T and L.
- Defines identities and the required distributional presentation of R/B.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any OBA claim that references “net”, “burden”, “buffer”, “affordability”, or “remainder” MUST map to B/T/N/L/R as defined here (or explicitly declare out-of-scope).
- Implementations MUST publish B, T, L with sufficient granularity to reproduce N and R and to reconcile to observed flows (micro→macro).
- Any ambiguity that could expand discretion (e.g., shifting L into “optional”) MUST be resolved conservatively, and dependent claims MUST fail closed when evidence is insufficient.

## B00.CH02.P05.S01 B (gross income/receipts): definition and measurement framework

**B** is **gross receipts** attributable to an actor/entity over a defined period *t*, before subtracting transfers-out classified as **T**. “Receipts” is used intentionally: B is not limited to wages; it covers any inflow that increases the actor/entity’s command over resources in the period.

Canonical definition.

- **Bᵢ(t)** = sum of all inflows to unit *i* during period *t* that are attributable as receipts under the attribution rules (actor/entity, consolidation, look-through where applicable).

Included (non-exhaustive, canonical categories):

- **Labour receipts**: wages, salaries, bonuses, employment-related cashflows.
- **Business receipts**: operating receipts attributable to the unit (with consolidation rules for groups).
- **Capital receipts**: dividends, interest, rents, royalties, realized gains where applicable under the chosen realization convention.
- **Transfers-in**: benefits, grants, subsidies, gifts, remittances (as inflows; classification is about flow direction, not moral status).
- **In-kind/quasi-cash**: only if valued under a canonical rule (fair value or specified proxy).

Measurement framework (minimum requirements):

- B MUST be defined with:
  - **time index** *t* (and resolution: month/year/etc.),
  - **unit** (currency/unit + deflator if real terms are used),
  - **scope boundary** (actor/entity/consolidation),
  - **valuation convention** (cash vs accrual; in-kind valuation rules; gain realization rules).
- B MUST be reconcilable to Flow objects (payer→payee), subject to consolidation and gross/net rules.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations MUST declare the B measurement convention (cash/accrual; valuation; realization) and apply it consistently across cohorts and time.
- B MUST NOT be inflated by internal flows within a consolidation boundary; internal receipts are excluded from external B aggregates unless explicitly stated and separately reported.
- Where receipts are bundled or opaque, B MUST be recorded conservatively with uncertainty bands if needed; claims that require precise B MUST fail closed if precision is not achievable.

## B00.CH02.P05.S02 T (transfer/tax/contribution): definition and classification

**T** is **transfer-out**: receipts that are **mandatorily** paid out under public/legal/regulatory order or equivalent compulsory regimes that function as public extraction for funding/redistribution/insurance.

Canonical definition.

- **Tᵢ(t)** = sum of outflows from unit *i* during period *t* that meet the **mandatory/compulsory criterion**.

Included (canonical classes):

- **Taxes**: income taxes, payroll taxes, consumption taxes where attributable to the unit, property taxes where applicable.
- **Social contributions**: mandatory social insurance premiums, compulsory pension contributions under statutory regimes.
- **Quasi-taxes / regulated mandatory contributions**: levies and mandatory fees imposed by law or regulatory compulsion (including compulsory licensing charges where unavoidable).
- **Court-ordered mandatory payments** (where legally unavoidable and not a private contract choice), if and only if canonically classified as order-imposed rather than contract-imposed.

Excluded (binding exclusions):

- **Voluntary consumption** and discretionary spending.
- **Contractually assumed payments** that are plausibly avoidable by market exit (unless they meet the lock-in/essentials criterion that makes them L).
- **Private tolls/fees** charged under market power; those are not T (they are candidates for E and materialize as L where essential/locked-in).

Classification discipline.

- The T boundary is intentionally strict to prevent mislabeling private extraction as “public” and to preserve comparability across jurisdictions.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- A flow MUST NOT be classified as T unless the mandatory/compulsory criterion is met and auditable.
- Where classification is ambiguous, the implementation MUST disclose the ambiguity and MUST avoid using the ambiguous component to support an OBA-critical claim (fail closed for dependent claims).
- T classification rules MUST be applied consistently across cohorts; selective reclassification to improve R/B is non-conform.

## B00.CH02.P05.S03 L (burdens/essentials/claim layers): definition and decomposition

**L** is **burdens**: outflows that are **not plausibly avoidable** without loss of baseline functioning or without incurring unacceptable exit friction. L operationalizes the notion of “essentials” and “claim layers” that compress autonomy.

Canonical definition.

- **Lᵢ(t)** = sum of outflows during period *t* that satisfy either:
  1) **Essentials criterion**: required to maintain baseline life/operational functioning (parameterized canonical basket), or
  2) **Lock-in criterion**: not plausibly avoidable due to contractual klem/switching costs/exit friction, or
  3) **Claim-layer criterion**: rent/fees/interest embedded within essentials that must be paid to maintain baseline access.

Essentials (canonical basket; parameterized):

- Housing/shelter, basic energy/utilities, basic water, basic food, basic mobility, basic healthcare, baseline connectivity, and other items explicitly enumerated by the applicable definitional regime.
- The basket may be parameterized by jurisdiction/cohort only within allowed parameter rules; it MUST NOT be broadened to include luxury or status consumption.

Decomposition requirement.

- L MUST be decomposable at least into:
  - **functional-cost component** (P-aligned cost of baseline provision), and
  - **claim-layer component** (E-aligned toll/lock-in/fee-stack within essentials), where measurable.
- This decomposition is essential for diagnosing E pressure and for evaluating whether commons/caps/ΔM channels are reducing L structurally.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations MUST publish the L basket definition, parameters, and the method for regional price/index adjustments.
- Contractual klem that makes an otherwise avoidable cost non-avoidable MUST be captured in L (with evidence via contract metadata where applicable).
- Luxury or elective upgrades MUST NOT be included in L; misclassification is non-conform.
- Where L decomposition (functional vs claim-layer) is not measurable, the implementation MUST (i) declare uncertainty and (ii) avoid making strong causal claims about E reduction without additional evidence.

## B00.CH02.P05.S04 Identities: N = B − T; R = N − L; distribution profiles

Canonical identities.

- **Nᵢ(t) = Bᵢ(t) − Tᵢ(t)** (net after transfers-out).
- **Rᵢ(t) = Nᵢ(t) − Lᵢ(t)** (remainder/buffer after unavoidable burdens).

These identities are definitional within OBA: if B, T, L are defined and measured, N and R follow mechanically. No alternative “net” or “remainder” definitions are permitted for OBA-critical claims unless explicitly declared as non-canonical and out-of-scope.

Distribution profiles.

Because OBA is order-oriented and distribution-sensitive, B/T/N/L/R MUST be evaluated as **profiles**, not only as macro averages. At minimum, implementations MUST support cohort profiling (e.g., income deciles, household type, region, sector) and provide tail information relevant to under-α assessment.

Minimum distributional outputs (baseline requirement):

- For each declared cohort scheme:
  - median **R/B** (or equivalent canonical ratio),
  - p10/p25 (or other specified lower-tail statistics),
  - share of units with **R < 0**,
  - share of units below the applicable **α** threshold (where α is defined),
  - declared uncertainty bands for key outputs where measurement is imperfect.

Reconciliation constraint.

- Aggregated B/T/L MUST reconcile to micro flows and to macro accounts within declared error bounds. If reconciliation fails materially, dependent OBA claims MUST be suspended (fail closed) until corrected.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations MUST compute N and R using the canonical identities and MUST NOT introduce alternative “net” constructs as substitutes.
- Any OBA claim that references “buffer”, “affordability”, or “autonomy” MUST report R (and typically R/B) distributionally, not only as averages.
- If B/T/L are not auditable at the level needed to reproduce N and R (or to reconcile micro→macro), any conformance claim relying on N/R MUST fail closed.
