# B00.CH02.P06 P/E/C classification (production, extraction, commons)

This paragraph introduces **P/E/C** as the canonical classification of economically relevant flows and embedded components: **Production (P)**, **Extraction (E)**, and **Commons contribution (C)**. The purpose is to force an auditable separation between (i) functional provision and maintenance, (ii) toll/lock-in/claim layers above functional cost, and (iii) allocations that build or maintain commons capacity and access.

P/E/C is **functional**, not rhetorical: classification is determined by *what the flow does in the system* (effects on access, burdens, lock-in, capacity), not by how it is labelled in contracts or marketing. Where classification cannot be supported by evidence and reproducible rules, evaluation MUST fail closed and the component MUST be treated conservatively under the applicable module (typically toward E for privileges).

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Every OBA-relevant Flow object MUST be classifiable into P/E/C at least at the **flow level** (and decomposable where mixed).
- Labels, narratives, or sector conventions MUST NOT override the functional tests defined here and in the dedicated definitional set for P/E/C.
- Where the same payment contains both functional and toll components, it MUST be split (P+E, and sometimes C) using auditable rules.

## B00.CH02.P06.S01 P: production- and maintenance-linked value creation (functional output)

**P** is the component of a flow that pays for **functional output** and **maintenance** required to deliver that output, within bounded, normal margins under contestable conditions. P is what would remain if toll power, lock-in rents, and claim stacks were removed while still preserving the delivered function and its lifecycle continuity.

Canonical definition.

A flow component is **P** if it satisfies all of the following:

- **Function criterion**: it corresponds to a deliverable service/product function that can be specified independently of market power (what is provided).
- **Cost criterion**: it is consistent with **functional costs** plus a **bounded margin** that is justifiable by risk, skill, and normal return under contestability.
- **Maintenance criterion**: where applicable, it funds the upkeep/repair/replacement required to prevent degradation of the delivered function over time.

Notes:

- “Innovation” is P only insofar as it is required to sustain function, safety, reliability, or interoperability as environments change (maintenance as improvement when necessary).
- P can exist in monopolistic contexts, but classification MUST use counterfactual functional-cost reasoning and benchmarking to strip the toll component into E.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- P classification MUST be supported by an auditable decomposition method (costing/benchmarks/engineering estimates) sufficient to separate P from E.
- Bounded margin bands MUST be declared (sector/jurisdiction parameters if permitted) and MUST be applied consistently.
- Where evidence is insufficient to establish functional cost + bounded margin, the contested portion MUST NOT be asserted as P (fail closed).

## B00.CH02.P06.S02 E: toll/lock-in/claim above functional cost (parasitic component)

**E** is the component of a flow that exists because of **power**, not because of function: tolls, lock-in rents, and claim layers that raise price or burden above what is required for functional provision and maintenance.

Canonical definition.

A flow component is **E** if it satisfies any of the following:

- **Toll criterion**: price/fee exceeds functional cost + bounded margin due to market power, exclusivity, or control over a chokepoint.
- **Lock-in criterion**: the payer cannot plausibly avoid the payment without disproportionate exit costs, switching costs, contractual penalties, or loss of baseline access.
- **Claim-stack criterion**: the flow is a rent/fee/interest layer that compounds on top of the same underlying function (fee stacks, monopoly rents, financialized layers).

E is “parasitic” in the formal sense: it extracts remainder-space (R) and/or inflates burdens (L) without proportionate contribution to function or maintenance.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Evidence of power/lock-in/claim-stacks MUST be captured as metadata or measurable proxies where available (market structure, contractual terms, switching costs, fee layers).
- If a component’s classification is contested and cannot be evidenced, the component MUST be treated as E for purposes of privilege/cap/channel eligibility (conservative default).
- Re-labelling E as “service”, “platform”, “innovation”, “brand”, or “risk management” without auditable decomposition is non-conform.

## B00.CH02.P06.S03 C: commons contribution (build/maintenance/access) and allocation attributes

**C** is a flow component that is **allocated to commons capacity and access**: funding or provisioning that increases or maintains **ΔG** (commons capacity/quality/continuity) and/or expands baseline access under non-discriminatory boundary rules.

Canonical definition.

A flow component is **C** if it satisfies all of the following:

- **Destination criterion**: the funds/resources are allocated to a commons-designated domain (as defined by the commons object model), not merely to a private asset pool.
- **Order-property criterion**: the allocation produces or maintains **access**, **maintenance duty**, and **contestability** (commons order-properties), not just “public branding.”
- **Traceability criterion**: the allocation is traceable via an auditable chain from source → channel → project/service → measurable outputs (ΔG metrics and/or access metrics).

Allocation attributes (required metadata class):

- **purpose**: build (capex), maintenance (opex), access expansion, resilience.
- **governance**: accountable steward/operator/auditor roles and contestability provisions.
- **non-capture constraints**: prohibited return flows to the original payer/controller (unless explicitly allowed as bounded compensation for P work).

Conformance requirements (binding):

- A flow MUST NOT be classified as C unless it is traceable to commons outputs and governance constraints that prevent capture.
- “Public” ownership alone does not imply C; C requires the commons order-properties (access/maintenance/contestability).
- When cap skimming or ΔM programs claim C classification, they MUST satisfy the traceability and non-capture constraints; otherwise they are not C.

## B00.CH02.P06.S04 Attribution at flow level and along chains (P/E/C splitting)

P/E/C attribution must work at two levels:

1) **Flow-level attribution**: classify each observed payment (Flow object) or its components.
2) **Chain attribution**: propagate and decompose along **supply chains** and **platform/finance stacks** to detect hidden toll layers and double extraction.

Flow-level splitting rule (canonical):

- For any flow amount **A**, assign a decomposition:
  - **A = P + E + C**, with each component ≥ 0.
- The decomposition MUST be justified by a reproducible method:
  - P derived from functional-cost + bounded margin,
  - E as residual above P (and above any explicitly classified C),
  - C only when destination/governance/traceability criteria hold.

Chain attribution requirements:

- Where a payer’s price includes upstream components (e.g., distribution, platform fees, financing), the implementation MUST attempt a **look-through decomposition** to prevent misclassifying downstream E as upstream P.
- **Fee-stack detection** is mandatory where multiple intermediaries charge for the same chokepoint function (payments, access, listing, compliance, credit).
- Internal flows within a consolidated group must be handled under the double-counting rules: internal recharges must not manufacture apparent P or conceal E.

Conservative defaults (binding):

- If chain decomposition is not possible due to opacity, the opaque component MUST be classified conservatively (toward E for privileges) and MUST be flagged as non-auditable for conformance-grade reporting.
- OBA-critical claims about E reduction or C build MUST cite the decomposition method and provide audit trails; otherwise claims are non-conform.

Outputs (minimum):

- P/E/C totals MUST be reportable by: (i) unit, (ii) contract type, (iii) essential domain vs non-essential, and (iv) cohort (where applicable), with uncertainty bands where decomposition relies on estimates.
