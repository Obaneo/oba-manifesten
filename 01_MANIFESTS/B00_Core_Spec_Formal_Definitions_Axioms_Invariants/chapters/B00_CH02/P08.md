# B00.CH02.P08 ΔM as allocation interface (monetary space)

ΔM is the canonical interface by which “monetary space” is represented and governed inside OBA. The Core Spec does not treat ΔM as a rhetorical lever (“more/less money”) but as a **traceable allocation capacity** whose legitimacy depends on **channel discipline**: where newly created or mobilized monetary capacity goes, under what conditions, with what leakage constraints, and with what measurable effects on order variables—especially **commons capacity (ΔG)** and **remainder-space (R/B)**.

ΔM must therefore be modeled as an object with explicit provenance and routing, not as a scalar that can be assumed to “stimulate” without specifying distributional destination and structural consequences. In OBA terms, ΔM is conforming only when it demonstrably supports **P and C** pathways (production/maintenance and commons build/maintenance) and avoids **E-consolidation** pathways (asset/claim support, rentier protection, lock-in reinforcement).

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any use of ΔM within an OBA claim MUST specify the ΔM definition, accounting boundary, and time index, and MUST be traceable to channel-level objects.
- ΔM allocations MUST be evaluated under prohibited-channel rules; “stabilization” does not exempt a channel from being classified as asset/claim support if the functional effect matches.
- Where destination, conditions, or outcomes are not auditable, ΔM classification MUST fail closed (non-conform) until specified.

## B00.CH02.P08.S01 Definition of ΔM: change in monetary capacity/space per t

**ΔM(t)** denotes the change in **monetary capacity** available to the economic system over a defined time period **t** (canonical resolutions defined elsewhere). “Monetary capacity” is understood functionally: the incremental ability to settle obligations and fund activity, whether created through credit expansion, balance sheet operations, guarantees, or fiscal/monetary instruments.

Canonical boundary (must be declared by implementation):

- **Institutional boundary**: which issuers/intermediaries are in scope (central bank, treasury, public credit channels, regulated commercial banks, designated payment rails, etc.).
- **Instrument boundary**: which instrument classes count as ΔM (base money, bank credit, guarantees, liquidity facilities, subsidized funding lines, etc.).
- **Recognition boundary**: cash vs accrual recognition, and how contingent support (e.g., guarantees) is measured as capacity.

Canonical interpretation:

- ΔM is **not** “money supply” in a single aggregate by default; it is a **change** in capacity that MUST be decomposable by source and channel.
- ΔM can be positive or negative; contraction events are part of the same formalism.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations MUST publish the chosen ΔM boundary and measurement rule, including which aggregates/instruments are included and excluded.
- ΔM MUST carry a time index t and be reconcilable (micro→macro) with the relevant accounting/monetary reporting domain.
- Contingent instruments (guarantees, backstops) MUST be treated under a declared canonical capacity metric (e.g., notional, expected exposure, stress exposure), and MUST not be omitted if they function as monetary capacity in practice.

## B00.CH02.P08.S02 Channel objects: {source, channel, destination, conditions, audit log}

ΔM is operationalized through **channel objects**. A channel object is the canonical unit of allocation for ΔM and is the minimum unit for audit, classification, and outcome attribution.

Canonical channel object schema (minimum fields):

- **source**: originating authority/issuer (e.g., CB facility, treasury program, public bank line, regulated bank credit under mandate).
- **channel**: named instrument/program/rail (facility/program identifier; instrument type).
- **destination**: ultimate beneficiary class and (where required) UBC-resolved beneficiaries; includes intermediaries and final recipients.
- **conditions**: contractual and operational constraints (pricing, collateral rules, eligibility, term, covenants, use-of-proceeds constraints, maintenance/ΔG obligations, caps interaction).
- **audit_log**: immutable event record of allocations, amendments, rollovers, exceptions, and enforcement actions, including references to evidence inputs.

Channel classification is functional:

- The channel’s intended label is non-decisive; classification depends on effect and enforceable conditions.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any ΔM used in an OBA claim MUST be represented as one or more channel objects with the minimum schema above.
- Destination MUST resolve beyond opaque intermediaries: if funds pass through vehicles/SPVs/funds, the channel MUST carry a look-through mechanism and evidence sufficient to identify ultimate beneficiaries (UBC where relevant).
- Audit logs MUST be tamper-evident and reproducible; black-box routing is non-conform for OBA-critical ΔM claims.

## B00.CH02.P08.S03 Prohibited channels and anti-leakage definitions (asset support, claim stacks)

OBA defines a set of **prohibited ΔM channels**: channels whose functional effect is to support asset prices, preserve rentier cashflows, or consolidate claim stacks—i.e., channels that materially reinforce **E** rather than reduce it.

Canonical prohibited-channel classes (functional definitions):

1) **Asset support (prohibited)**  
   Any ΔM channel whose primary functional effect is to support, elevate, or backstop asset prices (especially collateral-bearing assets) or to sustain leverage cycles by protecting valuations rather than maintaining real capacity. Examples include broad-based asset purchase programs or liquidity injections that predictably transmit into asset inflation without enforceable commons/maintenance constraints.

2) **Claim support / claim-stack preservation (prohibited)**  
   Any ΔM channel whose primary functional effect is to maintain or stabilize rentier cashflows, fee stacks, monopoly rents, or other extractive structures (E), including “stabilization” programs that bail out claimholders without structural remedies (caps enforcement, fee-stack unwinding, lock-in reduction).

3) **Opaque intermediation (prohibited unless fully traceable)**  
   Any ΔM routed through black-box funds, opaque SPVs, off-balance conduits, or structures that prevent beneficiary identification, condition enforcement, or outcome attribution.

4) **Surveillance-rent dependence (prohibited)**  
   Any ΔM program whose sustainability or repayment depends on data extraction/behavioral control as a revenue model (surveillance tolling), thereby creating an extractive control loop.

Anti-leakage principle:

- Even if a channel is nominally allowed (e.g., “productive lending”), it is non-conform if it leaks materially into prohibited classes (asset support, claim support), unless leakage is bounded by enforceable constraints and measured.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Implementations MUST define prohibited-channel detection rules and leakage metrics for each channel class.
- Channels that materially reinforce asset inflation or claim stacks MUST be classified as prohibited, regardless of label.
- Where leakage cannot be measured or controlled, the channel MUST be treated as prohibited for OBA conformance purposes (fail closed).

## B00.CH02.P08.S04 Traceability requirement: ability to trace ΔM→C/maintenance/R/B effects

Traceability is the binding requirement that connects ΔM allocation to OBA’s order targets. A ΔM claim is conforming only if an independent evaluator can trace, with declared uncertainty, the pathway:

**ΔM → channel object(s) → destination(s) → enforced conditions → realized outputs → effects on ΔG / L / R/B**

Canonical traceability components:

- **Lineage**: end-to-end record from authorization decision to disbursement to compliance checks to outcome measurement.
- **Outcome mapping**: explicit mapping from channel conditions to expected outputs (commons build, maintenance, burden reduction) and the metrics used (ΔG measures, essentials cost indices, R/B distribution profiles).
- **Counterfactual discipline**: where causal claims are made, the implementation MUST specify the evaluation method (pre/post with controls, quasi-experimental, etc.) and report uncertainty bands.

Minimum acceptable trace targets (must be specified as applicable):

- **ΔM→C**: allocation that results in verifiable commons build/maintenance under governance constraints (ΔG metrics, access rules, maintenance-first).
- **ΔM→maintenance**: allocation that prevents degradation of essential capacity/quality (ΔG stabilization) and avoids vendor lock-in/capture.
- **ΔM→R/B**: allocation that measurably improves or stabilizes R/B profiles (especially tail risk: under-α share), not merely macro averages.

Conformance requirements (binding):

- Any OBA claim involving ΔM MUST publish the channel objects and the traceability chain sufficient for independent audit, subject to privacy-minimalism.
- Claims of “supporting commons” or “improving R/B” MUST be backed by measurable outputs and declared uncertainty; narrative claims are non-conform.
- If traceability from ΔM to outcomes is missing, non-reproducible, or blocked by opacity, the ΔM component of the claim MUST be evaluated as non-conform (fail closed) until corrected.
