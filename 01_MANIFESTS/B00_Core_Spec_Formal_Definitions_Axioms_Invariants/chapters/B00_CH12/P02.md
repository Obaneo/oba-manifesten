# B00.CH12.P02 — Observability: what must be measurable (minimum observables)

This paragraph defines the **minimum observable set** required for OBA measurement and enforcement. An implementation cannot claim
compatibility if the core objects are not observable at an auditable resolution. Observability is defined as: (i) the variable exists
as a measurable quantity, (ii) the method to obtain it is specified, and (iii) provenance and lineage can be traced.

---

## B00.CH12.P02.S01 — Core observables: flows (P/E/C), B,T,L,R, cap bases, channel destinations, commons ΔG

The following objects are the **minimum core observables**. They are required whenever they fall within claim scope.

### Minimum observable set (required)
- **Flows (P/E/C)**:
  - observable flows sufficient to classify and decompose into P/E/C components per canonical procedure.
  - allocation keys and coverage boundaries for multi-activity entities.
- **R/B components**:
  - **B** (gross inflow), **T** (tax/mandatory transfers), **L** (necessary load/essentials cost burden), **R** (residual).
  - time resolution and cohort assignment rules.
- **Cap bases (W/I)**:
  - measurable bases used for cap evaluation (wealth base and income base as defined), including coverage and valuation rules.
- **ΔM channels**:
  - origins and **destinations** of monetary channels relevant to OBA evaluation (including prohibited channels where applicable),
  - routing identifiers sufficient to perform leakage tests.
- **Commons status / ΔG**:
  - a measurable commons state variable set, including **ΔG** (change in commons capacity/quality/coverage as defined),
  - access and maintenance observables where required.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If any required core observable cannot be measured with declared provenance and resolution, the affected claim scope MUST be treated
  as **non-conform** until observability is restored.
- Substitution of core observables with proxy composites is only permitted if the proxy is canonically defined and its error bounds
  are published (otherwise forbidden).

---

## B00.CH12.P02.S02 — Verifiability: inputs, transformations and outputs must be traceable

Observability without verifiability produces unverifiable dashboards. Verifiability requires end-to-end traceability.

### Traceability requirements (minimum)
- **Inputs**: source identifiers, collection method, sampling frame (if sampled), timestamps, units, and access route.
- **Transformations**: declared transformations with versioned code/specification, parameter settings, and change logs.
- **Outputs**: computed metrics and intermediate outputs with lineage links back to inputs and transformations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- For every published metric used in compatibility claims, the lineage MUST be traceable: **input → transformation → output**.
- Any broken lineage (missing steps, undocumented transformations, unverifiable inputs) invalidates the metric for compatibility and
  triggers **non-conform** treatment for dependent claims (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH12.P02.S03 — Distribution observables: cohort profiles (R/B tail, under-α share) as minimum

OBA claims are distribution-sensitive. Aggregate averages are insufficient for compatibility evaluation.

### Minimum distribution observables (required)
- **Cohort profiles** for R/B (defined cohorts and time windows).
- **Tail observables** for R/B:
  - lower-tail profile (minimum quantiles / tail bands as defined),
  - **under-α share** (share of population/cohort under the defined threshold parameter α),
  - persistence indicators where applicable (time spent under threshold).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any claim about improving R/B, reducing burden, or improving access MUST publish cohort-level distribution observables, not only means.
- Absence of required distribution observables triggers downgrade to **non-conform** for claims that depend on distribution effects.

---

## B00.CH12.P02.S04 — Prohibited non-observability: “proprietary metrics” as replacement for core observables

Non-observability is prohibited where it substitutes away canonical objects. “Proprietary metrics” are allowed only as optional,
non-governing supplements, never as replacements for core observables.

### Prohibited patterns (non-exhaustive)
- Replacing P/E/C classification with an undisclosed “value score”.
- Replacing R/B components with a composite index without published decomposition and lineage.
- Replacing cap bases with private valuations or closed scoring without auditable rules.
- Replacing channel destinations with aggregated labels that prevent leakage tests.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any compatibility claim that relies on proprietary/closed metrics in place of required core observables MUST be treated as
  **non-conform** immediately.
- Closed metrics MAY be published as additional analytics only if:
  - they are clearly labeled non-governing,
  - they do not affect decisions tied to OBA status,
  - core observables remain published and auditable.
