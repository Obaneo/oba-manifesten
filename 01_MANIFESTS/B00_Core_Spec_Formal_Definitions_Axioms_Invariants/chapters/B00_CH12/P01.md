# B00.CH12.P01 — Purpose, scope and normative status of evidence and measurement standards

This paragraph establishes evidence and measurement standards as **binding order requirements** in OBA. OBA compatibility is a **formal
claim** that depends on traceable observables, registered methods, audit lineage, and reproducible outcomes. Evidence is not an
“implementation detail”: it is a condition of enforceability. Where evidence, lineage, or reproducibility is missing, OBA applies a
fail-closed rule and treats the affected claim scope as **non-conform** until corrected.

---

## B00.CH12.P01.S01 — Standards as order invariant: without evidence there is no compatibility claim

Evidence and measurement standards are an **order invariant**: without them, there is no meaningful way to test, audit, or enforce
OBA requirements.

### Core rule
- **No evidence → no claim**: any OBA status label requires an evidence bundle sufficient for independent verification.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any OBA compatibility claim MUST be accompanied by:
  - definition-set ID + version,
  - a declared set of observables,
  - a registered measurement method specification,
  - audit lineage sufficient to reproduce the reported outputs.
- If any element is missing, the claim MUST be treated as **non-conform** (fail-closed) for the affected scope.

---

## B00.CH12.P01.S02 — Purpose: preventing black-box governance, measurement-washing and semantic drift

The purpose of evidence standards is to prevent three structural degradation paths:

### Threat model
- **Black-box governance**: decisions driven by opaque methods, inaccessible data, or uninspectable pipelines.
- **Measurement-washing**: polished dashboards and KPIs presented as proof while underlying data lineage is missing or non-verifiable.
- **Semantic drift**: redefinitions introduced via measurement choices (changing what is counted) rather than via canonical governance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Critical decisions MUST NOT rely on undisclosed methods or unverifiable inputs.
- Any published metric used for compatibility MUST be traceable to canonical definitions; “metric substitution” without canonical
  mapping is forbidden.
- Presentation layers (reports, dashboards, public comms) MUST link to the underlying method registration and evidence bundle.

---

## B00.CH12.P01.S03 — Scope: P/E/C, R/B, W/I caps, ΔM channels, commons status, anti-avoidance

Evidence and measurement standards apply to every OBA object that can affect compatibility status or enforcement.

### Covered domains (minimum)
- **P/E/C**: classification observables, decompositions, and allocation rules.
- **R/B**: B, T, L, R observables; cohort/distribution profiles; under-threshold shares.
- **W/I caps**: cap bases, coverage, exemptions (if any), enforcement traces, and avoidance surfaces.
- **ΔM channels**: origins, destinations, routing logic, leakage tests, and prohibited channels.
- **Commons status**: commons inventories, access/maintenance observables, ΔG and related performance variables.
- **Anti-avoidance**: shift detection indicators, reclassification triggers, leak tests, and audit trails.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- For each covered domain used in a claim scope, the minimum observables and methods MUST be published and auditable.
- Any domain that cannot meet auditability requirements MUST be excluded from the claim scope or forces downgrade to non-conform.

---

## B00.CH12.P01.S04 — Relation to Kernel Spec, Codex, governance and dispute protocol

Evidence standards are anchored to canonical meaning and governed through versioned procedures. The Kernel defines terms and
requirements; the Codex operationalizes measurement and audit mechanics without redefining the Kernel. Disputes are resolved through
a defined contestation process that can trigger restatements, recalculation, and suspension of status.

### Source relation
- **Kernel Spec**: defines canonical objects and required properties (what must be measured and why).
- **Codex/Standards**: defines measurement schemas, method registration formats, and audit procedures (how to measure).
- **Governance**: approves changes to measurement methods and resolves conflicts via versioned updates.
- **Dispute protocol**: provides time-bounded contestation and correction procedures; unresolved disputes fail closed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Measurement methods MUST reference the applicable definition-set ID + version and MUST be versioned artifacts themselves.
- Where disputes or conflicts exist about measurement validity, the affected compatibility scope MUST be treated as **non-conform**
  until resolution and verified recalculation are completed.
