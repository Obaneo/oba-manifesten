# B00.CH12.P07 — Attribution and causality (claims about effects)

This paragraph defines the standards for **attribution** and **causal claims** in OBA. Claims about effects (e.g., “this reduces E”,
“this improves R/B”, “this channel increases commons capacity”) are not validated by correlation or narrative alignment. They require
counterfactual discipline, explicit mechanism specification, and controls for confounding and selection. Causal claims without
testable design and evidence are treated as **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH12.P07.S01 — Causal claims require counterfactual discipline (pre/post, control groups, IV where appropriate)

A causal claim asserts that an intervention produces a change that would not have occurred otherwise. OBA requires a declared
counterfactual identification strategy.

### Accepted identification designs (non-exhaustive)
- **Pre/post with robustness**: clearly defined baseline and follow-up windows with disclosed shocks and concurrent changes.
- **Control groups**: difference-in-differences or matched controls with declared matching rules and balance checks.
- **Instrumental variables (IV)**: where endogeneity is material and a credible instrument exists; instrument validity must be tested.
- **Regression discontinuity / thresholds**: when assignment follows a rule; manipulation checks are mandatory.
- **Randomized or quasi-random rollout**: where feasible; compliance and spillovers must be measured.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any causal claim MUST publish: identification design, data sources, sample definition, and predeclared estimands.
- If no credible counterfactual strategy is available, the claim MUST be downgraded to a non-causal descriptive statement or treated
  as non-conform if presented as causal.

---

## B00.CH12.P07.S02 — Mechanism claims: explicit causal chain (ΔM→C/essentials→L→R/B)

Mechanism claims must specify the causal pathway by which an intervention operates. OBA requires an explicit mechanism chain with
measurable intermediate variables.

### Mechanism chain requirement
- Declare the pathway in measurable steps (minimum chain form):
  - **ΔM → (commons / essentials allocation) → L → R/B**
- For each link, declare:
  - the observable variable(s),
  - the expected sign/direction,
  - the time lag window,
  - the measurement method and uncertainty band.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A mechanism claim MUST include intermediate observables sufficient to test whether the pathway operated as claimed.
- If intermediate links are unmeasured or untestable, the mechanism claim MUST be treated as unsupported and therefore non-conform
  if used to justify compatibility status or enforcement.

---

## B00.CH12.P07.S03 — Confounding and selection: detection and correction requirements

Causal inference fails when treatment is correlated with unobserved drivers, or when selection changes the observed sample. OBA
requires explicit confounding and selection handling.

### Minimum requirements
- **Confounder set**: declare plausible confounders; justify inclusion/exclusion; measure where possible.
- **Selection checks**: document sample construction, attrition, missingness, and treatment uptake; test for differential attrition.
- **Robustness**: alternative specifications, placebo tests, negative controls where applicable.
- **Spillovers**: assess displacement and general equilibrium effects when interventions shift behavior across sectors/channels.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any causal claim MUST publish confounding/selection diagnostics and corrective methods used.
- If confounding cannot be bounded or selection invalidates the design, the claim MUST be treated as **uncertain** and therefore
  non-conform for any status-justifying use (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH12.P07.S04 — Ban on post-hoc storytelling: narrative without testing is non-conform

Narrative explanations are permitted as commentary, but are forbidden as substitutes for causal testing in compatibility claims.

### Prohibited patterns (non-exhaustive)
- Ex-post narratives presented as proof (“it worked because…”) without identification design and tests.
- Selective case studies used as general causal evidence without sampling discipline.
- KPI improvements attributed to interventions without controlling for concurrent changes or regression-to-the-mean.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any claim framed as causal MUST be backed by the required counterfactual design and published tests.
- If evidence does not meet causality standards, communication MUST be corrected to descriptive language; continued causal framing
  without testing constitutes misrepresentation and triggers non-conform treatment.
