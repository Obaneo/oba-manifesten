# B00.CH12.P06 — Uncertainty: intervals, error margins and conservatism

This paragraph defines how uncertainty must be handled in OBA measurement and enforcement. OBA treats uncertainty as a first-class
audit object: estimates must be reported as **intervals**, assumptions must be registered, and sensitivity must be tested. Where
uncertainty affects privileges (status labels, cap compliance, channel permissibility), OBA applies a **conservatism rule**: default
choices must protect invariants and prevent laundering through uncertainty. Updates must follow a revision policy that preserves
semantic continuity and prevents silent breaks.

---

## B00.CH12.P06.S01 — Uncertainty reporting: interval estimates, sensitivity analysis, assumptions register

All compatibility-relevant measurements must include explicit uncertainty documentation.

### Minimum uncertainty reporting requirements
- **Interval estimates**: confidence/credible intervals or error bands for key observables and derived metrics.
- **Error margins**: explicit reporting of sampling error, measurement error, and reconciliation uncertainty where applicable.
- **Sensitivity analysis**: results under plausible alternative parameter choices, thresholds, deflators, and allocation keys.
- **Assumptions register**: a versioned list of assumptions that materially affect outputs (definitions, imputations, missingness handling).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any published metric used for compatibility MUST include an uncertainty statement (intervals + assumptions) in the evidence bundle.
- If uncertainty is not reported, the metric MUST be treated as invalid for compatibility, and dependent claims MUST be treated as
  **non-conform** until uncertainty reporting is restored.

---

## B00.CH12.P06.S02 — Conservatism rule: under uncertainty, choose against privileges (towards E/cap/forbidden channel)

Where uncertainty creates ambiguity about whether an entity qualifies for a privilege or passes a constraint, OBA defaults against
granting privilege.

### Conservatism rule (binding direction)
- **P/E/C ambiguity**: when classification is uncertain, default classification must not understate **E** exposure.
- **Cap ambiguity**: when cap bases or valuations are uncertain, default treatment must not create an unverified cap pass.
- **Channel ambiguity**: when channel destinations or routing are uncertain, default treatment must not treat the channel as permitted.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If uncertainty prevents a clear pass determination, the status MUST be treated as **non-conform** (or the scope must be narrowed)
  until evidence resolves the ambiguity.
- Any “benefit of the doubt” regime that grants privileges under uncertainty is forbidden unless explicitly canonically defined.

---

## B00.CH12.P06.S03 — Model uncertainty: structural uncertainty made explicit (no pseudo-precision)

When models are used (statistical models, imputations, forecasting, classification support), OBA requires explicit disclosure of
structural uncertainty and prohibits pseudo-precision.

### Structural uncertainty requirements (minimum)
- **Model class disclosure**: what model type is used and why; what is assumed and what is not modeled.
- **Failure modes**: known breakdown regimes (distribution shift, missingness patterns, adversarial behavior).
- **Calibration evidence**: out-of-sample validation or backtesting where applicable, including error bands.
- **No point-only reporting**: point estimates must not be presented as exact when structural uncertainty is material.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a model’s structural uncertainty is not disclosed, any compatibility claim depending on it MUST be treated as **non-conform**.
- Where model uncertainty is high and cannot be bounded, model outputs MUST NOT sit in the compatibility-critical decision path.

---

## B00.CH12.P06.S04 — Revision policy: how updates are processed without semantic break

Updates to data, methods, or assumptions must not silently change meaning. OBA requires a revision policy that preserves continuity
through versioning, restatements, and explicit bridging.

### Minimum revision policy requirements
- **Versioned releases**: revisions produce new dataset/method versions with changelog and integrity proofs where required.
- **Restatements**: historical series must be restated where method changes affect comparability, or bridged with explicit mappings.
- **Revision windows**: declare when revisions are allowed (timing) and how long a series remains “open”.
- **Backcasting rules**: specify whether and how revisions are applied to past periods.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Silent revisions are forbidden. Any revision affecting published compatibility metrics MUST be publicly logged and versioned.
- If revisions materially change compatibility determinations, the public register MUST be updated and a correction notice issued
  where claims were communicated.
- If the revision regime is absent or broken, dependent compatibility status MUST be suspended or downgraded to **non-conform**
  until revision discipline is restored (fail-closed).
