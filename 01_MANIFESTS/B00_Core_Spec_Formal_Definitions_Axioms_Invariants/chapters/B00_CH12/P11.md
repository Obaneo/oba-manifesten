# B00.CH12.P11 — Tooling and model standards (incl. AI)

This paragraph defines standards for tooling and models used in OBA measurement and enforcement. Tools and models may support
classification, anomaly detection, reconciliation, and monitoring, but they MUST not introduce opacity into compatibility-critical
decisions. Any model used in an OBA pipeline must be documented, auditable, versioned, and secured against tampering and silent drift.

---

## B00.CH12.P11.S01 — Model cards and datasheets: assumptions, limits, failure modes

Any model or automated tool that influences OBA measurements or decisions MUST be documented with a standardized disclosure artifact.

### Required documentation artifacts (minimum)
- **Model card** (for models):
  - intended use and non-use,
  - training data provenance and coverage,
  - feature inputs (categories) and required preprocessing,
  - assumptions and constraints,
  - performance metrics and error bands on relevant populations,
  - known failure modes and boundary conditions,
  - update cadence and versioning policy.
- **Datasheet** (for datasets used in modeling or tooling):
  - provenance, collection method, sampling frame,
  - field definitions and units,
  - missingness and known biases,
  - allowed uses and privacy constraints,
  - lineage links to upstream sources.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- A model/tool MUST NOT be used in an OBA compatibility-relevant pipeline without a published model card and datasheets (or a public
  summary plus audit access where raw details are restricted).
- If documentation is missing or incomplete, dependent claims MUST be treated as **non-conform** (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH12.P11.S02 — Model audit: reproducibility, bias checks, drift monitoring

Models and automated tools must be auditable with the same discipline as measurement methods.

### Minimum audit requirements
- **Reproducibility**: given the same inputs, the tool/model produces the same outputs within declared tolerances (or bounded stochasticity).
- **Bias checks**: evaluate differential error and impact across relevant cohorts; publish results and mitigation where needed.
- **Drift monitoring**: detect distribution shift, performance degradation, and changes in error profiles over time.
- **Change control**: any update to model, features, training data, or thresholds is versioned and logged.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Models used in measurement or monitoring MUST undergo periodic audit and publish audit outcomes (or publish redacted summaries with justification).
- If drift or bias issues materially affect compatibility-critical outputs, the model MUST be removed from the critical path or the
  dependent claim scope MUST be treated as **uncertain** and therefore **non-conform** until corrected (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH12.P11.S03 — Black-box limitation: critical decisions require interpretable/inspectable pipelines

OBA prohibits black-box decisioning for compatibility-critical determinations. Automation is allowed, opacity is not.

### Critical decisions (non-exhaustive)
- assignment of compatibility status labels,
- cap compliance determinations (pass/fail),
- forbidden channel determinations,
- enforcement triggers and sanctions decisions,
- reclassification decisions that change status or obligations.

### Black-box limitation rule
- For critical decisions, the pipeline must be **inspectable**:
  - decision logic must be explainable in terms of canonical observables,
  - intermediate outputs must be accessible for audit,
  - thresholds and rules must be published or auditable under the access regime.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a critical decision depends on an opaque model that cannot be inspected or reproduced, the decision process MUST be treated as
  **non-conform** and must fail closed (no privileges granted) until replaced with an inspectable pipeline.
- Models may be used as advisory tools, but the final decision path must remain auditable and grounded in canonical observables.

---

## B00.CH12.P11.S04 — Supply-chain security: provenance of data/software, signed builds, tamper evidence

Measurement integrity depends on the security of the toolchain. OBA therefore requires supply-chain security and tamper evidence.

### Minimum security requirements
- **Provenance**: record provenance of data sources, software dependencies, and build artifacts.
- **Signed builds**: critical tooling releases are signed; signatures are verifiable via published keys.
- **Immutable artifacts**: release artifacts are content-addressed (hashes) and stored as immutable snapshots.
- **Tamper evidence**: logs and datasets include integrity proofs; any modification produces a detectable change.
- **Access control**: privileged modification paths are controlled, logged, and reviewable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If provenance cannot be established for critical data/software, or if tamper evidence is missing, dependent measurements and claims
  MUST be treated as **non-conform** until integrity is restored (fail-closed).
- Supply-chain incidents (breach, dependency compromise, integrity loss) MUST trigger incident procedure, re-verification, and
  potential suspension of affected compatibility status.
