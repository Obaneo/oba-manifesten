# B00.CH10.P06 — Financial shifts: typical patterns and counter-rules

This paragraph addresses **financial shifts** that are commonly used to **evade regulatory frameworks** such as OBA. These
shifts manipulate **financial instruments** or **structures** to **mask ownership**, **obscure control**, or bypass **caps**.
The key financial shifts discussed here include **securitisation**, **synthetic exposures**, **shadow banking**, and **collateral–asset inflation**. Counter-rules are provided to ensure that such tactics do not undermine the transparency and accountability principles central to OBA.

---

## B00.CH10.P06.S01 — Securitisation and fee extraction: originator→servicer fee stacks

**Securitisation** is a financial strategy where assets are **converted** into **securities** that can be traded or sold to
investors. In this process, **fee extraction** can occur when the **originator** (the party issuing the asset-backed security) and
**servicer** (the party managing the assets) charge fees at multiple stages, extracting excessive value without contributing to
productive capacity or public benefit.

### Key principles of securitisation and fee extraction:
- **Fee stacking**: multiple layers of fees are applied to the same underlying asset or **cash flow**. For instance, the originator
  charges fees for setting up the securitisation, while the servicer charges fees for managing and maintaining the underlying
  assets, resulting in **cascading fee structures**.
- **Asset-backed securities**: securitisation allows the **originator** to maintain **ownership** of assets while transferring the
  **economic risk** to others, allowing for **fee extraction** without bearing the full risks of ownership.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Fee stacking** must be transparent, with each fee clearly justified by the service provided.
- Any **excessive fees** or hidden **profit extraction** in securitisation arrangements must be corrected to ensure compliance
  with OBA principles.

## B00.CH10.P06.S02 — Synthetic exposures: TRS/swaps used to avoid ownership/caps

**Synthetic exposures** are financial instruments such as **Total Return Swaps (TRS)**, **swaps**, or **options** that provide
**exposure** to assets or market positions without the need for **actual ownership**. These instruments are often used to
**circumvent** ownership **caps** or to avoid the **consolidation** of assets, enabling the **economic benefit** of ownership
without the formal responsibilities or regulatory requirements.

### Key principles of synthetic exposures:
- **Total Return Swaps (TRS)**: a financial contract in which one party receives the total return of an asset (including
  price appreciation and income) without actually owning the asset.
- **Swaps and options**: instruments that allow parties to **exchange** future cash flows or returns from underlying assets
  without holding direct ownership.
- **Avoidance of caps**: synthetic exposure allows for **indirect control** or **benefit** without triggering regulatory
  **ownership caps**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any use of **synthetic exposures** to bypass **ownership caps** or conceal **benefits** from assets must be disclosed and
  reevaluated.
- **Non-compliant use** of these instruments to avoid regulatory oversight will lead to **reclassification** and **sanctions**.

## B00.CH10.P06.S03 — Shadow banking: off-balance conduits, repo chains, rehypothecation

**Shadow banking** refers to financial activities that occur **outside** traditional banking regulation, often through
**off-balance-sheet conduits** or **repo chains**, where assets and liabilities are hidden from view. Practices like
**rehypothecation**, where **collateral** is used multiple times as security for loans, can exacerbate this lack of
transparency.

### Key principles of shadow banking:
- **Off-balance-sheet conduits**: financial institutions set up **separate entities** to conduct financial transactions
  without them being recorded on the balance sheet, often to avoid regulatory scrutiny or tax obligations.
- **Repo chains**: the practice of repeatedly selling and repurchasing **securities**, which allows financial institutions to
  **leverage** their balance sheets without reporting the transactions.
- **Rehypothecation**: the practice of using **client assets** as **collateral** for the institution's own borrowings, often
  without client knowledge or consent.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Shadow banking practices** that **obscure ownership** or **misclassify liabilities** must be fully disclosed and
  **rectified** to ensure compliance with OBA.
- Any **rehypothecation** or **off-balance-sheet activities** that result in **hidden liabilities** or **illegitimate risk
  transfer** must be corrected to maintain transparency.

## B00.CH10.P06.S04 — Collateral–asset inflation: leverage cycles that undermine L and R/B

**Collateral–asset inflation** occurs when the **value of assets** used as **collateral** is artificially inflated through
**leverage cycles**. This can distort the true **financial position** of an entity or individual, undermining **life-world
buffers (L)** and **R/B (remainder space)** thresholds. This tactic can also be used to **bypass** asset-based **caps** or
to extract value in ways that are not aligned with the **public goods** principles of OBA.

### Key principles of collateral–asset inflation:
- **Leverage cycles**: assets are repeatedly used as **collateral** to obtain further credit or financing, inflating their
  apparent value and masking the true **risk exposure** of the entity.
- **Asset inflation**: artificially increasing the value of **collateral** or **assets** through financial engineering, which
  can lead to **undue influence** or manipulation of ownership.
- **Undermining L and R/B**: leveraging **inflated assets** can push **risk boundaries** and reduce the buffer required to
  ensure financial stability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Leverage cycles** or **collateral inflation** that **obscure true asset values** or circumvent OBA’s **caps** and **buffer
  thresholds** must be disclosed and corrected immediately.
- **Failure to rectify** inflated **asset values** or **collateral manipulation** will result in **non-compliance** and **sanctions**.
