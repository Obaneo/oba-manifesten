# B00.CH10.P07 — Technical shifts: digital avoidance and counter-rules

This paragraph discusses **technical shifts** used in the digital and technological domains to **circumvent** OBA principles.
These shifts often exploit digital platforms, **algorithms**, and technological infrastructures to create **opaque structures**
that mask true **ownership**, **control**, or **economic benefit**. The section provides **counter-rules** to detect and prevent
these avoidance tactics, ensuring that digital platforms and systems comply with the broader principles of **transparency**,
**fairness**, and **public goods provision** under OBA.

---

## B00.CH10.P07.S01 — Bundling and tying: opaque price discrimination as toll

**Bundling** and **tying** are tactics used in digital markets to **disguise** the **true cost** of a service or product by forcing
consumers to **pay for unwanted products** or services as part of a bundle. These practices can create **opaque price
discrimination**, where consumers are unable to see the full price or the true value of what they are purchasing.

### Key principles of bundling and tying:
- **Bundling**: selling **multiple products or services** together, often at a price that is **higher than the sum of individual
  products**, making it difficult for consumers to evaluate the **true cost** of each item.
- **Tying**: requiring consumers to purchase a **related product** or service in order to access or use the primary product,
  often creating **hidden costs** or **unfair market access**.
- **Opaque price discrimination**: using **bundles** or **tied sales** to create a **discriminatory pricing system**, where
  consumers cannot easily identify the actual price or the value of the individual components.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Bundling and tying** practices must be **transparent**, with clear **itemized pricing** and no **hidden fees** or forced
  purchases.
- If **price discrimination** is not **justified by clear economic function**, these practices will be considered **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH10.P07.S02 — Dark patterns: deception as hidden price (E)

**Dark patterns** refer to deceptive design practices used in digital platforms or services to **manipulate user decisions**
or **extract value** without their clear understanding. These manipulative tactics often involve **hidden prices**, **covert fees**, or
**misleading interfaces**, creating a **hidden toll** that consumers pay for using a service.

### Key principles of dark patterns:
- **Deceptive design**: the use of **misleading interfaces**, such as hidden **fees** or charges that are only revealed after
  a commitment is made (e.g., “bait and switch” or **forced opt-ins**).
- **Hidden price (E)**: users are led to believe that they are getting something for free or at a lower cost, while in
  reality, they are being charged **hidden fees** or subjected to **unfair costs**.
- **Manipulative tactics**: using **algorithmic deception**, such as default opt-ins, difficulty in canceling subscriptions,
  or **manipulative nudges** that lead to **extractive behaviors**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Dark patterns** are prohibited, and **platforms must disclose** all **fees** and **charges** clearly.
- If a platform is found using **deceptive design** or **hidden price tactics**, it will be considered **non-compliant** and
  face **sanctions**.

## B00.CH10.P07.S03 — API locks and data portability barriers: block power as E driver

**API locks** and **data portability barriers** refer to practices where **platforms** or **services** restrict access to
**data** or prevent users from transferring their data to other services. This practice can create **block power** and lead
to **extractive behavior** (E), especially when it forces consumers to stay on a platform or service, limiting their freedom
to choose alternatives.

### Key principles of API locks and data portability barriers:
- **API locks**: platforms that control access to **critical APIs** may **limit third-party developers** or users from accessing
  data, services, or tools, effectively blocking them from integrating with or migrating to competing services.
- **Data portability barriers**: when platforms **restrict data export** or impose **high costs** for transferring data to
  other platforms, it creates friction for consumers trying to switch services or use their data elsewhere.
- **Block power (E)**: this type of **exclusivity** or **restriction** prevents competition and keeps users dependent on one
  platform, creating a **toll** on the consumer’s freedom of choice.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **API locks** and **data portability barriers** must not be used to **restrict competition** or force **users into
  extractive practices**.
- Platforms engaging in **blocking access** to data or critical resources will be **reclassified** as **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH10.P07.S04 — Tokenisation/crypto rails: pseudo-decentralisation as avoidance vector

**Tokenisation** and **crypto rails** (blockchain and cryptocurrency systems) are increasingly being used to create the illusion
of **decentralisation** and **ownership** while maintaining hidden or unaccountable control. These practices are often leveraged
to **circumvent** OBA’s rules by creating decentralized structures that, in practice, are **controlled** by a small group
or entity.

### Key principles of tokenisation/crypto rails:
- **Pseudo-decentralisation**: creating a structure that appears **decentralized** (e.g., a **DAO (Decentralized Autonomous
  Organization)**) but is actually controlled by a few key actors who hold a disproportionate amount of **tokens** or have
  substantial **voting power**.
- **Crypto rails**: using **cryptocurrencies** or **blockchain-based platforms** to bypass traditional financial systems and
  regulatory oversight, while potentially **consolidating control** or enabling **untraceable** transactions.
- **Avoidance vector**: these systems can be used to evade **ownership caps**, **benefit distribution rules**, or other
  **OBA requirements** under the guise of decentralisation or anonymity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any platform or entity using **tokenisation** or **crypto rails** must **demonstrate true decentralisation** and ensure that
  **control** and **benefit** are transparently **distributed**.
- If **pseudo-decentralisation** is found, the platform will be treated as **non-compliant** and subject to **reclassification** and
  **sanctions**.
