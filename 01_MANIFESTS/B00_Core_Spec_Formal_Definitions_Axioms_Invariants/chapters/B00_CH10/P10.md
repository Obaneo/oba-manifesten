# B00.CH10.P10 — Sanctions and remedies (proportional but firm)

This paragraph defines the **sanctions** and **remedies** that apply when entities fail to comply with the OBA framework. The sanctions
are designed to be **proportional** to the violation but also **firm** in enforcing accountability. Remedies are intended to correct
non-compliant behaviors and prevent future violations. This section outlines **financial sanctions**, **structural remedies**, and
**discretion minimisation** practices, as well as **hard stops** for situations where non-compliance is serious or persistent.

---

## B00.CH10.P10.S01 — Financial sanctions: fines, clawback, interest, multipliers for recidivism

**Financial sanctions** are imposed to deter **non-compliance** and to **compensate** for any damage caused by avoidance tactics
or regulatory breaches. These can include fines, the **clawback of ill-gotten benefits**, and **interest** on outstanding
amounts. Repeat offenders face **multiplied penalties** to encourage full compliance.

### Key elements of financial sanctions:
- **Fines**: monetary penalties for failure to comply with OBA rules, which can be calculated based on the severity of
  the violation.
- **Clawback**: recovery of **illegally obtained** benefits or profits resulting from avoidance tactics.
- **Interest**: charges on any outstanding amounts due to non-compliance, ensuring that entities are incentivized to pay owed
  fines or penalties promptly.
- **Multipliers for recidivism**: repeat offenders face **increased penalties**, with **multiplied fines** and **penalties** for
  continued non-compliance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any violation of OBA must result in appropriate **financial sanctions**, with **escalating penalties** for recidivism.
- **Failure to pay fines** or **clawback** penalties results in further **sanctions** or **asset freezes**.

---

## B00.CH10.P10.S02 — Structural remedies: contract adjustment, unbundling, interoperability mandates

**Structural remedies** are corrective actions that aim to **restructure** or **unwind** arrangements that violate OBA principles.
These actions ensure that ownership, control, and benefit flows are fully transparent and compliant with the regulations.

### Key examples of structural remedies:
- **Contract adjustment**: changing the terms of **contracts** that were used to circumvent OBA rules, ensuring that all
  stakeholders are treated fairly and in compliance with the principles of **transparency** and **non-extractive behavior**.
- **Unbundling**: **splitting** complex or artificially bundled arrangements that were used to **mask** true ownership or control.
- **Interoperability mandates**: requiring parties to **make their systems interoperable** with competitors or public infrastructure
  to prevent monopolistic or anti-competitive practices.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Non-compliant** arrangements must undergo **structural remedies** like **contract adjustments** or **unbundling** to bring them
  into alignment with OBA principles.
- Any **refusal to implement** **structural remedies** will lead to **further sanctions** or potential **hard stops**.

---

## B00.CH10.P10.S03 — Discretion minimisation: sanction tables and mandatory justification

To **minimize discretion** in the enforcement of OBA rules, **sanction tables** are established, which clearly define the
appropriate penalties and remedies for specific violations. **Mandatory justification** for any deviations from these tables
ensures that sanctions are applied consistently and transparently.

### Key principles:
- **Sanction tables**: predefined **penalties** for various types of violations (e.g., **non-disclosure**, **misclassification**,
  **obstruction**) based on severity.
- **Mandatory justification**: when a sanction is **applied**, it must be **justified** according to the severity of the violation
  and aligned with the **sanction table**.
- **Minimizing discretion**: reducing the potential for subjective enforcement decisions by providing clear, consistent rules
  for penalties.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Failure to adhere to the sanction table** or **provide adequate justification** for deviations from it will result in **non-compliance**.
- **Clear and consistent** application of penalties must be maintained, with **justification** required for any deviation.

---

## B00.CH10.P10.S04 — Hard stops: in case of obstruction/black-box → immediate non-conform status

**Hard stops** are immediate actions taken when a violation is **severe** or **persistent**, such as when **obstruction** is
detected or when entities use **black-box structures** to conceal ownership, control, or benefit relationships. In these
cases, the entity is immediately placed in **non-conform status**, and corrective actions are required to regain compliance.

### Key principles:
- **Obstruction**: any action that intentionally **blocks audits**, **disclosures**, or **regulatory inquiries**, or where
  **cooperation** with OBA enforcement is withheld.
- **Black-box structures**: arrangements or entities where **ownership**, **control**, or **benefit** are deliberately
  obscured to avoid regulatory oversight.
- **Immediate non-conform status**: when **hard stops** are invoked, the entity is automatically treated as **non-compliant**,
  and enforcement actions will be initiated without further delay.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Obstruction** or **black-box arrangements** must be immediately **corrected**, and **full transparency** must be restored.
- **Failure to resolve** obstruction or black-box issues results in **non-compliance**, and the entity will face **additional
  sanctions** and **hard stops** until full compliance is achieved.
