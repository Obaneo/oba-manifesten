# B00.CH10.P05 — Legal shifts: typical patterns and counter-rules

This paragraph identifies typical **legal shifts** used by entities to **circumvent** OBA regulations, particularly those
related to **ownership**, **control**, and **benefit**. These shifts often involve **reclassification** of economic transactions
or the use of **legal structures** to mask or redistribute **value** in ways that avoid transparency. The counter-rules outlined
here provide strategies for detecting and addressing these manipulative practices.

---

## B00.CH10.P05.S01 — Reclassification of sale to licence/subscription with lock-in toll

**Reclassification of sales** into **licence** or **subscription** arrangements with **lock-in tolls** is a common legal shift used to
avoid **ownership caps**, **benefit distribution** rules, or **tax obligations**. By shifting a sale into a licence or subscription,
entities can retain **control** over the asset while creating ongoing, **extractive cash flows** through **tolls** or **recurring payments**.

### Key examples of this shift:
- **Sale reclassified as licence/subscription**: instead of selling an asset outright, an entity might reclassify the
  **transaction** as a **licence** or **subscription** agreement, where the buyer continues to pay ongoing fees without
  **ownership transfer**.
- **Lock-in tolls**: a **toll** is charged in exchange for the ongoing right to use the asset or service, often with **no exit
  options** or **high switching costs**, ensuring that the buyer remains financially tethered to the provider.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any transaction reclassified as a **licence** or **subscription** must be evaluated to ensure that it does not **obfuscate** the
  **transfer of ownership** or result in **extractive behavior**.
- **Failure to substantiate the true nature** of a transaction results in **non-compliance** and must be rectified.

## B00.CH10.P05.S02 — Franchise/royalty structures as toll-gate

**Franchise** and **royalty structures** are often used to create **toll-gates** for businesses, whereby the franchisor or rights-holder
benefits from **royalties** or franchise fees without truly **transferring control** or contributing to the productive use of the asset.

### Key principles of franchise/royalty shifts:
- **Franchise arrangements**: companies or individuals may set up **franchise models** that allow the **franchisor** to **extract
  ongoing fees** or royalties from the franchisee without transferring substantial **operational control** or economic value.
- **Royalty structures**: royalty fees that **extract value** from **IP** or **brand ownership** without requiring **active
  involvement** or a fair sharing of profits.
  
These **royalty fees** and **franchise costs** can be used to **shift benefits** from the operating business to the **franchisor** or
**licence-holder** while avoiding broader OBA rules, especially **benefit limits** or **income caps**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Franchise** and **royalty models** must be **fully disclosed** to ensure they do not function as **toll-gates** to extract
  disproportionate value from franchisees or licensees.
- **Failure to disclose** or attempt to use **franchise/royalty structures** to obscure true **economic benefit** will result in
  **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH10.P05.S03 — Consultancy/management fees between related parties (profit stripping)

**Consultancy** and **management fees** are commonly used by related parties (e.g., parent and subsidiary companies) to **strip
profits** from one entity and shift them to another. These payments often serve to **undermine caps**, **redistribute benefits**
unfairly, or avoid **tax liabilities**.

### Key principles of profit stripping:
- **Related-party transactions**: when **consultancy or management fees** are paid from one entity to another within the same
  corporate group, especially when the **fees are disproportionately high** compared to the value of the services provided.
- **Profit shifting**: these fees can be used to **strip profits** from high-tax jurisdictions and shift them to low-tax
  jurisdictions or entities with less oversight.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Consultancy/management fees** between **related parties** must be scrutinized to ensure that the **value of the service**
  corresponds to the **amount of the fee**.
- Any **excessive fees** or **profit-stripping schemes** designed to bypass OBA caps or regulations will result in **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH10.P05.S04 — Contractual lock-in (MFN, exclusivity, termination penalties) as E indicator

**Contractual lock-in** refers to agreements that **bind parties** into exclusive arrangements with high **exit penalties** or
**minimum terms**, often used to **lock-in** participants to **extractive practices**. These lock-in mechanisms are often
indicative of **extractive (E)** behavior, especially when they restrict access to markets, services, or product options.

### Key examples of contractual lock-in:
- **MFN (Most-Favored-Nation) clauses**: guarantees a party the **best terms** offered to any other party, effectively preventing
  **competitive** or **market-based pricing**.
- **Exclusivity clauses**: contractual agreements that prevent a party from engaging in **business with competitors** for the
  duration of the contract, potentially blocking access to better opportunities.
- **Termination penalties**: heavy **penalties** for terminating a contract early, which creates financial **barriers** and **lock-in**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **contractual lock-in** clauses, especially those involving **MFN**, **exclusivity**, or **termination penalties**, must
  be **evaluated** to ensure they do not create **unjustifiable restrictions** or constitute **extractive behavior**.
- **Non-compliant** contractual agreements that restrict access to **market alternatives** or hinder fair competition will
  result in **reclassification** and **sanctions**.
