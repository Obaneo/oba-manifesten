# B00.CH10 — Anti-avoidance: reclassification, shift taxonomy and detection rules

This chapter defines the **anti-avoidance rules** in the OBA framework, which focus on ensuring that ownership, control,
and benefit relationships remain transparent, non-extractive, and in alignment with the principles of the commons. It
addresses tactics used to **circumvent** OBA’s transparency and accountability measures, such as **shifting structures**, **reclassification**, and **avoidance strategies**. These practices could undermine the integrity of the system, allowing financial entities or individuals to **evade compliance** with regulatory thresholds and principles.

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **designing or reviewing ownership structures**: apply the **anti-avoidance principles** to ensure that no
  **shifting tactics** are used to bypass OBA principles.
- If you are **auditing**: monitor for **red flags** and **anomalies** in ownership, control, or benefit arrangements, and
  use the **detection rules** to uncover potential avoidance tactics.
- If you are **enforcing compliance**: use the **reclassification principle** and **detection rules** to ensure that
  ownership structures align with **substance over form** and are fully transparent.

## Status

This chapter outlines the mechanisms for identifying and addressing **avoidance tactics** in ownership and control structures.
The rules aim to ensure that entities **comply with OBA principles** by preventing the use of legal, financial, or technical
strategies that might distort transparency or allow for the evasion of regulatory requirements. It is crucial that **ownership**,
**control**, and **benefit** are fully disclosed and **non-extractive**, even in complex or cross-border structures.
