# B00.CH10.P01 — Purpose, scope and normative status of anti-avoidance

This paragraph defines the **purpose**, **scope**, and **normative status** of the **anti-avoidance** measures within
the **OBA framework**. It emphasizes the importance of preventing the use of **form** to obscure the **true economic function**
or intentions behind ownership, control, and benefit arrangements. Anti-avoidance ensures that **caps**, **P/E/C (production/extraction/commons)** rules, **ΔM channels**, and **commons status** remain effective by **disallowing manipulative structures** designed to circumvent these rules.

---

## B00.CH10.P01.S01 — Anti-avoidance as order invariant: “form must not trump function”

**Anti-avoidance** is an **order invariant** principle within the OBA framework, meaning that **the economic function** must
always prevail over **legal form**. This ensures that ownership structures and financial transactions are assessed based on
their **economic substance** rather than their **legal appearance**. Entities cannot disguise **non-compliant behavior** through
complex structures or financial instruments that appear compliant on the surface but do not meet the **fundamental objectives** of OBA.

### Key principles:
- **Form vs. function**: the **legal form** of an arrangement (e.g., the creation of trusts, shell companies, or complex contracts) 
  should not obscure the **true economic function** of the arrangement.
- **Substance over form**: the **real economic effects** (e.g., the distribution of benefits, control, and financial flows) should
  take precedence in determining compliance with OBA.
- **Prevention of manipulation**: entities cannot manipulate the **legal structure** to evade **P/E/C**, **caps**, or **ΔM**
  rules by using forms that do not reflect the actual functioning of the financial arrangement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Economic substance** must always be prioritized over **legal form** when determining ownership, control, and benefit.
- **Failure to disclose or manipulate structures** to hide **true function** will lead to **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH10.P01.S02 — Purpose: making caps, P/E/C, ΔM channel rules and commons status effective

The **purpose** of anti-avoidance measures is to ensure that **caps**, **P/E/C**, **ΔM channel rules**, and **commons status**
remain **effective** and **enforceable**. These principles are central to the OBA framework and are designed to promote fairness,
transparency, and the **equitable distribution** of resources. Without anti-avoidance measures, there is a risk that entities
may **circumvent** or **undermine** these rules, leading to **distorted outcomes** or the **privatization** of commons resources.

### Key purposes:
- **Enforcing caps**: ensuring that **wealth** (W) and **income** (I) caps are respected and that individuals or entities cannot
  use avoidance strategies to exceed set limits.
- **P/E/C principles**: ensuring that **production (P)**, **extraction (E)**, and **commons (C)** are correctly classified and
  that resources are allocated in line with public welfare goals.
- **ΔM channels**: ensuring that **monetary space (ΔM)** is used appropriately and that it does not flow into **prohibited
  channels** or lead to **financial capture**.
- **Commons status**: ensuring that **commons resources** remain accessible to all and are not captured by private entities
  through avoidance tactics.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Anti-avoidance measures must be used to **maintain the integrity** of caps, **P/E/C classifications**, **ΔM channels**, and
  **commons governance**.
- Any **evasion of these rules** will be considered **non-compliant** and must be addressed immediately.

---

## B00.CH10.P01.S03 — Scope: legal, financial, technical, international, institutional

The **scope** of anti-avoidance measures extends across multiple domains, ensuring that **no structure** or arrangement,
whether **legal**, **financial**, **technical**, **international**, or **institutional**, can be used to evade OBA principles.

### Key scope areas:
- **Legal**: ensuring that **legal structures** such as **trusts**, **partnerships**, and **corporations** do not obscure the
  true control or benefit behind the arrangement.
- **Financial**: preventing **financial engineering** that might obscure the true economic effects of an arrangement (e.g.,
  through **derivatives**, **synthetic exposures**, or **shadow banking**).
- **Technical**: addressing **digital avoidance** tactics, such as **bundling**, **API locks**, or **data extraction as toll**.
- **International**: preventing the **use of cross-border mechanisms** like **treaty shopping**, **IP boxes**, or **re-domiciliation**
  to evade OBA rules.
- **Institutional**: ensuring that **financial institutions**, **public entities**, and **multinational organizations** are held
  accountable to the same standards and do not create structures that evade OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Anti-avoidance measures must be applied **universally** across **legal**, **financial**, **technical**, **international**, and
  **institutional domains**.
- **Failure to address** avoidance tactics across any of these domains will result in **non-compliance** and the need for corrective action.

---

## B00.CH10.P01.S04 — Relation to evidence standards, control/benefit/block and compatibility

Anti-avoidance principles must be **integrated with evidence standards**, **control/benefit/block tests**, and **compatibility
checks**. These principles ensure that **evidence** is **adequately gathered**, that ownership and control are **correctly
classified**, and that no structures or arrangements **circumvent** the core principles of **OBA**.

### Key relationships:
- **Evidence standards**: anti-avoidance measures rely on **robust evidence** to detect avoidance tactics, ensuring that claims
  of control, benefit, or block are substantiated with transparent and verifiable data.
- **Control/benefit/block tests**: these tests help ensure that **economic control**, **benefit receipt**, and **blocking power**
  are **correctly attributed**, even in complex ownership structures or when avoidance tactics are used.
- **Compatibility**: anti-avoidance measures must ensure that any **ownership or control structures** align with OBA's goals
  and **public goods provision**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Full compliance** with **evidence standards**, **control/benefit/block tests**, and **compatibility** must be maintained
  to prevent avoidance.
- **Failure to integrate** these principles effectively will result in **non-compliance** and corrective action must be
  undertaken.
