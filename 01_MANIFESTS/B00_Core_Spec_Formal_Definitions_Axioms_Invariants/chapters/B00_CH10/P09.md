# B00.CH10.P09 — Automatic reclassification: triggers and procedures

This paragraph outlines the rules and procedures for **automatic reclassification** of ownership, control, and benefit
structures when specific **triggers** are met. These triggers include **thresholds**, **anomalies**, and patterns detected
through **audits**, which may indicate that certain financial flows, entities, or transactions need to be reclassified
to ensure compliance with OBA principles. Automatic reclassification serves as a corrective mechanism to ensure that
**misclassified** or **non-compliant** structures are identified and appropriately adjusted.

---

## B00.CH10.P09.S01 — Trigger classes: thresholds, anomalies, structural patterns, audit findings

**Trigger classes** define the types of **circumstances** that automatically activate reclassification processes. These
circumstances include deviations from **regulatory thresholds**, the detection of **anomalies** or **suspicious patterns**,
and the findings from **audits**.

### Key trigger classes:
- **Thresholds**: when entities exceed **regulatory limits** (e.g., wealth caps, income thresholds), this triggers the need
  for **reclassification** to bring the entity back into compliance.
- **Anomalies**: detection of **irregular patterns** in ownership, control, or financial flows (e.g., **circular flows**, **unjustified fee stacking**).
- **Structural patterns**: identifying patterns of ownership, control, or benefit that are **designed to circumvent** OBA principles
  or **mislead regulators**.
- **Audit findings**: discrepancies found during **audit procedures**, including failure to disclose **beneficial ownership**,
  **misclassified assets**, or **illegitimate financial structures**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any **trigger** that activates the need for reclassification must be **fully documented** and subject to appropriate follow-up actions.
- **Failure to address detected anomalies or thresholds** will result in **non-compliance** and must be rectified immediately.

---

## B00.CH10.P09.S02 — Reclassification outcome: (i) relabel P→E, (ii) inclusion in W/I base, (iii) channel ban

When **reclassification** is triggered, there are specific **outcomes** that follow to ensure proper alignment with OBA principles.

### Key reclassification outcomes:
1. **Relabel P→E**: if an entity or flow was **incorrectly classified** as **production (P)** but is found to be **extractive (E)**,
   it must be reclassified as **E** to reflect its true economic function.
2. **Inclusion in W/I base**: if an asset or income flow is found to be improperly excluded from the **wealth (W)** or **income (I)**
   caps or calculations, it must be **included** in the appropriate base to ensure compliance with OBA thresholds.
3. **Channel ban**: if a **prohibited channel** (e.g., those that are used to conceal ownership or evade regulatory requirements)
   is detected, that channel will be banned, and any transactions involving it will be deemed **non-compliant**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Reclassification must be **documented** clearly, and the relevant **outcome** must be implemented, including **adjusting the
  classification**, **inclusion in W/I base**, or **banning non-compliant channels**.
- **Failure to carry out the reclassification outcome** as required will result in **non-compliance** and further corrective
  action.

---

## B00.CH10.P09.S03 — Time-bounded dispute window: objection without “delay-as-avoidance”

Once a **reclassification** decision is made, the entity under scrutiny must have the ability to **contest** or **appeal** the decision,
but the process must be **time-bounded** to prevent delays used as a tactic to avoid compliance.

### Key principles:
- **Time-bounded dispute window**: entities must be allowed to raise **objections** or **appeals** against the reclassification decision
  within a defined period, after which the decision is considered **final** and enforcement actions will be taken.
- **No “delay-as-avoidance”**: entities should not be able to **delay** the process indefinitely or use the dispute window as a tactic to
  **avoid compliance** or further scrutiny.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Objections** or **appeals** must be submitted within the designated **time frame**, and entities must not use this process to **delay** or
  **circumvent** enforcement.
- **Failure to adhere to the time-bounded dispute process** will result in **non-compliance**, and the reclassification decision will be enforced.

---

## B00.CH10.P09.S04 — Publication: methods, triggers, error margins (without leaking sensitive details)

The reclassification process and the related **audit findings** must be **published** to maintain transparency and accountability,
but sensitive details must be protected to ensure **privacy** and **confidentiality**.

### Key principles:
- **Methods**: the methodologies used to identify triggers, anomalies, and discrepancies in ownership or financial flows must be
  clearly defined and published to ensure transparency.
- **Triggers**: **public disclosure** of the specific triggers that led to reclassification (e.g., exceeding wealth caps or triggering
  audit findings).
- **Error margins**: any **error margins** or uncertainty in the reclassification process should be **clearly communicated**, while
  ensuring that sensitive details are not disclosed.
- **Sensitive details**: personal, financial, or other confidential information must be **protected** during the publication process,
  to avoid leaking sensitive data.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- The **reclassification** process and the **methods** behind it must be **transparent**, but without violating privacy or confidentiality
  rules.
- Entities must ensure that **sensitive information** is **not disclosed** during the publication of the reclassification outcomes.
- **Failure to ensure confidentiality** or **disclosure of sensitive information** will lead to **non-compliance** and potential **sanctions**.
