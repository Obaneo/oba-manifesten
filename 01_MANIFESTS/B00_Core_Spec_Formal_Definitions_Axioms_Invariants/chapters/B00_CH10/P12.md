# B00.CH10.P12 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph outlines the **failure modes** and the **hard stops** that are triggered when OBA's **detection capacity**
or **enforcement procedures** are compromised. It emphasizes potential risks such as **rule overload**, **gameable detection
mechanisms**, and **political sabotage**, all of which can threaten the integrity of the OBA framework. The section also
describes how the system will scale back to a **more auditable level** in the event of a **detection failure**, ensuring that
**compliance** is maintained despite the challenges.

---

## B00.CH10.P12.S01 — Rule overload: too many discretionary exceptions → capture risk

**Rule overload** occurs when **too many exceptions** or discretionary decisions are added to the regulatory framework, allowing
entities to exploit loopholes and **capture** the system for their own benefit. When the number of exceptions or **discretionary rules**
becomes too large, it weakens the **effectiveness** of the framework and creates opportunities for **regulatory evasion**.

### Key indicators of rule overload:
- **Discretionary exceptions**: rules that allow entities to **circumvent certain regulations** based on subjective decisions
  or specific conditions that favor certain players.
- **Capture risk**: when entities with enough influence can manipulate the system to **get around regulations** by invoking exceptions
  that are not subject to proper scrutiny.
- **Increased complexity**: an increase in the number of **exceptions** or **special rules** that make it difficult to maintain the
  **integrity** of the framework.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Rule overload** must be **avoided** by limiting the number of **exceptions** and **ensuring transparency** in all regulatory decisions.
- **Failure to limit exceptions** or the presence of excessive **discretion** will result in **non-compliance** and require immediate correction.

---

## B00.CH10.P12.S02 — Goodhart: detection KPIs become gameable → triangulation mandatory

The **Goodhart** principle states that once a **key performance indicator (KPI)** is used as a target for regulatory purposes, it
becomes **gameable** by entities trying to meet the target without truly complying with the underlying regulation. To prevent this,
**triangulation** becomes mandatory—using multiple indicators to ensure that entities cannot **manipulate** the system simply
by hitting a single KPI.

### Key principles:
- **Gameability**: once a KPI is chosen for regulatory compliance, entities may **optimize their operations** solely to meet that
  KPI, often without addressing the **actual underlying issue**.
- **Triangulation**: to ensure **effective monitoring**, multiple KPIs or indicators should be used to measure compliance.
  **Triangulation** helps avoid the risk of entities gaming the system and ensures that **real-world outcomes** align with OBA’s
  principles.
- **Avoiding manipulation**: detection KPIs must be carefully designed to avoid being easily **manipulated** by entities looking
  to meet regulatory requirements superficially.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Triangulation** is required in all compliance and detection systems to ensure that no **single KPI** can be manipulated to
  meet regulatory requirements.
- Any **manipulation** of KPIs or **inability to triangulate** compliance metrics will result in **non-compliance** and the need
  for **remediation**.

---

## B00.CH10.P12.S03 — Political sabotage: selective enforcement → compatibility breach

**Political sabotage** occurs when enforcement is **selectively applied** or biased, often due to political influence or **lobbying**
efforts. This undermines the integrity of the OBA framework and breaches **compatibility** between the principles of **fairness**
and **transparency**.

### Key indicators of political sabotage:
- **Selective enforcement**: enforcement actions are taken **against certain entities** while others, often with political
  connections, are **exempt** or treated more leniently.
- **Bias in regulatory decisions**: regulatory bodies may show favoritism towards **powerful sectors** or **politically connected**
  entities, undermining the **fair application** of OBA rules.
- **Conflict of interest**: when regulatory decisions are influenced by **external interests** or **political pressure**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Selective enforcement** is strictly prohibited, and regulatory bodies must act in an **impartial** and **transparent** manner.
- Any **political interference** in enforcement actions will result in **compatibility breaches**, and entities involved will
  be subject to **sanctions**.

---

## B00.CH10.P12.S04 — Fail-closed: when detection capacity is lost, scope is scaled back to auditable level

In cases where **detection capacity** is compromised or **audit systems fail**, the OBA framework will adopt a **fail-closed**
approach. This means that the **scope of operations** will be **scaled back** to a level that can be properly **audited**,
ensuring that any untraceable or opaque entities are excluded from the system until they can demonstrate full transparency
and compliance.

### Key principles:
- **Detection failure**: when the **capacity to detect** ownership, control, or benefit issues is compromised (e.g., due to
  technical failure or insufficient data), the scope of what can be monitored or verified will be limited.
- **Scaling back**: in the absence of full detection, the **system narrows** its focus to **auditable entities**, excluding those
  that cannot be verified or traced.
- **Non-compliant entities**: entities that cannot meet **auditable standards** or fail to comply with transparency rules will
  be **excluded** from the system until they can be brought into compliance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If **detection capacity** is lost, entities must be **excluded** from the system until they can be verified as compliant.
- **Failure to restore detection** or maintain **auditable transparency** will result in **non-compliance** and exclusion from the OBA framework.
