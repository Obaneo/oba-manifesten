# B00.CH10.P08 — International shifts: cross-border avoidance and counter-rules

This paragraph examines **international shifts** used to exploit differences between **national laws** or **jurisdictions**,
allowing entities to circumvent regulatory frameworks like OBA. These shifts may involve **transfer pricing**, **IP boxes**,
**re-domiciliation**, and the use of **offshore vehicles** or **nominee arrangements** to shift assets, profits, or control
to jurisdictions with more **favorable tax** or **regulatory conditions**. The counter-rules here provide safeguards to ensure that
cross-border avoidance tactics do not undermine the core principles of **OBA**.

---

## B00.CH10.P08.S01 — Transfer pricing: IP royalty and management fee routing

**Transfer pricing** involves the manipulation of **prices** for goods, services, or intangibles (such as **intellectual property**)
sold or transferred between **related entities** in different jurisdictions. This practice can be used to **shift profits** to
lower-tax jurisdictions, thereby avoiding taxes or **circumventing ownership caps**.

### Key principles of transfer pricing:
- **IP royalty routing**: routing **intellectual property** royalties to jurisdictions with favorable tax rates, even though the
  **value creation** or **economic activity** may be occurring in a different location.
- **Management fees**: transferring **profits** through **management fees** or **administrative charges** between subsidiaries,
  affiliates, or related entities, allowing profits to be booked in **low-tax jurisdictions**.
- **Shifting taxable income**: using **transfer pricing** to adjust the **distribution of profits** in order to evade regulatory
  thresholds and avoid taxation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any use of **transfer pricing** to shift **profits** or **avoid regulatory obligations** must be fully disclosed and justified.
- **Non-compliance** in **transfer pricing arrangements** that evade OBA thresholds or **tax obligations** will be subject to
  **sanctions** and **reclassification**.

---

## B00.CH10.P08.S02 — IP boxes and treaty shopping: mismatch between value creation and profit location

**IP boxes** and **treaty shopping** are strategies that involve exploiting **international tax treaties** or creating **intellectual
property hubs** in low-tax jurisdictions to avoid taxes or **misallocate profits**.

### Key principles:
- **IP boxes**: **intellectual property** assets are often **placed in jurisdictions** with **favorable tax treatment** for IP
  income. The economic value created from these assets may be shifted to jurisdictions that provide **tax relief** or preferential
  treatment for royalty income.
- **Treaty shopping**: entities **exploit differences in tax treaties** between jurisdictions to **minimize taxes** by routing
  profits through **countries with favorable treaties**.
- **Mismatch between value creation and profit location**: these practices result in **profits** being reported in **low-tax
  jurisdictions** despite **economic activity** or **value creation** taking place elsewhere, undermining the fairness of
  taxation or public goods provision.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **IP boxes** and **treaty shopping** arrangements must be disclosed, and any **profits** shifted to jurisdictions to avoid
  taxes must be subject to scrutiny and justified.
- Any **misallocation** of profits based on **false or manipulated ownership structures** or tax advantages will lead to
  **reclassification** and **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH10.P08.S03 — Re-domiciliation and inversions: residence and control centres versus paper seat

**Re-domiciliation** and **inversion strategies** allow entities to **shift** their **legal residence** or **control center** to
jurisdictions with **more favorable tax or regulatory regimes**, while continuing to operate in their original location. This
practice creates a **disconnection** between the entity's **economic activity** and its **official domicile**.

### Key principles of re-domiciliation and inversions:
- **Re-domiciliation**: shifting a company’s **legal domicile** to a jurisdiction with more **favorable regulatory** or
  **tax conditions** without changing the location of **operations** or **management**.
- **Inversions**: companies **change their corporate structure** or **jurisdiction of incorporation** by merging with a
  foreign entity in a low-tax jurisdiction, allowing them to claim residence in that jurisdiction while maintaining their
  operational base in another.
- **Paper seat**: these **strategies** create a **legal seat** in a low-tax jurisdiction, while the **real control** and
  **economic activity** remain in a higher-tax jurisdiction, allowing companies to avoid regulatory or tax obligations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Re-domiciliation** and **inversions** must be fully disclosed and **justified** to ensure that the true **control** and
  **economic activity** align with the **legal domicile**.
- Entities engaging in **re-domiciliation** or **inversions** must demonstrate that these changes are not being used to **bypass
  OBA compliance**. **Failure to comply** will result in **reclassification** and **sanctions**.

## B00.CH10.P08.S04 — Offshore vehicles and nominees: look-through and UBC consolidation

**Offshore vehicles** and **nominee arrangements** are often used to **conceal ownership** and **control**, especially in
**low-regulation** or **tax haven** jurisdictions. These structures may involve **nominees** or **intermediaries** who hold
legal title to assets, while the **true beneficial ownership** lies elsewhere.

### Key principles of offshore vehicles and nominees:
- **Offshore vehicles**: entities created in **tax havens** or jurisdictions with **lenient regulations** to hold assets or
  **siphon profits** away from jurisdictions with higher taxes or more stringent regulations.
- **Nominee arrangements**: **nominees** (e.g., legal representatives or holding companies) may hold **legal title** to assets,
  but the **true economic ownership** lies with another party. This obfuscates control and makes it difficult to trace the true
  beneficial owner.
- **Look-through**: OBA requires that **ownership structures** be transparent, and any use of **offshore vehicles** or
  **nominees** must be subjected to **look-through analysis** to identify the **true beneficial owners**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Offshore vehicles** and **nominee structures** must be **disclosed**, and the **beneficial ownership** must be fully
  identified through **look-through rules**.
- **Failure to disclose** the true **beneficial owners** behind offshore vehicles or nominee arrangements will lead to
  **non-compliance** and **reclassification**.
