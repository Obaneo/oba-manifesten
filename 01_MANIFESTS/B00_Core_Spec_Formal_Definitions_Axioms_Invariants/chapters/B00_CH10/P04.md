# B00.CH10.P04 — Detection rules: red flags and anomalies (statistical + rule-based)

This paragraph outlines the **detection rules** for identifying potential **red flags** and **anomalies** within ownership,
control, benefit, and monetary flow structures under the OBA framework. These rules utilize both **statistical** and **rule-based**
methods to uncover discrepancies, manipulations, or inconsistencies in financial flows and ownership relations. The detection
rules aim to identify areas where entities might be **evading transparency** or **circumventing compliance** through complex
structures, non-transparent practices, or financial engineering.

---

## B00.CH10.P04.S01 — Margin extremes: deviation from cost bands and sector benchmarks

**Margin extremes** occur when an entity’s financial performance significantly deviates from **industry standards** or **sector
benchmarks**, indicating potential manipulation or financial anomalies. Large discrepancies in **margins** or **cost structures**
can signal that a company is hiding economic realities, potentially in an effort to avoid caps, **tax obligations**, or
**regulatory thresholds**.

### Key indicators of margin extremes:
- **Deviations from cost bands**: entities that report **costs** significantly higher or lower than industry **standards** or
  **benchmark ranges**, suggesting **under-reporting** or **over-reporting** of expenses to manipulate profits.
- **Discrepancies with sector benchmarks**: comparing an entity’s **operational margins** with those of similar companies
  in the same sector can reveal **outliers** that are worth further investigation (e.g., abnormally high profit margins
  due to **unrealistic pricing** or hidden fees).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any entity reporting **margin extremes** that cannot be justified or **explained** in terms of industry norms must be
  **audited** to confirm **compliance**.
- **Non-compliant** entities with significant margin deviations will need to **justify** the discrepancies or face corrective
  action.

---

## B00.CH10.P04.S02 — Fee stacks: multiple layers of fees on the same function (cascade patterns)

**Fee stacking** involves the practice of charging **multiple layers of fees** for the same service or function, often obscuring
the true cost of a transaction or arrangement. This practice can create **cascading** financial flows that extract value
unnecessarily and can be used to **mask** **benefit flows** or to manipulate how **profit** is attributed.

### Key indicators of fee stacking:
- **Multiple layers of fees**: charging fees at multiple levels for essentially the same **transactional function** (e.g.,
  management fees, transaction fees, administrative fees) that are not justified by distinct services or functions.
- **Cascade patterns**: the process where **one fee** leads to the imposition of additional fees within a chain, often
  without clear value added at each layer.
  
Fee stacking is typically a **red flag** for hidden **value extraction** or a method of bypassing regulations like **caps** or
**income reporting thresholds**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Fee stacking** must be **transparent**, with all fees clearly explained and justified as **necessary** for the service provided.
- **Non-compliant** entities using **fee stacks** to artificially inflate costs or obscure benefit flows will be **reclassified**
  and subject to **sanctions**.

## B00.CH10.P04.S03 — Concentration/lock-in metrics: switching costs, churn friction, API dependencies

**Concentration** and **lock-in metrics** are used to evaluate how entities use **switching costs**, **churn friction**, and
**platform dependencies** (e.g., API access) to maintain **market dominance** and prevent **competition**. These practices
can indicate **anti-competitive behavior** or **control mechanisms** that block access to public goods or create a barrier to
entry for new market participants.

### Key indicators of concentration/lock-in:
- **Switching costs**: **high costs** for consumers or users to **switch** to a competitor, which forces them to stay
  with one provider (e.g., penalties for early contract termination or high exit fees).
- **Churn friction**: barriers that prevent users from **leaving** a service or platform, such as **contractual obligations**,
  **complexity** in transferring data, or **restrictive terms**.
- **API dependencies**: when an entity controls access to **critical infrastructure** (e.g., APIs, data storage) and
  creates **barriers** to competition by making it difficult for competitors to interact with the platform or service.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Lock-in strategies** that are used to **prevent competition** or to restrict user choice must be **disclosed** and reviewed
  for **compliance**.
- Any **unjustified** lock-in mechanisms will be considered **non-compliant** and must be corrected.

## B00.CH10.P04.S04 — Flow-graph anomalies: circular flows, round-tripping, mismatch between P output and cashflows

**Flow-graph anomalies** occur when financial flows **do not align** with expected or legitimate outcomes, such as in the case
of **circular flows**, **round-tripping**, or a **mismatch** between the reported **output** of a function (e.g., production)
and the corresponding **cashflows**.

### Key indicators of flow-graph anomalies:
- **Circular flows**: financial transactions that flow from one entity to another and **back again** in a cycle, often to
  disguise the **real flow of value** (e.g., payments made to one entity, only to be returned through a different channel).
- **Round-tripping**: the process of **moving funds** between related entities to create the illusion of legitimate
  transactions, often to **avoid taxes** or to **inflate profits**.
- **Mismatch between P output and cashflows**: when the reported **production output (P)** does not align with the actual
  **cashflow** generated by the entity, suggesting that the financial flows are being **manipulated** or concealed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **flow-graph anomalies** must be flagged, investigated, and corrected. If **circular flows** or **round-tripping** are
  detected, they will be considered **non-compliant**.
- Entities must ensure that their **cashflows** and **outputs** are fully **aligned**, with no **discrepancies** or deceptive
  financial practices.
