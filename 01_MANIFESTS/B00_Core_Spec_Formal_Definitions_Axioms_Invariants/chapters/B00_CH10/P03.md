# B00.CH10.P03 — Reclassification principle (substance over form)

The **reclassification principle** asserts that **substance must always prevail over form** when assessing ownership,
control, or benefit within the OBA framework. This principle ensures that entities cannot **manipulate legal structures** to
circumvent regulatory thresholds or evade compliance by using **non-substantive legal forms**. If the **economic effect** is
the same, the **substance** of the transaction or structure should determine the **classification**. This ensures that
**non-extractive practices** are maintained and that **ownership and control** align with the true **economic impact**.

---

## B00.CH10.P03.S01 — Functional equivalence: same economic effect → same classification

**Functional equivalence** means that if two different legal or financial structures have the **same economic effect**, they
must be classified **the same way**, regardless of their **legal form**. This principle ensures that entities cannot manipulate
the legal structure of ownership, control, or benefit to evade regulatory rules or exploit loopholes.

### Key principles:
- **Economic substance over legal form**: regardless of whether an asset or arrangement is legally structured as a **lease**,
  **licence**, or **sale**, it should be classified based on the **economic effect** it has (e.g., if the arrangement functions
  as a transfer of ownership, it must be classified as such).
- **No loopholes**: entities cannot **relabel** transactions or arrangements to achieve a more favorable tax or regulatory
  treatment without reflecting the **true economic function** of the arrangement.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **If two arrangements** have the **same economic effect**, they must be classified in the **same manner**.
- Any attempt to **mislabel** or **reclassify** economic activities to avoid OBA compliance will result in **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH10.P03.S02 — Default conservatism: under uncertainty, classify towards E / include in cap base

The **default conservatism** rule stipulates that when there is **uncertainty** about how to classify an arrangement or asset,
the **conservative approach** is to classify it in a way that **protects the integrity** of the OBA framework. Specifically,
uncertain arrangements should be classified **towards E (extractive)** or **included in the cap base** to prevent **leakage**
or **avoidance**.

### Key principles:
- **Classify towards E**: if the classification of a flow or asset is unclear, **assume it is extractive (E)**, as this ensures that
  the **burden** falls on the **entity** to prove it is non-extractive.
- **Include in cap base**: if there is uncertainty about whether an asset or income falls under a **cap**, it should be
  included in the **cap base** to avoid the possibility of exceeding **wealth or income limits**.
- **Precautionary principle**: when in doubt, classification should lean towards more **restrictive**, **conservative**, or
  **compliant approaches**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- If there is **uncertainty**, classify the asset or flow **conservatively**, either as **extractive** or within the **cap base**.
- **Failure to follow the default conservatism rule** will result in **non-compliance** and the need for immediate correction.

---

## B00.CH10.P03.S03 — Anti-fragmentation: splitting flows/entities without function is recombined

**Anti-fragmentation** ensures that ownership, control, and benefit are not **artificially fragmented** across multiple entities
or financial flows without legitimate economic purpose. When **flows** or **entities** are split solely to evade regulation or
reduce transparency, they should be **recombined** and treated as a whole for regulatory purposes.

### Key principles:
- **Fragmentation for evasion**: when ownership, control, or benefit is **divided** into separate, smaller pieces (e.g., creating
  multiple entities to avoid caps or thresholds), it must be **recombined** for the purpose of **compliance**.
- **Economic function**: the splitting of **entities** or **flows** should be motivated by genuine **economic function** (e.g.,
  business needs, strategic reasons) and not as a tactic to **circumvent** OBA rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Entities** or **flows** that have been split for **evasion purposes** must be **recombined** and treated as a single
  **economic entity**.
- **Failure to recombine fragmented flows** or entities for compliance purposes results in **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH10.P03.S04 — Anti-opaque: black-box structures lose privileges and are treated as non-conform

**Anti-opaque** ensures that any **ownership** or **control structure** that is not fully transparent or is maintained as a
**"black-box"** loses its **privileges** and is treated as **non-compliant**. These structures are often used to **conceal
true ownership** or to **circumvent** regulatory oversight, undermining the transparency requirements of OBA.

### Key principles:
- **Black-box structures**: entities that are intentionally made **opaque**, with ownership or control that is difficult to
  trace or verify, typically to evade compliance.
- **Loss of privileges**: any **opaque structure** loses its **entitlements** or **privileges** under OBA, including access to
  certain financial flows or benefits.
- **Non-conform status**: any entity with a **black-box structure** is immediately **treated as non-compliant** and must
  be rectified to regain compliance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **opaque structure** or **black-box arrangement** that hides true **ownership** or **control** is **non-compliant**
  and must be restructured to ensure full **transparency**.
- **Failure to disclose** or clarify opaque structures will result in **non-compliance** and immediate **sanctions**.
