# B00.CH10.P02 — Canonical shift taxonomy (categories)

This paragraph provides a **taxonomy of shifts** used to **circumvent** or **evade** OBA principles. These shifts can occur
across various domains—**legal**, **financial**, **technical**, and **international**—and are designed to manipulate
ownership, control, or benefit relationships in ways that are **non-compliant** with the core objectives of the OBA framework.
The taxonomy categorizes these shifts into distinct types to ensure clear identification and provide guidelines for counter-rules
to prevent them.

---

## B00.CH10.P02.S01 — Legal shifts: relabelling to services/licences/consultancy/franchise

**Legal shifts** involve the **reclassification** or **relabeling** of assets or income in ways that **disguise the true nature** 
of the transaction. This is often done by changing the form of an agreement or arrangement, such as relabeling a **sale** as a
**licence**, **consultancy**, **service agreement**, or **franchise** model, which can obscure true control, benefit, or
ownership.

### Key examples of legal shifts:
- **Relabelling sales** as **licensing arrangements**: instead of selling assets outright, the **ownership** is represented
  by a **licence**, which can be used to avoid taxes or regulatory oversight.
- **Consultancy or management agreements**: **reclassification** of profit distribution as **fees** or **consultancy payments**
  instead of **dividends** or **wages**.
- **Franchise models**: structures designed to **circumvent** ownership regulations by allowing the franchisor to maintain
  **operational control** while the franchisee operates the business.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Legal shifts** must be thoroughly evaluated to ensure that **ownership, control, and benefit** are not being manipulated
  through **reclassification**.
- **Failure to disclose** such shifts or **misuse of these legal structures** results in **non-compliance** and must be corrected.

---

## B00.CH10.P02.S02 — Financial shifts: securitisation, fee stacks, synthetic exposures, shadow banking

**Financial shifts** involve the manipulation of financial instruments to **obscure the true ownership** or **benefit** of
assets. These structures often exploit the flexibility of **financial products** to achieve the same economic effect while
evading regulatory oversight.

### Key examples of financial shifts:
- **Securitisation**: converting **assets** (e.g., loans or receivables) into **securities** that can be traded, often allowing
  parties to **retain control** while shifting the **economic risk** to others.
- **Fee stacks**: layering multiple **fees** on a single transaction or service, often to obscure the true **cost** or **benefit**
  and extract excessive value.
- **Synthetic exposures**: financial products such as **total return swaps (TRS)**, **derivatives**, or **options** that create
  **exposure to assets** without actual ownership, enabling **avoidance of caps** or **benefit distribution rules**.
- **Shadow banking**: **off-balance-sheet entities** or **unregulated financial institutions** that conduct traditional banking
  activities, such as lending and borrowing, without adhering to the same regulatory standards.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any **financial shift** that hides or distorts **ownership**, **control**, or **benefit** must be **disclosed and rectified**.
- **Failure to track** or **evaluate** financial shifts results in **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH10.P02.S03 — Technical shifts: bundling, dark patterns, API locks, data extraction as toll

**Technical shifts** involve the manipulation of **technology platforms**, **data flows**, and **digital services** to
**bypass regulatory constraints** or to create **extractive practices** that are hidden behind opaque structures.

### Key examples of technical shifts:
- **Bundling**: the practice of **bundling multiple services** or products together in a way that forces consumers to pay for
  unwanted features or services, thereby increasing **fees** or **charges** that would not be acceptable if charged separately.
- **Dark patterns**: the use of **deceptive design** practices in **digital platforms** to **extract value** from users or customers,
  such as hiding fees or making it difficult to cancel subscriptions.
- **API locks**: creating **barriers** in a platform’s **API** (application programming interface) that prevent users from
  accessing data or services or that make switching to other platforms difficult.
- **Data extraction as toll**: using **data** or **user information** as a form of **payment**, such as **selling user data**,
  charging for **access to personal information**, or using data as a means to lock users into a platform.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Technical shifts** that use **bundling**, **dark patterns**, **API locks**, or **data extraction** to **mask the true
  costs** or **restrict access** must be **audited** and **corrected** to ensure compliance with OBA principles.
- Any **deceptive practices** that limit **consumer choice** or conceal **fees** are considered **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH10.P02.S04 — International shifts: transfer pricing, IP boxes, re-domiciliation, treaty shopping

**International shifts** exploit the differences between **national tax laws**, **jurisdictions**, and **cross-border regulations** to **evade taxes**, **circumvent ownership caps**, or **avoid OBA compliance**. These shifts often involve structuring transactions or entities in jurisdictions that have **weaker regulations** or **more favorable tax conditions**.

### Key examples of international shifts:
- **Transfer pricing**: the practice of manipulating **prices** for **goods, services**, or **intangibles** transferred between
  **related parties** in different countries, in order to shift profits to jurisdictions with lower tax rates.
- **IP boxes**: setting up **intellectual property (IP)** holdings in jurisdictions with **preferential tax treatment** for income
  generated by IP assets.
- **Re-domiciliation**: shifting the **legal domicile** of a company to a jurisdiction with **more favorable regulations** or
  **tax rates**, without changing the actual location of operations or management.
- **Treaty shopping**: structuring transactions to **exploit tax treaties** between jurisdictions, allowing entities to avoid
  taxes or gain access to **more favorable terms**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **International shifts** that involve **tax evasion**, **jurisdictional manipulation**, or **treaty shopping** must be
  thoroughly **disclosed** and **rectified** to ensure compliance with OBA.
- Any **cross-border avoidance tactics** that result in **misalignment** with OBA principles are **non-compliant** and must be
  restructured.
