# B00.CH10.P11 — Monitoring and early-warning indicators (order hygiene)

This paragraph outlines the **monitoring** and **early-warning indicators** for **order hygiene** in the OBA framework. 
**Monitoring** ensures that ownership, control, and benefit structures remain **transparent** and in compliance with the
principles of **OBA**. **Early-warning indicators** are used to detect potential violations or **distortions** in ownership,
control, benefit allocation, or financial flows that may signal **regulatory evasion** or **undue influence**. The goal is
to ensure that potential issues are identified and addressed **before** they undermine the effectiveness of the OBA framework.

---

## B00.CH10.P11.S01 — E-regrowth indicators: rising fee stacks, take rates, essential price indices

**E-regrowth** refers to the tendency of **extractive practices** (E) to resurface through **increased fees**, **high take rates**,
or **inflation in the prices of essential goods or services**. These indicators help to monitor whether entities or platforms
are engaging in **extractive behavior** after regulatory caps or limits have been imposed.

### Key indicators of E-regrowth:
- **Rising fee stacks**: an increase in **multiple fees** charged on the same service or product, suggesting that an entity may
  be **extracting more value** than necessary and potentially **circumventing OBA rules**.
- **Take rates**: an increase in the **percentage** taken by platforms or intermediaries, especially on **essential services**
  or products (e.g., platform take rates on transactions), which can indicate a shift towards **extractive behavior**.
- **Essential price indices**: rising prices for **essential goods** or services (e.g., housing, energy, healthcare), indicating
  **inflationary practices** or **price-gouging** that undermine **access** and **fairness**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Rising fee stacks** or **increased take rates** must be carefully monitored to prevent **unjustified** extraction.
- If **price inflation** for essential goods or services occurs due to **extractive practices**, corrective actions must be
  taken to ensure compliance with OBA’s **non-extractive** principles.

## B00.CH10.P11.S02 — Cap erosion indicators: growth of offshore holdings, derivatives exposures, deferral

**Cap erosion** occurs when **wealth** (W) or **income** (I) caps are bypassed or eroded through **offshore holdings**,
**derivative exposures**, or the use of **deferral tactics**. These practices allow entities to avoid or reduce their
**regulatory obligations** by shifting assets, income, or risk outside of the regulated system.

### Key indicators of cap erosion:
- **Growth of offshore holdings**: a significant **increase** in assets held in jurisdictions with **lower taxes** or
  **weaker regulations**, suggesting that entities are **shifting wealth** out of regulated systems to avoid caps.
- **Derivatives exposures**: the use of **derivative instruments** (e.g., swaps, futures, options) to **gain exposure** to assets
  or income while avoiding ownership caps or financial thresholds.
- **Deferral tactics**: shifting income or profits into future periods to **avoid exceeding** income or wealth caps in the current
  period.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Entities must **disclose offshore holdings** and **derivatives exposures** to ensure compliance with wealth and income caps.
- **Deferral tactics** used to avoid caps must be closely monitored, and any **misuse of deferral strategies** will be treated as
  **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH10.P11.S03 — Commons erosion indicators: vendor lock-in, maintenance backlog, declining access

**Commons erosion** occurs when **public goods** or **commons resources** (e.g., healthcare, education, infrastructure)
become **inaccessible** or **inequitable** due to **vendor lock-in**, **maintenance backlogs**, or other practices that limit
access and reduce the **quality** of services provided. These indicators highlight potential risks to **public goods** and
identify where regulatory attention is needed to ensure that **commons resources** remain accessible and equitable.

### Key indicators of commons erosion:
- **Vendor lock-in**: **exclusive contracts** or **long-term agreements** that restrict competition and force users to remain
  dependent on a single provider, thereby limiting choice and access to **essential services**.
- **Maintenance backlog**: **delays** or **failures** in maintaining critical infrastructure or **public services**, leading to a
  deterioration in service quality or capacity.
- **Declining access**: **barriers to access** or a reduction in the **availability** of essential services, particularly when the
  **public option** is restricted or replaced by private monopolies.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Commons erosion** practices must be **monitored**, and any **reduction in access** or **quality degradation** must be
  addressed.
- Entities engaging in **vendor lock-in**, **maintenance neglect**, or restricting access to commons resources must be subject
  to **sanctions** and **remedial actions**.

## B00.CH10.P11.S04 — ΔM leakage indicators: asset inflation, collateral dominance, shadow credit growth

**ΔM leakage** occurs when **monetary space (ΔM)** is used in ways that undermine **regulatory caps** or shift risks and
inflationary pressure into **shadow banking** or **off-balance-sheet activities**. These **leakage indicators** help to detect
when **credit creation**, **collateral dominance**, or other financial mechanisms are distorting the intended effects of
monetary policies and public goods frameworks.

### Key indicators of ΔM leakage:
- **Asset inflation**: a rapid **increase in asset prices** that is not reflective of actual **economic growth**, leading to
  potential **asset bubbles** and **misaligned wealth**.
- **Collateral dominance**: **over-reliance on collateral** for credit creation, which can inflate asset values and distort
  monetary policies or regulatory frameworks.
- **Shadow credit growth**: the growth of **unregulated credit channels** (e.g., shadow banks, off-balance-sheet financing)
  that allow credit to expand outside the **formal financial system**, undermining **monetary control**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **ΔM leakage** must be monitored for signs of **asset inflation**, **collateral dominance**, or **shadow credit** that could
  disrupt **economic stability** or public goods provision.
- **Failure to curb ΔM leakage** through proper oversight will result in **non-compliance**, and corrective actions must be
  taken immediately.
