# B00.CH13.P12 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph defines the principal failure modes that destroy canon integrity and the **hard-stop responses** required to prevent
semantic erosion. When integrity is compromised, OBA defaults to fail-closed: canon status and dependent compatibility claims are
suspended until verifiable integrity and registration are restored.

---

## B00.CH13.P12.S01 — Document sprawl: multiple “near-canons” → prohibited; consolidation required

Document sprawl becomes a failure mode when multiple “almost canonical” artifacts compete for authority and implementers select the
most convenient interpretation.

### Sprawl indicators (non-exhaustive)
- Multiple overlapping documents claim normative force without a single registered release identity.
- Sector/jurisdiction annexes introduce definitional content without canon anchoring.
- “Guides”, “playbooks”, or “FAQs” become de facto sources of meaning.
- Different repositories or branches contain divergent “current” texts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Near-canons are prohibited. Any normative claim must reference a registered canon release.
- Sprawl must be resolved by consolidation:
  - deprecate or demote near-canons to commentary, or
  - integrate content into canon via governed release, or
  - declare and register forks explicitly.
- Until consolidation is complete, disputed meanings MUST be treated as uncertain; compatibility claims depending on them fail closed.

---

## B00.CH13.P12.S02 — Drift via silent edits: no mutable publications; releases only

Silent edits are the direct route to semantic drift and audit non-reproducibility.

### Silent edit patterns (non-exhaustive)
- Editing a published canonical artifact in-place without new version/release ID.
- Changing tests, parameters, or definitions without changelog/diff.
- Updating “current” documents while leaving the version identifier unchanged.
- Rewriting history by deleting or replacing old artifacts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Canon publications MUST be immutable; changes occur only through new releases.
- Detection of silent edits triggers an integrity incident:
  - affected releases are suspended in the registry,
  - compatibility claims pinned to compromised artifacts are treated as non-verifiable and therefore non-conform (fail-closed),
  - remediation requires re-publication with verified hashes/signatures and a forensic report.

---

## B00.CH13.P12.S03 — Governance capture: stacking exceptions via major changes without fork label

Capture can occur through procedure: repeated “major changes” that gradually hollow invariants while keeping the OBA label.

### Capture indicators (non-exhaustive)
- Repeated major releases that add carve-outs or discretionary authority benefiting specific actors.
- Major changes justified as “implementation realism” that effectively suspend core constraints.
- Systematic ratification bias: dissent ignored, reviews become perfunctory, audits become attestation-only.
- Fork-risk changes shipped without explicit fork labeling and without invariant delta disclosure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any major release that weakens invariants MUST explicitly disclose invariant deltas and fork risk; failure to do so is an integrity breach.
- Exception stacking through major changes without explicit fork labeling triggers governance hard stops:
  - external audit of the change process,
  - suspension of release authority where warranted,
  - and fail-closed treatment of dependent compatibility claims until legitimacy is restored.

---

## B00.CH13.P12.S04 — Fail-closed: loss of integrity/registration suspends canon status

When canon integrity or registration cannot be verified, canon status is suspended. Without canon, compatibility claims cannot exist.

### Integrity/registration failure triggers (non-exhaustive)
- Canon registry unavailable or entries unverifiable.
- Hash/signature verification fails; signing keys compromised.
- Mirrors diverge; archived artifacts not retrievable.
- Provenance records missing or inconsistent with release identity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If integrity or registration is lost, the affected canon release MUST be treated as non-canonical until verification is restored.
- Dependent compatibility claims MUST be treated as **non-conform** for the affected scope (fail-closed).
- Restoration requires: verified registry entries, retrievable immutable artifacts, valid integrity proofs, and (where applicable)
  independent incident audit with published findings.
