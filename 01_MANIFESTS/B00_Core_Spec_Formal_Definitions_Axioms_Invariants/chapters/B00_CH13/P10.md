# B00.CH13.P10 — Incident and emergency patch regime

This paragraph defines the incident and emergency patch regime for OBA canon and related standards. Emergencies require speed, but
speed is a drift risk. Therefore OBA allows emergency patches only under strict constraints: explicit triggers, limited scope,
post-hoc audit, and automatic sunset. Where integrity cannot be verified, OBA fails closed and curtails decision space.

---

## B00.CH13.P10.S01 — Incident triggers: exploit/avoidance discovered, measurement error, security breach, drift event

An incident is any event that compromises canon integrity, enforcement correctness, or measurement validity.

### Incident triggers (non-exhaustive)
- **Exploit / avoidance discovered**: a newly discovered tactic that defeats invariants, caps, or prohibited-channel rules.
- **Measurement error**: material error in a canonical method, template, or published measurement standard that affects outcomes.
- **Security breach**: compromised signing keys, tampered releases, supply-chain compromise, or registry/mirror integrity divergence.
- **Drift event**: evidence of semantic drift via shadow canons, silent edits, or misclassified releases.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- When an incident trigger is confirmed, an incident record MUST be opened and registered with scope, affected artifacts, and interim risk posture.
- Until mitigations are verified, affected compatibility claims MUST be treated as **uncertain** and may be suspended (fail-closed) for impacted scope.

---

## B00.CH13.P10.S02 — Emergency patch procedure: rapid patch with post-hoc audit and sunset

Emergency patches are allowed only to restore integrity or close urgent exploit surfaces, not to introduce discretionary policy change.

### Emergency patch procedure (minimum)
1. **Declare incident**: publish incident ID, affected versions, and interim guidance.
2. **Define minimal patch scope**: smallest change that mitigates harm; explicitly state what is *not* changed.
3. **Issue emergency release**: versioned artifact with release ID (date/hash/signature), changelog, and diff.
4. **Deploy interim enforcement posture**: where needed, curtail decision space or apply conservatism defaults.
5. **Post-hoc audit**: independent audit of the patch and its effects within a defined time window.
6. **Sunset**: automatic expiry date for emergency regime or emergency rules, unless ratified through normal governance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Emergency changes MUST be time-bounded (sunset) and must not bypass review indefinitely.
- If post-hoc audit fails or is not performed, the emergency release loses canonical standing for compatibility grounding and triggers rollback/re-release.

---

## B00.CH13.P10.S03 — Rollback: criteria, procedure, communication and restoration of compatibility status

Rollback is the corrective mechanism when an emergency patch is defective, over-scoped, or integrity-compromised.

### Rollback criteria (non-exhaustive)
- Patch introduces semantic change beyond declared scope (misclassified as patch).
- Patch breaks reproducibility, lineage, or compatibility tests in unanticipated ways.
- Integrity proofs fail (signature mismatch, hash mismatch, mirror divergence).
- Post-hoc audit identifies material defects or capture/abuse concerns.

### Rollback procedure (minimum)
- Publish rollback decision with incident linkage and scope.
- Release rollback artifact (versioned) restoring last known good state, or publish superseding corrected release.
- Update registries and compatibility labels; issue public rectification for affected claims.
- Define restoration conditions: what evidence/tests are required before compatibility status can be reinstated.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- During rollback/instability, status claims MUST fail closed for affected scope (no privilege granted).
- Restoration of compatibility requires re-verification: reproducibility, lineage, audit outcomes, and updated registries.

---

## B00.CH13.P10.S04 — Ban on emergency-patch abuse: crisis as cover for semantic erosion

Emergency regimes are attractive capture vectors. OBA explicitly forbids using emergencies to smuggle semantic changes or to stack exceptions.

### Prohibited patterns (non-exhaustive)
- Introducing new policy meaning under “security fix” framing.
- Adding broad discretionary exemptions or weakening invariants “temporarily” without sunset or ratification.
- Repeated emergency patches that function as a parallel governance channel.
- Withholding diffs/mappings under urgency pretext when publication is feasible.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Emergency patches must be minimal, published, and auditable; any semantic change beyond mitigation scope must be processed as a normal
  major/minor release (or declared as a fork where applicable).
- Evidence of emergency-patch abuse triggers governance hard-stop responses: suspension of emergency authority, external audit, and
  fail-closed treatment of dependent compatibility claims until integrity is restored.
