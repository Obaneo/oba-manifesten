# B00.CH13.P09 — Governance of changes (procedure and roles)

This paragraph defines the governance system that controls canon changes. Canon evolution is a high-integrity process: roles are
separated, reviews are criteria-bound, and major changes require stronger legitimacy thresholds. Governance itself must be auditable
to prevent capture, exception stacking, and drift through procedure abuse.

---

## B00.CH13.P09.S01 — Roles: editor, reviewer, auditor, ratifier, public registrar

Canon change governance requires separated roles with clear mandates and accountability.

### Roles (minimum)
- **Editor**: drafts changes, maintains internal consistency, prepares diffs/mappings/impact analysis.
- **Reviewer**: evaluates proposed changes against review criteria; can request revisions; documents objections.
- **Auditor**: verifies procedural correctness, evidence integrity, and test artifacts; checks for drift vectors and hidden breaks.
- **Ratifier**: approves release according to decision thresholds; accountable for major-change legitimacy.
- **Public registrar**: publishes releases, registries, hashes/signatures, and ensures long-term accessibility and citability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No single actor may occupy all roles for a release that affects compatibility (role separation).
- Each release MUST record role assignments and signatures/approvals in the changelog and registry.
- If role separation cannot be established, the release MUST be treated as procedurally invalid (fail-closed) until corrected.

---

## B00.CH13.P09.S02 — Review criteria: invariant preservation, auditability, anti-avoidance, privacy minimalism

Reviews must be criteria-bound to prevent discretionary capture.

### Mandatory review criteria (minimum)
- **Invariant preservation**: confirm whether invariants are preserved (patch/minor) or explicitly changed (major) with declared deltas.
- **Auditability**: ensure lineage, reproducibility, method registration, and verification instructions remain intact.
- **Anti-avoidance**: assess whether the change introduces new avoidance surfaces or weakens detection/reclassification regimes.
- **Privacy minimalism**: ensure changes do not create unjustified data expansion; require justification mapping for any new data needs.
- **Compatibility implications**: determine effects on label criteria, tests, registries, and migration requirements.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Reviews MUST explicitly record findings against each criterion.
- A release that adds new data requirements without privacy-minimalism justification MUST be rejected or reworked.
- A release that weakens invariants without being classified as major MUST be rejected (misclassification integrity breach).

---

## B00.CH13.P09.S03 — Decision procedure: quorum, supermajority for major, dispute protocol

Canon changes require a decision procedure that scales legitimacy with semantic impact.

### Decision rules (minimum)
- **Quorum**: minimum number of qualified ratifiers/reviewers required for any release.
- **Supermajority for major**: major changes require stronger approval thresholds than minor/patch releases.
- **Recorded dissent**: minority objections must be recorded; unresolved objections must be explicitly addressed or the change deferred.
- **Dispute protocol integration**: disputes about classification, semantics, or procedure are resolved through a time-bounded dispute process.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- No release is canonical until quorum and required thresholds are met and recorded in the registry.
- If a major change is approved without supermajority (or equivalent legitimacy rule), the release MUST be treated as non-canonical
  for compatibility purposes until properly ratified (fail-closed).
- Where disputes remain unresolved, affected sections may be withheld or released as explicitly scoped drafts that cannot ground compatibility claims.

---

## B00.CH13.P09.S04 — Audit the guardians: external assessment of change processes

Governance itself must be audited to prevent procedural capture and semantic erosion.

### External assessment requirements (minimum)
- **Process audit**: verify that releases followed role separation, review criteria, decision thresholds, and publication integrity.
- **Capture checks**: detect exception stacking, conflicts of interest, and systematic approval bias.
- **Transparency checks**: verify completeness of registries, diffs, mappings, and impact analyses.
- **Incident checks**: assess response quality for integrity breaches (silent edits, compromised keys, divergent mirrors).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Governance processes MUST undergo periodic external assessment; results (or justified redactions) must be published.
- If governance audit fails or capture is detected, canon integrity is compromised and may require:
  - suspension of release authority,
  - re-ratification of recent releases,
  - and fail-closed treatment of compatibility claims dependent on compromised governance until integrity is restored.
