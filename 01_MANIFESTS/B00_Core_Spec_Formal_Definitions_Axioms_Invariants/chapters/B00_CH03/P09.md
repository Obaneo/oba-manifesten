# B00.CH03.P09 — Relation to R/B: how E translates into L and remainder space

This paragraph specifies the canonical linkage between **P/E/C classification** and the **R/B evaluation frame**
(remainder space and buffer). The linkage is causal and structural: P/E/C composition determines how burdens
accumulate into **L (claim layers)** and how much **R (remainder)** and **B (buffer)** remain available for
agency, resilience, and stability.

R/B is not a substitute for P/E/C. It is downstream: R/B outcomes can look “acceptable” in aggregates while the
underlying composition is extractive and unstable (e.g., temporary transfers masking rising claim layers).
Therefore, OBA-grade evaluation requires the joint reading: P/E/C at flow level + R/B at household/cohort level.

---

## B00.CH03.P09.S01 — E→L mechanism: essentials become claim layers (housing/energy/health/interest)

**E tends to become L** when extraction concentrates in **essentials** and in structurally unavoidable access
channels. The mechanism is that toll components are converted into recurring burdens, contractually enforced
claims, or unavoidable payment obligations.

Canonical E→L pathways include (non-exhaustive):

- **Housing:** rent extraction, scarcity rents, fee stacks (brokerage, admin fees), refinancing lock feeding mortgage
  spreads; unavoidable because shelter is non-optional.
- **Energy:** gatekeeper pricing, network monopoly rents, opaque tariffs, compulsory surcharges.
- **Health:** administrative toll layers, network lock-in, opaque billing, monopoly pricing of inputs/coverage.
- **Interest/finance:** refinancing lock, fee stacks, penalty pricing; extraction compounding via roll-over.

When extraction is embedded in essentials, it converts to persistent L because:
- demand is inelastic,
- exit is non-credible,
- failures cascade into broader instability (arrears, debt spirals, forced migration, health deterioration).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-critical evaluation MUST explicitly track E concentration in essentials and map it to L channels; treating
  E as “general inflation” is non-auditable.
- If essential-domain data is missing, evaluation MUST disclose the gap and fail closed on dependent claims that
  rely on the missing channel (because hidden L is the primary failure mode).

---

## B00.CH03.P09.S02 — P→R mechanism: productive + maintenance lowers vulnerability and increases buffer

**P tends to increase R (and support B)** by expanding real functional capacity and reducing vulnerability. The
mechanism is that productive and maintenance components:
- raise the effective availability/quality of essential services,
- reduce lifecycle costs and volatility (maintenance, durability, repairability),
- improve contestability and reduce dependence on chokepoints,
- stabilize income and essential access, making buffer accumulation feasible.

P matters even when nominal incomes rise: if the underlying cost structure is extractive, income gains are eaten
by L. Therefore, P must be evaluated in relation to actual reduction of burdens and volatility, not as “growth”
in abstraction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Claims that a policy/program increases R via “growth” MUST specify the P mechanism (what capacity is built or
  maintained, and how this reduces burdens or volatility).
- If P claims are made without linking to measurable burden reduction or resilience improvement, they are
  insufficient for OBA-grade evaluation.

---

## B00.CH03.P09.S03 — C→R mechanism: public option/commons reduces L structurally

**C reduces L structurally** by building or stabilizing commons capacity and by providing credible exit via
public option/access regimes. The mechanism is not redistribution after the fact; it is the reduction of toll
power at the source.

Canonical C→R pathways include (non-exhaustive):

- **Public option provision:** creates a real outside option that disciplines private tolls (housing, energy,
  health, education, mobility, digital infrastructure).
- **Open access / non-discrimination regimes:** reduce exclusion-based extraction and improve contestability.
- **Commons maintenance duties:** enforce durability/repairability and prevent degradation-as-rent.
- **Interoperability and open standards:** reduce ecosystem captivity and switching costs, compressing toll margins.

C therefore changes the feasible set: it reduces dependence and the ability of extractive nodes to convert power
into recurring claim layers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any C classification used in an R/B argument MUST specify the structural mechanism (public option, access regime,
  maintenance duty, interoperability) and its enforcement/auditability.
- If C is claimed but can be privately appropriated or converted into a toll gate without safeguards, it cannot be
  credited as structurally reducing L for OBA-grade evaluation.

## B00.CH03.P09.S04 — Distribution requirement: P/E/C evaluation at cohort level, not just macro averages

R/B and P/E/C effects are inherently **distributional**. Macro averages can hide:
- essential-burden concentration in vulnerable cohorts,
- extraction shifting to groups with low exit power,
- apparent stability driven by transfers masking rising claim layers.

Therefore, P/E/C must be evaluated at **cohort level** (deciles, household types, regions, contract exposure
profiles) and reconciled to macro aggregates without losing the distributional signal.

Canonical requirement:
- compute P/E/C composition and L/R/B outcomes for relevant cohorts;
- report dispersion and tail risk (e.g., bottom deciles), not only means;
- treat cohort-level degradation as an OBA failure even if macro aggregates are stable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-conformance claim that relies on R/B MUST include cohort-level evaluation (or justify why cohort effects
  are provably negligible under auditable assumptions).
- If cohort-level data is missing in ways that could hide extraction or burden concentration, evaluation MUST fail
  closed for conformance claims and report the coverage limitation explicitly.
