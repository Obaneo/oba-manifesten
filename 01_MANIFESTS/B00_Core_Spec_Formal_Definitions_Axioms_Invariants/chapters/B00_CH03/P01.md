# B00.CH03.P01 — Purpose, scope and normative status of P/E/C

This paragraph establishes the **purpose**, **scope**, and **normative status** of the P/E/C classification.
P/E/C is the canonical mechanism by which OBA distinguishes **production & maintenance (P)**, **extraction (E)**, and **commons contribution (C)** at the **flow level**.

The purpose is not descriptive taxonomy. It is **semantic closure** for evaluation: P/E/C must yield
classifications that are **auditable**, **reproducible**, and **contestable**, and that remain stable under
relabeling, ownership-form changes, or narrative reframing.

Normative status: P/E/C is **binding** for any document, dataset, implementation, or policy instrument that claims
OBA conformance or compatibility. Implementations may extend P/E/C only via explicitly versioned and testable
extensions; they MUST NOT redefine the canonical meaning of P, E, or C.

---

## B00.CH03.P01.S01 — P/E/C as canonical classification for all relevant flows

P/E/C is the canonical classification for all economically relevant flows that can affect order properties.
“Relevant flows” include (non-exhaustive):

- payments and transfers (wages, prices, fees, interest, rents, premiums, taxes, subsidies);
- platform and intermediary charges (take rates, commissions, access fees, ranking fees);
- financial and contractual claims (debt service, refinancing spreads, penalty clauses);
- public and commons contributions (funding, maintenance duties, access provisions);
- implicit-priced exchanges where a hidden price is present (e.g., attention, data, coercive bundling).

The unit of analysis is the **flow object** (a transfer under a contract/relationship), not the slogan,
industry label, or institutional form. A single observed payment MAY contain multiple components and must
be decomposable (P+E, and sometimes C) where required for auditability.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-critical evaluation that uses economic flows MUST classify them in P/E/C terms, at least at the level
  required to determine R/B effects, cap relevance, or ΔM channel compliance.
- Any flow used as evidence for an OBA claim MUST be classifiable without relying on intent narratives or
  non-verifiable framing.

---

## B00.CH03.P01.S02 — P/E/C is functional (effect on order), not rhetorical (label)

P/E/C is **functional**: it classifies a flow by its **order effect**, not by its stated purpose, branding,
or legal/ownership wrapper.

- **P** denotes the functional component that delivers or preserves capacity/value without a toll mechanism.
- **E** denotes the toll component: value extracted above functionally justified cost/margin due to power,
  lock-in, claim stacking, or information asymmetry.
- **C** denotes allocation to commons capacity or access/maintenance regimes that increase or stabilize ΔG.

Rhetorical labels (“service”, “innovation”, “premium”, “efficiency”, “market rate”) are non-decisive.
If a relabeling changes P/E/C classification without changing underlying power, lock-in, or claim structure,
the classification MUST remain invariant under the relabeling.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Classification MUST be invariant to naming/marketing/ownership-form changes (substance over form).
- Where interpretation would enlarge discretion or authority, classification MUST be conservative and evaluation
  MUST fail closed until the functional basis is demonstrated with auditable inputs.

---

## B00.CH03.P01.S03 — Scope of application: households, firms, state, platforms, finance

P/E/C applies across the full economic surface where order properties can be shaped:

- **Households:** wages, consumption prices, housing, energy, health, education, debt service, insurance.
- **Firms:** input costs, supply contracts, pricing rules, licensing, maintenance, interoperability constraints.
- **State:** taxation, transfers, procurement, concessions/licensing regimes, public option and commons provision.
- **Platforms:** access fees, take rates, ranking/curation control, API choke points, data leverage and bundling.
- **Finance:** interest spreads, refinancing lock-in, collateral dominance, fee stacks, roll-over pressure.

The point is to prevent jurisdictional/sectoral carve-outs that allow E to migrate to “uncounted” domains.
If a domain materially affects claim layers, lock-in, or buffer space, it is within scope.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- No domain that materially affects essentials/claim layers may be excluded from P/E/C classification by convention.
- If data constraints prevent classification in a domain, this must be recorded as an uncertainty/coverage failure;
  OBA claims cannot be made on the unclassified component.

## B00.CH03.P01.S04 — Relation to caps, R/B and ΔM (linkages and dependencies)

P/E/C is upstream of the other core evaluation interfaces:

### Link to R/B (remainder/buffer space)
- **E → L mechanism:** extraction tends to translate into burdens/claim layers (especially in essentials), shrinking
  remainder space and buffer capacity.
- **P → R mechanism:** productive and maintenance components stabilize capacity and reduce vulnerability.
- **C → R mechanism:** commons/public-option capacity reduces structural burdens and lock-in.

Therefore, R/B evaluation MUST treat P/E/C composition as a causal input, not an after-the-fact narrative.

### Link to caps (W-MAX, I-MAX)
- Persistent **E** is a primary driver of claim accumulation into W and I via rents/fee stacks/interest.
- Caps act as a brake on storage of extracted surplus; cap skimming may be classified as C only under explicitly
  defined conditions (traceability, non-reversibility, non-avoidance).

### Link to ΔM (allocation interface)
- ΔM channel rules require that monetary expansion/allocation does not feed E-driven asset inflation or claim
  amplification.
- ΔM programs must therefore be evaluated on whether they expand **P** and **C** capacity and reduce **E** channels,
  with traceability from allocation → effect.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any cap proposal or ΔM program that does not specify its interaction with P/E/C (how it reduces E or expands P/C)
  is non-conform as an OBA implementation claim.
- Any R/B evaluation that uses aggregates while ignoring P/E/C composition at cohort level is insufficient for
  OBA-grade assessment; distributional evaluation is required where order impact differs by cohort.
