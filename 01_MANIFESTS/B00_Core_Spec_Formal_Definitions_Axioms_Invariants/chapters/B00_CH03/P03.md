# B00.CH03.P03 — Definition of E: extraction (parasitic component)

This paragraph defines **E (extraction)** as the **parasitic component** of a flow: the portion of price,
terms, or claim that exceeds functional remuneration because it is enabled by **power**, **lock-in**, **claim
stacking**, or **information asymmetry**, rather than by delivered function.

E is not a moral judgement. It is a functional classification: E is present when a party can impose a toll
because the counterparty lacks a credible exit, lacks informed consent, or faces structural dependence.
Where a single payment includes both productive function and toll, the toll component MUST be classified as E
and separated from P (and sometimes C) under decomposition rules.

Normative posture is conservative: where ambiguity enlarges discretion or authority, evaluation MUST fail closed
until the extractive component is rebutted with auditable evidence.

---

## B00.CH03.P03.S01 — Toll criterion: price > functional costs through power/lock-in

A component is **E** when the effective price (including hidden price) exceeds **functional costs + bounded normal
margin** and the excess is attributable to **power** or **lock-in**, not to additional delivered function.

The toll may appear as:
- above-cost pricing sustained by market power or structural chokepoints;
- compulsory add-ons or forced bundles to access a basic function;
- discriminatory pricing enabled by dependence (e.g., “because you can’t leave”);
- artificially created scarcity or access restriction that is not functionally required.

E is evaluated on the **mechanism** that sustains the excess, not on rhetoric (“premium service”) or legality
(“contract allows it”). If the excess is sustained because exit is non-credible or alternatives are blocked, it
meets the toll criterion.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any classification of a component as non-extractive where price materially exceeds functional costs MUST provide
  an auditable rebuttal (cost basis, margin bound, and mechanism).
- If cost/margin cannot be established and the structure exhibits power/lock-in features, the disputed excess MUST
  be treated as E or left as non-conform/unknown under fail-closed discipline.

---

## B00.CH03.P03.S02 — Lock-in criterion: switching costs, exit friction, contractual lock

A component is **E** when it is sustained by **non-trivial exit friction** that makes switching or refusal
economically irrational, practically infeasible, or contractually prohibited.

Lock-in proxies include (non-exhaustive):
- high switching costs (technical migration, retraining, compatibility breakage, loss of data/history);
- contractual penalties or long-duration commitments without proportional functional justification;
- interoperability denial or proprietary standards that prevent substitution;
- ecosystem captivity (complements, identity, distribution, or tooling required to operate);
- “take-it-or-leave-it” terms in essential domains where refusal is not a real option.

Lock-in is not automatically E if it is genuinely required for function (e.g., safety constraints) and is
minimised; but any residual rent enabled by lock-in is presumptively E.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Where a flow is classified as P in the presence of significant lock-in, the implementation MUST document the
  lock-in features and justify why they are functionally required; otherwise the rent component MUST be treated as E.
- Exit frictions MUST be measurable via declared proxies (switching cost estimates, penalty schedules, portability
  constraints) and recorded as metadata where classification depends on them.

---

## B00.CH03.P03.S03 — Claim-stack criterion: interest/fee stacks/monopoly rents above function

A component is **E** when it appears as a **claim stack** that extracts surplus without adding proportional
function—especially when stacked layers compound and become structurally unavoidable.

Claim stacks include (non-exhaustive):
- interest spreads and refinancing rents enabled by collateral dominance or roll-over pressure;
- platform fee stacks (multiple intermediaries each taking a toll on the same underlying function);
- licensing and subscription stacking where access is fragmented into paywalled layers;
- monopoly rents, gatekeeper tolls, and “access fees” to essential infrastructure;
- internal transfer pricing and pass-through structures that conceal extraction across a chain.

The classification focus is on whether the stack is **functionally necessary** for delivery, or whether it is a
rent-bearing layer enabled by position/control. If multiple layers can be removed without reducing delivered
function, they are E layers.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST detect and report fee stacks/cascades where they materially affect totals (double extraction risk).
- If a chain structure prevents attribution (opacity, internal fees not disclosed), OBA claims on the affected
  component MUST fail closed or be conservatively classified as E until auditable splitting is possible.

---

## B00.CH03.P03.S04 — Information asymmetry as E-driver: deception, dark patterns, bundling

A component is **E** when surplus is extracted through **information asymmetry** that defeats informed consent or
rational comparison—even if formal consent exists.

E-driving asymmetry mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):
- deception, misdirection, omission of material terms, or obscured total cost of ownership;
- dark patterns (friction to cancel, bait-and-switch, default traps, forced continuity);
- price obfuscation and hidden fees revealed only after lock-in or sunk costs;
- coercive bundling where separation is possible but strategically denied;
- attention/data extraction where the “price” is hidden and not meaningfully contestable.

If the counterparty would not agree under full, salient information, or cannot practically evaluate alternatives
because of designed opacity, the resulting surplus is extractive.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Where information asymmetry is present, implementations MUST treat the resulting surplus as presumptively E
  unless salience and contestability are demonstrated with auditable evidence.
- Any OBA-critical evaluation MUST disclose material price/term assumptions; hidden-price domains (data/attention)
  MUST be explicitly modeled or the affected claims must fail closed.
