# B00.CH03.P02 — Definition of P: production and maintenance (productive)

This paragraph defines **P (production & maintenance)** as the productive component of a flow: the part that
delivers, sustains, or restores **real functional capacity** without relying on a **toll mechanism** rooted in
power, lock-in, or claim-layer leverage.

P is assessed **functionally**: what is delivered or preserved, at what cost structure, under what access and
interoperability conditions. P is not granted by label (“service”, “innovation”), ownership form, or intent
narrative. Where a flow mixes productive function and toll extraction, it MUST be decomposed into P + E (and
sometimes C) in later rules; this paragraph defines the P-side boundary conditions.

---

## B00.CH03.P02.S01 — Output criterion: deliverable function/value without toll mechanism

A component is **P** if it corresponds to a deliverable function or value that is actually provided (or made
available) **without** a toll mechanism as the basis of its price and conditions.

“Deliverable function/value” includes (non-exhaustive):
- provision of goods or services that satisfy a real use-function;
- maintenance/repair that preserves capacity, quality, safety, or reliability;
- operation of infrastructure required for function delivery;
- provisioning that is contestable or substitutable without punitive lock-in.

The key boundary condition is that the price and access conditions are not primarily maintained by power/lock-in.
If access is conditioned on coercive bundling, unavoidable switching costs, or structural choke points, the toll
component belongs in **E**, even if a functional service is present.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Claims of P MUST reference the deliverable function and the concrete deliverable/maintenance outcome.
- If the same deliverable could be provided under contestable conditions at materially lower total cost but is
  not, the excess component must be tested under the E criteria (toll/power/lock-in), not assumed to be P.

---

## B00.CH03.P02.S02 — Cost criterion: functional costs + normal margin (bounded)

A component qualifies as **P** only up to **functional costs** plus a **normal margin** that is explicitly
bounded under the canon’s conservatism discipline.

- **Functional costs** are the minimum cost basis required to deliver the function at the stated quality and
  service level (inputs, labor, depreciation/maintenance, compliance, risk costs that are functionally required).
- **Normal margin** is the bounded surplus compatible with contestable supply and non-extractive conditions.

Where price materially exceeds functional costs + bounded margin, the excess is presumptively **E** unless
rebutted with auditable evidence (e.g., temporary innovation rent under defined edge rules, or proven scarcity
without manipulable choke-point power).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any P classification that relies on the cost criterion MUST be auditable: cost basis, margin bound, and method
  must be reproducible.
- Where cost data is missing, classification must carry an uncertainty band; where ambiguity enlarges discretion,
  evaluation MUST fail closed on the disputed component until resolved.

---

## B00.CH03.P02.S03 — Maintenance as P: preservation of capacity/quality (ΔG stabilisation)

**Maintenance** is P when it preserves or restores **capacity/quality** over time: it stabilizes or improves ΔG
(commons or system capacity metrics) by preventing degradation, failure, or loss of function.

Maintenance-as-P includes (non-exhaustive):
- repairability and lifecycle servicing that prevent premature replacement;
- resilience work (safety, reliability, redundancy) that stabilizes critical capacity;
- upkeep of public or shared systems where sustained function is the deliverable.

Maintenance is not automatically P if it is an avoidable layer created by prior extraction (e.g., complexity,
proprietary lock-in, administrative fee stacks). If the maintenance burden is primarily caused by E drivers,
that portion must be separated and treated under E criteria.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Maintenance claims MUST specify the preserved capacity/quality dimension (what is stabilized, for whom, and
  at what service level).
- If maintenance is conditioned on proprietary choke points (parts, tooling, authorization), the toll component
  must be evaluated as potential E.

---

## B00.CH03.P02.S04 — P in networks: interoperability, open standards, non-exclusivity

In networked systems (infrastructure, platforms, ecosystems), P is constrained by **interoperability** and
**non-exclusivity** properties that determine whether the function is delivered without embedded toll power.

A component is more strongly classifiable as P when:
- interfaces and standards are open or contestable;
- switching is feasible without punitive exit friction;
- access is not conditioned on exclusive control of complements (APIs, identity, distribution, data portability);
- the provider does not hold a structural choke point that converts function delivery into a toll gate.

Where the provider uses interoperability denial, proprietary standards, or API choke points to enforce rents,
the incremental surplus generated by that control is **E**, not P—even if the baseline service remains functional.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- For networked services, P classification MUST state the interoperability/access conditions and any exclusivity
  constraints that materially affect contestability.
- If non-exclusivity or interoperability is absent, the classification must either (i) justify why this absence
  is functionally required, or (ii) treat the resulting toll component as E.
