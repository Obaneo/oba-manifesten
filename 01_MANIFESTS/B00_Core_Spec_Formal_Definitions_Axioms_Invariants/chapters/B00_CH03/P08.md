# B00.CH03.P08 — Edge cases and border cases (canonical decision rules)

This paragraph defines canonical decision rules for **edge cases** and **border cases** where P/E/C classification
is commonly gamed or becomes ambiguous. The aim is not perfect taxonomy; it is **semantic closure under
adversarial conditions**.

General rule: classification is **functional** and **mechanism-based**. Where ambiguity enlarges discretion or
authority, evaluation MUST be conservative and MUST fail closed unless rebutted with auditable evidence.

---

## B00.CH03.P08.S01 — Innovation rent vs toll: when it is temporarily P and when it becomes E

Innovation can justify a **temporary surplus** above functional costs (innovation rent). However, surplus becomes
**E** when it is sustained primarily by power/lock-in rather than by ongoing delivered function or contestable
value creation.

### Canonical decision rule

A surplus component may be treated as **temporarily P** only if ALL of the following are satisfied:

1) **Novel deliverable:** there is a materially new or improved function delivered (not just repackaging).
2) **Contestability trajectory:** credible pathways exist for entry, imitation, standardization, or expiry such that
   the surplus is not structurally permanent.
3) **Transparency:** the basis for surplus (costs, R&D recovery logic, time window) is auditable.
4) **Non-coercive access:** access is not conditioned on unrelated bundling, captive switching costs, or forced
   ecosystem tie-ins beyond what is functionally required.
5) **Time-boundedness:** the claim includes an explicit sunset condition or measurable triggers for reclassification.

A surplus component MUST be treated as **E** when any of the following hold:

- surplus persists without continuing functional differentiation,
- surplus is maintained via lock-in, exclusivity, ecosystem captivity, or chokepoints,
- entry barriers are artificial (legal/technical foreclosure) and not functionally required,
- “innovation” is primarily rhetorical and not demonstrable as delivered function.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any claim of “temporary innovation rent” MUST declare: the measurable functional novelty, the time window or
  triggers, and the contestability assumptions. Otherwise the surplus component is E by default.
- If the innovation basis cannot be audited, evaluation MUST fail closed on the surplus component.

## B00.CH03.P08.S02 — IP/licences: boundary between functional remuneration and lock-in toll

IP and licensing can remunerate real productive work, but also function as lock-in toll gates.

### Canonical boundary test

Treat a licence/IP charge as **P** only to the extent that it satisfies:

- it is proportional to delivered functional value and/or auditable cost recovery,
- it is not used to block interoperability, repairability, portability, or substitution without functional necessity,
- it does not require unnecessary tying/bundling to access baseline function,
- the terms permit contestability (standards, reasonable FRAND-like access where appropriate, time-bounded exclusivity).

Treat the licence/IP charge as **E** to the extent that it exhibits:

- **interoperability denial** or API/tooling gating to enforce rents,
- **repair restriction** or aftermarket capture (parts, diagnostics, authorization),
- **portfolio tolling** (stacked licences unrelated to incremental function),
- **contractual lock-in** (termination penalties, perpetual clauses, non-portable entitlements).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any IP/licence classification MUST specify the relevant restrictions (interoperability, portability, repairability,
  exclusivity) as metadata; absent this, claims cannot be audited and must fail closed.
- Where IP creates a choke point over an essential, the toll component is presumptive E unless disciplined by an
  access regime (public option, regulation, or enforceable openness).

## B00.CH03.P08.S03 — Health/education: P-maintenance versus administrative E-layers

Health and education include large **P-maintenance** components (preserving human capacity and capability), but
are also common carriers of administrative fee stacks and extractive intermediaries.

### Canonical decomposition rule

Classify as **P**:
- direct care/teaching delivery,
- essential support and maintenance functions required for delivery quality and safety,
- capacity-building investments that measurably improve outcomes or service reliability.

Classify as **E**:
- administrative layers whose primary function is billing complexity, gatekeeping access, or claim stacking,
- intermediary rents sustained by network lock-in, opaque pricing, or compulsory bundling,
- financial extraction embedded in access (unavoidable fees, interest-like structures tied to essentials),
- predatory productization of compliance or credential gating beyond function.

Border decision rule:
- if an administrative activity is necessary and minimal for safe delivery, it may be P;
- if the layer scales primarily with billing/claims complexity rather than care/education outcomes, the incremental
  surplus is E.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST decompose totals into delivery vs administrative layers where material; treating entire
  sectors as “public good” without splitting is non-auditable.
- Where price opacity or billing complexity blocks splitting, evaluation MUST fail closed (or treat disputed
  administrative surplus as E) and disclose coverage limits.

## B00.CH03.P08.S04 — Data/advertising: attention extraction, manipulation and hidden price (E)

Data/advertising systems often present as “free” or “discounted” services while extracting value via **hidden
prices**: attention capture, behavioral manipulation, data harvesting, and competitive foreclosure.

### Canonical rule: hidden price implies E unless rebutted

Treat as **E** any surplus generated by:
- attention extraction that is not a voluntary, informed, and contestable exchange,
- manipulative design (dark patterns, compulsive engagement engineering) that defeats autonomy,
- data capture used to entrench power (data lead → foreclosure → sustained tolls elsewhere),
- cross-subsidized pricing that hides the real cost and prevents transparent comparison.

A data/advertising component can be treated as non-extractive only if:
- the hidden price is made explicit (salient terms, real choice),
- users have credible opt-out/portability without punitive degradation,
- targeting/collection is bounded to function and does not create structural foreclosure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-critical evaluation involving “free” platform services MUST model the hidden price (attention/data) or
  explicitly mark the affected component as non-auditable and fail closed for conformance claims.
- Where manipulation or non-salient consent mechanisms exist, the resulting surplus is presumptively E unless
  robust contestability and salience are demonstrated.
