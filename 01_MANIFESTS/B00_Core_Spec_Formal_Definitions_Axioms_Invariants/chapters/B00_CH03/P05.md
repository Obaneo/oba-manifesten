# B00.CH03.P05 — P/E/C at flow level: classification rule and minimum metadata

This paragraph defines how **P/E/C is applied at the flow level**. P/E/C is not assigned to firms or sectors
as an identity; it is assigned to **flow objects** (payments/transfers under a contract/relationship) and, where
required, decomposed into components.

The goal is **auditable attribution**: a third party must be able to reproduce the classification from recorded
inputs using canonical rules, without relying on hidden assumptions, narratives, or discretionary interpretation.

The classification procedure is staged:

1) define the **flow object** and record minimum metadata;  
2) apply **primary attribution** (default rules by contract type and market structure);  
3) apply **secondary attribution** (corrections based on power/exit/alternatives);  
4) attach **uncertainty** explicitly; where ambiguity enlarges discretion, evaluation fails closed.

---

## B00.CH03.P05.S01 — Flow object: amount, counterparty, contract, price rule, lock-in features

The unit of classification is the **Flow object**: a specific transfer (or obligation) between counterparties
under specified terms.

A minimally admissible flow object MUST include:

- **Amount**: numeric value and currency/unit; gross vs net must be explicit.
- **Direction**: payer/payee roles and sign convention (who bears the burden).
- **Counterparties**: actor/entity identifiers (who pays, who receives), including any intermediaries if known.
- **Contract / instrument**: the governing agreement type and key clauses relevant to price and exit.
- **Price rule**: how the price is formed (tariff, subscription, markup, regulated rate, auction, indexation).
- **Time**: period or timestamp and resolution (monthly/annual/transaction).
- **Lock-in features**: switching costs, exit penalties, portability constraints, bundling, exclusivity.
- **Chain position (if applicable)**: whether this flow is a pass-through, internal fee, or stacked charge.

Optional but recommended metadata (where material):
- quantity delivered (units of service/goods),
- service levels / quality commitments,
- cost basis proxies (if available),
- market structure proxies (concentration, entry barriers, regulated monopoly status).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- A flow MUST NOT be classified as P/E/C if the minimum flow object fields are missing in a way that affects the
  outcome; in such cases classification MUST be marked unknown/non-conform and handled under fail-closed rules.
- Where a flow is known to include intermediaries or stacked charges but chain fields are absent, the resulting
  classification must be treated as incomplete and escalated.

## B00.CH03.P05.S02 — Primary attribution: default rules per contract type and market structure

**Primary attribution** assigns a baseline P/E/C split using default rules tied to:
- **contract type** (wage, rent, interest, subscription, platform fee, procurement, tax/transfer, license), and
- **market structure** (contestable market, regulated natural monopoly, platform choke, essential scarcity).

The purpose is to establish a **reproducible first-pass** that is stable across implementations and avoids ad hoc
interpretation.

Primary attribution SHOULD:
- treat clearly functional remuneration as P (bounded by cost + normal margin);
- treat contributions to commons capacity or access regimes as C when traceable;
- treat toll-like features as presumptive E when structure indicates power/lock-in or claim stacking.

Where the contract type is ambiguous or hybrid, the classifier MUST either:
- decompose into recognized sub-contract components, or
- assign a conservative baseline (err toward E on disputed surplus components).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Primary attribution rules MUST be explicit and versioned; “common sense” defaults are non-auditable and non-conform.
- Market structure assumptions used in defaults MUST be declared (e.g., “regulated monopoly” vs “contestable”) and
  linked to observable proxies.

---

## B00.CH03.P05.S03 — Secondary attribution: correction based on power/exit/alternatives

**Secondary attribution** applies corrections when observed conditions contradict baseline assumptions.
It updates the P/E/C split using evidence about:

- **Power**: concentration, entry barriers, gatekeeping control, platform curation/API choke, collateral dominance.
- **Exit / alternatives**: realistic substitutability, switching cost magnitude, portability, capacity constraints.
- **Contractual reality**: penalty clauses, bundling, renewal traps, discriminatory pricing, take-it-or-leave-it terms.
- **Chain effects**: fee stacks, cascade tolls, internal transfer pricing, pass-through layers.

Secondary attribution is where “functional, not rhetorical” is enforced: relabeling does not change classification
unless power/exit conditions change.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any correction MUST be justified by recorded evidence or declared proxies; undocumented overrides are non-conform.
- Where secondary attribution increases discretion (e.g., “seems excessive”), the method MUST specify thresholds,
  bands, and decision rules; otherwise evaluation fails closed on the affected component.

---

## B00.CH03.P05.S04 — Uncertainty: bands, conservatism, fail-closed when data is missing

P/E/C classification must explicitly represent **uncertainty** when inputs are incomplete or noisy.

Uncertainty handling requirements:

- **Bands**: where exact splitting is not possible, use bounded ranges (e.g., P ∈ [x1, x2], E ∈ [y1, y2]) that sum
  consistently to the observed total.
- **Conservatism**: where ambiguity would enlarge scope, discretion, or authority, interpret in a way that does not
  create unwarranted OBA credit (especially for disputed surplus components).
- **Fail-closed**: where minimum metadata is missing and the missingness affects classification, the component MUST be
  treated as non-conform/unknown for OBA claims, and any dependent evaluation MUST not pass by assumption.

Publication/audit posture:
- uncertainty and coverage limitations MUST be reported alongside aggregates;
- any “best effort” classification must be flagged as non-certifying unless the canon’s audit threshold is met.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA claim that relies on P/E/C outputs MUST disclose uncertainty bands and coverage.
- Missing data that could hide extraction (fees, lock-in terms, hidden price) MUST trigger escalation: classify the
  disputed component as E or mark as non-conform/unknown; it MUST NOT be silently treated as P.
