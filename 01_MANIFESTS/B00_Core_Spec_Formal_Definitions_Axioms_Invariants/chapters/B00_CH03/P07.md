# B00.CH03.P07 — Power as classification parameter (structural cause of E)

This paragraph specifies **power** as a canonical classification parameter: **E is structurally caused by power**.
Power is not an “external context” added after classification; it is part of the mechanism by which tolls become
sustainable.

Accordingly:
- Power determines whether a price/term is a contestable remuneration (P) or a sustained toll (E).
- Power is proxied by observable features (concentration, exit friction, chokepoints, collateral dominance, legal
  gating, capture risk).
- Where power proxies indicate non-credible exit or strategic foreclosure, disputed surplus components MUST be
  treated conservatively as E unless rebutted with auditable evidence.

This paragraph defines canonical classes of power relevant for P/E/C: **market**, **platform**, **financial**, and
**state** power.

---

## B00.CH03.P07.S01 — Market power: concentration, entry barriers, natural monopolies

**Market power** is present when a supplier can sustain price/terms above functional remuneration because
competition cannot discipline it.

Canonical market power proxies include (non-exhaustive):

- **Concentration:** high market share, HHI/CRn indicators, persistent dominance.
- **Entry barriers:** licensing hurdles, capital intensity, network effects, access to scarce inputs/locations.
- **Natural monopoly features:** high fixed costs + low marginal costs; duplication is inefficient (utilities,
  networks), creating structural dependence.
- **Switching constraints:** compatibility constraints, retraining, relocation lock, non-portable contracts.
- **Essentiality:** the good/service is an essential (housing, energy, health) or a required input for livelihood.

Classification implication:
- Where market power proxies are strong, the margin above functional costs is presumptively **E** unless a bounded,
  auditable justification exists (e.g., regulated rate base, transparent cost-of-service).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- If P is claimed in a domain with strong market power proxies, the implementation MUST document the contestability
  basis (regulation, access obligations, open entry) or split the toll component as E.
- Market structure assumptions used in classification MUST be explicit and linked to observable proxies.

---

## B00.CH03.P07.S02 — Platform power: ranking/curation, API choke, data lead, bundling

**Platform power** is present when intermediaries control access to markets, attention, or functionality via
curation and technical gates.

Canonical platform power mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):

- **Ranking/curation control:** visibility, search placement, recommendation, de-listing, shadow bans.
- **API choke points:** access to core functionality conditioned on platform permission or restrictive terms.
- **Data lead and feedback loops:** data network effects that foreclose entrants and entrench dominance.
- **Bundling and tying:** forcing complements (payments, ads, identity, logistics) to access baseline reach.
- **Governance opacity:** unilateral rule changes, non-transparent enforcement, non-appealable decisions.

Classification implication:
- Fees or margins sustained by platform gatekeeping (take rates, access fees, “promotion” payments required for
  visibility) are presumptively **E** beyond any bounded functional service component (hosting, processing) and
  must be decomposed accordingly.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any platform fee classification MUST separate functional service components from gatekeeping rents using
  documented rules; “the platform provides value” is not sufficient.
- If ranking/curation materially affects effective access, it MUST be treated as a lock-in/power feature in the
  flow metadata.

---

## B00.CH03.P07.S03 — Financial power: collateral dominance, refinancing lock, roll-over pressure

**Financial power** is present when creditors/intermediaries can extract surplus because counterparties are trapped
by collateral structures, refinancing dependence, or roll-over constraints.

Canonical financial power mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):

- **Collateral dominance:** control of required collateral channels; pro-cyclical margin calls.
- **Refinancing lock:** dependence on refinancing; inability to switch lender without punitive spreads/fees.
- **Roll-over pressure:** short maturities and continuous renewal requirements that force acceptance of terms.
- **Fee stacking:** origination fees, servicing fees, penalties, forced insurance, cross-default clauses.
- **Asymmetric optionality:** creditor can enforce; debtor cannot exit without loss.

Classification implication:
- Interest spreads/fees above functional intermediation cost that persist due to refinancing lock or collateral
  leverage are **E** components, even if a baseline P component exists (risk management, payment infrastructure).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Financial flows used in OBA-critical evaluation MUST disclose term structure and lock-in features (maturity,
  penalty clauses, refinancing conditions); otherwise classification fails closed on the disputed surplus.
- Where credit is essential for access to essentials (housing/energy), financial E must be treated as a primary
  driver of L-layer pressure in downstream evaluation.

---

## B00.CH03.P07.S04 — State power: licences/concessions as potential E source (capture risk)

**State power** is present when legal authority controls access to markets, rights, or essential infrastructures
through licences, concessions, permits, or delegated monopolies.

State power can generate:
- **C** if it is used to build/stabilize commons capacity and guarantee access under non-discrimination;
- **E** if it is captured or used to create toll gates, rents, or discretionary extraction.

Canonical state-power risk mechanisms include (non-exhaustive):

- **Licence/concession scarcity:** limited permits creating rent-bearing scarcity without functional necessity.
- **Delegated monopolies:** exclusive rights granted without adequate rate discipline or access obligations.
- **Regulatory capture:** rules shaped to protect incumbents, block entrants, or enable fee stacks.
- **Discretionary enforcement:** selective application that creates pay-to-play dynamics.
- **Opaque procurement and concessions:** hidden markups, non-auditable terms, revolving-door incentives.

Classification implication:
- State-linked charges that function as unavoidable tolls without commons capacity justification are **E**.
- Public ownership or state involvement does not immunize a flow from E classification; order properties prevail.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any classification that treats state-mediated charges as C or P MUST specify the access/commons regime and its
  auditability; otherwise the component cannot be credited as C and may be E.
- Capture risk indicators MUST be recorded where relevant; if terms are opaque or non-auditable, dependent OBA
  claims must fail closed.
