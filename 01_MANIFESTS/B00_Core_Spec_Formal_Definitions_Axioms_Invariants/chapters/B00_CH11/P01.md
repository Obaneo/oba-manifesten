# B00.CH11.P01 — Purpose, scope and normative status of compatibility

This paragraph defines **compatibility** in OBA as a **formal, testable claim** that constrains how “OBA” may be used in
implementations, documentation, and public communication. Compatibility exists to prevent **semantic drift**, **OBA-washing**,
and the emergence of **incompatible interpretations** that hollow out OBA invariants while preserving the label. The rules in this
chapter are therefore **normative** for any entity, jurisdiction, standard, or publication that asserts an OBA status label.

---

## B00.CH11.P01.S01 — Compatibility as a formal claim: OBA is a class with testable requirements

Compatibility is not a sentiment, aspiration, or rhetorical alignment. It is a **formal claim** that an implementation belongs to
the **OBA class**, where class membership is determined by **testable requirements**.

### Core principles
- **Class membership**: an implementation is either *in the class* (meets requirements) or *not in the class* (fails requirements).
- **Evidence-bounded**: every compatibility claim must be accompanied by **evidence** and **methods** sufficient to reproduce the claim.
- **No “compatibility by intention”**: stated goals, values, or narratives cannot substitute for passing tests.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any use of an OBA status label (**OBA-conform**, **OBA-compatible**, etc.) MUST be treated as a **formal claim** and MUST be
  supported by published **tests, evidence, and references**.
- If required evidence or methods are missing, the claim MUST be treated as **non-conform** until the deficiency is corrected.

---

## B00.CH11.P01.S02 — Purpose: preventing semantic drift, OBA-washing and “incompatible interpretations”

Compatibility rules exist to protect OBA against **meaning erosion** over time, especially through subtle redefinitions,
exceptions, and selective emphasis that preserve the brand while breaking the invariant rails.

### Threat model (non-exhaustive)
- **Semantic drift**: gradual redefinition of terms so that invariant constraints become optional in practice.
- **OBA-washing**: marketing or institutional messaging that claims OBA status without passing compatibility tests.
- **Incompatible interpretations**: parallel “OBA” versions that conflict on definitions or invariants without declaring a fork.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Compatibility governance MUST ensure that definitions remain **stable, referenceable, and versioned** across time.
- Any interpretation that changes an invariant or weakens auditability MUST be treated as a **fork** (see fork protocol) and MUST
  NOT be presented as OBA-conform or OBA-compatible.

---

## B00.CH11.P01.S03 — Scope: definitions, parameters, implementations, jurisdictions, sector canvases

Compatibility applies across the full surface where OBA can be altered, diluted, or misrepresented, including both technical and
institutional layers.

### Covered objects
- **Definitions**: term meanings and canonical objects used in claims.
- **Parameters**: allowed numeric or procedural degrees of freedom within invariant rails.
- **Implementations**: operationalization in policies, systems, audits, registries, templates, and reporting.
- **Jurisdictions**: local legal overlays and cross-border application.
- **Sector canvases**: derived sector-specific mappings that fill parameters and disclosures but may not change definitions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any artifact that asserts or implies OBA status and uses OBA terms MUST fall under compatibility governance.
- Sector canvases and local variants MUST be constrained to **parameter filling** and MUST NOT redefine canonical terms or alter
  invariants.

---

## B00.CH11.P01.S04 — Relation to Kernel Spec, Codex, Books 1–4 and public communication

Compatibility is anchored in a strict **source hierarchy** to prevent “shadow canons” and ad-hoc redefinitions. Canonical meaning
originates from the Kernel Spec, is operationalized by the Codex, and is elaborated by the books; public communication must remain
consistent with these sources and must not create independent definitions.

### Source relation
- **Kernel Spec**: defines canonical terms, invariants, and class requirements.
- **Codex/Standards**: specifies operationalization (schemas, methods, audit templates) without redefining the Kernel.
- **Books 1–4**: elaborate domain logic and application guidance consistent with Kernel and Codex.
- **Public communication**: must reference canonical sources and status labels; must not invent new meanings or exceptions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any compatibility claim MUST reference the applicable **definition-set ID + version** (as part of the claim).
- If a statement in public communication conflicts with canonical sources, the canonical sources prevail and the communication
  MUST be corrected; until correction, any associated compatibility claim MUST be treated as **uncertain** and therefore
  **non-conform** (fail-closed rule applies).
