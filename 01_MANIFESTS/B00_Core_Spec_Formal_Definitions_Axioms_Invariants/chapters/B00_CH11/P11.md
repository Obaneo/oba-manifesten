# B00.CH11.P11 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph defines the main **failure modes** that degrade compatibility governance over time and the **hard-stop responses**
that prevent continued misuse of the OBA name. The general principle is **class integrity over continuity**: when compatibility
cannot be verified or meaning is being hollowed out, status fails closed to **non-conform** until evidence and canonical alignment
are restored.

---

## B00.CH11.P11.S01 — Compatibility capture: stacking exceptions until the core is empty

Compatibility capture occurs when exceptions accumulate until invariants remain nominally present but effectively unenforced. This is
a structural corruption pattern: the label survives while constraints evaporate.

### Typical capture patterns (non-exhaustive)
- **Exception stacking**: each exception is “small”, but the aggregate removes bindingness.
- **Selective carve-outs**: powerful actors or high-impact sectors receive exemptions that neutralize enforcement.
- **Discretion drift**: case-by-case waivers substitute for tests and published rules.
- **Scope slicing**: the claim scope is narrowed repeatedly to exclude failing mechanisms while brand use remains broad.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Exception stacking that weakens invariants MUST be treated as a **fork trigger** and triggers immediate downgrade to
  **Non-conform / Fork**.
- All exceptions MUST be (i) canonically grounded, (ii) published, (iii) time-bounded (sunset), and (iv) reviewed against invariant
  bindingness; otherwise they are forbidden.

---

## B00.CH11.P11.S02 — Goodhart on labels: “OBA-compatible” abused as marketing (detection)

When labels become targets, actors optimize communication to obtain the label rather than to meet underlying requirements. The
failure mode is “label compliance”: exploiting ambiguity, selective disclosure, and superficial module adoption.

### Detection indicators (non-exhaustive)
- **Evidence gap**: label used without a complete evidence bundle or without reproducible methods.
- **Selective metrics**: only favorable indicators are published; required disclosures are omitted.
- **Module theater**: optional-module adoption is showcased while core auditability/invariants remain weak or unverified.
- **Rapid rebranding cycles**: frequent label changes without corresponding versioned evidence updates.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If label abuse indicators are present, the claim MUST undergo a status review; missing evidence triggers immediate downgrade to
  **non-conform** (fail-closed).
- Repeated label abuse constitutes proven washing and triggers hard-stop treatment under enforcement rules.

---

## B00.CH11.P11.S03 — Semantic drift via document sprawl: shadow canons (forbidden)

Document sprawl becomes a drift vector when multiple “semi-official” documents start functioning as alternative sources of meaning,
creating shadow canons that compete with Kernel/Codex authority.

### Drift mechanisms (non-exhaustive)
- **Unofficial glossaries** that redefine terms in implementation notes or partner decks.
- **Sector-specific “mini-kernels”** that quietly diverge from canonical definitions.
- **Patch notes without governance**: changes are introduced via memos instead of versioned canonical updates.
- **Public comms as pseudo-source**: marketing language becomes treated as normative.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Shadow canons are forbidden. Any artifact that functions as a competing source of meaning MUST be either:
  - downgraded to non-normative commentary, or
  - elevated into canonical governance via versioned integration, or
  - declared as a fork.
- If shadow canon influence is detected in a compatibility claim, that claim MUST be treated as **non-conform** until the source
  graph is cleaned and re-anchored to canonical sources.

---

## B00.CH11.P11.S04 — Fail-closed: when status is uncertain, non-conform applies until evidence is provided

Uncertainty about compatibility is treated as a risk to class integrity. OBA therefore applies a default rule: **no evidence, no
status**.

### Uncertainty triggers (non-exhaustive)
- Missing definition-set ID + version reference.
- Missing or inaccessible evidence bundle.
- Unresolved definition or jurisdiction conflicts affecting claim scope.
- Non-reproducible methods or unavailable inputs.
- Refusal to submit to audit where required.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If status is uncertain for any reason, the implementation MUST be treated as **non-conform** for the affected scope until
  evidence and canonical alignment are restored.
- During uncertainty, public communication MUST reflect non-conform status (no implied compatibility).
- Restoration of status MUST require publication of the missing evidence and re-passing of the compatibility tests.
