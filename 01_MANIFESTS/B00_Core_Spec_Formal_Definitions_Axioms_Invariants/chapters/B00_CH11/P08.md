# B00.CH11.P08 — Compatibility for sector canvases and local variants

This paragraph defines how **sector canvases** and **local variants** may exist within OBA without causing semantic drift. Sector
canvases are treated as **derivative artifacts**: they may operationalize OBA for a sector by filling parameters and publishing
sector-specific mappings, but they MUST NOT redefine canonical terms or weaken invariants. Local institutional choice is permitted
only within **auditability** and **invariant rails**.

---

## B00.CH11.P08.S01 — Sector canvas as derivative: may only fill parameters, cannot change definitions

A sector canvas is a scoped derivative that applies OBA to a sector context (e.g., housing, energy, platforms) by providing
parameter choices, bottleneck mapping, and disclosure templates.

### Derivative rule
- Sector canvases MUST treat the Kernel Spec and Codex as fixed sources.
- Sector canvases MAY introduce sector-specific *instances*, mappings, and parameter values **within bounds**.
- Sector canvases MUST NOT introduce new definitions for canonical terms, nor introduce sector-specific exceptions that weaken invariants.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any sector canvas that redefines a canonical term or alters an invariant MUST be treated as a **fork**.
- Sector canvases MUST include a published list of parameter values and the definition-set ID + version they derive from.

---

## B00.CH11.P08.S02 — Local institutional choice: allowed within auditability and invariant rails

Local variants (jurisdictional or institutional) may choose among implementation options (governance bodies, enforcement sequencing,
measurement infrastructure), but those choices may not compromise auditability or invariant enforcement.

### Allowed local choices (examples)
- Institutional arrangement (which bodies audit/enforce; dispute routing), provided independence and transparency requirements are met.
- Measurement cadence and time resolution (within canonical bounds).
- Commons programme design details (access mechanisms, maintenance workflows) within required order properties.

### Non-allowed local choices (examples)
- Replacing public methods with opaque scoring or unpublished decision logic.
- Granting discretionary exemptions that effectively suspend invariants for influential actors or high-impact channels.
- Redefining P/E/C, R/B, cap principles, or prohibited channels under local terminology.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Local variants MUST publish an auditable institutional description and the complete method set used for classification, caps, and R/B.
- If local choice reduces auditability or weakens invariant bindingness, the affected scope MUST be treated as **non-conform** until remedied.

## B00.CH11.P08.S03 — Minimum publication per sector: bottlenecks, E sources, commons options, cap compatibility, R/B effect

To claim OBA compatibility at the sector level, a minimum disclosure set is required so that sector claims are reviewable and cannot
hide extraction in sector-specific complexity.

### Minimum sector publication (required set)
- **Bottlenecks**: the sector’s primary chokepoints and control surfaces (including ownership/control/benefit concentrations).
- **E sources**: principal extraction mechanisms and proxies (fees, tolls, lock-ins, gating, scarcity rents, leakage channels).
- **Commons options**: feasible commons infrastructures for the sector (access, maintenance, governance, funding).
- **Cap compatibility**: how sector design respects and enforces cap principles (including avoidance surfaces).
- **R/B effect**: expected and observed effects on R/B profiles (distributional, not only averages), with declared lag windows.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Sector compatibility claims MUST include the minimum publication set as a public artifact.
- If any element is missing or non-auditable, the sector claim MUST be treated as **non-conform** until the publication set is completed.

## B00.CH11.P08.S04 — Anti-avoidance: standard check on legal/technical/international shifts per sector

Each sector has its own avoidance surfaces. Sector canvases must therefore include a standardized anti-avoidance check that covers
the main shift categories relevant to the sector.

### Required anti-avoidance coverage (minimum)
- **Legal shifts**: contractual form changes, entity nesting, reclassification attempts, liability routing.
- **Technical shifts**: gating mechanisms, access control, ranking/curation, digital tolling, telemetry-based discrimination.
- **International shifts**: cross-border routing, transfer pricing, regulatory arbitrage, off-shore concealment.
- **Leak tests**: explicit tests for the sector’s high-probability leakage paths.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Sector canvases MUST publish a sector-specific avoidance surface map and the corresponding detection + reclassification procedures.
- If avoidance coverage is missing for any high-impact surface, the sector claim MUST be treated as **non-conform** until coverage is restored.
