# B00.CH11.P10 — Enforcement of name usage and status

This paragraph defines the enforcement rules that bind **use of the name “OBA”** to **compatibility status** and **published
evidence**. Enforcement is designed to prevent label abuse, deter misrepresentation, and protect the integrity of the OBA class.
Where evidence is missing or governance is opaque, OBA applies **fail-closed** rules and may trigger immediate hard stops.

---

## B00.CH11.P10.S01 — Name right: use of “OBA” tied to compatibility status and evidence

Use of the name “OBA” is permitted only under defined compatibility labels and only when the required evidence is published.

### Rule
- The string **“OBA”** (and derived labels) may be used to describe an implementation only if:
  - the implementation has an explicit status label (per compatibility label taxonomy), and
  - the claim is registered and backed by published evidence, and
  - the claim references the canonical definition-set ID + version.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any public use of “OBA” that implies compatibility (explicitly or implicitly) MUST include:
  - status label,
  - definition-set ID + version,
  - evidence bundle references (tests/methods).
- Absent evidence or registration, the implementation MUST use **“OBA-inspired”** with a clear no-compatibility disclaimer, or must
  remove OBA name usage entirely.
- Ambiguity is treated as misrepresentation: if a reasonable reader could infer compatibility, the name usage is bound to the
  compatibility evidence requirement.

---

## B00.CH11.P10.S02 — Sanctions: ban, fines, public downgrade, loss of access to commons programmes

Sanctions are proportional but firm. Their function is to prevent ongoing harm from mislabeling and to remove incentives for washing.

### Sanction types (non-exhaustive)
- **Ban** on OBA name usage for a defined period or until corrective conditions are met.
- **Fines** tied to severity, scale, recurrence, and benefit obtained from misrepresentation.
- **Public downgrade** in the register (status change + published rationale and evidence).
- **Loss of access** to OBA-gated commons programmes, registries, procurement preferences, or certification pathways.

### Escalation factors
- Repeat offenses, intentional concealment, refusal to publish evidence, or high-impact deception amplify sanctions.
- Voluntary correction and cooperative remediation mitigate sanctions but do not erase the duty to correct.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Sanctions MUST be recorded in the public register with scope, duration, and remediation conditions.
- If sanctions include access loss, reinstatement MUST require verified remediation and passing the compatibility tests again.

---

## B00.CH11.P10.S03 — Dispute protocol: objection/appeal with time-bounded procedures

Enforcement decisions must be contestable through a defined dispute process, but disputes must not be used to delay correction or
continue ambiguous name usage.

### Minimum dispute procedure
1. **Objection filing**: written objection with scope, claimed status, and supporting evidence references.
2. **Preliminary check**: validate that required artifacts exist (definition-set reference, evidence bundle, methods).
3. **Time-bounded review**: fixed timeline for evaluation; extensions only under published criteria.
4. **Decision**: uphold, modify, or reverse status; publish reasoning and evidence references.
5. **Appeal**: one or more appeal steps with independence requirements and fixed deadlines.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- During dispute, if compatibility evidence is missing or governance is opaque, status remains **non-conform** (fail-closed).
- Public communications during dispute MUST reflect current registered status and MUST NOT imply a higher status pending appeal.

---

## B00.CH11.P10.S04 — Hard stops: for black-box, non-disclosure or proven washing → immediate fork status

Certain violations are incompatible with OBA integrity and trigger immediate hard stops. Hard stops are not negotiated; they are
classification events.

### Hard stop triggers (non-exhaustive)
- **Black-box governance**: decision logic or enforcement methods are undisclosed or non-auditable.
- **Non-disclosure**: refusal to publish required evidence, methods, or scope information.
- **Proven washing**: demonstrated misrepresentation of status labels or systematic compatibility-by-branding practices.
- **Repeated concealment**: hidden exceptions, scope slicing, or deliberate ambiguity after notice.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If any hard stop trigger is present, the implementation MUST be designated **Non-conform / Fork** immediately and MUST cease use of
  compatibility labels.
- Reinstatement after hard stop MUST require full disclosure, external audit (where applicable), and re-passing all compatibility
  tests for the claimed scope.
