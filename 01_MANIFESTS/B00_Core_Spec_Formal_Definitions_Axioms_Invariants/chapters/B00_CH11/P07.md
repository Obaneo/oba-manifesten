# B00.CH11.P07 — Conflict rules for definitions and implementations

This paragraph defines the conflict rules that apply when OBA artifacts, implementations, or jurisdictions disagree. Conflict
resolution in OBA is **procedural and evidence-driven**: canonical sources govern meaning, tests govern implementation outcomes, and
uncertainty fails closed to protect class integrity.

---

## B00.CH11.P07.S01 — Definition conflict: canonical resolution procedure (Kernel Spec governance)

A **definition conflict** exists when two sources (or two readings) produce incompatible meanings for a term, invariant, or rule.

### Resolution procedure (minimum)
1. **Identify the conflict**: specify the term(s), conflicting statements, and impacted claim scope(s).
2. **Apply source hierarchy**: determine the highest-authority canonical source applicable to the term/rule.
3. **Strict reading default**: where multiple canonical readings exist at equal authority, apply the strictest reading until resolved.
4. **Canonical patch**: submit the conflict to Kernel governance for a versioned clarification, correction, or explicit fork boundary.
5. **Propagate**: update Codex/Standards and dependent artifacts to match the resolved canonical meaning.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Compatibility claims MUST NOT rely on a permissive reading in the presence of a known definition conflict.
- Until canonical resolution is published, affected claims MUST be treated as **non-conform** (fail-closed) or must reduce scope to
  unaffected components with explicit disclosure.

---

## B00.CH11.P07.S02 — Implementation conflict: test outcome prevails over intention or rhetoric

An **implementation conflict** exists when an implementation claims compatibility but **fails** the formal checks or produces outcomes
inconsistent with its claims.

### Rule
- **Tests prevail**: compatibility status is determined by passing the formal checks and evidence requirements, not by intention,
  mission statements, or rhetorical alignment.
- **Artifact discipline**: if the implementation’s behavior differs from its published specification, the behavior governs the audit
  result until the implementation is corrected.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any failed compatibility test MUST trigger downgrade to **non-conform** for the affected scope until remediation is verified.
- Public claims MUST be corrected to match test outcomes; refusal or delay triggers hard-stop treatment under name-usage enforcement.

---

## B00.CH11.P07.S03 — Parameter conflict: outside allowed space = non-conform until remedied

A **parameter conflict** exists when an implementation uses parameter values, update rules, or time resolutions outside the allowed
parameter space or without the required procedure/evidence.

### Rule
- **Outside bounds**: any parameter outside canonical bounds invalidates compatibility for the claim scope that depends on it.
- **Procedure matters**: even in-bound values are invalid if adopted without the required publication, evidence, and change record.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If a parameter is outside allowed space, the claim MUST be treated as **non-conform** until values are brought into bounds and
  the change record is published.
- If parameter provenance is missing (no versioned record), status MUST be treated as **uncertain** and therefore **non-conform**
  (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH11.P07.S04 — Jurisdiction conflict: cross-border mismatches and priority rules

A **jurisdiction conflict** exists when local legal constraints, regulatory definitions, or enforcement practices produce cross-border
mismatches that affect canonical meaning, auditability, or invariants.

### Priority rules (minimum)
- **Canonical meaning is not localized**: jurisdictions may fill parameters within bounds, but must not redefine canonical terms.
- **Most restrictive applicable rule**: for cross-border chains, apply the rule-set that best preserves invariants and auditability
  across the chain (strictness principle) unless canonical governance specifies an explicit priority mapping.
- **Traceability requirement**: cross-border claims must publish the jurisdiction mapping and the chain of applicable rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- If cross-border mismatches prevent auditability or create unresolvable conflicts, the affected scope MUST be treated as
  **non-conform** until bridged by an explicit, published mapping or canonical resolution.
- Any jurisdictional carve-out that weakens invariants constitutes a fork trigger and MUST be declared as such.
