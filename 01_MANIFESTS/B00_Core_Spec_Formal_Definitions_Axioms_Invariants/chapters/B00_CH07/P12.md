# B00.CH07.P12 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph outlines the **failure modes** and **hard stops** for commons regimes. These conditions represent
situations where commons principles are violated, either through **commons-washing**, **capture**, **vendor lock-in**,
or **non-auditability**. Failure to adhere to these principles leads to the **suspension of commons status**, enforcing
the regime's commitment to non-extractive and contestable service delivery.

---

## B00.CH07.P12.S01 — Commons-washing: label without access/maintenance/contestability

**Commons-washing** occurs when a service or system is labeled as a "commons" but does not meet the essential criteria
for a commons regime: it may appear open or public, but in practice, it lacks the required **access**, **maintenance**, or
**contestability** mechanisms.

Canonical commons-washing indicators:
- **Lack of true access**: services are publicly labeled but not universally accessible (e.g., high fees, extensive waiting
  lists, restrictive entry conditions).
- **No maintenance duties**: services are provided without long-term maintenance or investment in sustainability,
  risking service failure or deterioration over time.
- **Non-contestable**: there is no mechanism for users or other stakeholders to challenge service provision, decision-making,
  or governance (e.g., opaque pricing, monopolistic control).

Commons-washing detection:
- Review access rules, maintenance investments, and governance transparency to determine if the service is truly open and
  equitable.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If a service is **labeled as commons** but lacks the essential properties of access, maintenance, and contestability,
  the regime is **non-compliant** and must be **suspended** until these elements are remedied.

---

## B00.CH07.P12.S02 — Capture: operator becomes toll gate (E in commons clothing)

**Capture** occurs when the **operator** of a commons service or infrastructure transforms it into an effective **toll gate**,
extracting value from users in ways that are inconsistent with commons principles. In these cases, the commons is **exploited**
for private benefit, turning the system into an **E-income** generator (monopoly rents, tolling, lock-in).

Canonical capture indicators:
- **Excessive fees or tolls**: operators charge for access to essential services or for basic participation in the commons,
  creating **extraction** instead of enabling equitable access.
- **Hidden costs or penalties**: the operator imposes additional fees (e.g., late fees, exit penalties) that trap users and
  create financial friction, often in the guise of “maintenance” or “administrative” fees.
- **Monopolistic practices**: the operator controls critical access points, or services are provided through closed
  ecosystems that stifle competition and create unfair advantages for the operator.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If the operator turns the commons into a **toll gate**, where users are effectively charged for access to essential
  services or face exploitative practices, the regime is **non-conform** and must be **suspended**.
- Any **E-income** generation from core commons services that violates non-extractive principles results in **fail-closed** status.

---

## B00.CH07.P12.S03 — Vendor lock-in: closed stacks in public infra (non-conform)

**Vendor lock-in** occurs when the design of commons services (particularly in digital and infrastructural commons) creates
**dependency** on a single vendor, technology, or provider. This prevents users from exiting the system or moving their
data and services to other providers without incurring high costs, both financially and in terms of service disruption.

Canonical vendor lock-in indicators:
- **Closed stacks**: reliance on proprietary platforms, services, or technologies that cannot be easily switched or
  integrated with other systems (e.g., closed software, proprietary data formats, or hardware that prevents interoperability).
- **Excessive switching costs**: high fees, long transitions, or data migration restrictions that make it practically
  impossible to exit the system without significant inconvenience or financial penalty.
- **Lack of standards or open protocols**: the absence of **open standards** or **interoperability** that allow for
  portability between systems or services.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If a **vendor lock-in** situation exists in a commons service, especially in the public infrastructure space, the
  system is **non-compliant** and **suspended** until the lock-in is removed, and interoperability is established.
- Failure to provide **open standards** and portability options results in **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P12.S04 — Fail-closed: non-measurability/non-auditability → suspension of commons status

**Fail-closed** refers to the practice of treating any instance where the commons system **cannot be measured** or
**audited** as a failure of the system. This ensures that **opaque systems** cannot escape scrutiny and that users are
protected from undetected abuses.

### Fail-closed conditions:
- **Non-measurability**: if a commons service’s performance or service levels (e.g., ΔG, access, maintenance) cannot
  be objectively measured or verified.
- **Non-auditability**: if there is no **audit trail** for contracts, procurement, or performance data, preventing
  independent verification of the commons system’s activities.
- **Hidden or unreported practices**: if the system hides essential practices, decision-making, or financial records,
  making it impossible for users, auditors, or regulators to assess compliance with commons principles.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If any component of the commons system is **non-measurable** or **non-auditable**, it **fails closed**, and the
  **commons status is suspended** until transparency and accountability are restored.
- **Non-auditability** and **non-measurability** are grounds for **fail-closed**, and the system cannot be considered
  **OBA-compatible** until resolved.
