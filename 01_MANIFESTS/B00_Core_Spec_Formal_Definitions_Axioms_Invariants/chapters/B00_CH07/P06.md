# B00.CH07.P06 — Boundary rules: rights, duties and enforcement

This paragraph specifies the canonical **boundary rules** for commons regimes: the rights and duties of users,
operators, and suppliers/contractors, and the enforcement/sanctions logic that keeps the regime non-extractive and
contestable.

Core intent: commons must not become (i) a chaotic open-access tragedy through unmanaged abuse, nor (ii) a private toll
gate through operator discretion and capture. Boundary rules therefore bind all parties to explicit duties, measurable
service levels, and reviewable enforcement.

---

## B00.CH07.P06.S01 — User duties: fair use, maintenance contribution, abuse prevention

Users have enforceable duties that protect continuity and fairness. These duties are not “fees by another name”;
they are proportional responsibilities necessary for commons viability.

Canonical user duties include:

- **Fair use:** comply with declared usage limits and scarcity rules; no hoarding or disproportionate consumption that
  degrades baseline access for others.
- **Maintenance contribution (where applicable):** contribute in the canonical form defined by the regime (could be
  tariff-based cost coverage, time-based contribution, or other transparent mechanism). Any contribution must be:
  - non-extractive (cost-linked),
  - proportional,
  - and must not recreate toll access for baseline rights.
- **Abuse prevention:** refrain from fraud, sabotage, harassment, and misuse; comply with safety and integrity rules
  (e.g., illegal connections to grids, false claims, automated abuse of digital rails).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- User duties MUST be explicit, published, and limited to what is required for continuity and fairness.
- Duties that function as covert exclusion (pricing/fines that effectively remove baseline access) are non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P06.S02 — Operator duties: service levels, transparency, non-discrimination, recoverability

Operators carry primary duty-of-care for commons continuity. Operator obligations are constitutive (linked to P02/P03):
if they are absent or non-measurable, the service cannot claim commons status.

Canonical operator duties include:

- **Service levels:** publish and meet measurable targets (uptime, waiting times, throughput, coverage, repair times).
- **Transparency:** publish tariffs, rules, capacity constraints, incident logs (redacted where strictly necessary),
  procurement outcomes, and change logs.
- **Non-discrimination:** enforce access rules uniformly; prohibit hidden prioritization, preferential deals, and
  algorithmic gatekeeping that produces de facto exclusion.
- **Recoverability:** maintain redundancy, incident response, and restoration capability; enforce maintenance-first
  resourcing; demonstrate recoverability through drills/tests where relevant.

Operator discretion must be bounded:
- where rationing is necessary, rules must be pre-declared and reviewable; ad hoc rationing is a capture surface.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Operators MUST be accountable against published service and recovery metrics; “best effort” without metrics is non-conform.
- Any private side-channel prioritization or opaque ranking allocation in commons domains is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P06.S03 — Suppliers/contractors: open standards, auditability, vendor lock-in prohibited

Suppliers and contractors are a primary enclosure vector: closed stacks and proprietary interfaces can turn commons
into de facto private toll infrastructure. Therefore procurement and contracting must enforce interoperability and
auditability.

Canonical supplier/contractor requirements:

- **Open standards:** interfaces, protocols, and data formats must be open and documented; no proprietary choke points
  in core rails.
- **Auditability:** contracts must permit audit of cost bases, performance, incidents, and change histories; black-box
  deliverables in core commons functions are non-conform.
- **Vendor lock-in prohibition:** contracts must preserve portability:
  - data portability,
  - operational handover feasibility,
  - escrow/transfer of critical documentation,
  - limits on switching costs and exit penalties.
- **IP and licensing discipline:** licenses must not create tolls on essential rails; “free now, toll later” patterns
  are treated as pseudo-commons.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons program MUST treat vendor lock-in as a hard stop risk and require portability by contract design.
- Closed proprietary stacks in core public infrastructure functions are non-conform by default.

---

## B00.CH07.P06.S04 — Sanctions: proportional, procedurally reviewable, no discretionary arbitrariness

Enforcement must exist, but it must be bounded by procedural guarantees to prevent operator capture and arbitrary
exclusion. Sanctions are legitimate only if they are proportional, reviewable, and consistent.

Canonical sanction properties:

- **Proportionality:** responses match severity and intent (warning → limitation → temporary suspension → last-resort exclusion).
- **Procedural reviewability:** clear notice, evidence disclosure, right to object/appeal, and time-bounded decisions.
- **No discretionary arbitrariness:** no “operator feels like it” sanctions; sanctions must map to published rule
violations and evidence thresholds.
- **Baseline protection:** sanctions must not deprive users of baseline essentials without strict criteria and emergency
safeguards (especially in life-critical domains).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Sanctions MUST be rule-based and appealable; opaque bans and irreversible administrative exclusion are non-conform.
- If enforcement cannot be reviewed or audited, commons status is suspended (fail-closed) due to capture risk.
