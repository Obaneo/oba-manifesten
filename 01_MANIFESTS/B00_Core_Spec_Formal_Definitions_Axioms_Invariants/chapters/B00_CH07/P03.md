# B00.CH07.P03 — ΔG as state variable (capacity/quality/continuity)

This paragraph defines **ΔG** as the canonical **state variable** for commons: the measurable change in commons
capacity/quality/continuity over a period. ΔG is the bridge between (i) financing/allocation and (ii) lived-world
outcomes: it turns “we funded a commons” into auditable claims about service capacity, quality, uptime, and
recoverability.

Canonical posture: commons status is conditional on measurable maintenance performance. If ΔG cannot be derived
reproducibly, the commons claim fails closed.

---

## B00.CH07.P03.S01 — Definition: ΔG(t) as change in commons capacity/quality per period

Let **G(t)** denote the commons service state at time **t**, capturing capacity, quality, and continuity properties.
Then:

- **ΔG(t) = G(t) − G(t−1)** at a declared resolution (year/month/quarter as defined by the regime).

G(t) is multi-dimensional. At minimum it must include measurable components such as:
- **capacity** (throughput/coverage/stock of service units),
- **quality** (meeting service specification; error rates; outcomes),
- **continuity** (uptime, downtime, recoverability, incident frequency and severity).

ΔG therefore expresses whether the commons is improving, stable, or degrading.

Auditability requirement:
- the definition of G(t) must be explicit (metric set and measurement method), and ΔG must be reproducible from raw
data and published methods.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime MUST define G(t) and the time resolution for ΔG; otherwise “maintenance” is not measurable.
- If ΔG cannot be reconstructed, commons status is suspended (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH07.P03.S02 — Components: capex build-out, opex maintenance, replacement, innovation as maintenance

ΔG is produced by multiple activity classes. Canonically:

- **Capex build-out:** expansion of capacity/coverage (new units, network expansion, new sites).
- **Opex maintenance:** keeping existing capacity functional (repairs, staffing, upkeep, patching, inspections).
- **Replacement:** lifecycle renewal (end-of-life replacement, modernization, decommissioning and rebuild).
- **Innovation as maintenance:** improvements required to preserve service continuity under changing conditions
  (security upgrades, climate hardening, standards updates, interoperability upgrades). Innovation counts as maintenance
  when it is required to prevent degradation or lock-in.

Classification discipline:
- spending categories are not sufficient; outputs must map to ΔG components. “We spent capex” is not evidence of ΔG↑
unless service state metrics show it.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Regimes MUST map financial inputs to ΔG outputs via auditable project/performance records.
- Rebranding discretionary expansion as “maintenance” without ΔG evidence is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P03.S03 — Minimum ΔG: lower bound for continuity (maintenance-first invariant)

Commons require a **maintenance-first invariant**: a minimum ΔG that prevents service degradation.

Canonical rule:
- there exists a **minimum ΔG_min(t)** (possibly zero or slightly positive, depending on depreciation and demand growth)
such that if **ΔG(t) < ΔG_min(t)** persistently, the commons is degrading and continuity is not assured.

Interpretation:
- ΔG_min is the lower bound required to offset depreciation, wear, technical debt, and demand shifts.
- below this bound, backlog accumulates and service collapses becomes a predictable outcome.

Operational implication:
- financing and allocation must fund opex/maintenance and replacement before prestige expansion projects (linked to P09).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Regimes MUST define how ΔG_min is derived (even if via conservative bands) and must monitor it.
- Persistent breach of ΔG_min triggers escalation: remediation plan, governance intervention, or suspension of commons status.

---

## B00.CH07.P03.S04 — Relation to R/B: ΔG affects L (essentials) and shock resilience

ΔG is the causal mechanism by which commons improve lived-world order:

- **ΔG↑ (capacity/quality improvement)** tends to **reduce L** by lowering essential service burdens:
  - fewer shortages and price spikes,
  - shorter waiting times,
  - reduced private toll reliance,
  - reduced lock-in and contractual grip.

- **ΔG stabilisation (continuity)** increases **shock resilience**:
  - fewer catastrophic failures,
  - faster recovery,
  - more predictable essential costs,
  - reduced vulnerability tails (improving R/B distribution, especially below α).

Canonical linkage chain:
- **ΔM / C-flows / cap reallocation → commons inputs → ΔG → essentials performance → L → R/B profiles.**

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Commons programs claiming OBA relevance MUST report how ΔG changes L and R/B profiles (at least directionally with
  measurable proxies).
- If ΔG is flat/negative while L rises and R/B deteriorates, the commons claim is either failing or mismeasured and
  requires escalation or suspension (fail-closed).
