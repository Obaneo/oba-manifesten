# B00.CH07.P09 — Financing and allocation (link to ΔM and caps)

This paragraph outlines the **financing** and **allocation** rules for commons systems, linking financial support and
resource distribution to key **ΔM** principles (economic stability) and **cap management** (ensuring services remain
non-extractive and equitable). The purpose is to ensure that commons services are sustainably funded, directed toward
maintenance, and serve the goal of reducing **L** (life-world burdens) while improving **R/B** (life-world buffers).

---

## B00.CH07.P09.S01 — Funding sources: C-flows, cap reallocation, tariff revenues, public funds

Commons services are funded through a variety of **non-extractive sources**, ensuring that funding supports public
goods without creating profit-driven incentives. The key sources of funding for commons must be **aligned with OBA principles** and cannot rely on private rent-seeking or exploitation.

Canonical funding sources include:

1) **C-flows**: Commons must be primarily funded through **C-flows** (contribution from public resources), ensuring
   that the services remain non-extractive and available to all eligible users. This could include public investments,
   grants, or social safety-net funds allocated to provide for public needs.
   
2) **Cap reallocation**: A portion of the funds from **W/I caps** or **excessive profits** (e.g., from top-income taxation,
   reallocation of capital) may be repurposed to fund public goods such as commons services, ensuring that wealth
   creation is redistributed in a way that improves public welfare.
   
3) **Tariff revenues**: Where applicable, **tariff revenues** can be used to fund the maintenance and service of the
   commons, but these must be cost-covering and **bounded** (linked to actual operational and maintenance costs, not
   excess profit generation).

4) **Public funds**: Government-funded contributions, either through taxation or direct fiscal support, must be used to
   cover essential service gaps that cannot be addressed by other funding sources.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any funding model for a commons regime MUST rely on **C-flows**, **cap reallocation**, and **tariff revenues** that
  directly fund maintenance and access, without creating extractive practices.
- **Private sector funding** that leads to **profit extraction** or **surveillance rents** is prohibited.

---

## B00.CH07.P09.S02 — Maintenance-first: opex/maintenance before expansion or prestige projects

The **maintenance-first** principle is a fundamental rule for commons regimes to ensure that service continuity and
quality are prioritized above expansion or prestige projects. Commons cannot justify expansion or non-essential development
at the cost of **service continuity** or **maintenance**.

Canonical rule:
- **Opex/maintenance** must always be fully funded before any new capital expenditure or expansion (e.g., build-out of
  new infrastructure). The goal is to prevent the commons from diverting funds away from essential upkeep to flashy or
  non-essential projects.
- **Maintenance-first** ensures that **ΔG** (capacity, quality, and continuity) does not degrade, and that service levels
  remain stable before investing in new developments.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Opex and maintenance** must be prioritized in the budget allocation process, ensuring that service reliability is never
  sacrificed for new, non-essential expansions.
- Any failure to adhere to **maintenance-first** principles will lead to **non-conform** status for the regime.

## B00.CH07.P09.S03 — Allocation rules: priority on reducing L and improving R/B

The allocation of resources within the commons must always prioritize **reducing L (life-world burdens)** and improving
**R/B (life-world buffers)**. Commons services should be structured and funded in such a way that **vulnerable populations**
have their needs met, and **equity** is ensured in the distribution of public goods.

### Allocation principles:
- **Priority on reducing L**: allocate resources first to reducing essential burdens (e.g., housing, healthcare,
  transportation, education). This ensures that the **R/B floor** is maintained and that vulnerable populations are not
  left without essential services.
- **Improving R/B**: after reducing L, resources should focus on improving R/B through measures such as increasing
  resilience, boosting income buffers, expanding access to essential services, and addressing systemic inequalities.
- **Equity-first**: funding must be allocated based on **need**, not **market demand** or **political influence**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons system must **prioritize allocations** that improve life-world stability (R/B) and reduce burdens (L),
  ensuring that **vulnerable groups** are supported and that no service distribution exacerbates inequality.
- If **resources are allocated** based on profit-driven motives or political influence rather than need, the regime is
  **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P09.S04 — Prohibited funding: dependence on extractive flows or surveillance rents

Funding for commons must come from **non-extractive** sources. Any **funding model** that relies on extractive flows,
**surveillance rents**, or private-sector profits that **disproportionately benefit a small number of actors** is strictly
prohibited.

### Prohibited funding sources:
- **Extractive flows**: any funding generated from activities that extract value from users without providing a fair
  exchange (e.g., tolls, rent-seeking behavior).
- **Surveillance rents**: funding derived from **user data collection**, exploitation of personal data, or other forms
  of **information asymmetry** that are non-consensual or exploitative.
- **Monopolistic profits**: any revenues generated by **monopolistic or oligopolistic practices** in critical commons
  services, where private providers control or manipulate pricing for essential goods.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Commons regimes MUST ensure that **funding comes from non-extractive sources** (e.g., C-flows, cap reallocation, public
  contributions).
- **Extractive or surveillance-based funding models** are non-conform and must be **avoided** at all costs.
