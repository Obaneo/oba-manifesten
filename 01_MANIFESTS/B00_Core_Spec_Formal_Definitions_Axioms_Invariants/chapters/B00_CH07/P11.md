# B00.CH07.P11 — Edge cases and boundary rules

This paragraph addresses **edge cases** and **boundary rules** for commons regimes. These are situations where
commons principles need to adapt to exceptional or complex scenarios, but where they must still maintain the core
objectives of ensuring fair access, service continuity, and transparency without reintroducing extractive practices.

---

## B00.CH07.P11.S01 — Natural monopolies: tariff regime and anti-private tolling

**Natural monopolies** occur in sectors where competition is impractical due to high fixed costs or where duplication
of infrastructure would be inefficient (e.g., electricity grids, water supply, public transport). These sectors still
require commons principles to ensure that services are **non-extractive** and that pricing remains equitable.

### Canonical rules for natural monopolies:
- **Tariff regime**: Natural monopolies must implement a **tariff regime** that covers the operational and capital costs
  of service provision, with strict rules that limit pricing to **cost coverage** and a **bounded margin**.
- **Anti-private tolling**: Private providers of natural monopolies may operate these services but must adhere to
  **non-extractive** rules. Excessive profit extraction, price gouging, or lock-in practices (such as hidden fees) are
  prohibited.
- **Public option**: A **public option** should remain available to ensure that users are not trapped in monopolistic systems
  and that there is a clear alternative to private control.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Natural monopolies MUST provide **transparent tariffs**, ensuring that prices reflect the actual cost of provision and are
  **non-extractive**.
- If any monopolistic service engages in **private tolling** (e.g., monopolistic pricing) or fails to provide a viable
  **public option**, the regime is **non-conform**.

## B00.CH07.P11.S02 — Data/AI commons: privacy minimalism, model transparency, access governance

As data and **artificial intelligence** (AI) become integral to the commons, it is critical that **data/AI commons** are
designed to ensure that user privacy is respected, that models are **transparent**, and that access governance remains
fair and equitable.

### Canonical principles for data/AI commons:
- **Privacy minimalism**: Data collection for commons purposes must adhere to **privacy minimalism**, collecting only the
  minimum amount of personal or sensitive data necessary to provide the service.
- **Model transparency**: AI models used in the commons (for example, algorithms for healthcare, justice, or education) must
  be **transparent** and **explainable**. Users and auditors should be able to understand how decisions are made and what
  data or assumptions drive the model.
- **Access governance**: Data and AI models should be **open** where possible, allowing for transparency and access, but
  also ensuring **equitable distribution** and preventing exclusive control by a few actors.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any data/AI commons system MUST **prioritize privacy minimalism** and **model transparency**.
- If data collection or AI models become **opaque** or exploit **personal information**, the commons system is **non-conform**.

## B00.CH07.P11.S03 — Crisis regimes: emergency access and rationing with sunset/rollback

During **crises** (e.g., natural disasters, pandemics, or economic shocks), commons systems must implement **emergency
access protocols** and, if necessary, ration resources. However, these measures must include a **sunset** or **rollback**
mechanism to ensure they do not become permanent features of the system.

### Canonical crisis regime principles:
- **Emergency access**: Commons must ensure that during crises, resources or services remain available to **vulnerable
  populations**, and access is expanded where necessary.
- **Rationing**: In extreme cases, where demand exceeds supply, **rationing** must be applied in a **fair** and **equitable**
  manner. Priority must be given to those most in need (e.g., healthcare during a pandemic, water during a drought).
- **Sunset/rollback**: Emergency measures (such as rationing) must be **temporary** and linked to clear **criteria** that
  mark the end of the crisis (e.g., recovery indicators, the stabilization of supply chains).

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- **Crisis regimes** must ensure that **temporary measures** like rationing or prioritization are time-limited and
  **transparent**, with clear criteria for when they are no longer necessary.
- If emergency measures become permanent without justification, the commons system is **non-conform** and must rollback
  those practices.

## B00.CH07.P11.S04 — International commons: cross-border interoperability without governance capture

**International commons** (such as global data sets, climate action initiatives, or shared digital infrastructure)
require **cross-border interoperability** to function effectively. However, these commons must avoid **governance capture**,
where a small number of actors (governments or corporations) gain disproportionate control over the commons, to the
detriment of broader participation.

### Canonical international commons principles:
- **Cross-border interoperability**: Commons that operate across national or regional boundaries must be designed to
  ensure **interoperability** across different legal, political, and economic systems. Standards and rules must be
  **uniform** to allow fair participation and access by all relevant stakeholders.
- **Governance capture avoidance**: International commons must have robust mechanisms in place to prevent **capture** by
  any specific group or country, ensuring **democratic governance**, **stakeholder participation**, and **transparency**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)
- Any **international commons** system must ensure **cross-border interoperability** and guard against **governance
  capture**. This includes ensuring that no single entity or group can monopolize control or use the commons for
  private gain.
- If governance is **centralized** or **opaque**, or if it fails to support democratic **stakeholder checks**, the
  system is **non-conform** and must be corrected.
