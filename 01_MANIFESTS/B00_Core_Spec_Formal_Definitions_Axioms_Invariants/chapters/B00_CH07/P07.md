# B00.CH07.P07 — Governance minimum (institutional)

This paragraph defines the **governance minimum** required for commons regimes to ensure transparency, accountability,
and the **integrity** of service delivery. Effective governance is the key to preventing capture, ensuring compliance,
and maintaining **contestability** within the commons system. The chapter defines the roles, powers, liabilities, and
anti-capture mechanisms that must be in place for a commons system to be **institutionally sound**.

---

## B00.CH07.P07.S01 — Role model: owner/mandate giver, operator, executor, auditor, user

Commons regimes require clearly defined **institutional roles** to ensure responsibilities are distributed and
governance is maintained without concentration of power.

### Key roles:
1) **Owner/mandate giver**: the **entity** or **public authority** responsible for setting up and overseeing the commons
   regime. This entity defines the purpose and boundaries of the commons and mandates the rules, services, and duties.
   It may be public or private but must not directly control the service provision.
   
2) **Operator**: the **entity** responsible for managing the daily operation of the commons, ensuring service levels,
   fairness, and compliance with the defined rules. This is typically the provider of the service (e.g., utility operator, 
   platform manager).

3) **Executor**: responsible for the **implementation of services**, including maintenance, service provision, and
   continuity. This can be the same as the operator or a separate party.

4) **Auditor**: an **independent third-party entity** tasked with ensuring that the commons operates according to
   regulations and does not engage in exploitation or unfair practices. The auditor is responsible for checking the
   transparency of the commons and ensuring that services meet required standards.
   
5) **User**: the **stakeholder** who consumes or uses the services provided by the commons, and who has rights and duties
   as defined by the commons rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must clearly define and **segregate these roles** to avoid conflicts of interest and prevent
  consolidation of power in one entity.
- If roles overlap without explicit checks (e.g., one entity controls both operator and executor), the system is **non-conform**.

## B00.CH07.P07.S02 — Powers: allocation, prioritisation, emergency mode, contracting (bounded)

The **powers** within a commons system must be **bounded** to avoid discretionary decision-making and ensure that the
commons serves its intended purpose.

### Key powers:
1) **Allocation**: powers of **resource distribution**, ensuring that services are distributed fairly and equitably
   according to the rules (e.g., capacity allocation, access to services).
   
2) **Prioritisation**: powers of **resource allocation during scarcity**, ensuring that critical services are maintained
   and that the most vulnerable users are not left without access (e.g., prioritising healthcare or essential utilities
   during crises).
   
3) **Emergency mode**: the ability to **override standard rules** temporarily during emergencies to ensure continuity
   and prevent collapse (e.g., in the case of natural disasters or national emergencies).

4) **Contracting powers**: the ability to enter into contracts with suppliers, contractors, or operators for service
   provision, but within a framework that ensures these contracts serve the public interest and do not enable
   monopolistic or extractive practices.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Powers must be **explicitly bounded** in scope, with clear rules and procedures for decision-making, especially in
  cases of scarcity or emergency.
- Any emergency or crisis response should not lead to **discretionary exceptions** that allow for the capture of commons
  services for private interests.

## B00.CH07.P07.S03 — Liability: duty of care, negligence, conflict-of-interest rules

Effective governance requires **clear liability** provisions to hold relevant parties accountable for their actions.

### Key liability principles:
1) **Duty of care**: all actors (owners, operators, contractors, auditors) have a duty to act in the **best interest** of
   the commons, ensuring that services are provided fairly and consistently without negligence or harm.
   
2) **Negligence**: liability for **failure to meet service levels**, maintain facilities, or provide transparent and
   accurate reporting that impacts the users or the broader system.

3) **Conflict-of-interest rules**: clear and enforceable rules that **prevent conflicts of interest** among those
   governing, operating, or contracting in the commons regime. This includes:
   - prohibition of **self-dealing**,
   - public disclosure of any potential conflicts,
   - and separation of powers to avoid undue influence over decisions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Liability for **negligence** and **conflict of interest** must be clearly defined and actionable in any commons regime.
- Any regime that fails to provide clear liability structures, especially for **service failure** or **misuse**, is **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P07.S04 — Anti-capture: term limits, transparency, procurement rules, stakeholder checks

To ensure **accountability** and prevent **capture**, commons governance must be structured to avoid undue influence
or the concentration of power in the hands of a few.

### Canonical anti-capture measures:
1) **Term limits**: for key governance roles (e.g., mandate givers, operators, auditors) to prevent long-term entrenchment
   of decision-makers and ensure regular **accountability** and **fresh perspectives**.
   
2) **Transparency**: full disclosure of governance processes, decision-making, resource allocation, and contracting
   decisions. Transparency must apply to **procurement rules** (ensuring fair bidding processes) and **service outcomes**.

3) **Procurement rules**: clear **open procurement processes** for contracting and awarding of services. Contracts must be
   awarded based on **merit** and **service quality**, not **political influence** or **personal connections**.
   
4) **Stakeholder checks**: mechanisms to ensure **regular review** by external stakeholders (e.g., civil society, users) to
   prevent governance from becoming a closed circle and to ensure that **public interest** remains the central goal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime MUST include **anti-capture mechanisms** like term limits, transparency, procurement rules, and
  stakeholder checks.
- If capture risks (e.g., extended terms, opaque procurement, unbalanced stakeholder influence) are allowed to persist,
  the regime is **non-conform**.
