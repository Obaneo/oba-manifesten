# B00.CH07.P01 — Purpose, scope and normative status of Definition set V

This paragraph defines the **purpose**, **scope**, and **normative status** of **Definition set V** for **commons**
and **maintenance** regimes within OBA. The chapter’s goal is to establish a **commons framework** that serves as an
**order invariant**, reducing extraction and ensuring fair **access** to essential services while maintaining
resilience in service provision.

Commons are not defined by **ownership** but by their ability to provide **access** and **maintenance** with clear
rules for user rights, duties, and operator responsibilities. Commons-based services must ensure that access is
non-discriminatory, services are continuously available, and there is no tolerance for extractive or monopolistic
practices.

---

## B00.CH07.P01.S01 — Commons as order invariant: exit options, discipline of power, reduction of L

Commons function as an **order invariant** by maintaining the **integrity** of essential services, reducing the
influence of monopolistic powers, and improving overall **life-world buffers (R/B)**.

Key principles:
- **Exit options**: Commons must provide **public options** as an **alternative** to monopolistic services, ensuring
  users can exit exploitative systems and switch to better alternatives (e.g., public utilities, free software platforms).
- **Discipline of power**: Commons constrain **power dynamics** by setting clear **boundary rules**, preventing the
  concentration of control in monopolistic or extractive entities.
- **Reduction of L**: Commons play a critical role in **reducing L** (life-world burdens), ensuring that essential
  services (housing, healthcare, water, education) are provided fairly and universally, not subject to the whims of
  extractive market forces.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must **maintain and protect public options** as a viable **exit** strategy for users in essential
  service sectors.
- If the regime enables monopolistic control over key services without public alternatives, it fails to meet **OBA**
  compliance and the regime is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P01.S02 — Commons is not an ownership label but an access/maintenance regime

The concept of **commons** is defined by its **access/maintenance** functions rather than by ownership status.
Ownership alone does not qualify a service or resource as a commons. The defining feature of commons is the **public
access** and **maintenance duties** tied to essential services.

Key components:
- **Access**: Commons guarantee **non-discriminatory access** to services for all. This is a core feature that allows
  individuals to utilize services (e.g., housing, healthcare) without barriers, such as excessive pricing or restricted
  entry.
- **Maintenance**: Commons are not static; they require continuous **maintenance** to ensure long-term sustainability
  and equitable service levels for all. This includes investments in infrastructure, service recovery, and improvements
  over time.
- **Public management**: While ownership can vary (public or private), the central principle is the **maintenance of
  access** and the prevention of **extractive practices**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any service or resource labeled as “commons” must demonstrate **access** and **maintenance duties** and cannot be
  defined by ownership alone.
- Failure to ensure **access/maintenance** or reliance on ownership-based definitions without the supporting governance
  of access and maintenance constitutes **non-conform** with OBA.

---

## B00.CH07.P01.S03 — Scope: essentials, networks, natural monopolies, knowledge/data, digital rails

The scope of commons extends across several critical domains, where the lack of regulation or fair access would lead
to **disproportionate power** and **life-world burdens (L)**.

Key commons categories include:

1) **Essentials**:
   - Basic services like **housing**, **energy**, **water**, **healthcare**, **education**, and **mobility** that are
     required for human welfare and functioning society.
2) **Networks**:
   - **Critical infrastructures** such as **payment rails**, **logistics**, **communication networks**, **identity systems**,
     and other **interoperability layers** that are fundamental to the economy and society.
3) **Natural monopolies**:
   - Sectors or resources that have a **natural monopoly** (e.g., electricity grids, water supply) due to high
     infrastructure costs and where competition is not feasible.
4) **Knowledge and data**:
   - Public goods related to **knowledge**, **standards**, and **data** (e.g., open scientific research, public models,
     data dictionaries, public assumptions).
5) **Digital rails**:
   - Open **APIs**, **protocols**, and digital infrastructures that enable access and innovation in the digital economy.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must clearly define and cover the essential sectors outlined above and establish rules for ensuring
  access, maintenance, and non-discriminatory governance.
- **Failure to include critical sectors**, such as essentials or natural monopolies, in the commons framework leads to non-conform.

## B00.CH07.P01.S04 — Relation to P/E/C, R/B, W/I caps and ΔM (linkages)

Commons interact with other OBA principles, including **P/E/C**, **R/B**, **W/I caps**, and **ΔM**. The relationship is
critical for ensuring that commons-based services contribute to overall societal order without enabling extraction or
capture.

### Key linkages:

1) **P/E/C**: 
   - Commons serve to provide non-extractive resources (C) that prevent excessive burden on **P**-linked remuneration
     and **E-income** extraction, reducing pressures on **R/B** and supporting a stable flow of public services.
2) **R/B**:
   - Commons provide essential services that support **R/B** stability by reducing **L** (life-world burdens), thereby
     maintaining or enhancing remaining space (R) and improving resilience.
3) **W/I caps**: 
   - Commons help **contain inequality** by providing **public alternatives** to monopolistic or privatized services,
     ensuring that wealth creation does not lead to the exclusion of others.
4) **ΔM** (Monetary policy and allocation): 
   - Commons are supported by **ΔM** rules to ensure their **financial sustainability**, but their funding sources
     must not rely on **extractive income flows** (e.g., monopolistic rents, surveillance).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime claiming **OBA compatibility** must demonstrate how commons frameworks **interact** with the broader
  P/E/C, R/B, and I/W caps systems.
- If commons are used to **mask extractive practices** or circumvent **R/B improvement goals**, the regime is non-conform.
