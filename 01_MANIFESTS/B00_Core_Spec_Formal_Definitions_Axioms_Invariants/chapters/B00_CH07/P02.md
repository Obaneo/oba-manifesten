# B00.CH07.P02 — Canonical definition of commons (order properties)

This paragraph defines **commons** canonically by **order properties**, not by ownership form. A commons is a regime
that (i) guarantees baseline access, (ii) defines enforceable boundary rules, (iii) carries a binding maintenance duty,
and (iv) is contestable and auditable. If any of these constitutive properties is absent, “commons” is only a label and
must be treated as non-conform (commons-washing risk).

Canonical intent: commons create **exit options**, discipline power/lock-in, and structurally reduce **L** by removing
toll mechanisms from essential domains.

---

## B00.CH07.P02.S01 — Access: non-discrimination, baseline rights, public option logic

**Access** is constitutive: the commons must provide a **baseline service/entitlement** that is available to all
eligible users on non-discriminatory terms.

Canonical access properties:

- **Non-discrimination:** access criteria must be objective, public, and reviewable. No hidden exclusion via pricing,
  bundling, ranking, or administrative friction.
- **Baseline rights:** a minimum service level/volume/quality is guaranteed as a right (parameterisable, but explicit).
- **Public option logic:** a commons must be a viable **exit** from private toll regimes. “Available in theory but
  unusable in practice” is non-conform.

Access may be differentiated above baseline, but differentiation must be transparent and cannot create extraction
channels that reintroduce lock-in or tolling (linked to P05).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Baseline access MUST be specified (what is guaranteed, to whom, under which conditions).
- Access rules MUST be auditable; hidden rationing or de facto exclusion via friction is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P02.S02 — Boundary rules: who may do what, under what conditions, with which duties

A commons is defined by enforceable **boundary rules**: the permission and duty structure that governs users,
operators, suppliers, and overseers.

Canonical boundary rule elements:

- **Eligibility:** who is in-scope (residents, contributors, users, institutions) and how eligibility is determined.
- **Permitted actions:** what users and operators may do (consume, connect, build, modify, extend) and under what constraints.
- **Conditions:** technical, safety, fairness, and capacity constraints; queueing/rationing rules in scarcity regimes.
- **Duties:** user duties (fair use, abuse prevention), operator duties (service levels, non-discrimination), supplier duties
  (open standards, auditability), and auditor duties (independence).

Boundary rules must be explicit enough that compliance is testable and discretion is bounded.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Boundary rules MUST be written as enforceable rules (not aspirations) with explicit scope and duties.
- Any boundary rule that creates discretionary gatekeeping without appeal is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P02.S03 — Maintenance duty: lifecycle, recoverability, service levels, resiliency

Commons are not “resources”; they are **maintained systems**. The defining obligation is the **maintenance duty**:
the commons must preserve and renew capacity/quality through lifecycle provisioning.

Canonical maintenance properties:

- **Lifecycle duty:** capex build-out is not sufficient; opex maintenance, replacement cycles, and end-of-life planning are binding.
- **Recoverability:** ability to restore service after incidents (failover, redundancy, repairability, spare capacity).
- **Service levels:** explicit SLO/SLA-like targets for uptime, throughput, waiting times, quality, and responsiveness.
- **Resiliency:** stress-tested continuity under shocks (supply disruption, disasters, cyber incidents, demand spikes).

Maintenance duty is measured via ΔG dynamics (defined in P03) and cannot be satisfied by nominal budgets without outputs.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Commons regimes MUST define maintenance obligations and measurable service levels.
- If maintenance is not measurable or is systematically underfunded (continuity failure), commons status is suspended (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH07.P02.S04 — Contestability: objection/appeal, auditability, transparency as constitutive

A commons must be **contestable**: users and stakeholders must be able to challenge exclusions, service failures,
pricing, and governance decisions. Contestability is constitutive because without it, the operator becomes a toll gate.

Canonical contestability properties:

- **Objection/appeal:** clear procedure, timelines, and remedies; no “black hole” complaints process.
- **Auditability:** decisions, procurement, pricing/tariffs, incidents, and performance metrics must be reconstructable.
- **Transparency:** publication of rules, metrics, and change logs; secrecy is permitted only where strictly necessary
  (security/privacy minimalism) and must be justified.

Contestability is not optional: it is the mechanism that disciplines power and prevents capture.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Commons regimes MUST provide an appeal mechanism and publish audit trails for key decisions and performance.
- Black-box governance or opaque allocation decisions are non-conform and trigger suspension of commons status.
