# B00.CH07.P10 — Measurement and audit standards for commons and ΔG

This paragraph defines the **measurement** and **audit standards** required to ensure that commons regimes meet the
expectations for **capacity**, **quality**, and **continuity** as defined by ΔG. The goal is to establish a transparent
and verifiable framework for tracking and ensuring the **performance** of commons services, as well as the integrity of
their management processes. 

All commons regimes must use **auditable** and **reproducible** methods for tracking service outputs, with clear
metrics for assessing ΔG (service state changes) and ensuring that services meet their core functions and obligations.

---

## B00.CH07.P10.S01 — ΔG metric set: capacity, quality, uptime, recoverability, cost per unit

The **ΔG metric set** tracks the **change in commons capacity, quality, and continuity** over time, ensuring that
service delivery is consistent and reliable.

Canonical ΔG metrics include (minimum set):
- **Capacity**: the total **service output** (e.g., number of housing units, energy supply, healthcare appointments)
  relative to demand. For example, the number of households that can be served by a given housing system, or the
  energy output relative to population needs.
- **Quality**: the **service standard** met in delivery, as measured by user satisfaction, compliance with service
  specifications, and independent audits. This includes factors such as reliability, timeliness, and accuracy.
- **Uptime**: the percentage of **time** that a service remains available for use, excluding downtime due to repairs
  or maintenance.
- **Recoverability**: the ability of the commons to **recover** from incidents (e.g., disaster recovery, incident
  response time, fault tolerance).
- **Cost per unit**: the **unit cost** of providing the service, broken down by operational expenditure (opex) and
  capital expenditure (capex).

Each of these metrics must be tracked over time to evaluate whether the commons system is improving (ΔG↑), stable (ΔG=0),
or deteriorating (ΔG↓).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- The commons regime must define and publish a clear **ΔG metric set**, ensuring that performance is measured by these
  dimensions.
- **Lack of measurable ΔG** metrics, or the inability to audit ΔG outputs, renders the commons regime non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P10.S02 — Access metric set: coverage, waiting times, price/baseline, non-discrimination

The **access metric set** tracks the degree to which the commons are accessible and equitable, focusing on availability,
timeliness, and fairness in the distribution of services.

Canonical access metrics include (minimum set):
- **Coverage**: the percentage of eligible users or areas that are **covered** by the commons service (e.g., percent of
  population with access to essential healthcare, housing, energy, or education).
- **Waiting times**: the average **time** that users must wait to access services, whether for appointments,
  resources, or service initiation.
- **Price/baseline**: the cost of accessing the commons service relative to the **baseline access guarantee**. This is a
  key indicator of whether **tolling** or **barriers** to access have been introduced above baseline levels.
- **Non-discrimination**: ensuring that services are provided **equally** to all eligible users, irrespective of race,
  income, or status. Non-discrimination also covers preventing discriminatory pricing, ranking, or access prioritization.

These metrics ensure that commons systems do not become exclusive or extractive through **exclusionary pricing** or
**barriers** to access.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- The commons regime must track and report these **access metrics** regularly to ensure that the system is serving all
  eligible users and does not discriminate or introduce new barriers.
- Failure to measure or report **access metrics** is a violation of the commons principle, resulting in **non-conform** status.

## B00.CH07.P10.S03 — Audit trail: contracts, procurement, performance, incidents, change logs

An **audit trail** is the record of all **key decisions**, **contracts**, and **transactions** related to the operation
of the commons. This record is critical to ensure that commons regimes are **transparent**, **accountable**, and not
susceptible to **capture** or **abuse**.

The audit trail should include:
- **Contracts**: records of agreements between the commons operator and any **suppliers**, **contractors**, or
  **partners** (e.g., procurement documents, service level agreements).
- **Procurement**: transparent procurement processes for goods or services related to the commons (e.g., public bids,
  cost analyses, supplier selection).
- **Performance**: detailed logs of the performance of the commons, including regular assessments, metrics, and the
  results of service delivery.
- **Incidents**: logs of any **service failures**, **incidents**, or **disruptions**, including responses and recovery
  efforts.
- **Change logs**: records of any **changes** to the commons service, rules, pricing, or service level agreements, and
  justification for those changes.

Auditability is critical to ensure that the **integrity** of the commons regime is maintained, and that all decisions
can be verified.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- The commons regime MUST maintain a **comprehensive audit trail** for contracts, procurement, performance, incidents, and
  changes.
- If the **audit trail** is not publicly accessible or if key documents are missing, the regime is **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P10.S04 — Reproducibility: methods, definition-set ID, versioning of metrics

All measurement and reporting within the commons regime must be **reproducible**, meaning that the methods used to
calculate performance and outcomes can be independently verified and replicated.

Canonical requirements for reproducibility:
- **Methods**: the calculation and measurement methods for ΔG, access metrics, and performance indicators must be
  published and clearly defined.
- **Definition-set ID**: all metrics, standards, and definitions must be versioned and linked to a unique ID for
  transparency.
- **Versioning**: changes to the methods, definitions, or metrics must be recorded, with clear documentation and rationale
  for each version update.

Reproducibility ensures that the system is **transparent** and **auditable**, and allows external stakeholders to
verify that performance claims are valid.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must provide **reproducible** methods and publish a clear **versioning system** for metrics and definitions.
- If methods or definitions cannot be reproduced, the system is **non-conform** and the commons claim is suspended until
  remediation.
