# B00.CH07.P05 — Access as canonical design: baseline and differentiation

This paragraph defines **access** as the canonical design principle for commons systems. Access is the **right** to
a minimum level of service, guaranteed universally without discrimination. Above this baseline, **differentiation** is
allowed only if it is non-extractive and **transparent**. The key design goal is to ensure that commons systems remain
open and equitable, providing essential services and reducing **L** (life-world burdens) without toll mechanisms or
lock-in practices.

---

## B00.CH07.P05.S01 — Baseline access: minimum service/volume as a right (parameterisable)

**Baseline access** is the core right of any commons system. It guarantees a **minimum level of service** or
**volume** to all eligible users, ensuring that essential needs are met without exploitation.

### Canonical baseline elements:
- **Minimum service levels**: a **defined baseline** service must be available to all users, ensuring no one is excluded
  from essentials (e.g., a minimum threshold for water, housing, healthcare, energy, education).
- **Volume guarantees**: for services where demand varies, **volume guarantees** ensure a baseline of access (e.g., how
  much water or energy is provided to each user, within reasonable bounds).
- **Non-discriminatory access**: access criteria must be clear, objective, and **transparent**; no barriers to access
  other than usage-based limits within baseline parameters.

Parameterisation:
- The baseline level of service is **parameterisable**, with each commons domain defining what qualifies as “minimum”
  (e.g., 50kWh/month of electricity, basic healthcare coverage, a reasonable housing unit).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must **define its baseline service levels** and **ensure non-discriminatory access**.
- If a service does not guarantee baseline access, or if it creates de facto barriers through pricing or limited supply,
  it is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P05.S02 — Differentiation above baseline: allowed if non-extractive and transparent

**Differentiation** above the baseline is permissible in commons systems, but it must meet strict criteria to ensure it
does not reintroduce **extractive practices** or create unequal access.

### Canonical differentiation rules:
- **Non-extractive**: any differentiated service must not be exploitative. It can involve **premium services**, but
  **price signals** must reflect real costs (e.g., luxury housing or expedited services) rather than capture mechanisms
  (e.g., price gouging).
- **Transparency**: the process and pricing for differentiation must be **clear**, ensuring users understand why
  premiums exist and can choose whether to participate.
- **No lock-in**: differentiated services must allow **exit options** and must not tie users into ongoing extraction
  (e.g., subscription traps, lock-in fees, cancellation penalties).

Differentiation examples (permissible):
- **Tiered access**: premium services for those who wish to pay for faster or higher-quality service, but basic service
  must remain universally accessible.
- **Time-of-use pricing**: charging higher prices during peak demand times is permissible if it is transparent, non-extractive,
  and does not deny baseline access.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any differentiation above baseline must be **non-extractive**, **transparent**, and **allow for exit**.
- Systems that allow for extractive practices under the guise of premium offerings (e.g., tolling essentials) are non-conform.

## B00.CH07.P05.S03 — Price and tariff rules: cost coverage + bounded margin; prohibition of tolls

Price and tariff systems for commons services must reflect **cost coverage** and **bounded margins**. There should be
no **tolling** for access to essential services, and pricing must be designed to prevent **extractive profit-maximising
behaviours**.

### Canonical pricing and tariff rules:
- **Cost coverage**: prices should reflect the actual cost of providing the service (both operational and capital
  expenditure).
- **Bounded margins**: any surplus generated through pricing should be within a **bounded margin** (a reasonable,
  predefined range). For example, utilities may allow a margin of 10% above costs for maintenance and growth, but
  anything above this would be considered extractive.
- **Prohibition of tolls**: services that are **essential** (e.g., water, energy, housing) must **never** be subject to
  **tolling** or any system that forces users to pay for basic access. Surplus may only be generated for service improvements
  or investments in infrastructure.
- **Public cost mechanisms**: tariffs must be open and transparent, with a clear link between pricing and the cost of
  delivering the service.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must ensure that **prices do not exceed cost coverage** plus a reasonable margin, and that no
  **tolling** of essential services is allowed.
- If pricing exceeds reasonable bounds, or if it introduces **unjustifiable tolls** for access to the commons, it is
  non-conform.

## B00.CH07.P05.S04 — No-exit-friction: limiting lock-in and contractual grip in commons domains

A key feature of commons systems is that they must enable **exit options** for users. **No-exit-friction** means that
users should be able to leave or switch services freely without facing undue penalties, lock-in clauses, or other
forms of **economic or contractual grip**.

### Canonical exit-friction limits:
- **No long-term lock-in**: users should not be **locked** into long-term contracts that are difficult or expensive to
  break (e.g., 10-year housing contracts, early exit fees for utilities).
- **Portability of benefits**: users should be able to take their benefits or service credits with them when switching
  service providers (e.g., transferring energy credits, housing points, or healthcare records).
- **Non-captive platforms**: in the case of digital commons, users must be able to move their data, identity, or services
  to other platforms without high switching costs (e.g., email, social media, or financial platforms).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must **guarantee exit** and prevent excessive lock-in or contractual grip on users.
- Systems that require users to pay high exit fees or create economic barriers to switching services are **non-conform**.
