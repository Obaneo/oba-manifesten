# B00.CH07.P04 — Commons categories and scope rules

This paragraph defines the canonical **commons categories** and the **scope rules** that determine when a domain is
treated as commons-relevant under OBA. The goal is semantic closure: “commons” is not a vibe; it is a regime applied
to domains where exit options, contestability, and maintenance duties are structurally required to prevent extraction
and L inflation.

Canonical scope logic:
- a domain is commons-relevant when (i) it is essential to lived-world viability, or (ii) it is a network layer whose
gatekeeping power can create tolls/lock-in, or (iii) it is a knowledge/information substrate necessary for auditability
and interoperability, or (iv) it is a digital rail where de facto standards can become private choke points.

---

## B00.CH07.P04.S01 — Essentials commons: housing, energy, water, healthcare, education, mobility, basic connectivity

**Essentials commons** are domains where lack of reliable access forces coercive participation, making private tolling
and lock-in structurally capable of inflating **L** and degrading **R/B**.

Canonical essentials set (baseline; parameterisable extensions allowed, but omissions require justification):
- **Housing**
- **Energy**
- **Water**
- **Healthcare**
- **Education**
- **Mobility** (baseline access to reach work/health/education)
- **Basic connectivity** (communications/internet baseline)

Scope rule (essentials):
- if a service is required for baseline social participation and survival, it is in-scope for commons logic.
- commons status requires baseline access + maintenance duty + contestability (P02), not necessarily public ownership.

Boundary note:
- differentiation above baseline may exist, but baseline access must remain non-extractive and non-discriminatory,
and must not be undermined by scarcity engineering.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- A jurisdiction or program claiming commons compatibility MUST explicitly define its essentials set and baseline
service levels.
- Excluding a canonical essential without an explicit, auditable alternative mechanism is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P04.S02 — Network commons: payment rails, logistics, identity/credentialing, interoperability layers

**Network commons** are infrastructural layers where network effects and switching costs produce natural choke points.
Private gatekeeping here creates systemic extraction risk (E) and fragility under shocks.

Canonical network commons candidates:
- **Payment rails** (clearing, settlement, retail payments, rails interoperability)
- **Logistics** (core freight/ports/critical distribution infrastructure)
- **Identity and credentialing** (identity, authentication, credential issuance/verification)
- **Interoperability layers** (standards and interfaces that enable multi-provider ecosystems)

Scope rule (networks):
- if the domain exhibits strong network effects, high switching costs, or natural monopoly characteristics such that
a gatekeeper can impose tolls, it is commons-relevant.

Public option logic:
- network commons often require a **minimum viable public option** or mandatory interoperability to preserve exit.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Network commons regimes MUST cap switching costs, mandate interoperability where needed, and prohibit vendor lock-in
in core rails.
- If a private gatekeeper controls essential rails without contestability/exit, commons status is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P04.S03 — Knowledge and information commons: standards, data dictionaries, public models/assumptions

**Knowledge and information commons** are constitutive for auditability and interoperability. Without shared public
definitions and transparent assumptions, evaluation becomes discretionary and “order claims” become unfalsifiable.

Canonical knowledge commons elements:
- **Standards** (technical and semantic standards; open specifications)
- **Data dictionaries** (canonical definitions/units/indexing; e.g., OBA object models)
- **Public models/assumptions** (parameter regimes, valuation conventions, measurement methods)
- **Reference datasets** where feasible (benchmarks, indices, methodological baselines)

Scope rule (knowledge):
- if a domain is required to make public claims auditable and interoperable, it is commons-relevant even if not
“material infrastructure”.

Anti-capture constraint:
- standards governance must be contestable; otherwise standards become private toll gates.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-compatible system MUST publish the knowledge commons required for its claims (methods, definitions, indices).
- Black-box assumptions or proprietary definition layers in public order functions are non-conform.

---

## B00.CH07.P04.S04 — Digital commons: open APIs, protocols, public options alongside platforms

**Digital commons** address digital choke points: platforms, protocols, and API ecosystems where lock-in and ranking
power can impose extraction and suppress exit.

Canonical digital commons elements:
- **Open APIs and protocols** enabling portability and multi-homing
- **Public options** (minimum viable alternatives) in key digital domains
- **Portability/interoperability** requirements (data portability, identity portability, social graph portability where applicable)
- **Protocol-level governance** that prevents unilateral enclosure

Scope rule (digital):
- if a platform/protocol layer functions as a de facto public rail (communication, payments, identity, marketplace access),
it is commons-relevant.

Pseudo-commons warning:
- “open branding” while retaining de facto lock-in, proprietary extensions, or tolling is non-conform (linked to P08.S04 and P12).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Digital commons regimes MUST ensure portability and interoperability with caps on switching costs.
- If openness exists only at the surface while control remains enclosure-capable (API choke, ranking gate), commons
status is suspended (fail-closed).
