# B00.CH07.P08 — Public option, hybrid layers and interoperability

This paragraph defines the principles governing **public options**, **hybrid layers**, and **interoperability** in
commons systems. The goal is to ensure that commons services remain **open, accessible, and contestable** while allowing
for **competitive innovation** where appropriate. At the core, public services must provide **minimum viable alternatives**
to private options without creating monopolistic or extractive practices.

---

## B00.CH07.P08.S01 — Public option as disciplining exit: minimum viable alternative

The **public option** is a critical component of any commons regime, as it serves as a **disciplining exit** for users
who are dissatisfied with private or monopolistic services. A public option ensures that there is always a **minimum
viable alternative** to private control, particularly in sectors that are essential to the well-being of individuals.

Canonical principles of the public option:
- **Disciplining exit:** users can choose to exit private monopolistic systems without losing access to essential
  services.
- **Minimum viable alternative:** the public option must meet **basic service standards** (as defined in P05) and be
  offered without tolls or excessive conditions that reduce its accessibility.
- **Non-discriminatory:** the public option must be available to all, without preferential access or pricing for any
  specific group.

The public option guarantees that essential services remain **open to all** and that users are not **captured** by
monopoly pricing or restrictive terms.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must provide a **public option** that meets the minimum viable service criteria for all eligible
  users.
- Failure to provide a **true public option** that functions as a viable alternative to private providers leads to **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P08.S02 — Hybrid layers: commons core with competitive edges (allowed)

**Hybrid layers** allow for a **commons core** to coexist with competitive, market-driven layers. This can foster
innovation and ensure that **competitive edges** are encouraged, without compromising the core commons duties of access
and maintenance.

Canonical hybrid layer principles:
- **Commons core**: the foundational service or resource must remain public and accessible to all, ensuring **minimum
  service levels** (e.g., the base housing, energy, water, healthcare, etc.).
- **Competitive edges**: above the core, optional services or features that offer enhancements, specialization, or
  premium options, but these cannot undermine the accessibility or fairness of the core commons.
- **Non-extractiveness**: competitive features cannot turn essential service provision into **tolling** or create **lock-in**
  effects (e.g., exclusive premium services that result in monopolistic practices).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime with hybrid layers must ensure that the **commons core remains non-extractive**, and **competitive edges** 
  should not come at the expense of baseline service access.
- If a hybrid model creates **monopolistic lock-in**, or if competitive services interfere with baseline access, the system
  is **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P08.S03 — Interoperability: open standards, portability, caps on switching costs

**Interoperability** is a core principle of commons systems, ensuring that users can freely move between providers
without incurring excessive costs, losing access, or being locked into specific platforms.

Canonical interoperability principles:
- **Open standards**: all services and platforms within the commons must adhere to open, transparent standards to ensure
  compatibility and easy transferability of data, services, or benefits.
- **Portability**: users must be able to **move data**, **transfer services**, or **switch providers** without barriers.
  This includes portability of digital assets, healthcare records, or identity services.
- **Caps on switching costs**: if switching providers is necessary, the cost should be minimal. Excessive switching fees,
  data transfer barriers, or artificial obstacles (e.g., exit fees, proprietary formats) are prohibited.

Interoperability ensures that commons remain **contestable**, preventing platform owners or service providers from
unfairly locking users into their ecosystems.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime must include provisions for **interoperability**, using **open standards** and **minimal switching
  costs**.
- If switching providers incurs **excessive costs** or if portability is blocked by **closed systems**, the regime is
  **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH07.P08.S04 — Prohibition of pseudo-commons: apparent openness with de facto lock-in or tolling

**Pseudo-commons** are systems that appear to be open but, in practice, maintain **de facto lock-in**, **tolling**, or
other exploitative practices. These systems may claim to be **publicly available** or **open** but, in reality, create
barriers to exit, obscure costs, or control access in a way that undermines the commons model.

Canonical pseudo-commons indicators:
- **Hidden tolls**: pricing that is hidden or obscured, such as access fees that increase over time, or “free” services that
  later become monetized through other means (e.g., “free” platforms that monetize user data or services that
  eventually require premium payments).
- **Lock-in effects**: restrictions on moving data, switching service providers, or migrating to alternative solutions
  without substantial costs.
- **Exclusive control**: private monopolistic control that claims public access but restricts use or extraction in
  practice (e.g., platforms with exclusive rights over services or data, despite claiming openness).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any commons regime MUST prohibit **pseudo-commons** systems that appear open but rely on **hidden tolls**, **lock-in**,
  or **exclusive control**.
- If a service or system maintains de facto **extraction** through lock-in mechanisms, even if it appears open, it is
  **non-conform**.
