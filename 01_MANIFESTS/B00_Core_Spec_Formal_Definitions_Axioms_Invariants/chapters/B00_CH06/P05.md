# B00.CH06.P05 — Netting and costs: what may be deducted

This paragraph defines the canonical deductibility and netting discipline for I-MAX. A flow cap is only robust if
the cap base cannot be artificially reduced through engineered “costs”, internal fees, or cross-border shifting.

Canonical posture: **Gross I** is the starting point. Deductibility is **limited** and **functional**: only genuine
production inputs (P-costs) may be deducted within strict bounds. Costs that function as extraction (E) or as base
erosion mechanics are prohibited.

---

## B00.CH06.P05.S01 — Gross I as starting point; limited deductibility for genuine production inputs

I-MAX applies to **gross income flows** attributable to the unit, with only narrowly defined deductions permitted.

Canonical rule:
- Start from **Gross I** (sum of all I-base components per period).
- Permit deductions only for **genuine production inputs** that are necessary to produce the income stream and that
  do not constitute claim-layer extraction.

Deductible cost properties (all required in spirit; regime may parameterize thresholds):
- **necessity:** cost is required to produce the income (not optional consumption).
- **arm’s length:** cost is priced at market-equivalent terms; related-party costs require heightened scrutiny.
- **non-circularity:** cost does not route back to the same ultimate unit (no backflow).
- **boundedness:** cost is within normal bands for the activity/sector (prevent “cost inflation” to evade I-MAX).
- **auditability:** invoices/contracts exist; classification is reproducible.

Purpose: avoid a situation where high income can be turned into low I by purchasing self-serving “inputs”.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST define Gross I as the baseline and specify a bounded deduction list; broad “net income” bases
  are leaky and non-conform.
- If a claimed deduction cannot be audited and bounded, it is disallowed or triggers fail-closed conservative inclusion.

---

## B00.CH06.P05.S02 — Prohibited deductions: sham costs, internal fees, IP-royalty circles

Certain deductions are prohibited because they are structurally used to erode the base while preserving economic
benefit.

Prohibited deduction classes include (non-exhaustive):

- **Sham costs:** costs with no real service/product, inflated invoices, circular consulting.
- **Internal fees:** management fees, advisory fees, “licensing” fees charged by controlled entities that route value
  back to the same UBC unit.
- **IP-royalty circles:** royalties paid to an IP box or controlled holding that function as internal extraction.
- **Related-party rent:** rent paid to controlled property vehicles that are functionally self-payments.
- **Debt/interest engineering:** interest costs created primarily to reduce measured income while preserving control,
  especially where debt is intra-group or circular (linked to W/I arbitrage).
- **Expense-account personal consumption:** personal costs booked as business expenses.

Canonical rule:
- if the deduction routes value back to the same ultimate unit, or if it is priced off-market in a way that functions
  as E extraction or base erosion, it is prohibited and requalified into I.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime permitting internal fee deductions without strict related-party and backflow controls is non-conform.
- IP-royalty and management-fee loops must be detected and requalified automatically where evidence indicates circularity.

## B00.CH06.P05.S03 — Self-employed/firms: separation between P-costs and E-fee stacks

Self-employed and firm structures create the main seam between:
- **P-costs** (genuine production inputs),
- and **E-fee stacks** (internalized extraction layers that mimic costs).

Canonical separation rule:
- costs are deductible only if they are P-linked inputs needed for functional production, not E-driven claim layers.

Canonical checks (minimum):
- **functional test:** does the cost purchase real productive input (labour, materials, necessary services) or is it
  a claim-layer payment?
- **market band test:** is pricing within normal bands for the service/input?
- **relation test:** is the counterparty controlled/related? If yes, presumption shifts to non-deductible unless rebutted.
- **stack test:** do multiple fee layers exist (management → advisory → licensing) that cascade without new function?

Outcome:
- P-costs may be deducted within bounds.
- E-fee stack components are not deductible and may be requalified as I to the ultimate unit (and potentially as E-income).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST publish the P-cost vs E-fee separation logic; otherwise self-employment becomes a universal bypass.
- Black-box cost classifications are non-conform; audit trail per material cost category is required.

## B00.CH06.P05.S04 — Anti-mismatch: cross-border cost shifting and transfer pricing

Cross-border structures allow cost shifting:
- move income to low-I-MAX scope,
- move costs to high-I scope to reduce measured I,
- or use transfer pricing to relocate profit and fee streams.

Canonical anti-mismatch rule:
- where cross-border cost shifting is used to reduce I-base while economic activity and control remain domestic, costs
  are requalified and disallowed to the extent they are non-arm’s-length or circular.

Canonical controls (minimum set):
- **substance test:** does the foreign counterparty have real operations and independence?
- **beneficial attribution:** do the flows ultimately benefit/control the same unit?
- **transfer pricing bounds:** pricing must sit within declared arm’s-length bands; otherwise adjust to canonical bands.
- **IP box requalification:** royalties to low-tax/IP-box jurisdictions are scrutinized as internal extraction unless
  independence and function are demonstrated.
- **treaty shopping detection:** routing through intermediates without substance is treated as avoidance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include anti-mismatch controls for transfer pricing and cross-border internal fees; otherwise
  global actors can evade the cap by accounting geography.
- If cross-border arrangements cannot be audited due to opacity, fail-closed applies: conservative inclusion and/or
  disallowance of disputed deductions until resolved.
