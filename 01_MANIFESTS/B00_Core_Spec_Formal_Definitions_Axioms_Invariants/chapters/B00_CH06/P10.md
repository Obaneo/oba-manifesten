# B00.CH06.P10 — Interaction with P/E/C: E-income versus P-income

This paragraph defines how I-MAX interacts with the canonical **P/E/C** classification by distinguishing **E-income**
(extraction-derived) from **P-income** (function- and maintenance-linked). I-MAX caps flows regardless of narrative,
but P/E/C matters for:
- diagnostics (what the cap is braking),
- burden-of-proof in disputes and decompositions,
- and preventing “productive” rhetoric from legitimising extraction flows.

Core principle: the relevant distinction is **functional**, not rhetorical. Income classification must be auditable and
must not rely on intent stories, reputation, or ownership form.

---

## B00.CH06.P10.S01 — E-income: toll/lock-in fees, monopoly rents, platform take rates, fee stacks

**E-income** is income that derives primarily from extraction mechanisms: power, lock-in, claim stacks, and
information asymmetry, rather than from delivering function at bounded cost.

Canonical E-income channels include (non-exhaustive):
- **toll/lock-in fees:** charges above functional cost enabled by switching costs, exit friction, contractual lock.
- **monopoly and oligopoly rents:** markups sustained by entry barriers, concentration, concessions, or regulatory capture.
- **platform take rates:** gatekeeping fees enabled by ranking/curation power, API choke points, data leads, bundling.
- **fee stacks:** layered fees (management/advisory/licensing/processing) that cascade without added function.
- **rentier flows in essentials:** rents/interest/insurance compulsion inside essentials baskets that inflate L.
- **dark-pattern and deception yields:** revenue generated via manipulation, hidden pricing, bundling, and opacity.

E-income is not “illegal income”. It is functionally parasitic: it increases claim layers and reduces R/B through L
pressure. I-MAX is expected to brake the reward gradient for E-income.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations claiming P/E/C-aware I-MAX MUST be able to flag and report E-income channels (minimum E-indicators),
  even if cap application itself is agnostic to the label.
- If a regime treats E-income channels as unclassifiable by default, it must fail closed for disputed exclusions or
  favourable treatment claims.

---

## B00.CH06.P10.S02 — P-income: function-linked remuneration and maintenance-linked output

**P-income** is income attributable to production and maintenance: delivering function/value without toll mechanisms,
within bounded normal margin, including preserving capacity/quality (ΔG stabilisation).

Canonical P-income properties:
- remuneration is linked to deliverable function/value,
- pricing is within normal cost + bounded margin bands,
- the income stream is not primarily sustained by lock-in or monopoly power,
- maintenance outputs (repair, lifecycle service, reliability) count as P-linked.

Examples (functional categories, not labels):
- wages for essential labour,
- normal entrepreneurial profit within competitive bands,
- remuneration for measurable maintenance/service levels,
- returns attributable to genuine productivity improvements that remain contestable and non-toll.

P-income can still be capped by I-MAX; the distinction matters mainly for diagnostics, justification claims, and
conflict resolution where subjects assert that a component should be treated differently (e.g., mandatory reinvestment
instead of skimming).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any P-income claim used to seek favourable treatment MUST be supported by auditable evidence of bounded margins and
  absence of toll mechanisms.
- If function/cost linkage cannot be shown, the component is treated conservatively as non-P (included, and possibly E-flagged).

## B00.CH06.P10.S03 — Splitting mixed roles: executive comp, founder comp, IP royalty + labour

High-income packages are commonly mixed: part wage, part equity, part royalties, part fees, part capital return.
These mixtures are a primary avoidance surface.

Canonical mixed-role classes include:
- **executive compensation:** salary + bonus + LTIPs + options + perks + severance + consulting tail.
- **founder compensation:** equity appreciation + liquidity events + control rights + advisory fees.
- **IP royalty + labour:** creator works + licenses IP through controlled entity; royalties can function as wage substitute.
- **management + capital:** carried interest/performance fees plus own capital return.

Canonical split rule:
- decompose each component into functional subcomponents:
  - labour/service remuneration,
  - passive capital return (bounded),
  - control premium / extraction premium,
  - and in-kind/perk components.
- treat reclassified components in I-base accordingly; treat E-like premiums as E-income for reporting/diagnostics and
  dispute posture.

Default posture:
- where decomposition is not provided or not auditable, treat the entire mixed component conservatively as included and
  presume E-premium presence for diagnostic purposes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime MUST require decomposition for mixed roles where material; narrative classification is non-conform.
- If a subject claims a component is “capital” or “IP” to avoid inclusion or to seek favourable treatment, burden of
  proof is on the subject.

## B00.CH06.P10.S04 — Evidence standard: classification requires auditable decomposition (no black boxes)

Because P/E/C is functional and adversarially contested, classification must meet an explicit evidence standard.

Canonical evidence requirements:
- contracts and term sheets for remuneration and rights (including side letters),
- valuation basis for equity/option components (model + inputs where applicable),
- cost and margin evidence for “function-linked” claims (bounded margin bands),
- power/lock-in indicators for toll-like flows (market structure, switching costs, concession rights),
- traceable cashflow mapping and counterparty identification under UBC consolidation.

Prohibited:
- **black-box classification** (“trust us, it’s productive”),
- undisclosed valuation models,
- opaque waterfall structures that prevent attribution,
- discretionary “case-by-case” classification without reproducible rules.

Fail-closed posture:
- if evidence is insufficient, classification defaults to conservative inclusion and enforcement proceeds; favourable
  claims are denied until rebutted.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any implementation making P/E/C-linked I-MAX claims MUST be auditable end-to-end; otherwise it is non-conform.
- If a component cannot be decomposed reproducibly, the system must treat it as included and apply provisional
  remedies (escrow/lockbox/skim) until resolved.
