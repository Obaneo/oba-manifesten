# B00.CH06.P09 — Interaction with W-MAX: stock/flow arbitrage

This paragraph defines the canonical interaction between **I-MAX** (flow cap) and **W-MAX** (stock cap) and the
anti-arbitrage requirements that prevent value from becoming exempt by shifting between stock and flow forms.

Core principle: **no stock/flow arbitrage**. If value can move from I to W (or W to I) to escape caps, the cap system
is porous and non-auditable. Joint coherence requires:
- explicit detection rules for conversion channels,
- conflict rules to prevent double exemption or double counting,
- and combined audits using the UBC consolidation graph.

---

## B00.CH06.P09.S01 — Conversion I→W via buybacks, dividends, carry, equity grants (detection)

I→W conversion is the dominant avoidance path for a flow cap: reduce recognized income while increasing claim stock.

Canonical I→W conversion channels include (non-exhaustive):
- **share buybacks** that convert corporate flows into increased equity value rather than wages/dividends,
- **dividend and distribution design** that shifts timing or classification to reduce I recognition,
- **carried interest/performance allocations** that crystallise as asset value rather than direct income,
- **equity grants/options** that defer income into future appreciation or vesting,
- **retained earnings** in closely held entities that suppress distributions while increasing owner claim stock,
- **credit against unrealised gains** (borrowing against appreciation to fund consumption without recognizing I).

Detection requirements (minimum):
- reconcile subject’s changes in W (stock) against declared I (flows) over time: persistent W growth with suppressed I
  is a deferral/avoidance signal.
- detect disproportionate buyback activity as a substitute for wages/dividends in high-comp sectors.
- monitor equity-comp prevalence and vesting schedules for timing engineering.
- flag repeated low I near I-MAX with large unrecognized appreciation or retained earnings.

Canonical response:
- apply deferral attribution rules (CH06.P04), requalification (if components function as income), and W-MAX
  consolidation/valuation to capture the stock effect.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime claiming both I-MAX and W-MAX MUST implement cross-checks that detect I→W conversion pathways.
- If I can be suppressed via buybacks/retention/equity comp without triggering either I-MAX attribution or W-MAX
  capture, the combined regime is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH06.P09.S02 — Conversion W→I via management fees, royalties, rent extraction (detection)

W→I conversion is a second arbitrage direction: a capped wealth holder can extract flows in forms designed to appear
as deductible costs or “business revenue” while preserving control and potentially bypassing I-MAX classification.

Canonical W→I conversion channels include (non-exhaustive):
- **management fees** from controlled structures paid to the cap subject,
- **royalties** and IP licensing flows from entities operating on assets controlled by the subject,
- **rent extraction** from real assets held in vehicles (including related-party rent),
- **consulting/advisory fees** that substitute for dividends/salary,
- **interest and structured payoffs** engineered to look like neutral financing but function as rent.

Detection requirements (minimum):
- relate fee/royalty/rent flows to UBC control relations: if payor and payee are within the same control unit or
  within related-party networks, treat as high-risk for requalification.
- detect fee stacks: multiple layers of fees attached to the same underlying function.
- test whether flows are priced above functional cost bands (E indicator) and whether they increase L pressures.

Canonical response:
- treat such flows as in I-base (CH06.P03), disallow related-party deduction claims (CH06.P05), and classify as E-income
  where functionally toll-like (CH06.P10; CH03 E definition).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime MUST monitor W→I conversion channels and apply related-party and functional pricing tests; otherwise
  capped wealth becomes a rent machine that bypasses the flow cap via “business fees”.
- If royalties/rents can be routed through IP boxes or vehicles without consolidation, the regime is non-conform.

## B00.CH06.P09.S03 — Consistency: double exemption or double counting (conflict rules)

Joint caps require conflict rules to ensure that the same value is neither:
- exempt twice (excluded from both I and W),
- nor counted twice in a way that is inconsistent and non-reproducible.

Canonical consistency rules:

- **Mapping completeness:** every material value channel must map to at least one of:
  - I-base (flow),
  - W-base (stock),
  - or both with explicit reconciliation (e.g., income retained increases W).
  “Neither” channels are forbidden.

- **No double exemption:** if a flow is excluded from I-base under a carve-out, the resulting stock accumulation must
  not be exempt from W-base unless explicitly justified and tightly bounded (rare; capture risk).

- **No inconsistent double count:** if a flow is counted in I, and it directly increases W (e.g., after-tax saving),
  W should reflect the stock change but the system must avoid counting the same event as a separate “income event”
  twice. This is solved via clear reconciliation identities (stock-flow accounting).

- **Shared control conflicts:** where multiple units share benefit/control, apply declared allocation rules; unresolved
  ambiguity fails closed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any combined regime MUST publish its stock/flow mapping and reconciliation logic; otherwise enforcement becomes
  discretionary and contestability collapses.
- If the system permits channels that are neither I nor W (opaque perks, undisclosed vehicles), compatibility is suspended.

## B00.CH06.P09.S04 — Joint enforcement: combined audits and cross-checks (UBC graph)

I-MAX and W-MAX must be enforceable as a **joint system**. Joint enforcement uses the same underlying **UBC graph**
(control/benefit/block) and the same disclosure infrastructure.

Canonical joint enforcement requirements:

- **Shared consolidation graph:** UBC relations used for W must also attribute I across entities/vehicles.
- **Cross-checks:**
  - ΔW (change in stock) vs declared I (flows),
  - retained earnings vs distributions and compensation,
  - derivative exposure changes vs reported income,
  - related-party fees/royalties vs deductible cost claims.
- **Combined audits:** audits must be able to trace a value channel across time and form: grant → vest → exercise →
  holding → sale, or profit → retention → buyback → appreciation → collateralized borrowing.
- **Event-driven coordination:** major events (IPO, buyback, restructuring, migration) trigger both I and W reassessment.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime claiming both caps MUST implement combined audits and cross-checks using the UBC graph; separate siloed
  enforcement is leaky by design.
- If disclosure is insufficient to perform joint reconciliation, fail-closed posture applies and compatibility claims
  may be suspended until remediation.
