# B00.CH06.P11 — Cross-border income and residency

This paragraph defines the cross-border and residency rules for **I-MAX** to prevent leakage via **tax arbitrage**,
**treaty shopping**, and **re-domiciliation**. A flow cap is only enforceable if cross-border income and residency
are consistently applied and reported, with **substance-over-form** tests for cross-border arrangements and
**source rules** that prevent strategic jurisdiction-hopping.

The **UBC (Ultimate Beneficial Control) graph** and cross-border regulations must align with **capital controls**
and **payment rails** to ensure I-MAX cannot be bypassed through global reallocation or shifting of income sources.

---

## B00.CH06.P11.S01 — Residency and source rules: where income counts

**Residency** and **source rules** must clearly define where income is attributed, especially for cross-border workers,
entities, or income sources. These rules are necessary to prevent subjects from artificially relocating income to low-tax
jurisdictions while retaining effective control or benefit.

Canonical residency tests:
- **Centre of life test** (where the subject lives, works, and has personal ties),
- **Centre of control test** (where key decisions, work performance, and economic activities are directed),
- **Place of incorporation or control** for business-related income (for entities, vehicles, trusts).

Source rules:
- **Income source** is determined by where the economic value is generated, not where the income is formally reported
  (e.g., where the labour is performed, where the intellectual property is created, where assets generate value).
- **Cross-border income:** If a person/subject maintains residency in one jurisdiction but generates income in another,
  the income must be allocated based on economic substance, not just formal location or treaty definitions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include clearly defined residency and source rules, using UBC-style economic control/benefit
  tests.
- If income is reported to be sourced outside the relevant jurisdiction while economic activity and control are inside,
  it must be requalified under fail-closed rules (conservative inclusion).

## B00.CH06.P11.S02 — Re-domiciliation, treaty shopping and IP boxes: anti-mismatch and requalification

**Re-domiciliation**, **treaty shopping**, and **IP boxes** can allow high-income subjects to reassign income flows
and assets to low-tax jurisdictions while maintaining effective control.

Canonical rules:
- **Re-domiciliation:** if a subject moves jurisdiction or entity domicile solely for tax avoidance reasons, the regime
  must treat the income as originating from the original jurisdiction, and apply requalification measures.
- **Treaty shopping:** using jurisdictional treaties to route income through intermediates without genuine economic
  substance is non-conform.
- **IP boxes:** income moved to “IP holding boxes” or low-tax entities for royalty income must be scrutinized as part of
  UBC look-through. These income channels must be classified as either **E-income** (extraction-related) or **W-equivalent**
  where value is artificially routed.

Anti-mismatch principle:
- income must be assigned to its actual economic source, not formal domicile. Any attempt to route through low-tax zones
  or carve-out jurisdictions must trigger requalification and anti-mismatch tests.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include **anti-mismatch** rules for re-domiciliation, treaty shopping, and IP boxes, with
  **look-through** for UBC.
- If cross-border income allocation cannot be verified, the system must treat it as requalified and apply provisional
  enforcement until resolved.

## B00.CH06.P11.S03 — Remote work and digital services: source determination and platform components

**Remote work** and **digital services** create new cross-border income dynamics, especially as work is performed
and paid across jurisdictions without physical presence.

Canonical rules:
- **Source of income**: for remote workers, income should be attributed to the jurisdiction where the work is actually
  performed (not the employer's location).
- **Platform income**: for gig economy and digital platforms, income should be attributed based on where the service is
  provided and consumed (e.g., digital services provided in jurisdiction A but paid to a platform in jurisdiction B
  should be taxed according to jurisdiction A’s source rules).
- **Service revenue**: services rendered remotely should be treated as sourced from where the worker (subject) is
  performing the work, not where the client or platform is based.

Digital platforms (e.g., online marketplaces) must disclose income streams, even if those flows cross borders. **UBC**
look-through applies for services rendered via such platforms.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include **source determination** rules for remote work and digital services, allocating income
  to the jurisdiction where work is performed.
- If digital platforms fail to provide traceable records of income or enforceable tax obligations, fail-closed enforcement
  applies until clarified.

## B00.CH06.P11.S04 — Coherence with capital controls/payment rails (consistency requirement)

**I-MAX enforcement** and **cross-border income attribution** must be compatible with **capital controls** and
**payment rails**.

Canonical requirements:
- **Capital controls coherence:** I-MAX must be enforceable in jurisdictions with **capital controls**. If a jurisdiction
  applies capital restrictions (e.g., restrictions on large-scale capital outflows or inflows), I-MAX must be enforced
  using transparent channels that comply with such controls.
- **Payment rails integration:** enforcement of I-MAX cannot be bypassed through opaque **payment rails** (untraceable
  platforms, offshore custodians, or informal channels). Payment systems must support transparency and traceability
  of income flows for enforcement.

The regime must specify **cross-border payment integration** and **enforcement coordination** to ensure compliance with
both national and international standards.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST ensure consistency with capital controls and payment rails to prevent income leakage.
- If capital flows can bypass detection or tax rules due to non-coherent payment systems or unreported custody, fail-closed
  enforcement applies and compatibility claims are suspended.
