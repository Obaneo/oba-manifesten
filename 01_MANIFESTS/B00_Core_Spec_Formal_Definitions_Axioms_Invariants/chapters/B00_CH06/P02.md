# B00.CH06.P02 — Canonical definition of I (income/flow)

This paragraph defines **I** as the canonical OBA variable for **income/flow**. I is not “taxable income” and not a
jurisdiction-specific accounting artifact. It is a functional, time-indexed flow of economic benefit attributable to
an ultimate unit, measured under explicit resolution, valuation, attribution, and consolidation rules.

Canonical purpose: enable an auditable I-base and I-MAX enforcement that cannot be bypassed by relabeling, deferral,
or vehicle layering.

---

## B00.CH06.P02.S01 — I as flow per time period t (year/month) with standard resolutions

**I** is a **flow variable** defined over a time period *t* at a declared resolution.

Canonical form:
- **I(t; r)** where *r* is the resolution (e.g., yearly, monthly).

Standard resolutions (minimum supported):
- **Annual** (r = year): default for most policy and distribution reporting.
- **Monthly** (r = month): required where bonus cycles, vesting, platform payouts, or volatility makes annual-only
  measures leaky.
- Additional higher-frequency resolutions may be used, but MUST map cleanly to the standard set via explicit
  aggregation rules.

Type discipline:
- I is a **flow**; aggregation must respect the time base (sum of flows over aligned periods).
- If annualization or normalization is used, it MUST be explicit (no implicit annualization).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX implementation MUST declare the time resolution used for enforcement and reporting.
- If a component is measured at a different cadence, the conversion/aggregation method MUST be explicit and
  reproducible; otherwise usage is ambiguous and must fail closed.

---

## B00.CH06.P02.S02 — Cash vs in-kind: canonical valuation rules

I includes both **cash** and **in-kind** economic benefit when the benefit is consumption-equivalent or can be
converted into value for the subject.

### Cash income
- measured at amount received/realized under the attribution rule (S03), with currency/unit declared.

### In-kind income (benefits, perks, services)
Included when the subject receives economic benefit not captured as cash but equivalent to consumption or value.

Canonical in-kind valuation principles:
- **Fair value of benefit** to the subject, using observable market equivalents where possible.
- **No “cost to provider” default** if it understates the real benefit (e.g., company jet use at marginal cost).
- Where valuation is uncertain, apply **conservative valuation** (upper-bound within auditable band) or require
  independent valuation.

In-kind includes (non-exhaustive):
- housing provided, transport, personal services, private insurance/premiums paid, access rights, expense-account
  spending that is personal consumption, and preferential purchase rights where value is material.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include in-kind benefits and define valuation rules; excluding perks is a leaky base.
- If in-kind value cannot be audited, the component is included conservatively or triggers fail-closed posture.

---

## B00.CH06.P02.S03 — Realisation and attribution moment: accrual vs cash (canonical choice)

I-MAX requires a canonical **realisation/attribution moment** to prevent timing avoidance (deferral, bonus banks,
vesting games). Jurisdictions may use cash or accrual for tax, but OBA needs a rule that is enforceable and
anti-deferral by design.

Canonical choice (binding default):
- **Attribution to the earn-year / entitlement period** (accrual-like) where feasible, with explicit rules for
  vesting and conditionality.

Operationally:
- income is attributed to the period in which the subject **earns** the entitlement (or the service/return is
  generated), not necessarily when paid.
- where entitlement is contingent (e.g., vesting), the regime must specify whether attribution occurs:
  - at grant (with discount for forfeiture probability),
  - at vesting (most common enforceable boundary),
  - or through a look-back allocation method upon payout.

The regime must prevent a simple bypass where I is “low” every year because value is deferred and then realized in a
single engineered period or outside residency.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX implementation MUST specify a canonical attribution rule (cash vs accrual vs hybrid look-back) and must
  include anti-deferral controls; silence is non-conform.
- If attribution cannot be established from disclosed schedules, the component is included in the earliest plausible
  period conservatively (fail-closed) or triggers provisional enforcement.

## B00.CH06.P02.S04 — Consolidation: income across entities/vehicles (substance over form)

I must be consolidated across entities and vehicles to the **ultimate benefit unit**. Otherwise I-MAX can be bypassed
by routing flows through companies, trusts, family offices, partnerships, or offshore vehicles.

Canonical consolidation rule:
- attribute income to the unit that ultimately controls/benefits (UBC-style: control/benefit/block), regardless of
  legal payee.

Consolidation includes (non-exhaustive):
- wages paid to controlled entities,
- management fees routed through SPVs,
- profit distributions retained in controlled holding structures,
- royalties and IP fees to controlled IP boxes,
- carried interest allocations to vehicles,
- nominee arrangements and family dispersion where economic reality is unified.

Conflict handling:
- where multiple units share benefit/control, allocation must follow declared conflict rules to prevent double counting
  or strategic ambiguity; unresolved ambiguity fails closed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST consolidate income across entities/vehicles; ownership-only or payee-only treatment is leaky
  and non-conform.
- If beneficial attribution cannot be established due to opacity, conservative attribution applies and compatibility
  claims for the affected scope may be suspended until disclosure is remediated.
