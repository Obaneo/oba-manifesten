# B00.CH06.P08 — Interaction with wage formation and working time (link to lived world)

This paragraph defines how I-MAX interacts with **wage formation** and **working time** as lived-world order
variables. I-MAX is not only a fiscal constraint; it changes incentive gradients in labour markets and therefore
affects:
- bargaining power and reservation conditions,
- allocation of labour across roles and sectors,
- the prevalence of high-pay “coordination/administration/finance” roles versus maintenance and essentials work,
- and the feasible reduction of working hours without collapsing maintenance capacity.

Canonical posture: I-MAX must be evaluated not only by top-share compression, but by its effect on **R/B floors**,
labour allocation into P and C functions, and the reduction of E-driven “bullshit job” incentives.

---

## B00.CH06.P08.S01 — Reservation condition: R/B floor changes bargaining space

R/B (and the α floor regime) changes the worker’s reservation condition: the minimum acceptable compensation and
conditions to participate in labour contracts.

Mechanism:
- if R/B floors improve (via L reductions and commons capacity), workers are less forced into low-quality or
extractive employment to survive.
- I-MAX interacts by constraining top-end capture, reducing the ability of high-income actors to bid labour away from
essentials and maintenance purely via extreme pay.

Canonical linkage:
- **R/B floor** sets the baseline bargaining space;
- **I-MAX** caps the upper tail, reducing distortions where extreme compensation becomes the primary allocator.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime claiming OBA compatibility MUST specify how it is expected to interact with R/B floors (CH04),
  including measurable indicators (reservation wage proxies, job-quality distribution, essentials staffing).
- If the regime compresses top pay but R/B floors remain structurally below α for large cohorts, the “order” claim is
  incomplete and requires diagnostic.

---

## B00.CH06.P08.S02 — Wage compression: effect on labour allocation, “bullshit job” incentives, scarcity

I-MAX compresses the wage distribution at the top end. This has structural effects:

- reduces the payoff to roles whose value is largely extraction-enabled (rent-seeking, fee-stack engineering, monopoly
  optimization, regulatory arbitrage),
- reduces bidding power that pulls scarce talent into E-heavy sectors,
- can reallocate labour toward P and C functions (maintenance, essentials, infra, care) if complementary institutions
  and demand exist.

However, wage compression can also create scarcity responses if:
- essential work remains low-status/under-resourced,
- or if compensation is shifted into perks or non-wage channels.

Canonical evaluation lens:
- measure not only wage compression, but **labour allocation shifts**: where high-skill labour goes, and whether
  essentials/maintenance capacity improves.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Regimes MUST monitor for substitution into non-wage compensation (perks, equity wrappers) and treat it as avoidance
  unless canonically included (links to P03 and P07.S04).
- If compression leads to essential-sector scarcity, the regime must respond via C-building and maintenance-first
  policies, not by opening sector privilege exceptions (forbidden variation).

---

## B00.CH06.P08.S03 — Time as order variable: hours reduction and maintenance capacity (linkage)

Working time is an order variable because it shapes:
- life-world autonomy,
- maintenance capacity (family care, health, community participation),
- and the balance between production and recovery (ΔG stabilisation).

I-MAX can indirectly enable working-time reduction by:
- reducing top-end wage competition that forces long-hour norms upward in high-pay sectors,
- supporting redistribution into commons and R/B floors that reduce survival overwork pressure,
- weakening extraction-driven business models that require long-hour labour extraction.

Canonical linkage:
- if I-MAX + C-building improves R/B floors, reduced hours can become feasible without pushing cohorts below α.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any OBA-compatible I-MAX regime SHOULD specify how working-time outcomes are expected to change (hours distribution,
  part-time feasibility, burnout proxies), even if not directly regulated by I-MAX.
- If the regime drives hours reduction only for privileged cohorts while essentials labour becomes more strained, the
  order claim is suspect and requires correction via commons/maintenance policy.

---

## B00.CH06.P08.S04 — Forbidden compensation: avoidance via non-wage perks or price hikes in essentials

Compression creates incentives for compensatory channels. Two are explicitly forbidden as bypass mechanisms:

1) **Non-wage perks substitution**
- shifting pay into housing, vehicles, services, expense accounts, preferential rights, or opaque equity structures
  to keep measured I below I-MAX.
- canonical response: requalification into I-base or W-equivalent as appropriate (P02.S02, P03.S04).

2) **Price hikes in essentials**
- firms or high-income actors attempt to restore returns via price increases/monopoly strategies in essentials,
  raising L and negating R/B gains.
- canonical response: treat as E-regrowth/compensation and escalate under P/E/C diagnostics and anti-gaming rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST treat perks substitution as in-scope income (or requalify as W-equivalent) and apply fail-closed
  rules to opaque packages.
- Any regime that tolerates compensatory essentials price inflation while claiming I-MAX improves order is non-conform;
  monitoring and escalation must be specified (link to CH04 L discipline and CH03 E diagnostics).
