# B00.CH06.P13 — Enforcement and dispute regime

This paragraph outlines the **enforcement chain** and **dispute regime** for I-MAX, ensuring that the system is
effective in preventing avoidance while offering a **transparent** and **fair** process for subject challenge and
remediation. Enforcement is not discretionary but follows a strict, auditable chain of actions, from detection to
audit.

### Key principle:
Enforcement must be **operational**, **transparent**, and **non-discretionary**, with clear procedural guarantees to
prevent avoidance via delays or discretionary settlements.

---

## B00.CH06.P13.S01 — Enforcement chain: detection → assessment → cap event → remedy → audit

The **enforcement chain** must be followed rigorously and be fully auditable, ensuring that a cap is **enforced** in a
transparent manner. The steps in the chain are as follows:

### Step 1: Detection
- **Triggering event**: I is measured and compared to I-MAX at defined intervals (e.g., annual, monthly) and event-driven
  triggers (major financial events such as bonuses, vesting, and buybacks).
- **Initial detection**: The breach of I-MAX is detected by the system through periodic reporting or event-based reporting.

### Step 2: Assessment
- **Review and analysis**: Once a cap event is triggered, a full assessment of the subject’s income must occur. This includes
  consolidating income sources, assessing whether the reported income aligns with the subject’s total income and ensuring
  the correct valuation and timing methods were applied.
- **Requalification and verification**: Any discrepancies, gaps, or uncertainties are flagged and addressed by requalifying
  components or applying conservative default inclusion measures (fail-closed posture).

### Step 3: Cap event declaration
- **Declaration of breach**: Once the assessment confirms that I exceeds I-MAX, a formal declaration of the cap event is made.
  The event should be publicly recorded and include the exact amount of the breach, the components of income, and any
  requalification steps.

### Step 4: Remedy
- **Immediate remedy application**: The regime must apply appropriate remedies to bring I back within the cap. This may
  include skimming, conversion of excess income to commons (C), or other remedies as defined in the regime (e.g., mandatory
  reinvestment, lockbox).
- **Timeliness**: Remedies must be implemented promptly, and the subject must comply within a defined period.

### Step 5: Audit
- **Post-remedy audit**: After the remedy is applied, an audit is conducted to ensure that the remedy was correctly
  executed and the subject’s income remains within the prescribed limits.
- **Public disclosure and transparency**: Relevant information about the cap event and the remedies applied must be made
  publicly available, while respecting privacy constraints.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- The **enforcement chain** must be clearly defined and auditable at every stage. Any breach of this chain (e.g., delayed
  enforcement, lack of assessment, or failure to audit) results in non-conformity, and the regime’s compatibility claim
  must be suspended.
- **Failure to detect breaches** and enforce remedies promptly is non-conform and must trigger corrective measures.

## B00.CH06.P13.S02 — Objection/appeal: contestability without delay-as-avoidance

While enforcement must be effective, the system must also allow for **contestability** in cases of **misclassification**
or **error**, without allowing **delayed avoidance**.

### Contestability rules:
- **Right to appeal**: The subject must have the ability to **contest** an enforcement decision. This includes objections to
  income classifications, requalification decisions, remedy executions, and valuation methods.
- **No delay-as-avoidance**: Filing an appeal cannot automatically halt enforcement. Delays should not function as a
  strategy to bypass I-MAX. Instead, the burden of proof is on the subject to demonstrate a valid reason for the appeal
  and provide necessary evidence within a defined timeline.
- **Clear timelines**: Objections must be filed within a reasonable period, and responses from enforcement authorities
  must be made within a defined time.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include an **appeal process** that allows contestability, but this process MUST NOT suspend
  enforcement without a clear, verifiable reason.
- If an appeal is filed, **remedies** must continue to be enforced in the interim, and failure to enforce during the
  contestability process results in **fail-closed** treatment.

## B00.CH06.P13.S03 — Proportionality: procedural guarantees, but no “discretionary deals”

I-MAX enforcement must comply with the **principle of proportionality**, ensuring that enforcement actions and
penalties are **appropriate**, **fair**, and not **discretionary**.

### Key elements of proportionality:
- **Consistency and fairness**: Enforcement actions should apply **equally** across all subjects, based on the same
  standards. No **discretionary exceptions** should be allowed based on status, lobbying, or sector.
- **Proportional penalties**: Penalties for non-compliance must be proportional to the severity of the breach, the level
  of cooperation, and the intent behind the violation.
- **Procedural guarantees**: The enforcement process should be fair, transparent, and free from external influence or
  bias. Clear rules must govern the process, and subjects must be notified promptly of any enforcement actions.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime that allows **discretionary enforcement** or exceptions based on subjective factors (e.g., political
  lobbying, sector influence) is **non-conform**.
- Enforcement actions and penalties must follow **clear, transparent rules**. Any failure to apply proportionality will
  trigger **hard stops** (CH06.P13.S04).

## B00.CH06.P13.S04 — Hard stops: obstruction, non-disclosure, black-box compensation → non-conform status

**Hard stops** are triggered when a subject **obstructs** the enforcement process or engages in **non-disclosure** or
**black-box compensation**.

### Hard stop triggers:
- **Obstruction of enforcement**: Actions that intentionally hinder the enforcement process, including refusal to
  cooperate with audits or providing false/misleading information.
- **Non-disclosure**: Failure to disclose income components, deferral schedules, related-party transactions, or
  vehicle structures necessary for proper enforcement.
- **Black-box compensation**: Opaque compensation packages where components cannot be audibly classified or valued
  (e.g., undisclosed equity grants, bonus banks, side letters).

### Hard stop consequence:
- If any of the above triggers occur, the subject or regime is **non-compliant**, and the **compatibility claim** is
  **suspended** until the issue is resolved.
- Remedies for non-compliance may include **fail-closed enforcement**, **provisional inclusion**, or **audit suspension**,
  depending on the severity of the failure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime that allows obstruction, non-disclosure, or black-box compensation without remediation will be considered
  **non-conform**.
- **Hard stops** must be automatically triggered when evidence of obstruction or non-cooperation is found, and
  **compatibility claims must be suspended** until remediation is complete and verified.
