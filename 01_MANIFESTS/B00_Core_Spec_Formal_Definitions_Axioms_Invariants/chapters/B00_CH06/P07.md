# B00.CH06.P07 — Cap mechanics: what happens above I-MAX

This paragraph specifies the canonical **cap mechanics** for I-MAX: what occurs when consolidated **I** exceeds
**I-MAX** within the enforcement period. A flow cap is only an order instrument if breach handling is:
- explicit (defined triggers and stages),
- non-discretionary (no bespoke deals),
- auditable (reproducible derivations),
- and robust against deferral, relabeling, and mixed-component ambiguity.

I-MAX cap mechanics define: trigger conditions, remedy set (skimming/conversion/commons reinvestment), timing duties
and burden-of-proof logic, and fail-closed treatment for unclear income components.

---

## B00.CH06.P07.S01 — Trigger: periodic breaches and event-driven triggers

A cap event is triggered when:

- **I_consolidated(t) > I-MAX(t)** under the regime’s definition of I, attribution moment, and consolidation boundary.

Triggering must occur through two channels:

### (a) Periodic assessment (baseline)
- at the declared cadence (annual/monthly/rolling), compute consolidated I and compare to I-MAX.
- periodic assessment must specify reference dates, aggregation windows, and reconciliation checks.

### (b) Event-driven triggers (anti-gaming)
Event triggers are mandatory because many high-income channels are episodic or engineered:

- large bonuses and severance packages,
- equity vesting/option exercise events,
- major profit distributions, carried interest crystallisation,
- liquidity events (sale/IPO/buybacks),
- large royalty/license payments,
- major changes in entity structure (new SPV, re-domiciliation, trust creation),
- cross-border moves that shift where income is booked.

Event triggers require immediate reassessment or provisional inclusion to prevent “wait until year-end and rewrap.”

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST define both periodic and event-driven triggers; periodic-only enforcement is leaky.
- Trigger rules MUST be stated so implementations cannot “choose” when a breach exists.

## B00.CH06.P07.S02 — Remedy options: skimming, conversion to C, mandatory reinvestment in commons (canonical)

When I exceeds I-MAX, the regime must apply a canonical remedy set. Remedies must neutralize excess flow capture and
prevent simple form shifts into W or into opaque compensation.

Canonical remedy options (regime must specify availability and constraints; non-discretionary within the chosen form):

1) **Skimming of excess I**
- automatic capture of a defined portion/function of **(I − I-MAX)**.
- skimming function must be explicit (linear, progressive, piecewise) and auditable.

2) **Conversion to C (commons contribution)**
- excess (or skim proceeds) may be converted into C only under admissibility conditions:
  - traceable channel,
  - defined commons object,
  - ΔG output reporting,
  - anti-backflow controls (mirrors the W-MAX → C admissibility logic).

3) **Mandatory reinvestment in commons / maintenance capacity**
- impose mandatory allocation of excess to commons/maintenance projects or public option capacity, with:
  - maintenance-first/lifecycle provisioning,
  - governance that prevents capture and private toll conversion,
  - independent audit and publication of outputs.

Anti-arbitrage constraint:
- remedies must block immediate conversion of excess I into exempt W channels (e.g., “retain in company” loophole),
  by linking to deferral rules (P04) and W-MAX cross-checks (P09).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime MUST specify a remedy set and enforce at least one remedy path that cannot be bypassed by relabeling or
  entity retention.
- C conversion without ΔG traceability is non-conform; it is accounting relabeling.

## B00.CH06.P07.S03 — Timing and burden of proof: who demonstrates that a component is P and not E

I-MAX interacts with P/E/C because many high flows are justified as “productive reward” while functioning as
extraction. Therefore cap mechanics must define burden of proof for classification and decomposition.

Canonical rules:

- **Timing duty:** remedies must be executed within defined windows; delay cannot function as avoidance.
- **Decomposition duty:** where income components are mixed (labour + capital + IP + fees), the subject must provide an
  auditable decomposition.
- **Burden of proof on the subject:** the subject must demonstrate that a disputed component is:
  - function-linked remuneration (P-income) rather than E-income,
  - priced within normal bands,
  - not a fee-stack or monopoly rent channel,
  - and not a disguised perk/benefit.
- **Default presumption:** absent evidence, treat disputed components as included and (where relevant) as E-driven.

This burden-of-proof discipline prevents “interpretation by narrative”.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST assign burden of proof to the subject for exclusions or favourable classifications.
- If decomposition is opaque (black-box comp, undisclosed side letters), conservative inclusion and enforcement
  escalation apply.

## B00.CH06.P07.S04 — Fail-closed: unclear income components are included until clarified

I measurement includes uncertainties (valuation of in-kind benefits, equity comp models, carried interest waterfalls,
cross-border attribution). Under OBA, uncertainty cannot become exemption.

Fail-closed rule (canonical):
- if an income component cannot be classified, valued, or attributed within auditable bounds, it is **included**
  conservatively until clarified.

Typical fail-closed triggers:
- missing or non-reproducible valuation for equity comp/options,
- undisclosed deferral schedules or bonus bank terms,
- opaque partnerships/fund structures or side letters,
- ambiguous related-party fee chains,
- uncertain source/residency attribution in cross-border cases.

Fail-closed enforcement tools:
- provisional skimming,
- escrow/lockbox of disputed amounts,
- conservative valuation bands,
- temporary treatment as E-income pending rebuttal.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST implement fail-closed rules; otherwise opacity is rewarded.
- Persistent non-clarification triggers hard-stop non-conform status and may suspend compatibility claims for the
  subject/channel until remediation.
