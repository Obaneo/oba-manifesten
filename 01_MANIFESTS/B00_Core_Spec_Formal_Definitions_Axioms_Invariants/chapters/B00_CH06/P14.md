# B00.CH06.P14 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph defines **failure modes** and **hard stops** for the I-MAX cap regime. These conditions occur when the
regime’s enforcement or measurement capacity is bypassed or when components of I-MAX are excluded from enforcement.
Failure modes reflect **systemic weaknesses** that allow for circumvention, and hard stops are triggered when
non-compliance or structural audit failure occurs.

### Key principle:
I-MAX is a cap that operates based on **economic reality**. If the cap can be bypassed by excluding major income
components or by gaming the system, the regime cannot claim OBA conformance. Hard stops are non-negotiable and suspend
the regime’s compatibility claim until remediation.

---

## B00.CH06.P14.S01 — I-MAX-washing: cap exists but major components are excluded

**I-MAX-washing** occurs when the regime claims to enforce a cap but excludes **major income components** or **income
streams** that should logically fall under the cap.

Canonical leakage sources (non-exhaustive):
- **Income reclassification**: excluding key components such as stock-based compensation, carried interest, royalties,
  or related-party management fees that should be considered income but are excluded from the cap.
- **Inconsistent base inclusion**: certain income channels may be excluded (e.g., perks, deferred compensation) while
  others are included, leading to a misrepresentation of the subject’s income.

I-MAX-washing detection:
- compare the declared income base against well-established categories of income (wages, bonuses, royalties, dividends,
  carried interest) to ensure all relevant components are included.
- monitor for **systemic exclusions** where subjects consistently report low I but have high potential income through
  deferred, reclassified, or opaque compensation channels.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If major components are excluded from the I-MAX cap without a canonical justification (e.g., legitimate carve-outs),
  the regime is **non-compliant**.
- The **compatibility claim** is suspended until the missing components are properly included in the income base.

---

## B00.CH06.P14.S02 — Gaming: deferral, relabelling, splitting across family/nominees/entities

**Gaming** occurs when subjects manipulate the system by **delaying income recognition**, **relabeling income**, or
**splitting income across entities** to evade the I-MAX cap.

Canonical gaming patterns include (non-exhaustive):
- **Deferral:** shifting income into future periods (e.g., deferred compensation, stock options, carried interest) to
  reduce taxable income in the current period while still accumulating wealth.
- **Relabelling:** reclassifying income as something other than wages (e.g., turning salary into royalties, consulting
  fees, or passive income) to avoid caps.
- **Income splitting:** distributing income across family members, nominee entities, or different jurisdictions to stay
  below the cap while maintaining control and benefit.

Canonical response:
- I-MAX must have clear attribution and **deferral rules** to prevent these actions, and a system of **look-through**
  to consolidate income in cases where splitting or relabelling is used to obscure true income.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If income gaming (e.g., deferral, relabelling, splitting) is detected, the subject is considered **non-compliant** and
  the regime’s compatibility claim is **suspended**.
- The **fail-closed posture** applies, and any gaming behavior that artificially lowers measured I is flagged for requalification.

---

## B00.CH06.P14.S03 — Capture: carve-outs, special regimes, sector privileges

**Capture** occurs when the system allows for **discretionary carve-outs**, **sector-specific exceptions**, or **special
regimes** that undermine the effectiveness of I-MAX and create privileged treatment for certain subjects or sectors.

Canonical capture indicators include (non-exhaustive):
- **Status-based carve-outs**: exceptions granted to high-status or politically influential sectors (e.g., “finance
  exception”, “tech exception”).
- **Sectoral privileges**: specific sectors (e.g., technology, finance) given preferential treatment with looser
  restrictions or thresholds for I-MAX.
- **Special regimes**: sectors or groups granted specific exemptions or relaxed rules for determining income, leading to
  arbitrary and non-transparent treatment.

Canonical anti-capture rule:
- Any **exception** or **carve-out** must be **function-justified**, **parameterised**, and **auditable** through the
  **Kernspec procedure** (CH06.P06.S04).
- Capture through **sector privilege** or **status-based exceptions** is strictly forbidden and must be considered a
  violation of OBA principles.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If carve-outs, sector exceptions, or privileged treatment is introduced outside of the canonical change procedure, the
  regime is **non-compliant**.
- The **compatibility claim** is suspended until such exceptions are removed or ratified through the formal procedure.

---

## B00.CH06.P14.S04 — Fail-closed: structural audit failure suspends compatibility claim

A **structural audit failure** occurs when the I-MAX regime cannot consistently enforce or measure compliance due to
persistent **opaque structures**, **missing disclosures**, or **inconsistent application** of rules.

Canonical structural failure conditions include (non-exhaustive):
- **Opaque compensation structures**: where key income components (e.g., equity-based compensation, royalties, perks)
  cannot be fully disclosed or traced.
- **Incomplete disclosures**: failure to report full income streams or to consolidate income across related entities or
  nominees (see CH06.P11 for UBC).
- **Non-reproducible attribution methods**: failure to apply consistent and auditable rules for income attribution,
  particularly for mixed-income components (e.g., executive comp with equity).

Fail-closed rule:
- If a subject’s income cannot be traced or reproduced due to structural failure (e.g., black-box pay packages, opaque
  vehicles), the system will **fail-closed**. This means the component will be included conservatively, or provisional
  enforcement will apply until clarification.
- If structural failure persists, the **compatibility claim** for the entire regime is **suspended**.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- Any **structural audit failure** (e.g., black-box compensation, non-disclosure) automatically triggers a **hard stop**.
  The subject or regime is considered **non-compliant**, and the compatibility claim is suspended until remediation is
  verified and the subject complies with disclosure and attribution rules.
