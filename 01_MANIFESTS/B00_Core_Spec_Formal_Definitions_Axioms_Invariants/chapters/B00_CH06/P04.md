# B00.CH06.P04 — Realisation and deferral: anti-avoidance in time

This paragraph defines the canonical **time anti-avoidance layer** for I-MAX: how income is attributed across time
to prevent deferral, lock-in, and engineered volatility from bypassing the cap.

I-MAX is a cap on flows per period. Therefore timing is an attack surface: subjects can delay recognition (deferral),
convert into unrealised appreciation (lock-in), or smooth/shape flows to keep measured I below I-MAX while preserving
economic benefit and W build-up.

Canonical posture: the system must attribute income to the period in which it is earned or economically accrued,
or apply explicit alternative timing rules (e.g., mark-to-market) that are auditable and anti-deferral by design.

---

## B00.CH06.P04.S01 — Deferred compensation: attribution to earn-year (look-back)

Deferred compensation is any arrangement where economic value is earned over time but paid later, often via vesting,
bonus banks, deferred equity, carried interest crystallisation, or entity-retained distributions.

Canonical rule:
- deferred comp must be attributed to the **earn-year(s)** in which it was earned, not only to the payout year,
  using a declared attribution method.

Canonical attribution methods (regime must choose one or define an auditable hybrid):

1) **Accrual allocation:** allocate value pro rata over the earning period (service period) as it accrues.
2) **Vesting allocation:** attribute at vesting, with optional look-back smoothing over service period if needed.
3) **Look-back upon payout:** when paid/realized, reallocate backward to the relevant earn-years using disclosed
   schedules and a canonical allocation rule.

Anti-avoidance requirements:
- if deferral is used systematically to keep annual I below I-MAX, the regime must apply look-back reallocation or
  interim provisional inclusion.
- residency changes and cross-border payouts do not void attribution; earned income remains attributable under
  residency/source rules (see CH06.P11).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST define how deferred compensation is attributed to earn-year(s); payout-only treatment is leaky.
- Missing deferral schedules trigger fail-closed: conservative allocation or provisional enforcement until disclosed.

## B00.CH06.P04.S02 — Capital gains timing: anti–lock-in effects, mark-to-market options (canonical)

Capital gains create lock-in incentives: subjects can hold appreciated assets indefinitely to suppress realized I,
then realize outside the regime window (or outside residency). This defeats a flow cap unless timing rules are explicit.

Canonical requirement:
- capital gains treatment must be explicit and must include an anti-lock-in mechanism.

Canonical options (regime must select and publish; choice must be stable within release):

### Option A — Realisation-based with anti-lock-in triggers
- gains count in I when realized (sale/disposal), but anti-avoidance triggers apply, such as:
  - deemed realization on major collateralization/borrowing against gains,
  - deemed realization on exit/migration,
  - deemed realization on certain restructures or conversions.

### Option B — Mark-to-market (periodic revaluation)
- gains/losses are included in I as the period change in value, using valuation discipline and bands for illiquids.
- requires robust valuation rules; otherwise it becomes a manipulation surface (links to valuation discipline).

### Option C — Hybrid
- realization as baseline + mark-to-market for specified asset classes or thresholds (e.g., publicly traded),
  with anti-lock-in triggers for others.

Anti-avoidance principle:
- the regime must prevent “borrow against unrealized gains” from serving as income without I recognition.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST specify capital gains timing treatment and anti-lock-in controls; silence is non-conform.
- If capital gains can be suppressed indefinitely while economic benefit is extracted via collateral/credit, the
  regime is non-conform.

## B00.CH06.P04.S03 — Carried interest and performance fees: canonical classification (labour vs capital)

Carried interest and performance fees often re-label labour/management remuneration as “capital return” to obtain
preferential treatment and to bypass flow constraints.

Canonical classification rule:
- classify carried interest/performance fees by **function**:
  - if compensation is for management effort, control, or service provision, it is labour-like income (I-base).
  - only the component that is a genuine passive capital return qualifies as capital income.

Canonical decomposition requirements:
- identify the base capital at risk and normal return band,
- treat excess return attributable to control/selection rights as remuneration for service/power unless evidence proves
  it is passive return,
- include preferential rights and asymmetric upside as indicators of labour-like remuneration.

Burden of proof:
- subject bears burden to show the passive-capital component; default is conservative inclusion as I.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any I-MAX regime MUST include carried interest/performance fees and define decomposition rules; otherwise it is a
  dominant bypass channel.
- Black-box fund fee structures or undisclosed side letters trigger conservative inclusion and fail-closed posture.

## B00.CH06.P04.S04 — Income smoothing: rules against artificial spikes/dips and “bonus banks”

Subjects can engineer spikes and dips to pass period caps while preserving multi-period benefit:
- deferring bonuses into “banks”,
- pulling income forward or backward across fiscal boundaries,
- smoothing distributions through vehicles,
- or using one-time events to reset baselines.

Canonical smoothing controls:

- **Bonus bank reallocation:** amounts parked in bonus banks are attributed to earn-years or allocated across periods
  by declared rules; they cannot be treated as “not income yet” when entitlement is earned.
- **Artificial dip detection:** if I is systematically low in capped years and high in uncapped years due to timing
  engineering, apply look-back reallocation or averaging enforcement window.
- **Averaging option (if adopted):** cap may be tested on rolling averages (e.g., 3-year) in addition to annual, but
  only if explicit and used to reduce gaming, not to soften enforcement discretion.
- **One-off event handling:** major liquidity events must be captured by event-driven triggers and cannot be parked
  outside measurement by rewrapping.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime MUST include rules against bonus banks and engineered smoothing; otherwise timing games defeat I-MAX.
- If smoothing/volatility rules are opaque or discretionary, the regime is non-auditable and non-conform.
