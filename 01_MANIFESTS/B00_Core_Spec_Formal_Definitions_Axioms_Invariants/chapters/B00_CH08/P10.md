# B00.CH08.P10 — Measurement and audit standards (monetary)

This paragraph outlines the **measurement** and **audit standards** necessary to ensure that the allocation of
**ΔM (monetary space)** is properly tracked, transparent, and aligned with OBA principles. Effective measurement and
auditing are crucial for ensuring that monetary flows contribute to public goods, reduce **L (life-world burdens)**, and
support the stability of **R/B (life-world buffers)**. These standards must be adhered to in order to maintain the integrity
and accountability of the system.

---

## B00.CH08.P10.S01 — Minimum dataset: instrument, channel, destination, beneficiary, conditions

To ensure proper **monitoring** and **accountability**, a **minimum dataset** must be collected and reported for every
monetary flow within the system. This dataset must include key information about the flow’s characteristics and destination.

### Required elements of the minimum dataset:
1. **Instrument**: the financial **tool** used to facilitate the flow (e.g., loans, bonds, grants, subsidies).
2. **Channel**: the **pathway** through which the monetary flow is allocated (e.g., public credit systems, commercial banks,
   government programs).
3. **Destination**: the **final recipient** of the monetary flow (e.g., infrastructure projects, public goods programs,
   specific sectors such as healthcare or education).
4. **Beneficiary**: the individual or entity that directly benefits from the monetary flow (e.g., households, contractors,
   public service providers).
5. **Conditions**: the **terms** attached to the monetary flow (e.g., repayment terms, eligibility criteria, project-specific
   conditions).

These elements must be captured for each monetary flow to ensure that the resources are allocated correctly and in a
traceable manner.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Every monetary flow must have a **complete dataset** that includes information on the **instrument**, **channel**,
  **destination**, **beneficiary**, and **conditions**.
- **Failure to provide this dataset** for any monetary flow leads to **non-compliance** and suspension of the flow.

---

## B00.CH08.P10.S02 — Outcome metrics: R/B profiles, L indices, ΔG, P output, E indicators

To measure the effectiveness of monetary allocations, **outcome metrics** must be defined and tracked to ensure that the
funding contributes to the intended **R/B (life-world buffers)**, reduces **L (life-world burdens)**, and aligns with
**P/E/C** objectives.

### Key outcome metrics:
1. **R/B profiles**: the **distribution of resources** across the population, tracking how **life-world buffers (R/B)**
   are maintained or improved over time, with particular attention to vulnerable populations (e.g., under-α).
2. **L indices**: **indicators** of **life-world burdens** (e.g., poverty rates, unemployment rates, access to basic services),
   tracking the reduction of burdens as a result of monetary flows.
3. **ΔG**: the **change in commons capacity** and **quality** over time (e.g., number of housing units constructed,
   hospitals built, or service reliability increased).
4. **P output**: measures of **productive output** that result from the allocation of credit or investment (e.g., economic
   productivity, infrastructure projects completed).
5. **E indicators**: **measures of extractive practices** (e.g., increased financial claims, tolls, rent-seeking activities).

These metrics allow for the **auditability** and **evaluation** of whether monetary flows are achieving their intended
goals and whether they are contributing to sustainable, equitable outcomes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Outcome metrics such as **R/B profiles**, **L indices**, and **ΔG** must be **measurable**, **traceable**, and **auditable**.
- If the intended outcomes are not clearly defined or are **inconsistent with OBA principles**, the system is **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH08.P10.S03 — Audit trail: end-to-end linkage from decision → allocation → outcome

An **audit trail** is essential for verifying that monetary allocations are made in accordance with OBA principles. It
should provide an **end-to-end linkage** from the initial decision to the final outcome of the allocation.

### Key elements of the audit trail:
1. **Decision**: the **initial decision** to allocate resources, including the reasoning, approval process, and governance
   steps involved.
2. **Allocation**: the actual **distribution** of funds through the identified **channels**, including tracking the flow of
   money from source to destination.
3. **Outcome**: the **final result** of the allocation, including whether it met the intended objectives (e.g., improving
   public goods, reducing life-world burdens, increasing R/B).

An effective audit trail ensures **accountability** and **transparency**, making it possible to trace the path of funds
and assess their impact.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Every monetary flow MUST have a **complete audit trail**, linking **decision-making**, **allocation**, and **outcome**.
- **Failure to maintain a clear and traceable audit trail** renders the system **non-compliant** and requires immediate
  correction.

## B00.CH08.P10.S04 — Uncertainty and attribution: causal claims require triangulation and counterfactuals

**Uncertainty** in monetary allocation outcomes must be addressed through robust methods that ensure **attribution** of
causal effects. Claims about the **impact** of monetary flows (e.g., how they reduce life-world burdens or contribute to
public goods) must be **triangulated** and supported by **counterfactuals** to verify their effectiveness.

### Key principles for uncertainty and attribution:
- **Triangulation**: use multiple sources of data, evidence, and analysis to validate the outcomes of monetary allocations
  and ensure that causal claims are well-supported.
- **Counterfactuals**: establish baseline comparisons to measure the effect of monetary flows. What would have happened
  without the monetary intervention? Counterfactual analysis helps isolate the true impact of the allocation.
- **Methodological rigor**: all causal claims must be made with **robust statistical methods** and **clear evidence** to
  ensure that monetary flows are achieving their intended goals.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- All causal claims about the **impact of monetary flows** must be supported by **triangulated evidence** and **counterfactuals**.
- If the attribution of outcomes is **uncertain** or lacks evidence, the system is considered **non-compliant** and
  must be reviewed for corrective action.
