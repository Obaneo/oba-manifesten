# B00.CH08.P04 — Permitted channels (allowed) and conditions

This paragraph defines the **permitted channels** through which monetary resources may flow within the system. These
channels must adhere to the principles of **non-extraction**, **fair access**, and **R/B stability**, and they are
classified based on their role in building commons, maintaining essential services, and stabilizing life-world buffers
(R/B). All channels must operate within **defined conditions** to ensure compliance with OBA principles.

---

## B00.CH08.P04.S01 — Commons build-out: investments with ΔG metrics and lifecycle duties

**Commons build-out** channels are those that facilitate the creation or expansion of **commons infrastructure** such as
housing, healthcare, energy systems, and education. These channels are critical for improving **ΔG** (capacity/quality/continuity)
and must include long-term **lifecycle duties** to ensure that the infrastructure remains functional and resilient.

### Key principles for commons build-out:
- **ΔG metrics**: all commons build-out projects must have measurable **ΔG outcomes** (e.g., number of units built, quality of infrastructure,
  service accessibility). These metrics must be tracked throughout the lifecycle.
- **Lifecycle duties**: funds allocated for commons build-out must consider the entire lifecycle, from **construction** to **maintenance**,
  ensuring that **long-term service delivery** remains robust.
- **Sustainability**: projects should incorporate sustainability measures (e.g., **energy efficiency**, **resilience to climate change**,
  **resource management**) to ensure long-term success.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **investment in commons build-out** must be tied to **ΔG metrics** that are regularly monitored, and must ensure that
  infrastructure is maintained and upgraded over time.
- **Failure to account for lifecycle duties**, or misallocation of funds that reduce long-term commons capacity, renders the
  channel non-conform.

## B00.CH08.P04.S02 — Maintenance-first: financing of opex/maintenance where ΔG would otherwise degrade

The **maintenance-first** principle ensures that **operating expenditures (opex)** and **maintenance** costs are funded
prior to any capital expenditures (capex) or expansions. This ensures that essential services remain functional and that
**ΔG** does not degrade due to neglect.

### Key principles of maintenance-first financing:
- **Opex and maintenance priority**: funding for **essential services** should always prioritize the **upkeep** of existing
  infrastructure, ensuring that **ΔG** does not decrease due to deferred maintenance or insufficient funds.
- **Service continuity**: the maintenance of capacity and service levels (e.g., healthcare facilities, utilities, roads) must
  take precedence over expansion or prestige projects.
- **Resilience**: ensure that the commons infrastructure is **resilient** and capable of adapting to future challenges, including
  increased demand, climate change, or economic shocks.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Opex and maintenance** must be prioritized in any commons financial model. **Failure to prioritize** these elements
  and instead focusing on capital expansion will result in **non-compliance**.
- If **maintenance** is neglected and **ΔG** deteriorates as a result, the regime must be **fail-closed** until remedied.

## B00.CH08.P04.S03 — Floor stabilisation: improving R/B profiles (reducing under-α share) via direct instruments

**Floor stabilisation** channels are designed to improve **R/B profiles** by ensuring that **vulnerable populations**
receive the resources they need to maintain a baseline standard of living. This is achieved by directing **monetary flows**
into systems that address **under-α** (the portion of the population below the **R/B floor**) and reduce social or economic
vulnerability.

### Key principles for floor stabilisation:
- **Direct instruments**: monetary flows are directed into **programmes** or **subsidies** that directly support the most
  vulnerable, such as income support, essential services subsidies, and public housing.
- **R/B improvement**: these channels aim to improve overall **R/B profiles** by providing targeted interventions to
  populations in need, ensuring that they have access to **essentials** and the **resources** to improve their long-term
  stability.
- **Non-extractive mechanisms**: funding must be allocated without creating new barriers to access or forcing recipients into
  exploitative situations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Funds directed towards floor stabilisation** must be targeted, traceable, and ensure improvements in **R/B profiles**.
- Any **inefficiency**, **leakage**, or **misallocation** of funds that fails to improve **R/B** or addresses only non-vulnerable
  groups will result in **non-conform** status.

## B00.CH08.P04.S04 — Productive credit: capex for P/commons, no accumulation of claim stacks

**Productive credit** is the allocation of **capital expenditure (capex)** that is used exclusively for productive
purposes, such as **commons building** or **public services expansion**, without resulting in the accumulation of
**claim stacks** (i.e., increasing wealth inequality or creating private financial claims).

### Key principles of productive credit:
- **Capex for P/commons**: credit should be used to fund **productive investments** that create public goods, infrastructure,
  or services that benefit the entire population. Examples include public housing, hospitals, transportation systems, and
  renewable energy projects.
- **No accumulation of claim stacks**: funds must not be used to **increase private claims** or inflate asset prices. For
  example, credit used for real estate development should **not** create speculative housing markets that price out lower
  income populations.
- **Non-extractive credit**: productive credit should stimulate **real economic growth** and **public service provision**,
  rather than serve as a mechanism for private enrichment.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **capex funded by credit** must be directed towards **public goods** and **productive** projects that directly contribute
  to the commons.
- **Credit used to accumulate private claims** or inflate asset prices (e.g., speculative real estate) is non-compliant,
  and the channel must be **fail-closed** until corrected.
