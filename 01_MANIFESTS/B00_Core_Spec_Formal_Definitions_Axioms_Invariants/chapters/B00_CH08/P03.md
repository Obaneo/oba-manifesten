# B00.CH08.P03 — Channel object model and classification of channels

This paragraph introduces the **channel object model** and the **classification of channels** within the context of
ΔM (monetary space) and public goods financing. Channels are the pathways through which monetary flows are allocated,
tracked, and utilized. Each channel must be carefully structured and monitored to ensure that resources are directed
efficiently and that **extraction** or **misallocation** does not occur. Channels must be classified to ensure they
align with **commons-building** and **R/B stability** goals.

---

## B00.CH08.P03.S01 — Channel object: {source, intermediary, instrument, destination, conditions, audit log}

A **channel object** is the model through which monetary flows pass, and it contains the following essential elements:

### Key elements of a channel object:
1. **Source**: the entity or mechanism that **originates** the monetary flow (e.g., central bank, treasury, public credit systems, or fiscal injection).
2. **Intermediary**: the **entity** responsible for **facilitating** or **transferring** the monetary flow (e.g., commercial banks, financial institutions, funds, or government programs).
3. **Instrument**: the **mechanism** or **instrument** used to facilitate the monetary flow (e.g., credit, debt issuance, guarantees, direct fiscal payments).
4. **Destination**: the **final recipient** of the monetary flow (e.g., public goods, commons build-out, healthcare programs, infrastructure projects).
5. **Conditions**: the **terms** or **criteria** attached to the monetary flow (e.g., repayment terms, interest rates, performance-based conditions, eligibility criteria).
6. **Audit log**: a **record** of the flow's lifecycle, from origin to destination, including **all intermediary steps** and **any adjustments** made along the way.

Each of these elements must be clearly defined and auditable, ensuring that the monetary flow can be traced and verified at
any stage of its life cycle.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Every **monetary flow channel** must adhere to the **channel object model**, ensuring that **source**, **intermediary**,
  **instrument**, **destination**, **conditions**, and **audit logs** are clearly defined and tracked.
- If a flow lacks any of these traceable components, it must be **fail-closed**, and the system is considered **non-conform**.

---

## B00.CH08.P03.S02 — Channel categories: commons capex, maintenance/opex, floor stabilisation, crisis buffer

Channels are classified based on the purpose they serve within the commons system. The following categories are used to
ensure that monetary flows align with the objectives of **R/B stabilization**, **commons build-out**, and **emergency
resilience**:

### Canonical channel categories:
1. **Commons capex (capital expenditure)**: channels used to **fund the construction** or expansion of commons-related
   infrastructure (e.g., building healthcare facilities, expanding public transportation networks).
2. **Maintenance/opex (operating expenditure)**: channels that **fund the ongoing operational costs** required to
   maintain commons services (e.g., maintenance of public utilities, staffing healthcare services, running schools).
3. **Floor stabilisation**: channels used to **stabilize the R/B profiles** of vulnerable groups or communities by
   providing direct financial instruments to reduce **under-α share** (e.g., subsidies for essential services, income
   support).
4. **Crisis buffer**: channels that serve as a **buffer** during **emergencies**, ensuring that essential services are
   maintained or rapidly restored in the face of crisis (e.g., emergency relief funds, disaster response budgets).

These categories ensure that **monetary flows** are purposefully allocated to areas that either build the commons or
safeguard **R/B stability** in times of crisis.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Channels must be categorized clearly according to these four key types, with **specific allocation rules** for each.
- Misallocation of funds (e.g., using capex funds for operational expenses) or failure to meet defined **category rules**
  is **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH08.P03.S03 — Intermediaries: public banks, commercial banks, funds, programmes (role rules)

Intermediaries are entities that **facilitate** the flow of monetary resources between the **source** and the **destination**.
Intermediaries play a critical role in ensuring that funds are properly managed, allocated, and tracked. They must adhere to
clear **role rules** to ensure that they are acting in the public interest.

### Key intermediary types:
1. **Public banks**: institutions owned or regulated by the government that manage public funds, provide financing to
   government projects, and support the distribution of **ΔM** to the public sector.
2. **Commercial banks**: private financial institutions that participate in **monetary flows** by providing credit to
   businesses and individuals. While they operate in the private sector, they must adhere to rules set by public entities to
   ensure fairness and transparency.
3. **Funds**: public or private investment funds that aggregate resources for **commons-related projects**, such as
   infrastructure or service provision.
4. **Programmes**: **government or NGO-run initiatives** that channel funds to specific projects, services, or sectors,
   such as **healthcare programmes**, **housing initiatives**, or **educational support funds**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Intermediaries must operate under **clearly defined rules** that ensure they serve the public interest and do not divert
  resources for private or extractive purposes.
- Any intermediary that **fails to meet** these criteria or engages in **opaque or exploitative practices** is considered
  **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH08.P03.S04 — Channel frictions: leakage, capture, moral hazard, and how they are measured

**Channel frictions** refer to any forces that impede the proper flow of resources or distort the intended outcomes of
monetary allocation. These frictions must be **measured** and **managed** to ensure that resources are effectively used
for public good.

### Types of frictions:
1. **Leakage**: when funds are **diverted** or **misallocated** outside of the intended channel or destination (e.g., funds
   earmarked for public infrastructure being diverted to private gains).
2. **Capture**: when **private actors** or **special interests** gain control of commons resources, effectively turning
   public services into tools for private gain (e.g., monopolistic pricing, exclusive access).
3. **Moral hazard**: when parties **take excessive risks** or engage in **irresponsible behavior** because they do not
   bear the full consequences of their actions (e.g., lenders or contractors acting recklessly because the commons absorbs
   the financial risk).

Each friction must be **measurable**, and **mitigation strategies** must be put in place to minimize their impact.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- All channels must have **friction measurement mechanisms** to detect **leakage**, **capture**, and **moral hazard**.
- If **friction** is not measured or remains unchecked, the channel is **non-compliant** and must be **fail-closed** until
  remediated.
