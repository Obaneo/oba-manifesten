# B00.CH08.P13 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph outlines the **failure modes** and **hard stops** within the **ΔM (monetary space)** framework. These
conditions are triggered when a channel, allocation, or program violates core OBA principles, such as **non-extraction**,
**transparency**, or **accountability**. Failure to comply with these principles will result in immediate **suspension**
of the monetary flow or channel and corrective actions.

---

## B00.CH08.P13.S01 — ΔM-washing: “commons” label on programmes that are in fact asset support

**ΔM-washing** occurs when a program is labeled as **“commons”**, but in reality, it functions as **asset support** or
facilitates **financial rent-seeking** rather than providing **public goods** or improving the **commons**. This deceptive
labeling undermines the integrity of OBA by misallocating resources into private, extractive channels.

### Key indicators of ΔM-washing:
- **Asset support disguised as commons**: programs that claim to serve **public goods** but ultimately inflate asset
  values or concentrate wealth in private hands.
- **Rent-seeking behavior**: financial flows that **benefit a narrow group** under the guise of commons-building (e.g.,
  speculative real estate, private sector bailouts, or monopolistic pricing for public goods).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any program that **misrepresents** itself as a **commons-building initiative** but instead supports private **asset
  accumulation** is **non-compliant** and must be immediately **fail-closed**.

---

## B00.CH08.P13.S02 — Moral hazard: private risks socialised without caps/anti-E conditions

**Moral hazard** refers to the situation where **private risks** are shifted onto the **public**, usually through
government-backed programs, without appropriate **caps** or **anti-E** (anti-extractive) conditions. This leads to **unaccountable risk-taking** by private entities, with the public bearing the consequences.

### Key principles:
- **Private risks socialised**: programs where **private entities** benefit from **public backing** (e.g., bailouts or
  guarantees) without assuming the financial or operational risk associated with their actions.
- **No caps/anti-E conditions**: failure to place limits on the **exposure** of public resources or to require that
  recipients of public support act in ways that do not exploit the system for private gain.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any program that socializes private risks without **proper caps**, **accountability**, or **anti-E safeguards** is
  **non-compliant** and must be **fail-closed** until remedied.

---

## B00.CH08.P13.S03 — Black-box allocation: unknown beneficiaries/conditions = non-conform

**Black-box allocation** refers to monetary flows or programs where the **beneficiaries**, **conditions**, and **allocations**
are not transparent or publicly disclosed. This lack of transparency undermines the ability to audit, verify, and ensure that
funds are being used for their intended purposes.

### Key characteristics of black-box allocation:
- **Unknown beneficiaries**: recipients of the monetary flow are not disclosed, leaving **financial flows opaque** and
  non-auditable.
- **Opaque conditions**: there are no **clear conditions** tied to the allocation, making it impossible to determine
  whether the funds are being used for **public goods** or if they are supporting **private interests**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Transparency** is a fundamental requirement. All monetary flows must be fully **disclosed**, with **known
  beneficiaries** and **clear conditions** for allocation.
- Any allocation that **lacks transparency** or **does not disclose its beneficiaries** is **non-compliant** and must be
  **fail-closed**.

---

## B00.CH08.P13.S04 — Fail-closed: if untraceable or with prohibited leakage, the channel is shut down

A **fail-closed** system is a safeguard that ensures **channels** or **monetary allocations** are suspended if they are
**untraceable** or if they exhibit **prohibited leakage** (e.g., diversion of funds, untracked intermediaries, or
unapproved reallocations). This principle ensures that only fully auditable and **compliant channels** remain operational.

### Key principles of fail-closed:
- **Untraceable allocations**: any allocation that cannot be traced from **source to destination** is immediately
  suspended. This includes channels that do not provide clear audit trails or documentation.
- **Prohibited leakage**: if a channel involves **leakage** (e.g., funds diverted to unauthorized recipients or purposes),
  it must be shut down until compliance is restored.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **channel** or **monetary flow** that is **untraceable** or exhibits **prohibited leakage** must be immediately
  **fail-closed**.
- **Failure to ensure traceability** or **leakage prevention** results in **non-compliance**, and the channel will be
  suspended until remediated.
