# B00.CH08.P07 — Embedding in existing banking system and credit mechanism

This paragraph discusses how the **monetary space (ΔM)** framework should be integrated into existing financial
structures, such as the **banking system**, **public credit infrastructure**, and **payment rails**, while maintaining
compliance with OBA principles. The goal is to ensure that **commercial banks**, **public credit systems**, and **payment
rails** operate transparently, efficiently, and in alignment with the broader objective of **public goods provision** and
**R/B stabilisation**.

---

## B00.CH08.P07.S01 — Role of commercial banks: credit under channel rules and audit requirements

**Commercial banks** play a critical role in the distribution of **credit** within the economy. Under the **ΔM framework**,
banks are intermediaries that must adhere to **channel rules** and maintain strict **auditability** to ensure that
credit flows are non-extractive and serve the public good.

### Key commercial bank principles:
- **Credit allocation**: Commercial banks must direct **credit** in accordance with the **channel rules** outlined by the
  commons system, ensuring that loans are provided for productive purposes that align with public goods (e.g., housing,
  healthcare, infrastructure) and **commons building**.
- **Audit requirements**: Banks must operate in a fully **auditable** manner, maintaining transparent records of all credit
  transactions, recipients, and the terms of the loans. This ensures that credit does not contribute to **asset bubbles**
  or **claim stacking**.
- **Non-extractive lending**: Credit must be allocated for productive purposes, not for speculative activities or to
  increase wealth concentration. **Extractive lending**, such as financing that creates large financial claims, is non-compliant.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Commercial banks must **adhere to the channel rules** and ensure that all **credit allocation** serves **public welfare**.
- Failure to comply with **auditability** or if commercial banks divert credit to **non-productive purposes** will lead to
  **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH08.P07.S02 — Public credit infrastructure: governance, mandate, risk management

**Public credit infrastructure** must be structured to serve public goods and ensure the **equitable distribution** of
resources. It should be governed by clear rules and mandates that prioritize **public welfare** and ensure **systemic
resilience**.

### Key principles for public credit infrastructure:
- **Governance**: Public credit systems must have **clear governance structures** to ensure transparency and accountability
  in their operations. This includes **independent oversight** and **stakeholder representation** in decision-making.
- **Mandate**: The public credit infrastructure must operate with a **public mandate** that focuses on financing public
  goods, reducing **life-world burdens (L)**, and strengthening **R/B** profiles. It must not serve private or speculative
  interests.
- **Risk management**: Public credit systems must have **robust risk management protocols** to avoid creating financial
  instability or amplifying systemic risks. This includes measures to ensure that credit is directed towards **productive
  investments** rather than speculative ventures.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Public credit systems must be governed by **clear rules and oversight mechanisms**, ensuring **transparency** and
  **public accountability**.
- Failure to adhere to **public mandates** or if public credit infrastructure is used for **private benefit** will result
  in **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH08.P07.S03 — Payment rails governance: choke points, compliance, and anti-leakage

**Payment rails** are critical infrastructure that facilitate monetary transactions and ensure that resources flow
through the economy efficiently. **Governance of payment rails** must prioritize **compliance**, **anti-leakage**, and the
prevention of **choke points** that limit access or increase costs unfairly.

### Key principles for payment rails governance:
- **Choke points**: Payment rails must be designed to avoid the creation of **single points of failure** or monopolistic
  control. No actor should have exclusive control over the movement of money, as this can result in rent-seeking and
  **extraction**.
- **Compliance**: Payment systems must be **compliant** with the principles of **non-extractiveness**, **transparency**,
  and **equity**. This ensures that the rails remain open to all participants without creating barriers to entry or
  participation.
- **Anti-leakage**: Payment systems must ensure that **no leakage** occurs—meaning that funds should not be diverted or
  misallocated outside of the intended channels.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Payment rails must be governed by **open standards** and operate **transparently** to avoid monopolistic control or
  extraction.
- Any **payment system** that creates **choke points** or **misallocates resources** will be considered **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH08.P07.S04 — Macroprudential coupling: rules against collateral and leverage cycles

**Macroprudential coupling** refers to the integration of financial stability measures that prevent the creation of
**collateral bubbles** and excessive **leverage cycles** that can destabilize the economy and the commons system. These
rules are designed to ensure that the credit and monetary system operates in a **sustainable** and **resilient** manner.

### Key principles for macroprudential coupling:
- **Collateral cycles**: prevent the accumulation of excessive **collateral** that can create systemic risks, especially in
  speculative markets or when collateral is used to support **extractive credit**. Collateral must be **non-extractive**
  and used for productive purposes.
- **Leverage cycles**: ensure that financial leverage is limited to productive investments and not used to inflate asset
  prices or create excessive debt that could destabilize the economy.
- **Financial stability**: coupling macroprudential rules with monetary policy ensures **economic resilience** and prevents
  financial crises that would disproportionately harm vulnerable populations and increase **life-world burdens** (L).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any system using credit or collateral must adhere to **macroprudential** rules that **prevent excessive leverage** or
  **collateral bubbles**.
- If **excessive leverage** or **collateral inflation** is used for non-productive purposes, the regime is **non-compliant**.
