# B00.CH08.P06 — Channeling rules and priority (allocation hierarchy)

This paragraph outlines the **channeling rules** and the **priority hierarchy** for allocating monetary resources
within the OBA framework. These rules ensure that resources are distributed in a manner that supports **public goods**
and **commons-building** while avoiding the concentration of wealth or the reinforcement of exploitative financial
systems. The allocation hierarchy establishes clear priorities for where monetary flows should be directed, ensuring
that the most pressing public needs are addressed first.

---

## B00.CH08.P06.S01 — Priority 1: maintenance and continuity of essentials (ΔG lower bound)

**Priority 1** in the allocation hierarchy is the **maintenance and continuity of essential services**. Ensuring that
fundamental public goods remain functional is the first responsibility of any commons regime. This priority ensures
that **ΔG** (changes in commons capacity and quality) does not fall below a **lower bound** for continuity, preventing
service degradation or collapse.

### Key principles for Priority 1:
- **Essentials first**: funding must prioritize the **maintenance** of critical services like housing, healthcare,
  education, and energy. These services should remain operational and accessible to all, especially the most vulnerable.
- **ΔG continuity**: investments must ensure that the **capacity and quality** of essential services remain stable or
  improve, avoiding situations where **ΔG** deteriorates, leading to increased **L** (life-world burdens) and **reduced R/B**.
- **Service resilience**: **essential services** must be maintained **even during crises** or economic downturns, with
  provisions for **emergency funding** and rapid recovery.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **monetary allocation** system must ensure that **essential services** are the first priority in times of financial
  scarcity, and that **ΔG** for these services remains at or above the minimum required for service continuity.
- Failure to **maintain** or **continuously improve** essential services leads to **non-compliance**, and the system must
  be **fail-closed** until corrected.

---

## B00.CH08.P06.S02 — Priority 2: reduction of L through public option/commons in essentials

**Priority 2** focuses on the **reduction of L** (life-world burdens) through the provision of a **public option** or
**commons** in essential services. The goal is to ensure that all individuals have access to **basic rights** without
being subject to exploitative or monopolistic pricing.

### Key principles for Priority 2:
- **Public option**: a **minimum viable public alternative** should be available in key sectors such as housing, energy,
  water, healthcare, and education to **counteract market failures** and provide **access** to all, especially for low-income
  or vulnerable groups.
- **Non-extractive allocation**: commons in essential services must be structured to ensure that resources are used for
  public good, not for generating private wealth.
- **Equitable access**: priority funding must be allocated to ensure that the **under-α share** (the portion of the
  population below the R/B floor) is supported, reducing barriers to access and ensuring **universal coverage**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Funding** for the **public option** in essential services must be **prioritized** above other forms of allocation.
- If any public option system **fails to reduce L**, or **fails to provide equitable access**, the regime is **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH08.P06.S03 — Priority 3: productive capacity (P) that durably strengthens R/B

**Priority 3** concerns the **investment in productive capacity** (P) that contributes to the **long-term stability** and
growth of **R/B** (life-world buffers). The focus is on creating infrastructure or services that directly reduce **L** while
enhancing **R/B** over time.

### Key principles for Priority 3:
- **Productive credit**: funding should be directed to projects or investments that **increase productive capacity**, such as
  **public infrastructure**, **economic development**, and **innovation** that lead to sustainable improvements in public
  welfare.
- **Durable impact**: investments must be aimed at strengthening long-term **economic resilience**, improving public service
  delivery, and ensuring that wealth generation does not exacerbate inequality or result in **toll-based extraction**.
- **Sustained R/B growth**: projects that durably enhance **R/B** by increasing economic opportunities, improving education,
  or building infrastructure necessary for public welfare.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Investments in **productive capacity** must focus on **sustaining** or **improving** **R/B** in the long term, with a clear
  mechanism for tracking the **impact** of investments.
- If **productive investments** disproportionately benefit a narrow group or fail to deliver **sustained** improvement to
  R/B, they are **non-compliant** and must be excluded from the allocation plan.

---

## B00.CH08.P06.S04 — Prohibited priority: “calming markets” as de facto asset and claim support

A **prohibited priority** is the use of **monetary flows** to artificially **calm markets** or stabilize asset prices,
which can lead to **asset inflation** or **claim accumulation**. The goal of any commons system is to ensure equitable
access to services, not to stabilize financial markets at the cost of public welfare.

### Key prohibited priority activities:
- **Asset support through market calming**: funding channels that support asset prices or prevent asset price
  corrections (e.g., government programs that prop up real estate prices or bail out corporations).
- **Claim accumulation**: monetary flows used to inflate claims or private wealth at the expense of public goods or
  commons infrastructure.
- **Market manipulation**: using public funds to create artificial stability in financial markets, rather than addressing
  underlying structural issues or improving public goods provision.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Funding systems** that **calm markets** or stabilize **asset prices** through public intervention are **non-compliant**.
- **Financial interventions** must focus on **public welfare** and **commons build-out**, not **private asset or claim
  protection**.
