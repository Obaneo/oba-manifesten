# B00.CH08.P11 — Anti-avoidance: leakage and shadow channels

This paragraph defines **anti-avoidance** mechanisms within the **ΔM (monetary space)** framework, with a focus on
preventing **leakage** and the use of **shadow channels** that could undermine the goals of **OBA**. **Leakage** refers
to the diversion of monetary flows outside of the intended system, and **shadow channels** are those that facilitate
financial activity without proper oversight or accountability. Both are **non-compliant** and must be eliminated from
the system to maintain its integrity.

---

## B00.CH08.P11.S01 — Shadow banking: SPVs, securitisation, synthetic exposures as avoidance routes

**Shadow banking** refers to financial activities that occur outside the traditional banking system but still have a
significant impact on the monetary system. These activities are often used to bypass regulatory oversight or **channel
funds** in ways that do not align with OBA principles.

### Key shadow banking activities:
- **SPVs (Special Purpose Vehicles)**: legal entities created to isolate financial risk or structure financial products
  in ways that obscure their true nature, making it difficult to track or monitor them effectively.
- **Securitisation**: the process of **bundling assets** (e.g., loans, mortgages) into securities that can be traded. This
  often leads to the **disconnection** of the assets from their original purpose and can be used to conceal risks.
- **Synthetic exposures**: financial instruments that create exposure to assets without owning them directly (e.g., credit
  default swaps, synthetic CDOs). These instruments are often used for speculative purposes and can distort the financial
  system.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Shadow banking** mechanisms such as SPVs, securitisation, and synthetic exposures are **non-compliant** with OBA.
- If any financial flows occur through **shadow banking channels**, they must be **reclassified** as **prohibited** and
  removed from the monetary system.

---

## B00.CH08.P11.S02 — Cross-border leakage: carry trades, offshore funding, crypto rails

**Cross-border leakage** refers to financial flows that **escape** regulatory oversight by moving across national
boundaries, often to jurisdictions with **weaker regulations** or more favorable conditions for private accumulation.

### Key cross-border leakage activities:
- **Carry trades**: financial strategies that exploit differences in interest rates between countries. These trades often
  involve large flows of capital that bypass local regulations, contributing to financial instability.
- **Offshore funding**: the movement of capital to **offshore accounts** or tax havens, enabling avoidance of local
  taxation or oversight and facilitating capital flight.
- **Crypto rails**: the use of **cryptocurrencies** or **decentralized finance** (DeFi) platforms to move funds across
  borders without regulatory oversight, potentially leading to **untraceable financial flows**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **cross-border financial flows** that **bypass regulatory oversight** or contribute to **capital flight** are
  considered **non-compliant**.
- **Carry trades**, **offshore funding**, and **crypto rails** that lead to **leakage** or avoidance of proper financial
  governance must be shut down and classified as **prohibited channels**.

---

## B00.CH08.P11.S03 — Instrument innovation: new labels with the same effect (functional reclassification)

**Instrument innovation** refers to the creation of new financial instruments or structures that are designed to **bypass
regulatory rules** while still achieving the same **extractive or speculative outcomes** as previous instruments.

### Key principles of instrument innovation:
- **New labels**: financial products or services that are given new names or restructured in a way that makes them appear
  **non-extractive** or compliant with regulations, while still maintaining their **functional role** as sources of
  extraction (e.g., **new financial instruments** that replicate the functions of derivatives or **synthetic exposure**).
- **Functional reclassification**: instruments that are reclassified or renamed to avoid detection or regulatory oversight,
  but their impact remains **financially extractive**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Financial instruments that are **rebranded** or **restructured** in a way that avoids detection while still having the
  same **extractive effect** are **non-compliant**.
- If a financial instrument is **reclassified** for the purpose of avoiding scrutiny, it must be **prohibited** and **fail-closed**.

---

## B00.CH08.P11.S04 — Reclassification + sanctions: automatic reclassification to prohibited channel where appropriate

**Reclassification** of channels or instruments is necessary when they are found to be violating OBA principles.
Any financial flows or instruments that are involved in **leakage**, **shadow banking**, or other prohibited activities
must be **reclassified** and subject to **sanctions**.

### Key principles for reclassification and sanctions:
- **Automatic reclassification**: if a channel or instrument is found to be non-compliant, it is automatically moved to the
  **prohibited channel** category and must no longer be used for monetary allocation or financial flows.
- **Sanctions**: once reclassified, the channel or instrument is subject to **sanctions**, including suspension, elimination
  from the system, and potential penalties for those involved in its misuse.
- **Transparency in reclassification**: reclassified channels or instruments must be **publicly disclosed**, and their
  use must be **restricted** until compliance with OBA standards is achieved.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **channel** or **instrument** found to be in violation of OBA principles must be **automatically reclassified** and
  removed from the system.
- **Failure to reclassify** or **enforce sanctions** on non-compliant channels results in **non-compliance** and
  must be addressed immediately.
