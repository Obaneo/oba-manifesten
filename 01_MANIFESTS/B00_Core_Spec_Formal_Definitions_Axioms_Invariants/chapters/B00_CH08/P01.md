# B00.CH08.P01 — Purpose, scope and normative status of Definition set VI

This paragraph defines the **purpose**, **scope**, and **normative status** of **Definition set VI** concerning
**monetary space (ΔM)** as an allocation interface. It establishes that ΔM should not be seen simply as "more or less
money," but as a **channel discipline** governing the flow of resources through various financial systems. The primary
objective of ΔM is to stabilize **R/B** (life-world buffers) and ensure the growth of **commons** without relying on
extractive asset accumulation or claim-based support mechanisms.

---

## B00.CH08.P01.S01 — ΔM as allocation interface: not “more/less money” but channel discipline

**ΔM** (monetary space) is the **allocation interface** through which monetary resources flow within the system. The
purpose of ΔM is not merely to add or subtract money from the economy but to impose a **channel discipline** that governs
the pathways through which resources are allocated, ensuring that these pathways are used efficiently and non-extractively.

Key principles of ΔM:
- **Channel discipline**: ΔM ensures that financial flows are directed into **productive**, **sustainable** pathways
  that build and maintain commons (e.g., housing, healthcare, education) rather than accumulating private claims or
  inflating asset bubbles.
- **Non-extractive allocation**: monetary flows must serve public and commons-building functions without generating
  **extraction rents** or creating lock-in effects.
- **Flow tracing**: all ΔM allocations must be traceable through defined channels, with clarity on the source, destination,
  intermediary, and conditions.

ΔM is a control mechanism to ensure that the allocation of resources supports **public goods**, improves **R/B**,
and maintains **stability**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any monetary regime using ΔM MUST adhere to **channel discipline**: it should focus on allocating resources for
  **public and commons functions** rather than purely for wealth accumulation.
- Any **extraction-based** allocation that funnels resources into private claims or lock-in channels is non-compliant.

---

## B00.CH08.P01.S02 — Purpose: R/B stabilisation and commons build-out without asset and claim support

The core **purpose** of ΔM is to ensure the **stabilisation of R/B** (life-world buffers) and to facilitate the **build-out**
of **commons** without relying on **asset-based collateral** or the accumulation of private claims.

### Key purposes:
- **R/B stabilisation**: ΔM helps maintain and stabilize **R/B profiles** by directing resources into commons systems that
  improve public welfare, reducing **L** (life-world burdens) and enhancing the capacity for individuals and societies
  to weather shocks.
- **Commons build-out**: resources should be allocated to fund the construction and maintenance of **essential services**
  (housing, energy, healthcare, etc.) that are fundamental to well-being, not speculative asset accumulation.
- **No asset or claim support**: ΔM is designed to prevent the creation of **private asset inflation** or **claim stacking**
  mechanisms. Unlike traditional monetary policies that can inflate assets or generate financial claims, ΔM focuses on
  ensuring that resources are directed towards **public infrastructure**, not speculative financial growth.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime claiming OBA compatibility must ensure that **ΔM resources** are directed towards **stabilising R/B** and
  funding commons systems without creating **asset bubbles** or **claim accumulation**.
- **Asset inflation** or the creation of financial claims on future resources is non-compliant.

---

## B00.CH08.P01.S03 — Scope: central bank, treasury, public credit channels, banking system, payment rails

The **scope** of Definition set VI includes the key financial institutions and systems that channel **ΔM** and facilitate
public monetary policy:

### Canonical scope components:
- **Central banks**: institutions responsible for regulating monetary supply and controlling inflation. They should also
  ensure that monetary flows are directed into commons-building functions (e.g., public credit systems).
- **Treasury**: government entities that manage public finances and allocate resources for **public goods** without
  leveraging private financial claims.
- **Public credit channels**: systems like government bonds, public investment funds, and infrastructure financing that
  channel public funding into commons and essential services.
- **Banking system**: commercial banks and financial institutions that act as intermediaries for credit, ensuring that
  public goods can be funded through loans without extracting rents or inflating asset prices.
- **Payment rails**: infrastructure that facilitates monetary exchanges (e.g., payment systems, digital currency
  systems), ensuring that these channels are open and accessible, with minimal friction or lock-in.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **monetary system** must ensure that **ΔM flows** are managed through **public credit channels** that prioritize
  public goods and **R/B stability**.
- Commercial banks or financial institutions that funnel resources into speculative or extractive practices must be
  considered non-compliant.

## B00.CH08.P01.S04 — Relation to P/E/C, caps and commons (linkages and dependencies)

**ΔM** is intrinsically linked to **P/E/C**, **W/I caps**, and **commons** systems, as monetary flows are often the
primary means by which resources are allocated to essential services, production, and commons build-out. The relationship
between **ΔM** and other OBA components ensures that resources flow in a non-extractive, equitable manner.

### Key linkages:
- **P/E/C**: **ΔM flows** can be directed to **production (P)** or **commons (C)** projects, but must not create **E-income**
  through tolls or extraction. It is essential that **credit and monetary policies** are aligned with **P/E/C goals**.
- **W/I caps**: **ΔM** interacts with **caps** to prevent **asset inflation**. Monetary flows should never be used to inflate
  asset prices (W) or create claims (I) that reduce available **R/B** or inflate **L**.
- **Commons**: **ΔM** supports **commons build-out** by funding infrastructure, ensuring public access, and reducing the
  extraction of public wealth by private entities.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **ΔM flows** must always be directed towards **non-extractive commons-building** functions, and should never be used to
  inflate asset prices or create private claims.
- Any deviation that channels **ΔM** into speculative activities or undermines **R/B stability** violates the commons
  principles and is considered **non-compliant**.
