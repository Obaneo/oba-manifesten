# B00.CH08.P08 — Crisis regime: emergency ΔM without semantic erosion

This paragraph defines the principles for the **crisis regime** within the **ΔM (monetary space)** framework. The goal
of the crisis regime is to address **emergencies** (such as natural disasters, financial crises, or other major shocks)
by temporarily increasing **monetary capacity** (ΔM). However, this regime must ensure that **semantic erosion** does
not occur—meaning that emergency measures should not be used as pretexts for long-term **asset support** or **claim stacking**.
The rules outlined here aim to create an equitable, transparent, and temporary intervention system that does not distort the
underlying purpose of **non-extractive monetary flows**.

---

## B00.CH08.P08.S01 — Trigger criteria: shock definitions, measurable indicators, decision procedures

For the **crisis regime** to be activated, there must be clear and **objective criteria** that define the scope of the crisis
and the conditions under which **ΔM** should be increased. These criteria ensure that the regime is applied only in true
**emergencies** and that resources are allocated in an orderly and justifiable manner.

### Key principles for trigger criteria:
- **Shock definitions**: clear, widely accepted definitions of what constitutes a **crisis**. These may include natural
  disasters (e.g., floods, earthquakes), financial crises (e.g., banking collapse, sudden economic downturn), or other
  **large-scale emergencies** (e.g., pandemics, geopolitical shocks).
- **Measurable indicators**: indicators that can objectively measure the severity of the crisis (e.g., unemployment rates,
  economic contraction, health system overload). These indicators must be verifiable and measurable over time.
- **Decision procedures**: defined **decision-making processes** for triggering the crisis regime, ensuring that there are
  **no delays** or subjective judgments in emergency situations. Procedures should include clear documentation and
  verification steps.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- The **crisis regime** MUST have clear **shock definitions**, measurable indicators, and documented decision procedures.
- **Failure to clearly define triggers** or to ensure **objective decision-making** results in **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH08.P08.S02 — Temporariness: sunsets, caps on size/duration, rollback plan

One of the core principles of the **crisis regime** is that any monetary interventions made during a crisis must be
**temporary**. Once the crisis is over, measures should **roll back** to prevent long-term distortions in the monetary system
or commons services. 

### Key principles for temporariness:
- **Sunset clauses**: clear **sunset clauses** that define when emergency measures (e.g., increased ΔM) will automatically
  expire or be reduced. This prevents **crisis interventions** from becoming permanent features of the system.
- **Caps on size/duration**: define the **maximum size** and **duration** of the monetary intervention to ensure that
  the crisis regime does not distort the economy long-term. This includes limiting the **total amount of ΔM** injected into
  the system and setting time limits on how long funds are allocated.
- **Rollback plan**: a **clear, actionable rollback plan** that specifies how emergency measures will be phased out once
  the crisis has passed, including when and how monetary injections will be **reversed**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **crisis regime** must include **sunset clauses**, **caps** on size and duration, and a **rollback plan** to prevent
  the crisis measures from becoming permanent.
- **Failure to implement** a **rollback plan** or if emergency measures extend **beyond necessary periods** results in
  **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH08.P08.S03 — Prohibited emergency route: crisis as pretext for permanent asset support

A core principle of the crisis regime is that **emergency measures** cannot be used as a **pretext** for long-term
**asset support** or the **permanent extension** of financial interventions that artificially prop up asset prices or
claim-based systems. The **goal** of the crisis regime is to address urgent needs **without distorting the commons** or
financial systems for **private gain**.

### Key prohibited emergency routes:
- **Asset support disguised as crisis response**: using **emergency measures** to support asset prices (e.g., buying
  distressed assets or propping up markets) that do not directly address the needs of vulnerable populations.
- **Claim stacking**: leveraging emergency measures to **accumulate financial claims** or private wealth at the expense
  of commons development and public goods.
- **Permanent interventions**: allowing emergency interventions to **perpetuate financial systems** that increase wealth
  inequality or **private ownership** of essential services.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Crisis measures** MUST be used exclusively for addressing **emergency needs** and **cannot be used** to prop up asset
  prices or support **financial claims**.
- Any **emergency program** that leads to **private asset support** or contributes to **claim stacking** is **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH08.P08.S04 — Transparency: publication of instruments, beneficiaries, conditions, audits

**Transparency** is essential to ensuring that **crisis interventions** are **effective**, **fair**, and **non-exploitative**.
All instruments used in the crisis regime must be **publicly available**, and the beneficiaries of these measures must be
**clearly disclosed**. Additionally, audits must be conducted to ensure compliance with **OBA principles** and to maintain
trust in the monetary system.

### Key transparency principles:
- **Publication of instruments**: all **financial instruments** used in the crisis regime (e.g., fiscal tools, credit lines,
  subsidies) must be publicly disclosed, with clear information on their **purpose**, **conditions**, and **terms**.
- **Beneficiary disclosure**: the **beneficiaries** of crisis funds must be identified and disclosed, including **public
  service providers**, **private corporations**, and **individuals** who receive emergency support.
- **Audit trail**: a full **audit trail** must be maintained to ensure that all allocations are traceable and **non-extractive**.
  This includes tracking all transactions, allocations, and decision-making processes.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **crisis regime** must ensure **full transparency** by publishing details of the **instruments**, **beneficiaries**, and
  **conditions** for all monetary interventions.
- If **funding allocation** is opaque or **audit trails** are missing, the crisis regime is considered **non-compliant**.
