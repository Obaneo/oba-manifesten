# B00.CH08.P12 — Compatibility with caps and commons (integration tests)

This paragraph outlines the **compatibility checks** for monetary flows within the OBA framework, ensuring that they do
not undermine the goals of **W/I caps**, **commons principles**, and **R/B stability**. These integration tests ensure that
monetary flows follow rules that **safeguard public welfare**, **prevent financial instability**, and **ensure the
commons remain non-extractive**.

---

## B00.CH08.P12.S01 — No ΔM that undermines W/I caps via asset inflation or deferral

Monetary flows allocated as **ΔM** must not undermine **W/I caps** (wealth/income caps) by engaging in **asset inflation**
or **deferral** tactics. These practices could lead to increased wealth inequality or financial instability, which are
incompatible with the principles of **OBA**.

### Key principles:
- **No asset inflation**: **ΔM** must not be used to inflate asset prices (e.g., real estate, stocks) by artificially
  increasing the **monetary supply** or leveraging credit.
- **No deferral**: **ΔM** should not be used to **delay** or push forward asset or wealth-related obligations, such as
  by using financial instruments to extend credit or delay debt repayment without contributing to real productive output.

Monetary flows should contribute to **productive goods** and **commons infrastructure**, not to speculative asset bubbles.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **ΔM allocation** that leads to **asset inflation** or **wealth deferral** is considered **non-compliant**.
- If monetary interventions disproportionately support **asset price increases** or **create speculative bubbles**, they
  must be **fail-closed**.

---

## B00.CH08.P12.S02 — No ΔM that pushes commons into capture (vendor lock-in, private tolling)

Monetary allocations (ΔM) must never lead to **commons capture**, where private entities **gain control** over public
commons resources through **vendor lock-in**, **private tolling**, or other extractive mechanisms.

### Key principles:
- **Vendor lock-in**: ΔM must not support financial arrangements that **force users** or beneficiaries into dependency
  on a **single provider** or **service model** with high switching costs, thus preventing users from accessing a public
  **alternative** or a **public option**.
- **Private tolling**: Monetary allocations should **not lead to toll-based** service provision for **essential services**,
  such as utilities or public transport. Funding must go towards maintaining access to services without generating **private
  profit** or rents.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Any channel** or financial program that leads to **vendor lock-in** or the creation of **private tolls** in essential
  services must be immediately **reclassified** as **non-compliant**.
- **Failure to prevent capture** through monetary allocations is grounds for **fail-closed** status.

---

## B00.CH08.P12.S03 — Consistency with R/B: channel must reduce or stabilise under-α share

**ΔM** allocations must ensure that the **under-α share** (the portion of the population below the R/B floor) is either
reduced or stabilised, contributing to the overall improvement of **R/B profiles**.

### Key principles:
- **R/B stability**: Monetary allocations must be **directed towards reducing** the **under-α share**, ensuring that the
  most vulnerable populations have access to essential services and opportunities.
- **Improving access**: Funds should be used to improve **access to public goods** (e.g., healthcare, housing, education),
  and reduce disparities between different population cohorts.
  
The monetary flows must be aligned with the overarching goals of improving **R/B stability** for the entire population.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **ΔM allocations** must focus on improving **R/B profiles**, with special emphasis on reducing **under-α share**.
- Failure to **address under-α** populations or to improve **R/B stability** will result in **non-compliance** and **fail-closed** status.

---

## B00.CH08.P12.S04 — Integration check: “channel plan” as mandatory component of any OBA claim

Each **OBA claim** that involves **monetary flows** must include a **“channel plan”**—a comprehensive and transparent
document that outlines how monetary resources are allocated, tracked, and measured within the OBA system.

### Key principles:
- **Channel plan**: The **channel plan** must describe:
  - the **source** of monetary flows,
  - the **channels** through which funds will flow,
  - the **destination** (e.g., public goods, commons infrastructure),
  - the **conditions** attached to the allocation,
  - the **expected outcomes** (e.g., ΔG improvement, R/B stability).
  
The channel plan ensures that all **monetary flows** are traceable, accountable, and in compliance with OBA principles.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Every **OBA claim** must be accompanied by a **channel plan** that outlines the **allocation, tracking, and expected
  outcomes** of the monetary flows.
- **Failure to submit a channel plan** or provide insufficient detail in the plan renders the **OBA claim non-compliant** and
  results in **fail-closed** status.
