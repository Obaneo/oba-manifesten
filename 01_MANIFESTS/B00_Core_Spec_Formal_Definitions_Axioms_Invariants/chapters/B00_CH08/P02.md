# B00.CH08.P02 — Canonical definition of ΔM (monetary space)

This paragraph defines **ΔM** (monetary space) as the **change in monetary capacity** within the system over a
specific period, aligning with the **stock/flow convention**. It sets the framework for understanding how **monetary
flows** are managed, allocated, and tracked, ensuring that they contribute to the goals of stabilizing **R/B** and
building **commons** without engaging in extractive practices.

The **sources** of ΔM include various channels through which monetary capacity enters the economy, such as **credit
creation**, **fiscal injections**, and **balance sheet expansions**. All monetary flows must be traceable to their
**sources** and **destinations**, ensuring transparency and preventing leakage or misuse.

---

## B00.CH08.P02.S01 — ΔM as change in monetary capacity per period t (stock/flow convention)

**ΔM** is defined as the **change in monetary capacity** within the system during a given period **t** (e.g., year,
quarter, or month). 

Mathematically:
- **ΔM(t) = M(t) − M(t−1)** where **M(t)** is the monetary supply or total monetary capacity at time **t**, and **M(t−1)**
  is the monetary supply at the previous period.

This change is crucial for measuring the expansion or contraction of the monetary system and is the primary interface
for managing **public goods** provisioning and **commons funding**.

The key principle here is that **ΔM** is **not simply a matter of adding or subtracting money** but about channeling
resources through controlled **financial channels** to support public goods, reduce **L**, and stabilize **R/B**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any system using **ΔM** must ensure that it adheres to the **stock/flow convention**, where monetary flows are measured
  accurately over time, and any change in monetary capacity is auditable and traceable.

---

## B00.CH08.P02.S02 — Sources of ΔM: credit creation, balance sheet expansion, guarantees, fiscal injections (canonically delimited)

**ΔM** originates from multiple sources that increase the monetary capacity available within the system. These sources
must be clearly defined and kept within **canonical limits** to ensure that the allocation of money is non-extractive.

Canonical sources of ΔM:
1) **Credit creation**: Central banks, public banks, and commercial banks create credit through loans and lending
   activities, expanding the available monetary supply.
2) **Balance sheet expansion**: Central banks and public authorities may engage in **quantitative easing** or other forms
   of asset purchases, which expand the balance sheet and increase monetary capacity.
3) **Guarantees**: Government guarantees or commitments (e.g., bailouts, insurance programs) that ensure monetary flows
   and prevent liquidity crises, indirectly increasing monetary capacity.
4) **Fiscal injections**: Government spending programs, whether through **direct investments** or **subsidies**, that inject
   money into the economy to fund public goods or stabilize essential services.

Each source must be clearly **delimited** and tracked to avoid misuse or excessive reliance on one method that could lead
to **asset inflation** or the creation of **tolling** systems.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime using **ΔM** must clearly define and control the sources of monetary capacity. If sources are not
  **canonically delimited**, the system risks becoming **non-compliant**, leading to uncontrolled inflation or wealth
  concentration.

---

## B00.CH08.P02.S03 — Measurement framework: monetary aggregates versus functional capacity (canonical choice)

The measurement of **ΔM** must be aligned with the canonical **measurement framework**, distinguishing between
**monetary aggregates** (total money supply) and the **functional capacity** of the monetary system.

### Key components of the framework:
- **Monetary aggregates**: broad measures of money supply, such as **M1, M2, M3**, which track the total amount of money
  circulating in the economy.
- **Functional capacity**: refers to the **functional use** of monetary resources in terms of **public goods provision**,
  such as the **capacity to maintain public services**, **fund commons projects**, and **reduce life-world burdens (L)**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any system that uses **ΔM** must distinguish between **monetary aggregates** and **functional capacity**, ensuring that
  monetary flows are not measured solely by the total amount of money, but by their **functional contribution** to public
  goods and commons services.
- Systems that fail to account for **functional capacity** are **non-compliant** as they may drive inflation without
  tangible public benefit.

---

## B00.CH08.P02.S04 — Traceability: every ΔM must be traceable to channel and destination

**Traceability** is a core principle of OBA. Every unit of **ΔM** must be fully traceable, from its **source** to its
**destination**.

Canonical traceability requirements:
- **Source identification**: each monetary flow must be linked to a **source** (e.g., credit creation, fiscal injection,
  central bank operations).
- **Channel identification**: monetary flows must pass through specific **channels** (e.g., public credit systems, banking
  networks, government programs).
- **Destination tracking**: funds must be directed toward clearly defined **destinations** (e.g., public goods, commons
  build-out, R/B stabilization efforts).

This traceability ensures that no monetary flow is hidden or misused, and that all resources are allocated towards
the **public good** and **commons development**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any monetary flow or **ΔM adjustment** must be fully **traceable** through its entire life cycle—from source to
  destination. Failure to provide clear traceability leads to **non-conform**, and any opaque or unaccounted-for flow
  should be treated as suspect.
