# B00.CH08.P05 — Prohibited channels: canonical definition and detection rules

This paragraph defines the **prohibited channels** for monetary flows within the OBA framework. These channels violate
the principles of **non-extractive allocation**, **public goods provision**, and **R/B stabilisation**. Prohibited
channels are those that facilitate the **extraction** of value or create systemic imbalances through mechanisms like
asset inflation, claim stacking, or private rent-seeking behaviors. Such channels must be identified and eliminated
from the system to ensure integrity and compliance with OBA goals.

---

## B00.CH08.P05.S01 — Asset support: programmes that support asset prices or reinforce collateral dominance

**Asset support** channels are those that facilitate **inflated asset prices** or reinforce **collateral dominance**,
creating systemic risks and widening economic inequality. These channels divert resources from **commons-building** and
**R/B stabilisation** to speculative and extractive financial behaviors.

### Key asset support activities:
- **Supporting asset prices**: programs that inflate asset prices (e.g., government bailouts of asset markets, real estate
  interventions, or stock buybacks).
- **Reinforcing collateral dominance**: programs that increase the **leverage** and **power** of existing asset owners,
  making it harder for lower-income individuals to access essential services or accumulate wealth.

Asset support activities often fuel asset bubbles, **asset inflation**, and deepen social inequalities, making them
prohibited under OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any channel used to support **asset prices** or reinforce **collateral dominance** is **non-compliant** and must be
  eliminated.
- **Asset price stabilization** mechanisms that do not serve essential public goods or reduce life-world burdens
  (L) are considered **prohibited**.

---

## B00.CH08.P05.S02 — Claim support: buybacks, fee stacks, rentier cashflows, “stabilisation” of E-structures

**Claim support** refers to mechanisms that **restructure or reinforce private claims** over public goods or services,
allowing **rent-seeking** and **extractive behavior** to persist.

### Key claim support activities:
- **Buybacks**: the practice of **buying back shares** or repurchasing equity to inflate asset values and consolidate
  private ownership.
- **Fee stacks**: the layering of **fees** on top of essential services or goods, creating new financial claims that don’t
  correspond to real value or service provision.
- **Rentier cashflows**: continuous **cashflows** derived from **monopolistic control** over essential services, where the
  provider extracts payments without improving the core service (e.g., rent-seeking behavior in housing or healthcare).
- **“Stabilisation” of E-structures**: financial schemes that claim to stabilize services but actually inflate financial
  claims (e.g., financial mechanisms that prop up private profits under the guise of public good stabilization).

Claim support undermines **non-extractive** allocation systems by allowing financial rents and private claims to grow at
the expense of **public goods**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **buyback**, **fee stacking**, or **rentier cashflow** scheme in public goods provision is **non-conform** and must
  be **disqualified** from the monetary system.
- **Claim-based stabilization** mechanisms that reinforce inequality and private ownership in commons services violate
  OBA principles and are prohibited.

---

## B00.CH08.P05.S03 — Untraced intermediaries: black-box funds, opaque SPVs, off-balance conduits

**Untraced intermediaries** refer to financial entities or mechanisms that obscure the flow of funds or act as **off-balance
sheet conduits**, making it impossible to trace how resources are allocated, who benefits, or whether they support public
good outcomes.

### Key untraced intermediary activities:
- **Black-box funds**: investment funds that are **opaque** in terms of ownership, performance, and fund allocation,
  making it difficult to verify whether the resources are used for public goods or whether profits are funneled to
  private interests.
- **Opaque SPVs (special purpose vehicles)**: financial vehicles that hide the true beneficiaries or the real nature of
  the assets and liabilities they manage.
- **Off-balance sheet conduits**: entities or vehicles that are used to circumvent public scrutiny, often for speculative
  or extractive purposes.

These intermediaries undermine **transparency** and **accountability**, making them **non-compliant** under OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **untraced intermediary**, especially those using **black-box funds**, **SPVs**, or off-balance sheet conduits, is
  **non-conform**.
- Channels or intermediaries that **obscure the flow of funds** or hide beneficiaries must be eliminated from the OBA system.

---

## B00.CH08.P05.S04 — Surveillance rent: financing that becomes dependent on data extraction/control as toll

**Surveillance rent** is financing that becomes reliant on **data extraction** or **control over personal information**,
turning **personal data** into a form of **tolling**. This includes systems that extract value from user data, **privacy
violations**, or surveillance activities that ultimately fund **extractive practices**.

### Key surveillance rent activities:
- **Data extraction for profit**: financing that depends on gathering and selling **user data** or utilizing it to generate
  profits (e.g., advertising or behavioral profiling).
- **Control over personal information**: programs or channels that **capitalize on** or **exploit user data** without
  their informed consent, turning private information into a financial asset for **private gain**.

Surveillance rent systems conflict with the principles of **non-extractive allocation** and public benefit. They
exploited user data as a **monetary toll**, undermining privacy rights and commons integrity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Surveillance rent** financing is prohibited in any OBA-compatible regime. If the financing model depends on **data
  extraction** or **tolling** through surveillance, the system is **non-compliant**.
- Any channel that turns user data into a **monetizable asset** or violates privacy rights for financial gain must be
  **fail-closed** and removed from the system.
