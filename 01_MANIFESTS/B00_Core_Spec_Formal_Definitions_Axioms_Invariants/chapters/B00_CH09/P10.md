# B00.CH09.P10 — Detection: red flags and anomalies

This paragraph outlines the key **red flags** and **anomalies** that indicate potential **non-compliance** or efforts
to **circumvent** the principles of OBA. These detection mechanisms are designed to identify hidden ownership structures,
**obscured control**, or **masked benefits** that violate the core values of **transparency** and **public goods provision**.

---

## B00.CH09.P10.S01 — Dispersion across entities without economic rationale (nominee patterns)

One of the most common **red flags** in complex ownership structures is the **dispersion of ownership** across multiple
entities without a **clear economic rationale**. Often, this involves the use of **nominee arrangements** to conceal
the **true beneficial owner** and to create layers of complexity that make it difficult to determine who ultimately **controls**
or **benefits** from the assets.

### Key indicators of nominee patterns:
- **Nominee structures**: legal entities set up to act as **nominees** or **intermediaries** for the true **economic owner**.
- **Lack of economic rationale**: the ownership structure appears unnecessarily **complex**, with no clear business or financial
  reason for the number of intermediaries or entities involved.
- **Ownership dispersion**: assets or shares are scattered across a wide range of **entities**, making it difficult to trace
  the true **beneficiary** or **controller**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any ownership structure that lacks a **clear economic rationale** and involves **nominee patterns** must be fully
  disclosed and **looked-through** to ensure the true **beneficiaries** and **controllers** are identified.
- **Failure to disclose** or concealment through **nominee structures** will result in **non-compliance** and must be corrected.

---

## B00.CH09.P10.S02 — Fee stacks and circular flows as benefit masking

**Fee stacking** and **circular financial flows** are techniques used to mask **financial benefits** or **ownership stakes**,
often creating an illusion of fair value exchange while siphoning money away from the intended recipients or purpose.

### Key indicators of fee stacking and circular flows:
- **Fee stacking**: the addition of **multiple fees** or charges across various layers of a transaction or service, inflating
  costs and reducing the **transparency** of the actual financial flow.
- **Circular flows**: financial arrangements where money circulates between related entities or individuals without
  contributing to **economic value** or **public goods** (e.g., **rent-seeking** behaviors).
- **Benefit masking**: the **true benefits** of financial arrangements are hidden or obscured, making it difficult to track
  the **ultimate beneficiaries**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **fee stacking** or **circular flow** system that **obscures financial transparency** or **masks benefits** is
  considered **non-compliant** and must be adjusted to ensure that the true benefits are properly accounted for.
- **Failure to disclose** the **true beneficiaries** or financial flows is a **red flag** for **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH09.P10.S03 — Inconsistency between governance docs and operational reality (de facto control)

**De facto control** occurs when the **actual operations** of an entity differ from what is stated in the **governance
documents** (e.g., corporate charters, bylaws, shareholder agreements). This **inconsistency** is a **red flag** indicating
that control may be exercised in a manner that is **opaque** or **non-compliant** with OBA principles.

### Key indicators of de facto control:
- **Governance inconsistency**: the **governance documents** show one form of ownership or control, but the **actual decision-making**
  or **benefit allocation** occurs in a different manner (e.g., key decisions are made by individuals who do not have formal
  control).
- **Hidden control**: situations where **decision-making power** is held by **unlisted parties** or **informal agreements**,
  while the official governance documents suggest a different structure.
- **Operational discrepancies**: the day-to-day **operations** or **management** of an entity may indicate that control rests
  with another party not reflected in the governance documents.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- If any **discrepancies** between governance documents and actual control are identified, these must be **rectified** and
  fully disclosed.
- Any structure that engages in **de facto control** without proper **governance documentation** or **transparency** is
  **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH09.P10.S04 — Cross-border mismatch: treaty shopping, IP boxes, re-domiciliation signals

**Cross-border mismatch** refers to efforts to exploit differences in **national laws** or **tax regimes** by **shifting**
resources across borders through **treaty shopping**, **intellectual property (IP) boxes**, or **re-domiciliation**.
These tactics aim to avoid **regulatory oversight** or **tax obligations**, often undermining public welfare.

### Key indicators of cross-border mismatch:
- **Treaty shopping**: the practice of **shifting ownership** or operations to jurisdictions with more favorable tax treaties,
  allowing for the **avoidance** of taxes or other obligations.
- **IP boxes**: legal structures that allow entities to **move intellectual property** assets to jurisdictions with favorable
  tax treatment or minimal regulatory oversight, often to reduce **tax liabilities**.
- **Re-domiciliation signals**: when entities **move their headquarters** or operations to different jurisdictions to take
  advantage of favorable tax or legal conditions, typically when **financial obligations** or **regulatory scrutiny** are
  high in the original jurisdiction.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **cross-border structure** designed to exploit **tax avoidance strategies** or **regulatory loopholes** must be
  **disclosed** and **evaluated** for compliance.
- If a **cross-border mismatch** is found, or **tax avoidance** strategies like **treaty shopping** or **IP box use** are
  being employed, the structure will be considered **non-compliant** and must be corrected immediately.
