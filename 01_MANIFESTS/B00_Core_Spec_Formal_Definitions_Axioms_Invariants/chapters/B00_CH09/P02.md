# B00.CH09.P02 — Control test: definition and categories

This paragraph defines the **control test**, which is essential for determining who **controls** an entity or asset.
Control is a critical factor in assessing ownership and influence within the OBA framework. The test categorizes **formal**
and **de facto** control and establishes clear criteria for identifying **negative control** and **multi-layer control** in complex ownership structures. These distinctions are fundamental for assessing **ultimate beneficial control** (UBC) and ensuring the **transparency** of ownership.

---

## B00.CH09.P02.S01 — Formal control: voting rights, board appointment, contractual powers

**Formal control** is the **legal control** that an entity or individual has over a decision-making process. This is typically
outlined in corporate governance structures, where formal mechanisms like **voting rights**, **board appointments**, and **contractual powers** allow individuals or groups to control the direction of an entity.

### Key elements of formal control:
- **Voting rights**: the power to **vote** on corporate decisions, such as electing board members, approving mergers or acquisitions, or setting policies.
- **Board appointments**: the power to **appoint** or **remove** individuals from the board of directors, which governs the overall strategy and operations of the entity.
- **Contractual powers**: the ability to **control** key business decisions through **contractual agreements** (e.g., the ability to enforce specific business practices or agreements with third parties).

These mechanisms provide clear, **legal control** over an entity, making them central to the **control test**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any entity claiming **formal control** must provide evidence of **voting rights**, **board appointment powers**, or **contractual rights**.
- Lack of clear **formal control** mechanisms or efforts to **obfuscate control** through legal structures is **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH09.P02.S02 — De facto control: actual steering via dependencies, information, operational choke points

**De facto control** refers to the **actual steering** of an entity's operations, even in the absence of formal control
mechanisms. This form of control arises when an individual or group **exerts influence** through **dependencies**, **information asymmetry**, or the ability to control **operational choke points**.

### Key elements of de facto control:
- **Dependencies**: the ability to control operations or decisions by creating **dependencies** in contracts, supply chains, or partnerships (e.g., exclusive contracts, control over inputs or key resources).
- **Information asymmetry**: the ability to **steer decisions** by controlling critical **information** or having privileged access to strategic data.
- **Operational choke points**: control over key **resources** or **infrastructure** that allows for the blockage or acceleration of decisions (e.g., control over a critical technology platform or logistics channel).

De facto control can be more subtle than formal control but can be just as powerful in shaping the direction of an entity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any claim of **de facto control** must be backed by clear evidence of **dependencies**, **information control**, or **choke point management**.
- If de facto control is not clearly substantiated, the entity will be considered **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH09.P02.S03 — Negative control: vetoes, blocking rights, protective provisions (where relevant)

**Negative control** is the ability to prevent certain actions or decisions without having positive control over the entity. This is typically exercised through **veto rights**, **blocking rights**, or **protective provisions** in governance documents, contracts, or agreements.

### Key elements of negative control:
- **Veto rights**: the **power** to reject certain decisions or actions, such as blocking a merger or acquisition, preventing the issuance of new shares, or stopping specific corporate actions.
- **Blocking rights**: similar to veto rights, but typically applied to **specific decisions** (e.g., decisions that require unanimous board approval or shareholder consent).
- **Protective provisions**: clauses in governance agreements or contracts that provide **special protection** against certain actions (e.g., a founder’s right to block changes to the company’s mission or vision).

Negative control allows individuals or groups to **prevent undesirable outcomes** without actively directing the entity’s operations.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any entity claiming **negative control** must demonstrate **clear evidence** of **veto rights**, **blocking rights**, or
  **protective provisions**.
- Failure to clearly define and document **negative control** mechanisms results in **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH09.P02.S04 — Multi-layer control: cascades across holdings, trusts, funds and platform stacks

**Multi-layer control** refers to situations where control is **distributed across multiple entities**, such as **holdings**,
**trusts**, **funds**, and **platform stacks**. These complex structures often involve multiple layers of ownership or control,
making it difficult to ascertain the true beneficiary or controller of an asset.

### Key elements of multi-layer control:
- **Cascading holdings**: ownership that is structured through multiple layers of **subsidiaries**, **parent companies**, or **trusts**, creating a complex chain of control.
- **Trusts and foundations**: ownership through legal structures that involve **settlors**, **trustees**, and **beneficiaries**, often used to **conceal** the true control.
- **Funds and syndicates**: entities that pool resources, such as **venture funds**, **private equity**, or **real estate investment trusts (REITs)**, where control is spread across multiple stakeholders, but **one or a few** may have substantial influence.
- **Platform stacks**: control over a platform’s **infrastructure**, services, or algorithms, often hidden behind **layered ownership structures**.

These multi-layered systems are often used to **mask true ownership** and can complicate control analysis.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **multi-layer control** structure must be **disclosed** fully, with clear documentation of the **ownership chain** and
  **control rights** at each level.
- If the **true controller** or **economic owner** cannot be clearly identified due to complex layering, the system is
  considered **non-compliant** and must be rectified immediately.
