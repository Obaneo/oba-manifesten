# B00.CH09.P07 — Consolidation rules: when positions are combined

This paragraph outlines the **consolidation rules** for combining positions in **ownership**, **control**, and **benefit**
across multiple entities or entities within complex structures. Consolidation ensures that the full extent of **control**,
**benefit**, and **ownership** is properly accounted for, regardless of how many entities or intermediaries are involved.
These rules help ensure **transparency** and **accountability**, preventing ownership structures from being manipulated to
avoid regulatory oversight.

---

## B00.CH09.P07.S01 — Consolidation for W: assets, exposures, control premia, off-balance claims

**Consolidation for W** focuses on aggregating all positions related to **wealth (W)**, including direct and indirect
**assets**, **exposures**, **control premia**, and **off-balance claims**. These are combined to ensure that the **full wealth
position** is considered when determining ultimate beneficial control and ownership.

### Key principles:
- **Assets**: all tangible and intangible **assets** held by entities or individuals, whether on the balance sheet or
  off-balance sheet (e.g., physical property, intellectual property).
- **Exposures**: financial **exposures** that could indicate a beneficial interest or control, including **loan obligations**,
  **financial guarantees**, and contingent liabilities.
- **Control premia**: **additional value** accrued through **control**, such as premium payments for acquiring controlling
  shares or entities.
- **Off-balance claims**: liabilities or claims that are not recorded on the balance sheet but still represent a **financial
  interest** or **risk**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- All **assets**, **exposures**, **control premia**, and **off-balance claims** must be fully disclosed and aggregated
  to provide a comprehensive view of wealth ownership.
- **Failure to consolidate** all positions relevant to wealth, especially off-balance claims or **undisclosed assets**, results
  in **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH09.P07.S02 — Consolidation for I: flow attribution, deferral, pass-through vehicles

**Consolidation for I** addresses the aggregation of **income (I)**, ensuring that all **income streams** and **flow
attribution** are properly accounted for, especially when **deferral** mechanisms or **pass-through vehicles** (e.g.,
trusts, partnerships) are used to obscure the true flow of income.

### Key principles:
- **Flow attribution**: **tracking the flow** of income across multiple entities or structures, ensuring that it is
  attributed to the correct parties (e.g., dividends, interest, royalties).
- **Deferral**: **income deferral** mechanisms that delay income recognition, such as through tax strategies or financial
  instruments that push income into future periods.
- **Pass-through vehicles**: entities that **pass income directly** to their owners or stakeholders, such as partnerships,
  trusts, or certain investment funds.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **All income flows** must be **properly attributed**, even if deferral or pass-through mechanisms are used.
- If **income deferral** or **pass-through structures** are used to **avoid transparency** or mislead about the **true
  beneficiary**, the system is considered **non-compliant** and must be corrected.

---

## B00.CH09.P07.S03 — Consolidation for ΔM: beneficiaries behind intermediaries and programmes

**Consolidation for ΔM** focuses on the **traceability** of **monetary flows** through **intermediaries** and **programmes**,
ensuring that the **beneficiaries** of these flows are properly identified, even if the flow passes through multiple
intermediaries or channels.

### Key principles:
- **Intermediaries**: the **parties** that facilitate or manage the monetary flow (e.g., banks, funds, financial
  institutions).
- **Programs**: **public or private programs** that channel monetary resources for specific purposes (e.g., government
  spending initiatives, development aid programs).
- **Beneficiaries**: the **final recipients** or **economic owners** who ultimately benefit from the monetary flows.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- All **ΔM flows** must clearly identify the **beneficiaries** at the end of the chain, including those that pass through
  intermediaries or complex programmes.
- **Failure to trace beneficiaries**, or if the flow **diverts** resources from intended recipients, will result in
  **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH09.P07.S04 — Consolidation for commons: governance control, procurement, vendor lock-in routes

**Consolidation for commons** ensures that **ownership** and **control** within the **commons** are clearly identified, particularly in
areas such as **governance**, **procurement**, and **vendor relationships**.

### Key principles:
- **Governance control**: who **controls the decisions** related to the operation or management of commons (e.g., public
  services, infrastructure).
- **Procurement**: the process of **allocating contracts** and financial resources for the delivery of commons services
  (e.g., public tenders, government contracts).
- **Vendor lock-in**: when **exclusive contracts** or **vendor relationships** create a **monopoly** over essential services
  or resources, restricting access or competition in the commons.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Ownership and control** within the **commons** must be **clearly documented**, with no hidden **vendor lock-in** or
  opaque governance structures.
- If **vendor lock-in** or **undisclosed consolidation** practices are found, the system will be considered **non-compliant** and
  must be addressed immediately.
