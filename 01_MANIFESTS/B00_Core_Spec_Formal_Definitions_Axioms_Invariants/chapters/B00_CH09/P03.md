# B00.CH09.P03 — Benefit test: definition and categories

This paragraph outlines the **benefit test**, which is used to identify and classify the **benefits** derived from
ownership or control over assets, income streams, or entities. The test categorizes different types of benefits that may
be accrued by an individual or group and helps to establish whether these benefits align with the principles of non-extractive
ownership and public goods provision. The **benefit test** is critical for determining **ultimate beneficial ownership** (UBO)
and ensuring that all benefits are transparent and properly accounted for.

---

## B00.CH09.P03.S01 — Cashflow benefit: dividends, interest, rent, fees, distributions, royalties

**Cashflow benefit** refers to the direct **monetary benefits** an individual or entity receives from **ownership** of
assets or **control** over resources. These benefits come in the form of regular **payments** that result from the productive
or financial activities of the entity, such as **dividends**, **interest**, **rent**, **fees**, **distributions**, or
**royalties**.

### Key elements of cashflow benefit:
- **Dividends**: periodic **distributions** of profits made by a corporation to its shareholders.
- **Interest**: payments made to lenders or bondholders as compensation for the use of their capital.
- **Rent**: regular payments made for the use of property, land, or other assets owned by an individual or entity.
- **Fees**: charges paid for services or rights granted (e.g., management fees, platform access fees, transaction fees).
- **Distributions**: allocations of profits or capital to stakeholders in a partnership or fund.
- **Royalties**: payments made for the use of intellectual property or natural resources.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Cashflow benefits** must be fully **documented**, including the **source**, **recipient**, and **terms** of any payments.
- **Failure to disclose** or misallocate **cashflow benefits** results in **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH09.P03.S02 — Upside benefit: value appreciation, performance shares, options, carried interest

**Upside benefit** refers to the potential for **value appreciation** or **profit-sharing** that may result from the
increased value of assets or the performance of investments. This category includes **performance-based compensation**
and the **potential upside** that individuals or entities may gain through ownership or control.

### Key elements of upside benefit:
- **Value appreciation**: the increase in the **market value** of assets owned or controlled by an individual or entity
  (e.g., real estate, stocks, or intellectual property).
- **Performance shares**: stock or equity grants that are awarded based on the achievement of specific **performance metrics**.
- **Options**: financial instruments that provide the right to **purchase assets** at a future date for a specified price,
  typically used as compensation for key stakeholders or executives.
- **Carried interest**: a share of the **profits** in private equity, hedge funds, or other investment vehicles, typically given
  to fund managers as an incentive for performance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Upside benefits** must be disclosed, and the **performance criteria** must be clearly outlined.
- **Failure to disclose** or misclassify **upside benefits** can result in **non-compliance** and requires corrective action.

---

## B00.CH09.P03.S03 — Risk-transfer benefit: asymmetric downside, guarantees, limited recourse, bailouts

**Risk-transfer benefit** occurs when an individual or entity benefits from **asymmetric risks**, where they gain from
upside performance but **transfer the downside risk** to others. This may include guarantees, limited recourse provisions,
or **bailouts** that shield the entity from financial losses while still allowing them to benefit from profits.

### Key elements of risk-transfer benefit:
- **Asymmetric downside**: when an individual or entity receives **benefits** from an asset or investment but is not
  exposed to **the full risks** of loss (e.g., financial institutions or investors who profit from high-risk activities
  but are shielded from the consequences).
- **Guarantees**: financial **assurances** that protect investors or stakeholders from downside risks, often provided by
  government agencies or large institutions.
- **Limited recourse**: arrangements that limit the ability of creditors to recover losses in the event of financial
  distress, often seen in corporate financing or investment structures.
- **Bailouts**: public or private funds used to **rescue** an entity from failure, often at the expense of taxpayers or
  other stakeholders.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Risk-transfer benefits** must be **clearly disclosed** and the **terms** of any guarantees or bailout provisions
  must be transparent.
- **Non-compliance** arises if **risk-transfer benefits** are not fully disclosed or if they undermine the fairness of the
  financial system.

## B00.CH09.P03.S04 — In-kind benefit: perks, exclusive access, non-cash privileges (reclassification)

**In-kind benefit** refers to **non-cash benefits** that individuals or entities receive as part of their ownership or
control. These benefits may include **perks**, **exclusive access**, or **other non-cash privileges** that provide value
but are not directly monetary.

### Key elements of in-kind benefit:
- **Perks**: **non-cash benefits** such as access to exclusive services, luxury items, or preferential treatment in
  business or personal matters (e.g., private jets, VIP events).
- **Exclusive access**: the ability to gain entry to otherwise restricted opportunities, markets, or networks, such as
  exclusive investment opportunities or high-level industry meetings.
- **Non-cash privileges**: any other **non-monetary** benefits that provide value but are not represented by direct
  monetary compensation (e.g., free accommodations, access to clubs or elite services).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **In-kind benefits** must be fully disclosed and treated in the same way as **cashflow benefits** when assessing overall
  **ownership and control**.
- **Failure to disclose** in-kind benefits, or to accurately classify them, results in **non-compliance** and must be corrected.
