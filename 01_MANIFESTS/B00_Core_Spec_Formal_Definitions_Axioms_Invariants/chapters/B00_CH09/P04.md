# B00.CH09.P04 — Block test: definition and categories

This paragraph defines the **block test**, which is used to identify **block powers** that allow individuals or entities
to **prevent decisions** or **actions** without having formal control over an entity. The block test categorizes different
types of block power that may exist in complex ownership or control structures, ensuring that any **negative control** or
**interference** in decision-making is properly assessed and accounted for in determining **ultimate beneficial control** (UBC).

---

## B00.CH09.P04.S01 — Veto/exit-friction: ability to block choices without having positive control

**Veto/exit-friction** refers to the ability of an individual or group to **block decisions or actions** without having
**positive control** (i.e., the power to make decisions). This type of **negative control** is critical in ownership
structures where certain stakeholders can prevent key decisions from being made, even though they do not have full
decision-making power.

### Key principles:
- **Veto power**: the **right to block** decisions, such as mergers, acquisitions, or major corporate actions, without having
  to approve them. This can happen through special voting rights or specific agreements.
- **Exit friction**: the ability to prevent others from leaving or exiting a situation, whether through **contractual
  obligations** or **barriers** that make exit costly or difficult (e.g., non-compete clauses, restrictions on transferring
  shares).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- If an entity has the ability to **block decisions** through **veto rights** or **exit friction** mechanisms, this must
  be disclosed and evaluated for **non-compliance**.
- Any **veto power** or **exit restrictions** that are not clearly documented or that are used to obscure real control are
  considered **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH09.P04.S02 — Choke-point power: infrastructure, API, payment-rail, licence or supply-chain blockades

**Choke-point power** refers to the ability of an individual or entity to control critical **infrastructure**, **platforms**,
or **supply chains**, thereby having the power to block or restrict access to essential resources, services, or markets.

### Key choke-point powers:
- **Infrastructure control**: control over essential **infrastructure** such as utility networks, communication channels, or
  transport systems, allowing the controller to restrict access or **withhold** services.
- **API control**: having control over **APIs** (Application Programming Interfaces) that serve as the foundation for
  digital services or platforms, preventing others from interacting with the system or accessing data.
- **Payment-rail control**: controlling the **financial transaction systems** that enable payments and monetary flow,
  allowing the controller to impose restrictions or fees on transactions.
- **Licence or supply-chain blockades**: control over **licenses** or key supply chain points (e.g., restricting access
  to materials, technology, or intellectual property) to prevent others from participating in certain markets or
  industries.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any individual or entity that has **choke-point power** must ensure that its control over critical infrastructure is not
  used to **limit access** to public goods or prevent **equitable distribution**.
- If a **choke-point power** is found to be used for **exclusionary practices** or **anti-competitive behavior**, the
  channel will be classified as **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH09.P04.S03 — Contractual lock-in: exclusivity, non-competes, MFN, termination penalties

**Contractual lock-in** refers to agreements that prevent **exit** or restrict **competition** by creating **exclusive
arrangements**, **non-compete clauses**, **most-favored-nation (MFN) clauses**, or **penalties** for termination or
early exit.

### Key principles of contractual lock-in:
- **Exclusivity**: contractual clauses that require one party to **engage exclusively** with another party, effectively
  preventing the entry of competitors or the ability to switch to alternative suppliers.
- **Non-compete clauses**: clauses that prevent an individual or entity from working with or investing in competing
  businesses for a set period after termination of an agreement.
- **Most-favored-nation (MFN) clauses**: agreements that grant a party the **best terms** given to any other party,
  preventing other entities from accessing better terms and reducing market competition.
- **Termination penalties**: contractual penalties or fees that discourage **early exit** from agreements, binding parties
  into long-term arrangements.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Contractual lock-in** mechanisms that restrict access to markets, services, or competitors must be disclosed and
  evaluated to ensure they do not hinder **fair competition** or access to **public goods**.
- Any agreement that creates excessive **barriers to exit** or **anti-competitive practices** is considered **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH09.P04.S04 — Information and platform power: ranking/curation/algorithmic exclusion as block power

**Information and platform power** refers to the ability of an individual or entity to **control or manipulate access**
to critical information, services, or platforms through **ranking**, **curation**, or **algorithmic exclusion**. This
type of power allows entities to **block participation** by controlling how services or information are presented to users.

### Key forms of information and platform power:
- **Ranking and curation**: the ability to **control the ranking** or **curation** of content, products, or services, which
  can exclude competitors or prioritize certain actors.
- **Algorithmic exclusion**: using **algorithms** to filter, exclude, or favor certain content, users, or platforms,
  creating a **bias** that restricts access or distorts information flow.
- **Platform control**: controlling access to a platform that others rely on (e.g., social media platforms, e-commerce
  marketplaces, search engines), and using that control to **block competitors** or limit access to users.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Platform or information-based control** that limits access or **manipulates user choice** through **rankings** or
  **algorithms** must be disclosed and carefully managed.
- If a platform or information system is found to be engaging in **anti-competitive behavior** or **blocking access** to
  **fair market opportunities**, the system is considered **non-compliant** and must be corrected immediately.
