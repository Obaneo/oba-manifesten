# B00.CH09 — Control, benefit and block tests (ultimate control; look-through; consolidation)

This chapter defines the **control**, **benefit**, and **block tests** used to identify **ultimate beneficial control** (UBC)
and to establish the **consolidation** rules for **ownership**, **control**, and **benefit** within the OBA framework. It
addresses key issues such as how to identify **control** over entities, how to assess **benefit** from assets or flows, and
how to establish the **block power** that may affect decision-making within entities.

The purpose is to ensure that all relationships, whether direct or indirect, that confer **control** or **benefit** are
properly accounted for, to prevent **sham structures**, **nominees**, or any form of **ownership opacity** that could
undermine the integrity of the commons system. This includes clear rules for **look-through** (identifying indirect
ownership) and **consolidation** (determining when positions should be combined for regulatory purposes).

## How to use this chapter

- If you are **assessing ownership**: apply the **control**, **benefit**, and **block tests** to determine UBC and ensure
  compliance with OBA.
- If you are **designing or reviewing structures**: use the **look-through rules** to evaluate indirect ownership and
  control, ensuring that opaque or evasive structures are not used to bypass regulations.
- If you are **auditing or enforcing compliance**: ensure that ownership and control relationships are fully disclosed and
  that all **documentary evidence** (e.g., shareholder registers, contracts) is available for review.

## Status

This chapter sets the **normative framework** for assessing ownership, control, and benefit in the context of OBA. It
defines the key **tests** that must be applied to ensure that ownership structures are transparent, non-extractive, and in
line with the principles of public goods provision. This chapter must remain stable across versions, as changes here
could fundamentally alter how **ownership and control** are understood in the context of the commons.
