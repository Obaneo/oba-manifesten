# B00.CH09.P06 — Look-through rules: indirect structures and nominees

This paragraph outlines the **look-through rules**, which are essential for identifying **indirect ownership** and
**control** structures that may obscure the true **economic owner** or **ultimate beneficial controller** (UBC) of
assets, entities, or flows. These rules are particularly important for detecting and addressing **nominee arrangements**,
**trusts**, **foundations**, **funds**, and other complex ownership structures that attempt to hide or obscure real
control. The goal of these rules is to ensure transparency and accountability by attributing ownership and control to
their true economic owners, regardless of how ownership is legally structured.

---

## B00.CH09.P06.S01 — Trusts/foundations: settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiaries (attribution)

In the case of **trusts** and **foundations**, **look-through** rules require that the **economic ownership** and **control**
of assets held within these structures are clearly attributed to the **settlor**, **trustees**, **protectors**, and **beneficiaries**.

### Key principles of trust/foundation attribution:
- **Settlor**: the individual or entity that **establishes** the trust or foundation and contributes the assets.
- **Trustee**: the party responsible for **managing** the trust or foundation’s assets and ensuring that they are used in
  accordance with the settlor’s intentions.
- **Protector**: an individual or entity that has the power to **intervene** in the management or distribution of the trust
  or foundation’s assets, often acting as a safeguard to ensure the assets are used appropriately.
- **Beneficiaries**: the individuals or entities that **benefit** from the assets or income generated by the trust or foundation.

The **true economic ownership** and **control** of the trust or foundation are attributed to the relevant individuals
or entities, even though legal ownership may rest with the trustee.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **trust** or **foundation** structure must disclose the **settlor**, **trustees**, **protectors**, and **beneficiaries**,
  and their roles in the allocation of **control** and **benefits**.
- **Failure to disclose** or **obscure control** or **benefit relationships** within trusts or foundations results in **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH09.P06.S02 — Funds/GP–LP: management control, carried interest, side letters

**Funds** (e.g., private equity or hedge funds) operate under complex structures, particularly with the **General Partner (GP)**
and **Limited Partners (LP)**. **Look-through rules** ensure that **control**, **benefit**, and **attribution of ownership**
are properly allocated between these parties.

### Key principles for funds/GP–LP structures:
- **Management control (GP)**: the **General Partner (GP)** typically holds **management control**, making key decisions and
  executing strategy.
- **Carried interest**: a **share of the profits** from the fund that is paid to the GP, typically based on performance,
  aligning their interests with the fund's success.
- **Side letters**: private agreements that modify the terms of the fund's structure for specific investors, potentially
  creating **special privileges** or benefits that are not shared by other LPs.

These elements must be **clearly disclosed**, and the **economic ownership** and **control** in the fund should be attributed
correctly, especially when side letters or different compensation structures could affect the balance of control and benefit.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Side letters** and **carried interest arrangements** must be **disclosed** and included in the assessment of control and
  benefit.
- Failure to disclose special agreements, or if the GP has undisclosed control or benefit, leads to **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH09.P06.S03 — Custody/nominee: legal holder ≠ economic owner (attribution)

In **custody** or **nominee arrangements**, the **legal holder** of an asset may not be the **economic owner**. The
**look-through rules** ensure that the **true beneficial owner** is identified, even if the legal title is held by a nominee
or custodian.

### Key principles:
- **Custodian/nominee**: entities that hold **legal title** to assets on behalf of another party, such as a **nominee** or
  **custodian** bank.
- **Economic owner**: the **true beneficial owner** who has the rights to the asset’s income, decision-making power, and
  **ultimate control**.
- **Attribution**: it is critical to trace the economic ownership of an asset back to the real owner, regardless of the
  legal holder.

These mechanisms are used to **shield ownership**, and the **look-through rules** ensure that the **economic owner** is
correctly identified and held accountable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **custody** or **nominee** structure must be **transparent** regarding the **economic ownership** of assets.
- **Failure to disclose** the **true beneficial ownership** in these structures is **non-compliant**.

## B00.CH09.P06.S04 — Layering and cross-holdings: graph resolution and cycles (conflict rules)

In complex ownership structures, especially those involving **layering** (multiple layers of ownership) and **cross-holdings**
(ownership of one entity by another), **graph resolution** methods must be used to **untangle** these structures and identify
the **true economic owner**. **Conflict rules** ensure that these complex structures are not used to obscure ownership or control.

### Key principles:
- **Layering**: the use of **multiple layers** of ownership to hide the true controller or economic owner (e.g., holding
  companies, trusts).
- **Cross-holdings**: when one entity **owns shares in another entity**, and vice versa, creating a circular or **cyclical**
  ownership structure that can obscure true control.
- **Graph resolution**: using **graph analysis** techniques to untangle and identify the **true ownership** structure of
  entities, even when they involve complex interconnections or cross-ownership.
- **Conflict rules**: rules that govern situations where ownership is obscured or complex, ensuring that the true
  beneficial owners are identified and that **control** and **benefits** are correctly attributed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Layering** and **cross-holdings** must be **fully disclosed** and resolved to ensure that the true **economic owners** are
  identified.
- If ownership structures are **too complex** to resolve or if cross-holdings obscure control and benefit, the system will
  be considered **non-compliant** and must be rectified.
