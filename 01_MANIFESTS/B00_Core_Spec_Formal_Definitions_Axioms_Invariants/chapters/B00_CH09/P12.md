# B00.CH09.P12 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph addresses **failure modes** and the **hard stops** that are triggered when ownership, control, or benefit
structures do not meet the required transparency or compliance standards within the OBA framework. It identifies situations
where entities attempt to circumvent OBA principles, such as through **pseudo-compliance**, **regulatory arbitrage**, or **capture**.
When these issues arise, hard stops are applied to prevent non-compliant entities from engaging in OBA-related activities.

---

## B00.CH09.P12.S01 — Pseudo-UBO compliance: formally correct but de facto control hidden

**Pseudo-UBO compliance** refers to situations where ownership or control structures appear to comply with OBA requirements on
paper (i.e., formally correct) but **de facto control** remains hidden, often through complex structures or hidden arrangements
that obscure true control.

### Key principles:
- **Formal compliance, hidden control**: the entity may meet the formal requirements for UBO (Ultimate Beneficial Owner)
  disclosure but may still conceal the **true economic control** behind layers of legal entities, trusts, or nominee structures.
- **De facto control**: even if the formal documentation appears correct, if **real control** is hidden behind the structure, it
  is **non-compliant** with OBA principles.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Pseudo-UBO compliance** must be addressed by fully **disclosing the real control** and economic ownership.
- Any structure that hides **de facto control** behind formal compliance is **non-compliant** and must be corrected immediately.

---

## B00.CH09.P12.S02 — Regulatory arbitrage: structures designed to avoid thresholds

**Regulatory arbitrage** occurs when entities design **ownership structures** or financial arrangements to **avoid regulatory thresholds** set by OBA. This includes exploiting differences between jurisdictions or rules to bypass the intended protections of public goods, wealth caps, or other OBA requirements.

### Key principles:
- **Avoidance of regulatory thresholds**: entities may set up structures that **circumvent** limits on wealth, income, or other
  OBA parameters by exploiting gaps or inconsistencies in **local or international regulations**.
- **Evasion tactics**: creating entities in jurisdictions with **weaker regulations** or using complex financial products to
  avoid reporting, accountability, or compliance with OBA rules.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any entity found to be **engaging in regulatory arbitrage** must **rectify** their structure to comply with OBA rules, regardless
  of jurisdictional differences or loopholes.
- **Failure to comply** with OBA’s thresholds due to regulatory arbitrage results in **non-compliance** and must be immediately
  corrected.

---

## B00.CH09.P12.S03 — Capture: exceptions for “strategic” players or sectors

**Capture** refers to situations where certain entities, often labeled as **"strategic" players**, are given **exceptions** or
**special treatment** under OBA, despite their behavior being contrary to the principles of fairness, transparency, and
non-extraction. This undermines the integrity of the system, as special privileges may be granted to **powerful sectors**,
financial elites, or **politically connected entities**.

### Key principles:
- **Strategic players**: entities that **influence** policy or regulation by leveraging their size, market power, or
  political connections.
- **Exceptions for strategic sectors**: sectors deemed “critical” or “strategic” may receive special exceptions to OBA
  regulations, **undermining fairness** and allowing for **rent-seeking** or **monopolistic behavior**.
- **Non-compliance through capture**: any **exceptional treatment** granted to these sectors must be transparent and
  fully documented, ensuring that it does not undermine the principles of OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Capture practices** must be **fully disclosed** and scrutinized to ensure that no special exceptions are allowed for **strategic
  players** that violate OBA.
- Any **exception** made to allow certain players to bypass OBA principles leads to **non-compliance** and must be addressed
  immediately.

---

## B00.CH09.P12.S04 — Fail-closed: irresolvable ownership opacity → consolidate and enforce

A **fail-closed** system ensures that if ownership or control structures are **irresolvable** due to **opacity** or
**non-compliance**, they are **automatically consolidated** and **enforced** until transparency is restored. This principle
ensures that **ownership opacity** does not persist and that all relevant control and benefit relationships are clearly
understood and reported.

### Key principles:
- **Irresolvable opacity**: if **ownership** or **control** cannot be traced due to hidden or complex structures, the entity
  will be placed into **fail-closed** status.
- **Consolidation and enforcement**: once an entity is placed into **fail-closed** status, its ownership structure must be
  consolidated and re-evaluated for compliance with OBA.
- **Enforcement**: any entity that remains **opaque** or **non-compliant** will face immediate enforcement measures, including
  **reclassification**, **penalties**, and potential **disqualification** from participation in OBA.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Failure to resolve opacity** or provide **clear documentation** for ownership or control results in **fail-closed status**.
- Any entity found with **irresolvable opacity** or **non-compliance** must undergo **consolidation** and be re-evaluated for
  compliance with OBA.
