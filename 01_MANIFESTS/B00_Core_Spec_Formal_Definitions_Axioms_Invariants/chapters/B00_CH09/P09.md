# B00.CH09.P09 — Edge cases: modern structures

This paragraph explores the **edge cases** in modern ownership and control structures that may challenge traditional
interpretations of **control**, **benefit**, and **block** tests within the OBA framework. As financial markets and
ownership models evolve, new structures and instruments emerge that require careful analysis to ensure transparency and
compliance with OBA principles. These edge cases may involve **dual-class shares**, **derivatives**, **tokenization**,
and **platform ecosystems**, each of which presents unique challenges in determining **ultimate beneficial control** (UBC)
and **ownership transparency**.

---

## B00.CH09.P09.S01 — Dual-class shares and control without cashflow

**Dual-class shares** represent a **modern structure** that can concentrate **control** in the hands of a small group,
while providing limited **cashflow benefits** to those same individuals or entities. These structures are commonly used in
**startups** or **publicly traded companies** to maintain control with **founders** or **key stakeholders** while allowing
investors to participate in the financial returns through the issuance of **secondary shares**.

### Key principles:
- **Control without cashflow**: certain **dual-class share structures** allow founders or key executives to **maintain voting
  control** (e.g., Class A shares with **super-voting rights**) while offering **Class B shares** that carry **limited voting
  rights** but provide **dividends** and **other cashflow benefits**.
- **Non-convertibility**: dual-class shares often prevent the **conversion** of voting rights into economic benefits, allowing
  **control** to remain concentrated even as cashflow from assets or operations is distributed to a broader group of investors.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Dual-class share structures** must be fully disclosed and **properly evaluated** for their impact on control, benefit, and
  **block powers**.
- If **control** is disproportionately concentrated in the hands of a few individuals or entities without a corresponding
  **cashflow** benefit, it must be treated as **non-compliant** if it results in **disproportionate influence** over decision-making.

## B00.CH09.P09.S02 — Derivatives and synthetic exposures: TRS, swaps, options (benefit/risk)

**Derivatives** and **synthetic exposures** are financial instruments that allow **parties to gain exposure to an asset's
value or risk** without actually owning the asset. These instruments, such as **Total Return Swaps (TRS)**, **swaps**, and
**options**, can be used to either transfer **benefits** (e.g., price appreciation) or **risks** (e.g., downside exposure),
and can **obfuscate control** or **benefit attribution**.

### Key principles:
- **Total Return Swaps (TRS)**: allow the holder of the swap to gain the **total economic return** of an underlying asset,
  such as dividends or capital appreciation, without owning the asset itself.
- **Swaps and options**: allow for the exchange of future cashflows based on an underlying asset’s performance, often used to
  hedge risk or gain exposure to assets without direct ownership.
- **Synthetic exposures**: allow financial entities to replicate the returns or risks of holding an asset, often without having
  actual **legal ownership** of the asset.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Derivatives and synthetic exposures** that **mirror** ownership or control must be evaluated in terms of their **benefits**
  and **risks**.
- If these instruments are used to **mask control or benefit** from an asset or entity, they must be considered **non-compliant**
  and **fail-closed** until rectified.

## B00.CH09.P09.S03 — Tokenisation/DAO-like constructs: actual control and governance capture

**Tokenisation** and **Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs)** are emerging constructs that aim to **decentralize
control** and **distribute ownership** in a more transparent and blockchain-based manner. However, these systems raise
questions about who truly has **control**, **benefit**, and **block** powers, especially in the context of **governance
capture**.

### Key principles:
- **Tokenisation**: involves creating **digital tokens** that represent ownership, voting rights, or participation in a
  project, typically through blockchain technology.
- **DAOs**: decentralized organizations that aim to **distribute governance** and decision-making to all token holders,
  often without a central authority.
- **Governance capture**: the risk that **small groups of token holders** or participants could **capture control** by
  accumulating large quantities of tokens, leading to **centralized decision-making** despite the appearance of decentralization.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Tokenisation** and **DAO structures** must be **evaluated for control** and **governance transparency**, ensuring that no
  party can **illegitimately dominate** decision-making.
- **Failure to ensure decentralization** or if **governance is captured** by a small group in a **DAO or tokenized system**
  results in **non-compliance**.

## B00.CH09.P09.S04 — Platform ecosystems: API terms, ranking, payment gating as control/block

**Platform ecosystems** are increasingly used to **control access to markets** and **data** through mechanisms like
**APIs**, **ranking systems**, and **payment gating**. These platforms can exert **control** by determining what services
or participants are allowed access, how they are ranked, and what fees they must pay.

### Key principles:
- **API terms**: the **terms of service** for platforms that govern access to their application programming interfaces
  (APIs). These terms often dictate which users, services, or products are allowed to interact with the platform.
- **Ranking systems**: platforms often use **ranking algorithms** to determine which services, products, or participants
  receive **higher visibility** or better terms, effectively controlling who gains access to users and markets.
- **Payment gating**: **fees** or **payment requirements** that restrict access to a platform or service, often placing
  unnecessary financial barriers to entry.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **Platform ecosystems** must be transparent about their **API terms**, **ranking systems**, and **payment gating
  mechanisms**.
- **Any platform** that uses **restrictive access**, **unfair ranking systems**, or **payment gating** to block or limit
  participation must ensure compliance with **non-extractive principles**. Failure to do so will result in **non-compliance**.
