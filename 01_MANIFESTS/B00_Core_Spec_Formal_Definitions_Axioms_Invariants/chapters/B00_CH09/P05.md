# B00.CH09.P05 — UBC synthesis: composite decision rules

This paragraph outlines the **synthesis** of the **UBC (ultimate beneficial control)** criterion, combining the key tests of **control**, **benefit**, and **block** to create a comprehensive decision-making framework for identifying true owners and controllers. The **composite decision rules** integrate these tests into a **single, unified approach** to determining who ultimately controls or benefits from entities and assets within the OBA framework.

---

## B00.CH09.P05.S01 — UBC criterion: combination of control + benefit + block (canonical threshold)

The **UBC criterion** combines the tests for **control**, **benefit**, and **block** to establish a **canonical threshold** for determining **ultimate beneficial control**. This threshold helps identify whether a party has sufficient **control**, **benefit**, or **blocking power** to be considered the ultimate beneficial owner of an asset, entity, or income stream.

### Key principles:
- **Control**: Does the party have the **ability to make decisions** or **direct operations** through formal or de facto mechanisms?
- **Benefit**: Does the party **receive financial or in-kind benefits** (e.g., cash flow, appreciation, exclusive access)?
- **Block**: Does the party have the **ability to block key decisions** or actions (e.g., veto power, exit friction, choke points)?

The combination of these three factors determines whether a party should be classified as having **UBC**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- **UBC** must be assessed by combining **control**, **benefit**, and **block** tests. If the combined threshold is met,
  the party is considered the **ultimate beneficial owner**.
- Any attempt to artificially separate or obscure these factors in order to avoid UBC identification is **non-compliant**.

---

## B00.CH09.P05.S02 — Priority rules in case of conflict: control prevails unless benefit/block dominance (canonical)

In situations where there is a **conflict** between control, benefit, and block powers, the **control test** takes precedence
in determining ultimate beneficial ownership. This means that **control** will dominate in cases of dispute, unless the
**benefit** or **block** power is significantly more dominant.

### Key principles:
- **Control prevails**: In cases where a party has both control and some benefit or block power, **control** is the deciding
  factor for determining UBC.
- **Benefit/block dominance**: If the **benefit** or **block** power is significantly more dominant than **control**, then
  these factors may override control in determining UBC.
- **Canonical hierarchy**: This rule ensures that the structure of ownership and control is clear, and prevents circumvention
  by parties who might otherwise attempt to gain dominance through **non-formal mechanisms** like benefit extraction or
  blocking.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- The **control test** should always take priority, unless **benefit** or **block** power clearly outweighs it.
- **Failure to apply** this priority rule correctly leads to **non-compliance** in determining ultimate beneficial ownership.

## B00.CH09.P05.S03 — Shared control: joint arrangements, pacts, syndicates, “acting in concert”

**Shared control** occurs in cases where multiple parties collectively hold decision-making power over an entity or asset,
whether through **joint arrangements**, **pacts**, **syndicates**, or by **acting in concert**.

### Key principles of shared control:
- **Joint arrangements**: situations where two or more entities or individuals share control over a decision-making process
  (e.g., joint ventures, partnerships).
- **Pacts and syndicates**: formal or informal **agreements** between multiple parties to control or influence key decisions
  (e.g., shareholder agreements, collaborative business ventures).
- **Acting in concert**: when parties work together to achieve common objectives or exercise control, even if they do not
  individually have the control or benefit required to act independently.

Shared control arrangements must be thoroughly disclosed and assessed to ensure that they do not circumvent **OBA regulations**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **shared control** arrangement must be disclosed, with clear documentation of the parties involved and their respective
  powers.
- **Failure to disclose** or adequately account for **shared control** leads to **non-compliance**.

---

## B00.CH09.P05.S04 — Family/household consolidation: shared control/benefit as default, with exception rules

In the context of **family** or **household** structures, **shared control** and **shared benefit** are assumed as the default
principles. This recognizes that within families or households, ownership and control are often collectively shared.

### Key principles:
- **Shared control/benefit**: it is assumed that within families or households, control and benefit are collectively held
  unless evidence indicates otherwise.
- **Exception rules**: there are exceptions to this rule, particularly when formal agreements (e.g., wills, trusts, or
  shareholder agreements) dictate different arrangements for control and benefit allocation.
- **Consolidation**: in these cases, all family or household members may be considered to hold **shared control** and **benefit**
  for purposes of **consolidating ownership** and determining **UBC**.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any **family/household consolidation** must be clearly documented, with **formal agreements** or **evidence** to
  substantiate different control/benefit arrangements.
- **Failure to demonstrate** shared control/benefit or provide exceptions for non-compliance leads to **non-compliant** status.
