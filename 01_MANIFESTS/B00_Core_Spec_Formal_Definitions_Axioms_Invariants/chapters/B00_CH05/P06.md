# B00.CH05.P06 — Ultimate Beneficial Control (UBC) and look-through consolidation

This paragraph defines **Ultimate Beneficial Control (UBC)** and the canonical **look-through consolidation**
required for W-MAX. Because W can be held through vehicles, nominees, and layered structures, any cap regime that
does not consolidate by UBC is leaky by construction.

UBC is a functional relation test: **control**, **benefit**, and **block** determine whether assets and rights are
attributed to the same ultimate unit for cap purposes. Legal title is not decisive.

Normative posture is conservative: where ownership/control reality is opaque, the system must fail closed (provisional
attribution or temporary enforcement) until clarity is provided.

---

## B00.CH05.P06.S01 — UBC definition: control, benefit and block as consolidation rules

UBC is defined by three canonical relation tests, evaluated per pair (ultimate unit ↔ entity/asset/vehicle):

1) **Control**: the unit can direct decisions or outcomes (de facto or de jure), including through voting, board
   control, contractual governance, or operational dominance.
2) **Benefit**: the unit receives economic benefit (cashflows, upside, risk transfer, preferential rights), including
   through distributions, carried interests, fee extraction, or value realization pathways.
3) **Block**: the unit can veto, prevent exit, impose lock-in, or otherwise exert negative control that materially
   shapes outcomes (veto rights, supermajority blocks, key-holder rights).

Consolidation rule (canonical):
- If any of the UBC tests establishes decisive linkage at the regime threshold, the asset/vehicle is consolidated
  into the unit’s W-base, subject to overlap/conflict rules (S04).

UBC is evaluated by economic reality:
- nominal ownership percentages are not sufficient,
- dispersed formal ownership does not defeat UBC when control/benefit/block is unified in substance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST implement UBC tests as consolidation rules; “ownership-only” consolidation is
  non-conform.
- UBC thresholds and evidence requirements must be published and stable within a release; discretionary ad hoc
  thresholds are non-conform.

## B00.CH05.P06.S02 — Graph consolidation: indirect chains, cross-holdings, nominee structures

UBC consolidation must be performed on a **graph** of relations, not as a simple direct-ownership list.

Canonical graph requirements:

- **Indirect chains:** consolidate through multi-layer chains (A controls B controls C → A consolidates C).
- **Cross-holdings and cycles:** detect circular ownership and compute net control/benefit/block attribution without
  double counting.
- **Nominee structures:** treat nominees/custodians as passthrough nodes; consolidate to the ultimate controller/
  beneficiary unless evidence proves genuine independence.
- **Layered instruments:** include governance rights, preferential rights, and side letters as graph edges, not only
  equity shares.

Graph evaluation must prevent two failures:
- **under-attribution** (hiding assets in chains),
- **double attribution** (counting the same asset multiple times across overlapping chains).

Opacity handling:
- if key edges are missing (unknown nominee, undisclosed side letter), the system must apply conservative attribution
  rules and/or trigger fail-closed enforcement posture.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST represent UBC as a graph evaluation problem and must support indirect/cyclic structures.
- If nominee layers or SPVs cannot be traced, the affected W is non-auditable; cap enforcement must be provisional
  or the compatibility claim must be suspended for that scope.

## B00.CH05.P06.S03 — Family and household rules: joint control/benefit (canonical)

W-MAX is applied to ultimate units in economic reality. Households and families can operate as joint economic actors,
and vehicles are often controlled jointly.

Canonical household/family consolidation rules (parameterized but binding in structure):

- **Household unit:** where resources, liabilities, and control are pooled, W attribution must consolidate at the
  household level (or apply explicit split rules that do not create avoidance).
- **Joint benefit/control:** if two persons jointly benefit from or jointly control a structure (shared governance,
  shared upside, shared blocking), the structure is consolidated under a joint regime.
- **Anti-dispersion:** splitting assets across spouses/partners/children to remain below W-MAX is treated as avoidance
  when economic reality indicates unified control/benefit.

The precise household definition and joint attribution thresholds are regime parameters, but must be explicit,
auditable, and stable within a release.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST define household/family consolidation rules; absence creates a trivial dispersion loophole.
- If household splitting materially changes cap outcomes without a real change in economic control/benefit, the split
  is non-conform and must be requalified.

## B00.CH05.P06.S04 — Conflict rules: overlap, shared control, split-benefit situations

Real structures include overlap and shared power. Consolidation must therefore include conflict rules to avoid both
double counting and strategic ambiguity.

Canonical conflict patterns:

- **Overlap:** the same asset is linked to multiple ultimate units via different rights (e.g., one controls, another
  benefits).
- **Shared control:** multiple units jointly control or block decisions (consortium control, joint ventures).
- **Split benefit:** upside flows to one party while control/block resides with another (e.g., carried interest vs GP
  control; trusts with trustees vs beneficiaries).
- **Competing claims:** secured creditors, pledgees, and rehypothecation chains create layered rights.

Canonical conflict resolution principles:

1) **Attribute by functional power:** control/block rights are cap-relevant even without cashflow benefit; benefit is
   cap-relevant even without formal control.
2) **Prevent double counting:** an underlying asset’s value must not be fully counted multiple times across units; use
   allocated shares or regime-defined attribution weights.
3) **Fail closed on ambiguity:** if the split cannot be resolved due to missing data, apply conservative attribution
   (erring toward inclusion) and require disclosure to certify.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any implementation MUST publish its conflict-resolution rules (allocation weights, precedence rules) and apply them
  consistently.
- Where split-benefit/control structures are used to evade attribution, requalification is mandatory; unresolved
  ambiguity suspends compatibility claims for the affected structures until remediated.
