# B00.CH05.P17 — Failure modes and hard stops

This paragraph defines canonical **failure modes** and **hard stops** for W-MAX. These are non-conformance
conditions. If a hard stop is triggered, the affected outputs (and where relevant, the regime-level compatibility
claim) are suspended until remediation is completed, audited, and logged.

W-MAX is uniquely sensitive to leakage and capture. A “cap” that can be bypassed by wrappers, valuation games, or
discretion is not an order instrument; it is semantic theater.

---

## B00.CH05.P17.S01 — W-MAX-washing: “cap exists” but base is leaky (equivalents excluded)

**W-MAX-washing** occurs when a regime claims to cap wealth but defines a base that excludes the main claim-storage
channels. The cap exists in text, but the economic substance remains uncapped.

Canonical leakage sources (non-exhaustive):
- excluding **economic equivalents** (derivatives, total return swaps, structured replication),
- excluding **vehicles** (trusts, foundations, holding chains) by refusing look-through consolidation,
- excluding **private assets** or IP by treating them as “unvalued” or “non-wealth”,
- excluding **control rights** and concessions that confer durable power,
- netting against non-recognized or circular liabilities to shrink net W.

Detection rules (minimum):
- compare measured W distribution to known asset ownership structures and macro wealth aggregates (reconciliation).
- monitor growth in excluded channels (derivatives, offshore vehicles) after cap introduction.
- test whether “capped” subjects reduce direct holdings while increasing equivalent exposures.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If the base excludes a dominant share of cap-relevant claim channels (equivalents/vehicles/control rights) such that
  W can be preserved in substance while measured W declines, the regime is non-conform and any compatibility claim is
  suspended until the base is repaired and restated.

---

## B00.CH05.P17.S02 — Gaming: valuation manipulation, window dressing, dispersion over nominees

Even with a broad base, W-MAX can be defeated through gaming.

Canonical gaming patterns include (non-exhaustive):

### (a) Valuation manipulation
- using bespoke models with favorable assumptions,
- suppressing private company valuations via transfer pricing or related-party terms,
- internal “transactions” at non-arm’s-length prices to create faux comparables,
- understating control premiums and preferential rights.

### (b) Window dressing
- temporary transfers around reference dates (repo-like moves, parking with nominees),
- liability repayment/creation cycles to manipulate net W,
- short-term conversions into excluded forms during measurement windows.

### (c) Dispersion over nominees
- splitting holdings across family members/nominees while retaining unified control/benefit,
- using custodial opacity and offshore nominee directors to break traceability.

Canonical response expectation:
- requalification, conservative valuation overrides, event-driven triggers, and fail-closed provisional enforcement.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If valuation is materially non-reproducible (black-box) or if repeated window-dressing/nominee dispersion is detected
  without effective escalation, the subject/channel is non-conform and enforcement must switch to fail-closed posture.
- If such gaming is widespread and structural, regime-level compatibility is suspended.

---

## B00.CH05.P17.S03 — Capture risk: exceptions, discretionary deals, selective enforcement

W-MAX is highly exposed to **capture** because it targets concentrated power. Capture manifests as discretionary
exceptions and selective enforcement.

Canonical capture indicators include (non-exhaustive):
- expanding carve-outs beyond functional bounds (luxury “primary residence”, “entrepreneur” shells),
- bespoke negotiated valuation deals (“settlements”) that substitute for canonical rules,
- selective enforcement focused on politically weak subjects,
- delayed enforcement via endless appeals granted to high-power subjects,
- institutional funding/dependence links between enforcers and cap subjects.

Canonical principle:
- if enforcement becomes discretionary, the cap becomes a privilege regime, not an order invariant.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- Any discretionary exception outside canonical change procedure (or any selective enforcement without canonical basis)
  triggers non-conform status for the affected decisions and may trigger regime-level suspension if systematic.
- If exceptions are used to preserve status/legacy privileges, the regime is non-compatible with OBA by definition.

---

## B00.CH05.P17.S04 — Fail-closed: structural audit failure suspends compatibility claim

Structural audit failure occurs when the system cannot reliably measure and enforce W-MAX due to persistent opacity,
missing disclosures, or non-reproducible valuation.

Canonical structural failure conditions include (non-exhaustive):
- beneficial ownership cannot be established for a material share of cap-base assets,
- valuation depends materially on undisclosed models or unverifiable inputs,
- collateral/rehypothecation chains make claim layers untraceable,
- reporting duty is unenforced and non-compliance is tolerated,
- monitoring shows avoidance channels growing faster than enforcement capacity.

Fail-closed rule:
- when structural failure is present, the system must not continue to claim compatibility. It must suspend the claim,
  apply conservative provisional enforcement where possible, and remediate the measurement/enforcement infrastructure.

### Hard stop condition (binding)

- If structural audit failure persists beyond defined remediation windows, the W-MAX compatibility claim is suspended
  for the regime scope until the disclosure/valuation/consolidation infrastructure is repaired and independently audited.
