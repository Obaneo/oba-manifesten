# B00.CH05.P07 — Cap parameterisation: W-MAX as boundary and regime

This paragraph defines how **W-MAX** is parameterised as a canonical cap regime. W-MAX is not “a number”; it is a
binding interface consisting of:
- a cap form (absolute limit vs threshold + skimming),
- parameter values (unit, indexation, cohort rules),
- jurisdiction/residency scope rules,
- and strict controls against discretionary exceptions.

Parameterisation is normative: changing W-MAX parameters changes order outcomes and avoidance incentives. Therefore
parameter changes must follow canonical governance procedures (Kernspec) and must be versioned.

---

## B00.CH05.P07.S01 — Definition of W-MAX: absolute limit vs threshold + skimming function (canonical choice)

W-MAX can be implemented in one of two canonical forms. The regime MUST explicitly choose one form and specify its
mechanics.

### Form A — Absolute limit (hard cap)
- W must not exceed W-MAX.
- Breach triggers mandatory remedies that restore W to ≤ W-MAX within defined timing rules.
- Typical remedies: forced divestment, conversion to commons lockbox, mandatory skimming until compliance.

### Form B — Threshold + skimming function (soft cap with automatic reallocation)
- W may exceed a threshold, but any excess is subject to an automatic **skimming function**:
  - **skim = f(W − W-MAX)** with a declared function form and parameters.
- Skimming must be automatic and non-discretionary; otherwise enforcement becomes political and gameable.
- Skim destination is governed by the W-MAX → C reallocation path (CH05.P09) under traceability requirements.

Canonical choice requirement:
- the regime MUST state which form applies and must specify whether W-MAX is a boundary (hard) or a threshold (soft),
  including remedy and enforcement logic.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST specify cap form and remedy/skimming mechanics; “we cap wealth” without mechanics is
  non-conform.
- Any skimming function must be explicit, parameterized, and auditable; discretionary skimming is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH05.P07.S02 — Parameters: unit, indexation, cohort rules (only via Kernspec procedure)

W-MAX parameterisation includes (minimum):

- **Unit and currency basis:** nominal unit, real unit, or indexed unit (must be explicit).
- **Indexation rule:** inflation index, wage index, or other canonical index; frequency and method must be declared.
- **Reference time convention:** point-in-time or averaging window (links to valuation discipline and anti–window dressing).
- **Cohort rules:** if any cohort-specific parameterization exists (generally discouraged for W-MAX), it must follow
  canonical constraints and must not create privilege channels.

Governance constraint:
- parameter choices and changes may occur only through the canonical **Kernspec procedure** (defined evidence,
  impact analysis, ratification, versioning, and rollback logic). Ad hoc political exceptions are forbidden.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- W-MAX parameters MUST be published and versioned; silent indexation drift is non-conform.
- Any parameter change MUST include impact analysis (distribution, avoidance incentives, enforcement feasibility) and
  a published changelog; otherwise compatibility claims are suspended for the changed regime.

---

## B00.CH05.P07.S03 — Jurisdiction and residency rules: who is subject to which W-MAX regime

W-MAX is applied under jurisdictional scope. The regime must specify who is subject, under what nexus rules, and how
cross-border positions are treated.

Canonical scope fields include:

- **Residency tests:** where the person/household is resident under defined tests (centre of life/control, not paper residency).
- **Source/nexus rules:** treatment of assets located in the jurisdiction, entities incorporated there, and rights
  enforceable there.
- **UBC and look-through across borders:** vehicles and nominees offshore remain in scope when controlled/benefited by
  a subject unit (subject to treaty coherence rules in CH05.P11).
- **Temporal rules:** entry/exit timing, reference dates, and mark-to-exit logic (detailed in CH05.P11).

The scope regime must minimise arbitrage: W-MAX cannot be optional via re-domiciliation or wrapper export.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST define residency and nexus rules that prevent sham residency and offshore bypass.
- If the scope regime allows trivial migration avoidance without mark-to-exit or look-through enforcement, it is
  non-conform for OBA-grade claims.

---

## B00.CH05.P07.S04 — Forbidden variation: no ad-hoc exceptions without canonical change procedure

W-MAX must not devolve into a discretionary privilege system. Therefore:

- **No ad hoc exceptions** (sector, status, legacy, “national champion”, political deals).
- **No selective enforcement** (targeting some subjects while exempting others).
- **No bespoke valuation deals** that reduce W by agreement rather than by canonical rules.
- **No “temporary” exemptions** that become permanent via drift.

Any exception or carve-out must be:
- explicitly defined in canon (see CH05.P10),
- parameterized,
- auditable,
- and adopted only via the Kernspec change procedure with impact analysis and sunset/rollback where applicable.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime with discretionary exceptions is non-conform for OBA-grade compatibility claims.
- If enforcement or exceptions differ across subjects without canonical basis, the regime triggers capture risk and
  must be classified non-compatible until corrected.
