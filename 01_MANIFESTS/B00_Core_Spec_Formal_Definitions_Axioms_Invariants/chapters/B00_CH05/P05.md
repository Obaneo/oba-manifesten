# B00.CH05.P05 — Valuation and measurement rules (valuation discipline)

This paragraph defines the canonical **valuation discipline** for measuring **W** and applying **W-MAX**.
Valuation is the main attack surface of any wealth cap: if valuation can be manipulated, the base becomes leaky and
enforcement becomes discretionary.

Therefore OBA requires:
- a strict **valuation hierarchy**,
- conservative treatment of illiquidity and model risk,
- standardized methods for IP and private companies with explicit manipulation bans,
- rules for reference dates vs averaging and explicit anti–window dressing controls.

Default posture: where valuation is uncertain or opaque, the system applies conservatism and may fail closed (temporary
enforcement or non-certifying status) until auditable valuation is provided.

---

## B00.CH05.P05.S01 — Valuation hierarchy: market price > book > model (with conservatism)

W valuation must follow a canonical hierarchy, using the most objective valuation available.

**Hierarchy (binding):**

1) **Market price** (observable, arm’s-length):
   - listed assets, actively traded instruments, observable comparable transaction prices.
2) **Book/statement value** (audited accounting value):
   - only where market price is unavailable and accounting is audited and consistent with canonical adjustments.
3) **Model value** (valuation model):
   - only where neither market nor credible audited book is available, and only with disclosed method, inputs,
     and independent challenge.

Conservatism rules:
- choose valuation inputs and assumptions that reduce the ability to understate W.
- if multiple credible values exist, select the **cap-relevant conservative** value (typically the higher value for
  assets, lower value for liabilities, subject to domain constraints).
- model risk must be explicitly represented (bands/haircuts) rather than hidden as a point estimate.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST declare the valuation basis per asset class and follow the hierarchy; skipping to
  models where market evidence exists is non-conform.
- Any model valuation used in cap enforcement MUST be reproducible and auditable; black-box valuation is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH05.P05.S02 — Illiquid assets: haircut rules, bands, independent valuation

Illiquid assets (private equity, private real estate positions, thinly traded instruments, bespoke claims) require
special handling because the absence of a market creates scope for understatement.

Canonical requirements:

- **Haircuts / liquidity adjustments:** apply standardized haircuts or conservative adjustments where realizability
  is uncertain or where markets are thin.
- **Bands:** report valuation intervals, not only point estimates, when model risk is material.
- **Independent valuation:** require independent valuation (or independent challenge) for assets above defined
  thresholds or where owner has strong incentives to understate.
- **Documented comparables:** where comparables are used, the comparable set and adjustments must be disclosed.

Illiquidity cannot be treated as a reason for exemption. If anything, illiquidity increases the need for conservatism.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Illiquid assets MUST be included in W-base with conservative valuation; “cannot value” is not a valid exemption.
- If independent valuation is required by regime rules and not provided, the system must fail closed or apply a
  conservative default valuation for enforcement.

## B00.CH05.P05.S03 — IP/private companies: standard methods and prohibited manipulation patterns

IP and private companies are major cap-base assets and major valuation manipulation surfaces. OBA requires a standard
method menu and bans typical manipulation patterns.

Canonical admissible method families (to be parameterized by regime, but explicit):
- **Income-based methods** (discounted cash flow with disclosed discount rates and scenario bands),
- **Market comparables** (multiples with disclosed comparable set and adjustments),
- **Cost-based methods** (replacement cost where income comparables are weak), used conservatively.

Required disclosures:
- cashflow definitions and normalization,
- discount rate methodology and sensitivity,
- treatment of related-party transactions and transfer pricing,
- treatment of control premiums and preferential rights.

Prohibited manipulation patterns (non-exhaustive):
- shifting profits out via transfer pricing to depress valuation while retaining control/benefit,
- loading entities with related-party “liabilities” to net down W (circular leverage),
- “down rounds” or internal transactions at non-arm’s-length prices used as faux market evidence,
- selective comparable sets chosen to minimize multiples,
- burying value in excluded wrappers (IP holding entities) while claiming low operating entity value.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Private company/IP valuation MUST be computed using declared standard methods with sensitivity bands; ad hoc
  bespoke models without disclosure are non-conform.
- Evidence of manipulation patterns triggers requalification and conservative valuation overrides; repeated patterns
  may trigger enforcement escalation and hard-stop conditions (see CH05.P17).

## B00.CH05.P05.S04 — Point-in-time vs average: reference dates, smoothing, anti–window dressing

W is a stock measured at a time. Point-in-time measurement is vulnerable to **window dressing**: temporarily moving
assets out, repaying liabilities, or shifting exposures around measurement dates.

Canonical regime options (must be explicit and stable within a release):

- **Point-in-time (reference date):** measure W at specified dates; requires strong anti–window dressing rules.
- **Averaging (rolling window):** measure W as an average over a declared period (e.g., monthly average over year)
  to reduce date manipulation.

Anti–window dressing controls (minimum):
- disclosure of major transactions around reference dates,
- look-back/look-forward windows for suspicious transfers and repurchases,
- recharacterization of temporary transfers (repo-like moves, nominee parking),
- event-driven measurement triggers for large structural changes.

Where averaging is used, the window and weighting must be declared; where point-in-time is used, reference dates and
event-trigger rules must be declared.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST specify whether it uses point-in-time or averaging and must include explicit
  anti–window dressing controls.
- If measurement design allows easy date-based avoidance and controls are absent, the cap regime is leaky and
  non-conform for OBA-grade claims.
