# B00.CH05.P04 — Net and gross rule: debt and leverage

This paragraph defines how **debt and leverage** interact with **W (wealth/claims/rights)** and the **W-MAX** cap.
W is a stock of claims. Debt can be a legitimate liability, but it can also be engineered to bypass caps through
netting, collateral duplication, and structured “liability” labels that preserve control while shrinking reported W.

Therefore OBA requires:
- a canonical definition of **recognized liabilities** (what may reduce net W),
- anti-avoidance rules for leverage used as cap bypass,
- attribution rules for collateral and rehypothecation (double claim layers),
- and functional classification of synthetic debt/structured notes.

Default posture is conservative: where netting or leverage obscures economic control/benefit, the system must fail
closed or apply requalification.

---

## B00.CH05.P04.S01 — Net W: assets minus recognised liabilities (canonical liability definition)

Canonical **Net W** is:

- **W_net = W_assets − L_recognised**

where **L_recognised** are liabilities that meet all recognition criteria below.

A liability is *recognized* for W-MAX netting only if it is:

1) **Enforceable**: legally enforceable with a clear counterparty and terms.
2) **Non-circular**: not owed to a controlled/related party in a way that collapses under consolidation (UBC look-through).
3) **Non-illusory**: not contingent or artificially defeasible such that it does not represent a real reduction in
   claim position.
4) **Economically real**: incurred for real financing of assets/activities rather than engineered as a cap bypass.
5) **Non-duplicative**: not the product of collateral duplication that creates multiple claims on the same base while
   preserving effective control.

Liabilities failing these tests are not recognized for netting, or are recognized only with conservative haircuts or
limited netting allowances defined in the regime.

Boundary rule:
- liabilities are evaluated under the same consolidation boundary as assets (household/UBC graph). Related-party
  liabilities typically collapse unless the counterparty is outside the consolidated group and the obligation is
  non-avoidable and economically substantive.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST publish its liability recognition rules consistent with the criteria above.
- Netting against related-party or circular liabilities without UBC consolidation is non-conform.

## B00.CH05.P04.S02 — Leverage anti-avoidance: debt used to bypass the cap is requalified

Debt can be used to artificially reduce reported net W while preserving control and economic exposure.

Canonical avoidance patterns include (non-exhaustive):
- borrowing against assets to extract liquidity while keeping ownership/control (asset-backed borrowing as “cash-out”),
- circular borrowing within controlled structures,
- issuing “debt” instruments that function as equity/claim preservation,
- pledge-and-repurchase cycles designed to window-dress the cap date,
- leveraging to hold more cap-relevant assets while reporting lower net W through engineered liabilities.

Requalification rule:
- if the primary function of a liability is cap bypass (reducing reported W while preserving control/benefit), the
  liability is **requalified** (not recognized, or partially recognized) for W-MAX purposes.

Indicators of bypass intent (non-exhaustive):
- non-arm’s-length terms or counterparties,
- liability exists only around measurement dates (window dressing),
- proceeds used to acquire additional cap-base assets while maintaining control,
- repayment depends on the same assets in a way that preserves effective claim dominance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- W-MAX regimes MUST include leverage anti-avoidance tests and a requalification pathway; otherwise the base is leaky.
- If leverage structures cannot be audited sufficiently to assess bypass risk, the system must fail closed on netting.

## B00.CH05.P04.S03 — Collateral and rehypothecation: double claim layers and attribution

Collateralization and rehypothecation can create **multiple claim layers** on the same underlying asset base.

Canonical risk:
- one underlying asset supports several claims (lender claim, derivative claim, rehypothecated claim), creating
  stacked effective control and systemic fragility.

OBA treatment requires:
- identification of collateral chains and pledge rights,
- attribution of claim layers to controlling/benefiting units,
- preventing double counting that would understate W or misstate netting.

Canonical rules (high level):
- pledged assets remain in W-base for the pledgor unless control/benefit is truly transferred (substance test).
- the existence of collateral does not automatically create recognized netting; it may instead indicate higher
  effective claim layering and thus cap relevance.
- rehypothecation chains must be recorded to prevent “one asset, many claims” invisibility.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation operating in environments with collateral reuse MUST require collateral chain metadata or
  apply conservative assumptions; otherwise W is non-auditable and enforcement must fail closed or be provisional.
- Reporting net W while ignoring rehypothecation-driven claim stacks is non-conform for OBA-grade claims.

## B00.CH05.P04.S04 — Synthetic debt/structured notes: functional classification in W-base

Some instruments labeled “debt” are functionally **equity-like**, **option-like**, or **synthetic exposure**
instruments. Conversely, some structured notes create hidden leverage or embedded control rights.

Functional classification rule:
- classify by payoff and control reality, not by label.

Canonical examples (non-exhaustive):
- shareholder loans that behave like retained earnings with discretionary repayment,
- PIK notes and deeply subordinated instruments used to preserve control while claiming “liability” netting,
- structured notes that replicate equity exposure (thus W-base equivalents),
- convertibles/warrants embedded in “debt” that create equity-like claim positions.

Treatment:
- if the instrument provides exposure equivalent to cap-base assets, include the exposure in W-base as an economic
  equivalent (CH05.P03.S03).
- if the “liability” is not economically reducing the net claim position under recognition tests, do not net it.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST apply functional classification to structured notes and synthetic debt; label-based netting
  is non-conform.
- If embedded options/exposures cannot be valued or disclosed, conservative inclusion or provisional enforcement is
  required (fail-closed posture for ambiguity).
