# B00.CH05.P08 — Cap mechanics: what happens above W-MAX

This paragraph specifies the canonical **cap mechanics**: what occurs when consolidated **W** exceeds **W-MAX**.
W-MAX is only an order instrument if breach handling is:
- operationally explicit,
- non-discretionary,
- auditable,
- and robust against valuation games and wrapper moves.

Cap mechanics therefore define: trigger conditions, valuation rechecks, remedy set, duties and sequencing, and the
fail-closed posture under uncertainty.

---

## B00.CH05.P08.S01 — Trigger: breach, detection moment, revaluation

A **cap event** is triggered when:

- **W_consolidated > W-MAX** under the regime’s consolidation boundary (UBC look-through) and valuation rules.

Trigger specification must include:

- **Detection moment:** when breach is assessed (reference dates, event-driven triggers, periodic cycles).
- **Revaluation rule:** whether and how valuation is updated at trigger (market moves, transaction prices, illiquid
  valuation refresh). Revaluation prevents both stale undervaluation and opportunistic timing.
- **Material event triggers:** large transactions, restructures, transfers to vehicles, major derivative creation,
  cross-border moves, or control-right changes must trigger reassessment even between periodic dates.

Breach must be assessed on consolidated W (including equivalents and control premium attributes) to prevent
fragmentation avoidance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- A W-MAX regime MUST define detection timing and event triggers; “annual reporting only” without event triggers is
  leaky and non-conform where avoidance is feasible.
- Any cap event MUST be accompanied by a valuation snapshot and audit trail (inputs, basis, consolidation graph).

---

## B00.CH05.P08.S02 — Remedy options: skimming, conversion, lockbox, reallocation to C (canonical)

When W exceeds W-MAX, the regime must apply a canonical remedy set. Remedies must reduce breach and/or convert excess
claim storage into non-dominating forms, ideally feeding commons capacity under strict traceability.

Canonical remedy options (regime must specify which are available and under what constraints):

1) **Skimming (automatic capture of excess)**  
   - capture a defined portion/function of **(W − W-MAX)**.
   - must be non-discretionary and traceable.
   - destination typically follows W-MAX → C path (CH05.P09) under admissibility rules.

2) **Conversion**  
   - convert excess claim positions into instruments that do not preserve private dominance (e.g., non-transferable
     claims, capped-return instruments, time-locked commons notes), under defined rules.

3) **Lockbox / immobilisation**  
   - place excess assets/rights into a controlled lockbox structure where:
     - private control/benefit is constrained to a canonical bounded form,
     - governance prevents backflow and capture,
     - release conditions are explicit and auditable.

4) **Direct reallocation to C**  
   - allocate excess (or skim proceeds) to commons/public option capacity with traceable ΔG outputs and anti-capture
     safeguards.

Remedies must prevent “paper compliance” where excess is moved but economic power remains unchanged (e.g., parked with
nominees or swapped into excluded equivalents).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation MUST specify a remedy set and enforce at least one remedy path that cannot be bypassed by
  rewrapping.
- Remedy outcomes MUST be traceable and auditable; discretionary “settlements” that substitute for canonical remedies
  are non-conform.

## B00.CH05.P08.S03 — Choice space and duties: timing, order, burden of proof

Cap mechanics must define the **choice space** (if any) available to the subject and the **duties** associated with
remediation. Choice is a major gaming surface; it must be constrained.

Canonical requirements:

- **Timing duty:** remediation must occur within a defined window. Delays must not function as avoidance.
- **Order/sequencing rules:** define which assets/claims are remediated first (e.g., most liquid first, or those
  most directly tied to control rights), to prevent cherry-picking and preservation of dominance.
- **Disclosure duty:** subjects must disclose holdings, structures, and valuation inputs; failure triggers hard-stop
  enforcement escalation (CH05.P14/CH05.P17).
- **Burden of proof:** the subject bears the burden to prove exclusions, valuations, and independence of structures.
  Absence of proof defaults to inclusion/conservative valuation.
- **No self-serving selection:** subjects must not be allowed to choose remedies that preserve control while shedding
  only superficial value; control-right retention must be addressed explicitly (control premium is cap-relevant).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime allowing open-ended remedy selection without constraints on control rights is leaky and non-conform.
- The burden of proof MUST be specified and must default conservatively; “authority must prove avoidance” is not
  cap-robust and enables opacity strategies.

---

## B00.CH05.P08.S04 — Fail-closed: uncertainty about W → temporary cap enforcement until clarification

W valuation and consolidation can be uncertain, especially with illiquid assets, opaque vehicles, or derivative
exposures. Under OBA, uncertainty cannot become exemption.

Fail-closed rule (canonical):
- If W cannot be determined within auditable bounds, the system must apply **temporary cap enforcement** or treat
  the uncertain component as included at a conservative bound until clarified.

Canonical triggers for fail-closed enforcement include:
- missing disclosures for vehicles, nominees, trusts, side letters,
- non-reproducible valuation models or refusal of independent valuation,
- suspicious timing / window dressing patterns,
- unresolved overlap/conflict attribution in UBC graph,
- opaque derivative exposures or rehypothecation chains.

Temporary enforcement mechanisms may include:
- provisional skimming,
- immobilisation/lockbox until valuation is resolved,
- conservative default valuation/haircuts.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST implement fail-closed rules for uncertainty; otherwise opacity becomes a privileged loophole.
- If uncertainty persists without remediation, compatibility claims are suspended for the affected subject/scope and
  enforcement escalates under hard-stop provisions.
