# B00.CH05.P11 — Cross-border and migration: residency, exit and re-domiciliation

This paragraph defines the cross-border and migration interface for W-MAX. A wealth/claims cap is only robust if it
cannot be bypassed by:
- paper residency shifts,
- re-domiciliation of vehicles,
- treaty shopping,
- and cross-border payment/asset rail arbitrage.

Therefore W-MAX requires canonical **residency tests**, **exit treatment** for unrealised gains, look-through rules
for offshore structures, and coherence with capital controls and payment rails. Cross-border rules are not
optional add-ons; they are part of the cap’s non-leakage condition.

---

## B00.CH05.P11.S01 — Residency tests: actual living/control centres and sham residency

Residency for W-MAX purposes must be determined by **economic reality**: where life and control are actually
anchored, not where a certificate is issued.

Canonical residency tests include (non-exhaustive):

- **Centre of life:** primary dwelling, family location, schooling, health care, social ties.
- **Centre of economic control:** where key business control is exercised, where decisions are made, where key
  assets are managed, where boards/mandates are effectively directed.
- **Duration and continuity:** sustained presence/absence rather than short “counts days” games (day-count rules may
  exist but are not sufficient when control-centre evidence contradicts).
- **Sham indicators:** rapid jurisdiction hopping, nominee directors, mailbox addresses, artificial relocation of
  management, inconsistent documentation.

Canonical anti-sham rule:
- if formal residency conflicts with control/centre-of-life evidence, the regime treats the formal claim as sham
  and applies residency based on substance.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST define residency tests that include control-centre reality; day-count-only rules are leaky.
- If sham residency is plausible and evidence is missing, the system must fail closed (subject remains in scope or
  is treated provisionally in-scope) until resolved.

## B00.CH05.P11.S02 — Mark-to-exit: treatment of unrealised gains at departure

Exit and re-domiciliation can be used to “freeze” wealth growth outside the regime. To prevent this, W-MAX requires
canonical **mark-to-exit** treatment.

Canonical mark-to-exit principle:
- on departure (loss of residency/nexus), the subject’s cap-relevant positions are valued (under valuation discipline)
  and treated as having triggered a cap event on the unrealised component that would otherwise escape the regime.

Mark-to-exit design constraints:
- apply to cap-base assets and equivalents (including indirect holdings under UBC look-through),
- include illiquid assets with conservative valuation/bands,
- include deferred and wrapped exposures (trusts, foundations, nominee holdings),
- specify timing, reference date, and payment/settlement mechanics consistent with enforcement feasibility.

Purpose is coherence: exiting cannot be the primary avoidance route.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST define an exit treatment (mark-to-exit or equivalent mechanism) that prevents unrealised
  gains from escaping by migration.
- If exit treatment excludes major asset classes or vehicles, the cap becomes migratable and is non-conform for
  OBA-grade claims.

## B00.CH05.P11.S03 — Treaty shopping and offshore vehicles: look-through and anti-mismatch rules

Cross-border structures and treaty networks enable avoidance through mismatch: assets are placed in jurisdictions
where disclosure is weak or definitions differ.

Canonical controls:

- **Look-through offshore:** offshore vehicles are consolidated by UBC regardless of incorporation place.
- **Anti-mismatch rules:** where legal forms or accounting categories mismatch across jurisdictions, classification
  follows economic equivalence (substance-over-form) and the stricter cap-relevant treatment applies.
- **Treaty shopping detection:** detect routing through intermediate jurisdictions without real economic substance,
  using control-centre and nominee indicators.
- **Beneficial ownership transparency requirement:** participation in the cap regime requires minimum disclosure of
  beneficial ownership/control relationships; absence triggers fail-closed posture.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST treat treaty shopping and offshore vehicles as default avoidance surfaces and apply UBC
  look-through; otherwise the base is leaky by design.
- If beneficial ownership cannot be established due to jurisdiction opacity, the subject’s claim of exemption fails;
  conservative attribution or enforcement escalation applies.

## B00.CH05.P11.S04 — Coherence with capital controls and payment rails (consistency requirement)

W-MAX enforcement and reallocation depend on the ability to detect, value, and remedy claim positions. If capital
flows can bypass detection or if payment rails enable opaque transfer of cap-base value, coherence fails.

Canonical coherence requirements:

- **Rail coverage:** the regime must specify which payment/settlement rails are in-scope for reporting (banking,
  custody, broker-dealers, token custody, OTC settlement).
- **Capital control compatibility:** where capital controls exist, W-MAX must be coherent with them (avoid creating
  an incentive to evade controls; use consistent reporting and enforcement interfaces).
- **Event-driven reporting:** major transfers across borders, re-domiciliations, or custody changes trigger reporting
  and potential cap reassessment.
- **Asset mobility constraints:** where necessary to enforce remedies (lockbox, conversion), the regime may require
  immobilisation or reporting restrictions on cap-base assets under active enforcement.

Consistency is required to avoid a situation where the cap exists in text but is unenforceable in the real rail
infrastructure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST demonstrate operational coherence with payment/custody rails; otherwise the cap cannot claim
  OBA-grade compatibility.
- If the rail layer is opaque (unreported custody, untraceable transfers), the regime must treat affected positions
  under fail-closed rules and may suspend compatibility claims until rail coverage is remediated.
