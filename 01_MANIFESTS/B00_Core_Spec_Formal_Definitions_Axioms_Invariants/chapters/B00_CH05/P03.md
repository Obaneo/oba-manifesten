# B00.CH05.P03 — W-base: what counts in the cap base

This paragraph defines the canonical **W-base**: the set of holdings, exposures, and rights that count toward the
**W-MAX** cap base. W-base is not a “tax base”; it is an order-relevant base designed to be **non-leaky** under
wrapper proliferation, derivative replication, and control-right engineering.

Principle: if an instrument or structure provides a durable **economic claim** or **control-equivalent power** that
can sustain extraction capacity, it is cap-relevant and belongs in W-base unless a tightly bounded carve-out applies
elsewhere (CH05.P10).

---

## B00.CH05.P03.S01 — Direct holdings: shares, bonds, cash, real estate, participations

Direct holdings are included in W-base when the evaluated unit holds them on its own balance sheet or in directly
registered accounts.

Canonical direct inclusions (non-exhaustive):

- **Cash and deposits** (incl. cash equivalents, money-market instruments where effectively cash).
- **Public and private equity** (shares, partnership interests, membership units with claim rights).
- **Bonds and loans receivable** (fixed income claims, notes, receivables with monetizable value).
- **Real estate and land interests** (ownership, long leaseholds where monetizable, beneficial interests).
- **Participations** (direct stakes in entities, including partial interests).
- **Receivable claim rights** (royalty receivables, structured payout rights) when transferable/monetizable.

Direct holdings are valued under valuation discipline (CH05.P05) and netting rules (CH05.P04) but remain within the
base unless explicitly excluded by a canonical carve-out.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- W-base MUST include both liquid and illiquid direct holdings; “liquid-only” bases are non-canonical and leaky.
- Any exclusion of a direct holding class requires a canonical carve-out and must be parameterized and auditable.

---

## B00.CH05.P03.S02 — Indirect holdings: vehicles, funds, SPVs, trusts (look-through)

Indirect holdings are included via **look-through**: W-base follows economic control/benefit rather than legal title.

Canonical look-through targets include (non-exhaustive):
- **funds** (mutual funds, ETFs, private funds),
- **SPVs and holding companies** (including layered chains),
- **trusts and foundations** (discretionary, charitable, hybrid),
- **nominee and custodial structures**,
- **cross-holdings** and circular ownership where net exposure must be resolved via graph consolidation.

Look-through is governed by **UBC** (control/benefit/block) consolidation rules (CH05.P06). The cap base must
attribute underlying exposures to the ultimate controlling/benefiting unit, subject to conflict and overlap rules.

Opacity handling:
- if look-through data is missing or structures are intentionally opaque, the system must fail closed (temporary cap
  enforcement or non-certifying status) until clarity is provided.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST include look-through consolidation across vehicles; excluding vehicles is a hard leaky-base
  failure.
- Where look-through is not possible, conservative attribution or provisional enforcement is required; “unknown”
  cannot be treated as exempt.

---

## B00.CH05.P03.S03 — Economic equivalents: options, forwards, synthetic exposures, warrants

Economic equivalents are instruments that replicate exposure to underlying assets or flows without direct ownership.
They are included to prevent stock/derivative arbitrage.

Canonical economic equivalents include (non-exhaustive):
- **options** (calls/puts) and option-like rights,
- **forwards/futures** and committed purchase/sale rights with embedded value,
- **warrants/convertibles** and rights that become equity/claim positions,
- **total return swaps / synthetic exposure contracts** (functional replication),
- **structured notes** whose payoff replicates asset exposure.

Inclusion rule:
- count the economic value of the exposure and the embedded claim position under a declared method that is
  reproducible and conservative (valuation discipline applies).
- where a derivative creates exposure equivalent to holding the asset, it must be treated as cap-relevant even if
  legal form says “contract, not asset”.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Excluding derivatives and synthetic exposures from W-base is non-conform for any W-MAX claim (it enables immediate
  avoidance by replication).
- If valuation of equivalents is model-dependent, the model must be disclosed and auditable; otherwise the component
  is non-certifying and triggers fail-closed enforcement posture.

---

## B00.CH05.P03.S04 — Control premium: rights to block/steer as cap-relevant attribute

W-base includes not only value claims but also **control-relevant rights** that confer durable power to steer,
block, or extract rents. These rights carry a **control premium** that is cap-relevant even when cashflow claims
appear modest.

Canonical control-right inclusions (non-exhaustive):
- super-voting shares, veto rights, golden shares,
- board appointment rights, governance covenants that confer steering power,
- blocking rights over strategic assets, licensing, or pricing,
- preferential liquidation/priority rights that dominate outcomes in downside regimes,
- concession/permit rights that gate access to essentials markets.

Inclusion logic:
- control rights amplify extraction capacity and thus must be included as a cap attribute.
- valuation of the control premium may require conservative uplift rules or explicit treatment as an additive cap
  component (method must be declared, audited, and stable within a release).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-base that ignores control rights is non-conform because it leaves the primary power channel unmeasured.
- If control rights exist and cannot be valued audibly, the system must apply conservative treatment (uplift or
  provisional inclusion) rather than treating them as zero.
