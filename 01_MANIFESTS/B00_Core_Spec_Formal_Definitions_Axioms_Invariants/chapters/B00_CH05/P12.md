# B00.CH05.P12 — Anti-avoidance: typical patterns and requalification

This paragraph defines canonical **anti-avoidance** doctrine for W-MAX. Avoidance is expected: any cap on claim
storage creates incentives to rewrap, disperse, and synthetically replicate exposure. Therefore W-MAX must include
binding requalification rules that classify positions by **economic function**, not by legal label.

Anti-avoidance is not an “enforcement preference”. It is part of semantic closure: if avoidance patterns can pass by
interpretation, the cap becomes discretionary and non-auditable.

---

## B00.CH05.P12.S01 — Legal shifts: splitting, foundations, preferred rights, silent partnerships

Canonical legal-form avoidance patterns include (non-exhaustive):

- **Splitting / dispersion:** distributing assets across spouses, children, nominees, or controlled entities while
  retaining unified control/benefit.
- **Foundations and trusts:** placing assets into foundations/trusts with retained control, influence, or benefit
  via trustees, protectors, side letters, or informal power.
- **Preferred rights engineering:** converting common equity into preferred instruments, liquidation preferences, or
  super-votes to preserve control while manipulating “value” presentation.
- **Silent partnerships / nominee directors:** using silent partners or nominee governance to mask UBC while
  preserving steering power.
- **Contractual control without ownership:** governance covenants, veto rights, or concession rights that confer
  control-equivalent power while legal title is elsewhere.

Canonical response:
- apply UBC look-through (control/benefit/block) and substance-over-form classification.
- treat split structures that preserve unified economic reality as consolidated.
- treat retained control rights as cap-relevant attributes (control premium).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST treat these legal shifts as default avoidance surfaces and implement automatic look-through/
  consolidation triggers.
- If a structure cannot be audited to confirm independence, it is treated conservatively as consolidated (fail-closed).

---

## B00.CH05.P12.S02 — Financial shifts: securitisation, fee stacks, total return swaps, SPAC-like routes

Financial engineering can replicate asset exposure or convert stock claims into apparently “non-wealth” instruments.

Canonical financial avoidance patterns include (non-exhaustive):

- **Securitisation and tranching:** transforming ownership into tranches to park value in supposedly exempt slices
  while retaining effective upside/control.
- **Fee stacks:** shifting value extraction into management/consulting/royalty fee streams that preserve control and
  build new claim layers.
- **Total return swaps / synthetic replication:** replacing direct holdings with contracts that replicate returns
  while avoiding ownership reporting.
- **Structured notes:** embedding options/exposure inside “debt” to claim liability netting or hide equity-like value.
- **SPAC-like and pre-listing routes:** creating valuation discontinuities, warrant stacks, and vehicle layers that
  obscure cap-base attribution and timing.

Canonical response:
- classify by payoff exposure and control reality; include economic equivalents in W-base.
- detect fee-stack structures that are functionally claim positions and include them as claims/rights.
- apply anti–window dressing around major transactions and liquidity events.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime excluding synthetic exposures or structured replication is non-conform (it invites immediate arbitrage).
- Where financial instruments are opaque, conservative inclusion or provisional enforcement applies.

---

## B00.CH05.P12.S03 — Technical shifts: tokenisation, pseudo-decentralisation, custody constructions

Technical/legal-tech constructions can be used to create opacity, jurisdictional arbitrage, and nominee dispersion.

Canonical technical avoidance patterns include (non-exhaustive):

- **Tokenisation:** issuing tokens that replicate equity/royalty exposure while claiming “utility” or non-security
  status to avoid W-base inclusion.
- **Pseudo-decentralisation:** governance theater where a small group retains de facto control (admin keys, voting
  dominance, upgrade rights) while claiming no controller exists.
- **Custody constructions:** layered custody, omnibus accounts, offshore custodians, and “beneficial ownership unknown”
  claims.
- **Smart-contract wrappers:** encoding control/benefit through contracts while disclaiming ownership in legal terms.

Canonical response:
- apply substance-over-form and UBC-by-function (control/benefit/block) to technical governance reality.
- treat admin keys, upgrade rights, and concentrated voting as control rights (cap-relevant).
- require rail/custody disclosure sufficient for attribution; opacity triggers fail-closed.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Tokenised or “decentralised” exposure that is economically equivalent to cap-base assets MUST be included in W-base.
- If custody/beneficial ownership cannot be established, exemption fails and conservative attribution/enforcement applies.

## B00.CH05.P12.S04 — Requalification

Requalification is the canonical mechanism that converts avoidance forms back into cap-relevant substance.

### Core rule
If an arrangement has the **primary effect** of reducing measured W while preserving:
- economic exposure (upside/claims),
- control or block power,
- or benefit streams that function as wealth,

then the arrangement is **requalified** to its economic equivalent and included in W-base (and related liabilities may
be disallowed for netting).

### Triggers (non-exhaustive)
- non-arm’s-length counterparties or related-party chains,
- transactions concentrated around measurement dates,
- inconsistent governance reality vs formal documentation,
- embedded options/exposures that replicate ownership,
- backflow or circularity (funds return to the same UBC unit),
- refusal to disclose or non-reproducible valuation.

### Effects of requalification (canonical)
- include the requalified position in W-base at conservative value,
- collapse related-party liabilities where circular,
- attribute assets/exposures to the UBC unit via graph consolidation,
- escalate reporting and audit duty; repeated avoidance may trigger hard stops (CH05.P17).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST define requalification authority, trigger criteria, and conservative default treatment.
- Where evidence is insufficient to resolve form vs substance, the system MUST fail closed: treat as included until
  clarified, or suspend compatibility claims for the affected scope.
