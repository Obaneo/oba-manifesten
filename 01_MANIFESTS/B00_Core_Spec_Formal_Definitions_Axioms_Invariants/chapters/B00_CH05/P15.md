# B00.CH05.P15 — Interaction with I-MAX (substitution between stock and flow)

This paragraph defines the canonical interaction between **W-MAX** (stock cap) and **I-MAX** (income/flow cap).
A regime that constrains only flows invites conversion into stock; a regime that constrains only stock invites
consumption of flows and timing games. Therefore OBA requires explicit anti-arbitrage coherence between W-MAX and
I-MAX.

Core principle: **no stock/flow arbitrage**. Economic value cannot become exempt simply by changing its temporal
container (income vs wealth) or its label (salary vs carried interest vs perks).

---

## B00.CH05.P15.S01 — Deferral as avoidance: conversion of income→wealth (detection)

A primary avoidance strategy is **deferral**: reducing measured I (income) by converting compensation into
accumulating W (wealth/claims).

Canonical deferral patterns include (non-exhaustive):
- retaining profits in closely held entities rather than distributing (income suppression, wealth build-up),
- compensating via equity grants/options rather than salary,
- rolling compensation into deferred vehicles (insurance wrappers, “pension” shells) that are effectively controlled,
- using loans against future income/asset appreciation instead of realizing income,
- converting salary into fee streams or carried interests that accrue as asset value.

Detection requirements:
- compare subject’s operational receipts/profits to declared income flows; persistent divergence triggers review.
- monitor growth in W relative to declared I (W increases without corresponding I implies deferral).
- detect repeated patterns of low I with high capital gains/unrealised appreciation under subject control.

Canonical response:
- treat deferral mechanisms as cap-relevant in W-base (and, where appropriate, requalify into I-base under I-MAX rules).
- use event-driven triggers (liquidity events, buybacks, refinancing) to realize or attribute deferred value.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any combined W-MAX/I-MAX regime MUST include explicit deferral detection tests; absence makes the pair non-robust.
- If deferral cannot be audited due to opacity, fail-closed posture applies (conservative inclusion or provisional enforcement).

## B00.CH05.P15.S02 — Dividend/buybacks/carried interest: canonical treatment in W and I

Certain mechanisms sit on the boundary between stock and flow. They must be handled canonically to avoid conversion
games.

### Dividends
- Dividends are flow realization of underlying W; they typically count in I (flow side) and also affect W (stock side)
  via asset value changes and cash position changes.
- Regime must ensure dividends do not become “double exempt” (excluded from I while assets remain capped lightly).

### Buybacks
- Buybacks are flow-to-stock conversion mechanics: corporate resources are used to raise per-share value and can serve
  as wealth amplification without declared income.
- Canonical treatment requires recognizing buybacks as value realization pathways and monitoring them as deferral/avoidance signals.

### Carried interest
- Carried interest often labels labor/management remuneration as “investment return” to reduce I classification.
- Canonical rule: classify by function; if it is compensation for labor/control, it is I-relevant and/or creates W-equivalent claims.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime MUST state canonical classification rules for dividends, buybacks, and carried interest across W and I.
- Relabeling compensation as capital return to bypass I-MAX is non-conform; requalification is mandatory.

## B00.CH05.P15.S03 — Compensation via perks/benefits: requalification into I-base or W-equivalent

Perks and benefits can function as hidden income or as durable claim positions.

Canonical patterns include (non-exhaustive):
- employer-provided housing, transport, private services, personal staff,
- corporate asset use (jets, yachts, “business” residences) for personal benefit,
- below-market loans, forgiveness arrangements, insider purchase rights,
- access rights and concessions granted as compensation.

Canonical treatment:
- if the benefit is consumption-equivalent, requalify into **I-base** as income in kind (valued under declared rules).
- if the benefit confers durable rights/control (e.g., concession, preferential rights), treat as **W-equivalent**
  cap-base component.

Avoidance control:
- prevent systematic replacement of salary with perks to pass I-MAX while preserving standard of living and power.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any combined regime MUST include in-kind compensation rules and valuation methods; otherwise I-MAX is trivially bypassed.
- If perks are opaque or valuation is black-box, fail-closed treatment applies (conservative inclusion and enforcement escalation).

## B00.CH05.P15.S04 — Consistency requirement: no double exemption via stock/flow arbitrage

The combined system must enforce a consistency invariant:

- economic value must not be exempt both as a flow and as a stock by shifting form.

Canonical consistency requirements:

- **Mapping rule:** each value channel must map to either I-base, W-base, or both in a declared way; no channel may be “neither”.
- **No exempt wrappers:** vehicles that exempt I while also exempting W are forbidden unless canonically carved out
  with strict functional bounds (CH05.P10) and anti-privatisation rules.
- **Requalification symmetry:** if a channel is requalified in W-base as an economic equivalent, corresponding flow
  realizations cannot be treated as exempt (and vice versa).
- **Audit linkage:** reporting must support joint reconciliation of I flows and W stock changes at subject/cohort level.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any regime claiming both W-MAX and I-MAX MUST publish the stock/flow mapping and demonstrate non-arbitrage.
- If the system allows persistent low I with rapidly rising W through structured conversion without triggering
  requalification, the regime is non-conform for OBA-grade compatibility claims.
