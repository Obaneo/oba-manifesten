# B00.CH05.P10 — Exceptions and protected functions (tightly bounded)

This paragraph defines the canonical approach to **exceptions** under W-MAX. Exceptions are a primary capture and
avoidance surface. Therefore OBA permits exceptions only where they protect **functional life-world or productive
capacity**, and only under **tight bounds**, explicit parameterisation, and anti-avoidance controls.

Principle: exceptions protect **function**, not **status**. If an exception preserves private dominance or enables
storage arbitrage, it is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH05.P10.S01 — Functional assets: primary residence/work tools as parameterisable carve-outs

Certain assets have protected functional roles (shelter, basic productive capacity). A W-MAX regime may include
parameterisable carve-outs for such **functional assets**, but only under strict rules.

Canonical candidates (non-exhaustive):
- **Primary residence** (functional shelter, not investment portfolio).
- **Work tools** essential to productive activity (tools/equipment required to perform work).

Canonical carve-out constraints:
- **Definition:** functional use must be defined (occupancy/use tests, not ownership claims).
- **Boundedness:** carve-out must be capped by parameters (value ceiling, size ceiling, service-level ceiling).
- **Non-transfer gaming:** carve-out cannot be multiplied via vehicles, timeshare splitting, nominee parking.
- **Excess value treatment:** value above carve-out parameters remains cap-relevant (partial inclusion).

Carve-outs must not create “luxury shelter” loopholes or allow work-tool labeling of financial storage.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any functional carve-out MUST be parameterized and auditable (use tests + ceilings); “primary residence exempt”
  without bounds is non-conform.
- If a carve-out increases avoidance incentives (asset inflation or overconsumption of exempt categories), the regime
  must include anti-avoidance and may need revision under Kernspec procedure.

## B00.CH05.P10.S02 — Pensions/collective reserves: treatment, consolidation and anti-privatisation

Pensions and collective reserves can be legitimate risk-pooling and life-cycle smoothing mechanisms, but can also be
used as wrappers for private wealth storage and control.

Canonical treatment requirements:

- **Collective character test:** reserves qualify for special treatment only if they are genuinely collective,
  rule-bound, and not individually controllable/withdrawable as private wealth on demand.
- **Control/benefit boundaries:** where an individual has de facto control (self-directed vehicles, family office
  pension wrappers), look-through may apply and W inclusion is required.
- **Anti-privatisation:** structures that convert collective reserves into private controllable wealth (early access,
  bespoke borrowing against pension assets, self-dealing investments) trigger requalification into W-base.

The regime must specify how pension claims are valued and whether and how they count toward W, but must avoid
creating a universal “pension exemption” that becomes a storage channel.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Pension/collective reserve carve-outs MUST include consolidation and anti-privatisation rules; otherwise they are
  leaky and non-conform.
- If pension structures can be used as controlled investment vehicles for cap subjects, look-through inclusion is
  mandatory.

## B00.CH05.P10.S03 — Entrepreneurial tasks: genuine operational capex vs financial storage (boundary line)

W-MAX must not inadvertently block genuine productive/entrepreneurial operational capacity. However, “entrepreneurship”
is often used as a cover for financial storage.

Canonical boundary rule:
- **Operational capex** that is necessary for productive activity and is tied to real operations may receive
  protected treatment under defined parameters.
- **Financial storage** disguised as “business assets” remains cap-relevant.

Indicators of genuine operational assets (non-exhaustive):
- assets are used in operations (production/service delivery) and have a clear functional role,
- cashflow is primarily operational (P-dominant), not rent/fee-stack extraction,
- governance shows operational necessity and bounded reinvestment needs.

Indicators of storage disguised as operations:
- assets held primarily for appreciation, rent extraction, or control,
- shell entities with minimal operations but large asset bases,
- related-party transactions that shift value while preserving control.

Regime handling may include:
- time-bound operational allowances,
- audited operational-use tests,
- recapture rules if assets cease to be operational.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any entrepreneurial/operational carve-out MUST be test-based and auditable; “business assets exempt” is non-conform.
- If operational assets become passive storage or extraction channels, requalification into W-base is mandatory.

## B00.CH05.P10.S04 — Ban on privilege exceptions: status/sector/legacy is no ground

Privilege exceptions are forbidden. W-MAX cannot contain carve-outs based on social status, political power, sector
identity, or legacy claims.

Forbidden exception bases include (non-exhaustive):
- “national champion” status,
- sectoral privilege (finance, tech, landholding),
- hereditary/legacy protection,
- political office or institutional position,
- bespoke negotiated exemptions.

Canonical rule:
- any exception must be justified by function and must be generalizable, parameterized, and auditable.
- discretionary exceptions create capture risk and destroy semantic closure.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime with status/sector/legacy exceptions is non-conform for OBA-grade compatibility claims.
- If exceptions are introduced outside canonical change procedure, the regime’s compatibility claim is suspended
  until reversed or canonically ratified (with impact analysis and anti-capture safeguards).
