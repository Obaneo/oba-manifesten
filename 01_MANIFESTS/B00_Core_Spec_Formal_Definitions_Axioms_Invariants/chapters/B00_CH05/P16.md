# B00.CH05.P16 — Interaction with R/B and macro stability

This paragraph defines how **W-MAX** interacts with **R/B** (life-world buffer indicator) and with macro stability.
W-MAX is justified in OBA not as redistribution aesthetics, but as an order mechanism: it constrains the stock of
claims that sustains extraction capacity, which otherwise expresses itself as:
- essential burdens and claim layers (**L**) rising,
- remaining space (**R**) shrinking,
- and systemic fragility increasing (higher shock transmission, higher leverage and concentration).

Therefore W-MAX must be evaluated partly by its causal effects on L and R/B profiles, and must include monitoring and
constraints to prevent compensatory extraction responses.

---

## B00.CH05.P16.S01 — Target path: W caps lower claim pressure on essentials (L↓) and raise R/B

Canonical target path is:

**W caps → weaker claim stacks/control concentration → reduced toll capacity (E↓) → essentials pressure (L↓) →
remaining space (R↑) → cohort R/B profiles improve (tail above α rises).**

Mechanisms (non-exhaustive):
- reduced ability to outbid for scarce essentials purely via stored claims,
- reduced monopoly funding capacity and political capture capacity,
- reduced incentive and capacity for fee-stack layering in essentials markets,
- reallocation to C (where admissible) that expands public option capacity and lowers structural L.

Evaluation requirement:
- the regime must not claim success merely by reducing reported W; it must show the expected direction of movement in
  L and R/B profiles (with uncertainty), or explain why macro conditions dominate in the period.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX implementation claiming OBA compatibility MUST define the target path to R/B and specify measurable
  indicators (L components, R/B tails, essentials indices) used to test it.
- If W falls but L rises structurally and the regime offers no diagnostic, the claim of order improvement is non-conform.

---

## B00.CH05.P16.S02 — Crisis regime: shock absorption without reintroducing claim privileges

In crisis regimes (financial crisis, supply shocks, war, pandemic, major energy/housing shocks), governments may use
interventions that risk reintroducing claim privileges (asset bailouts, rent guarantees, privileged liquidity lines).

Canonical crisis regime requirements:

- **Shock absorption without privilege restoration:** interventions may stabilise essentials and prevent collapse, but
  must not permanently recreate private claim stacks that W-MAX is designed to constrain.
- **Sunset and rollback:** crisis exemptions or temporary mechanisms must include sunset clauses and rollback rules.
- **Conditionality:** if support flows to asset holders or large vehicles, it must be conditional on:
  - compliance with W-MAX disclosure and consolidation,
  - restrictions on distributions/buybacks/fee extraction,
  - and (where relevant) conversion into C-relevant capacity or public option expansion.
- **Audit traceability:** crisis measures must remain auditable and mapped to the canon (ΔM rules, W rules, C rules).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Crisis interventions that create durable claim privileges (permanent guarantees, privileged recapitalisations) without
  cap-consistent conditions are non-conform with W-MAX compatibility.
- Temporary deviations must be versioned, sunsetted, and audited; otherwise crisis becomes the loophole.

---

## B00.CH05.P16.S03 — Forbidden compensation: price hikes/monopolies as reaction to caps

A predictable reaction to caps is compensatory extraction:
- raising prices in essentials,
- tightening market power,
- expanding monopoly rents,
- or shifting extraction into less-regulated claim channels.

Canonical forbidden compensation patterns include (non-exhaustive):
- coordinated or unilateral **price hikes** in essentials sectors following cap enforcement,
- increased **bundling** and fee-stack layering to preserve returns,
- intensified **monopoly** or platform gatekeeping practices,
- shifting to **shadow fees** and contractual lock-ins.

Canonical response requirement:
- treat compensatory extraction as an E-regrowth signal and trigger enforcement/audit escalation (in P/E/C and in W/I linkages),
- apply anti-avoidance and anti-gaming tools; do not treat compensation as “market response” outside scope.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- A W-MAX regime MUST include monitoring and escalation against compensatory extraction in essentials; otherwise W-MAX
  can be neutralised by price channel responses and is non-conform as an order instrument.
- If compensatory price hikes are observed and ignored, claims that W-MAX improves R/B are non-conform.

## B00.CH05.P16.S04 — Monitoring: indicators for avoidance, concentration and E regrowth

W-MAX must be accompanied by a monitoring layer that tests whether the cap is functioning or being bypassed.

Canonical monitoring indicators (minimum set; parameterized but required in spirit):

### (a) Concentration indicators
- share of W held by top percentiles within the measured boundary,
- tail mass above W-MAX threshold and persistence over time,
- concentration of control rights in essentials-relevant sectors.

### (b) Avoidance indicators
- growth in opaque vehicles, nominee structures, offshore custody share,
- spikes in derivatives/synthetic exposures replacing direct holdings,
- window-dressing signals around measurement dates,
- increased related-party liabilities used for netting.

### (c) E-regrowth / L-pressure indicators
- fee-stack growth and shadow fees in essentials baskets,
- monopoly pricing indicators and markups in essentials sectors,
- L components rising faster than underlying functional cost indices,
- divergence between W reduction and L/R/B outcomes.

Monitoring must feed back into:
- requalification actions (CH05.P12),
- enforcement escalation (CH05.P14),
- and regime parameter review (CH05.P07 under Kernspec procedure).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST publish a monitoring set and use it to trigger audits/requalification; “monitoring as PR”
  without escalation logic is non-conform.
- If monitoring shows structural audit failure (opacity dominance, persistent avoidance), the regime’s compatibility
  claim must be suspended under fail-closed hard-stop logic (CH05.P17).
