# B00.CH05.P02 — Canonical definition of W (wealth/claims/rights)

This paragraph defines **W** as the canonical object for wealth/claims/rights in OBA. W is not a cultural notion of
“richness”; it is a measurable stock of **net claim position** that confers durable power over future flows, scarce
assets, and institutional outcomes.

W is defined functionally: any position that behaves as an economically meaningful claim on future flows or control
is within the semantic scope of W, regardless of legal form, label, or accounting presentation.

---

## B00.CH05.P02.S01 — W as stock: net claim position on future flows and assets

**W** is a **stock variable** representing the evaluated unit’s **net claim position** on:

- future income/receipts generated by assets and contracts,
- scarce real assets and their service flows,
- and enforceable rights that can be converted into future flows (including via sale, pledge, or monetization).

Canonical properties of W:

- **Stock (not flow):** W is measured at a reference time (or over a declared averaging window) and represents a
  balance-sheet-like claim position.
- **Net claim position:** W reflects claims net of recognized liabilities under canonical liability rules (detailed
  in CH05.P04), but anti-avoidance applies where “netting” is used to bypass the cap.
- **Order relevance:** W matters because it can be stored, leveraged, pledged, and used to sustain control and
  gatekeeping. It is therefore the primary storage variable that enables persistent extraction capacity.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W computation MUST declare: reference date (or averaging window), valuation basis, and consolidation boundary
  (including UBC look-through where applicable).
- Any attempt to treat W as “only liquid assets” or “only taxable wealth” is non-canonical unless explicitly mapped
  and shown to be non-leaky relative to the canonical W definition.

---

## B00.CH05.P02.S02 — Components of W: financial assets, real assets, IP, concessions, control rights

W includes economically meaningful claim positions across the following canonical component classes (non-exhaustive):

### Financial assets and claims
- cash and deposits,
- bonds and loans receivable,
- listed and unlisted equity,
- fund units and participations,
- derivatives and structured exposures (handled in base rules, but within W scope as claims).

### Real assets and scarce rights
- real estate and land interests,
- natural resource rights where transferable or monetizable,
- durable scarce assets whose service flows can be captured or pledged.

### IP and intangible claim assets
- patents, copyrights, trademarks when monetizable,
- licensing rights and royalty streams,
- platform/data rights that confer durable monetization power.

### Concessions and exclusive permissions
- concessions, licenses, quotas, franchises, spectrum rights,
- regulatory privileges that confer durable rents or control.

### Control rights (cap-relevant even when not “owned” as assets)
- voting/control rights in entities,
- rights to block/steer allocation or strategy,
- preferential rights (liquidation preference, super-votes),
- governance rights that confer durable extraction capacity.

The inclusion rule is functional: if the position is transferable, pledgeable, monetizable, or confers durable
control over flows, it is within W.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Implementations MUST treat control rights and concessions as cap-relevant attributes where they confer durable
  extraction capacity, even if accounting treats them as “off-balance-sheet”.
- Excluding entire classes (e.g., private equity, IP, concessions) without a canonical mapping creates a leaky base
  and is non-conform for W-MAX claims.

---

## B00.CH05.P02.S03 — Economic reality over legal form (substance over form)

W is governed by **substance over form**. Legal wrappers do not change economic reality.

Canonical substance rules include (non-exhaustive):

- If a person/household retains economic control/benefit/block power over an asset through a vehicle, the asset is
  within their W scope under consolidation rules (UBC look-through).
- If an instrument is structured to replicate exposure (synthetic, derivative, option) it remains within W scope as
  an economic equivalent claim.
- If rights are distributed across nominees but the economic benefit/control is unified, W is unified in substance.

This rule prevents W-MAX avoidance by wrapper proliferation and formal fragmentation.

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any W-MAX regime MUST apply substance-over-form equivalence rules consistently; otherwise the cap is cosmetic.
- Where substance cannot be determined due to opacity, the regime must fail closed (temporary enforcement or
  non-certifying status) until clarified.

---

## B00.CH05.P02.S04 — Prohibition of “non-wealth” labels for economic equivalents

It is forbidden to exclude economic claim positions by labeling them “non-wealth” through semantics, accounting
presentation, or contractual wording.

Non-conform “non-wealth” labels include (non-exhaustive):
- “control but not ownership” structures that confer durable control and monetization,
- “loan” arrangements that function as disguised distributions or equity value,
- “membership” or “usage” rights that are transferable and monetizable like property,
- “token utility” claims that replicate equity/royalty exposure,
- “charitable” or “foundation” structures that are controlled/benefited by a household in substance.

Canonical rule:
- If two positions are economic equivalents in exposure and control, they are equivalent in W scope unless a
  canonical exclusion applies (and exclusions are tightly bounded elsewhere).

### Conformance requirements (binding)

- Any claimed exclusion from W scope MUST demonstrate that the position is not an economic equivalent claim and does
  not confer durable control/benefit/block power.
- If equivalence is plausible and evidence is missing, the position must be included conservatively or flagged and
  treated under fail-closed rules for cap enforcement.
